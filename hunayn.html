The Battle of Hunayn

In Shawwal of year eight the Hawazin and Thaqif gathered their fighting men against the new conquest of Mecca, the twelve-thousand-strong Muslim army broke in the ambush at Hunayn, and the day was saved by the handful who stood. Part 26 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Gathering and the Rout

The conquest of Mecca lit the fuse under the Hawazin. Malik ibn Awf gathered them with all of Thaqif, and the old warlord Durayd ibn al-Simma heard the families' noise in the camp and knew Malik had brought the women and children as a fence.

In Shawwal of year eight the Prophet ﷺ marched out twelve thousand strong, and the army that had taken Mecca without a sword walked into the ambush and broke. The Qur'an names the fault, the multitude that pleased them.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"'When he came down at Awtas the people gathered to him, among them Durayd ibn al-Simma, and when he had come down he said, "In what valley are you?" They said, "Awtas." He said, "A good ground for horses, no rough ridge and no soft sand. Why do I hear the groaning of the camels, the braying of the asses, the crying of the children, and the bleating of the sheep?" They said, "Malik ibn Awf al-Nasri has driven down with the people their wealth and their women and their children." He said, "Where is Malik?" And it was said, "This is Malik," and he was called to him, and he said, "Malik, you have become the chief of your people, and this is a day whose outcome the days after it will carry. Why do I hear the groaning of the camels, the braying of the asses, the crying of the children, and the bleating of the sheep?" Malik said, "I have driven down with the people their children, their women, and their wealth." He said, "And why?" He said, "I wanted to put behind every man his family and his wealth, so he would fight for them. [the source's 'was a man' is a slip for 'every man']" Durayd rounded on him and said, "A shepherd of sheep, by Allah. Does the defeated man guard anything? If it is for you, nothing benefits you but a man with his sword and his spear, and if it is against you, you are disgraced in your family and your wealth."'"

- Ibn Ishaq's Sira, carried by Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.2, pp.213-214

Commentary

The Sources for Hunayn

Source spine: Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.2, pp.213-214

Other reports: Musta'dhab al-Akhbar, p.300, on the twelve thousand and the date. The standers, in Mawsu'at al-Imam al-Husayn, vol.23, p.474, on Ibn Asakir's chain

Scripture: Qur'an 9:25 and 9:26, Uthmani text

Honest limits: the reports gathered here give the gathering, the counsel of Durayd, the force and the date, the standers, and the Qur'an's verdict on the rout. They do not narrate the ambush or the recovery blow by blow, beyond the Qur'an's verses

The reports carry one frame in complementary parts. Ibn Ishaq gives the gathering and the counsel, the Musta'dhab gives the numbers and the date, and the report of al-Husayn gives the stand. The line of al-Husayn ﵇ names the standers, on a chain the Sunni record carries too, Ibn Asakir's.

The Ambush and the Stand

Enemy: the Hawazin confederacy, all Thaqif, Nasr, Jusham, and Sad ibn Bakr, under Malik ibn Awf, with Durayd ibn al-Simma as counsel, mustered at Awtas, with Ka'b and Kilab of Hawazin absent on the roll's word

The families: Malik drove the wealth, women, and children down with the army, and Durayd's objection, kept in the reports, did not move them from the train

Muslim force: twelve thousand, ten thousand Medinans and two thousand of the new Muslims of Mecca, six nights into Shawwal

The turn: the ambush broke the army, and the Qur'an itself names the fault, the multitude that pleased them

The standers: al-Abbas, Ali ﵇, Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith, Aqil, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, al-Zubayr, and Usama ibn Zayd, around the Prophet ﷺ

The two halves of the day teach one lesson, that pride in numbers fails. The Muslim army, proud in its new multitude, broke at the ambush, and the Qur'an carries the diagnosis, the multitude that pleased them. The enemy chief kept the families there anyway, though Durayd had told him the fence would guard nothing in defeat and add nothing in victory.

What became of the families belongs to the follow-on. The standers named by al-Husayn ﵇ held, and the recovery recorded in the next verse followed. And among them was Ali ﵇, who had stood in the front rank since Badr.

What the Day Left

Tactical: the rout ended in victory, on the Qur'an's record of the recovery

Strategic: a great tribal coalition against the faith broken in the field, days after the conquest

Aftermath: the captives and the return of the families belong to the follow-on of al-Ta'if

Dead: uncounted in these reports, on either side

The strategic outcome closed the circle the conquest opened. Mecca was taken, and the coalition that assembled to undo the conquest was broken in the field. The captives question belongs to the follow-on, whose reports carry the return of the Hawazin families as the Prophet's ﷺ own act in the weeks after.

Was the Battle of Hunayn Justified

The march was the pursuit of a war the enemy had begun. The Hawazin confederacy gathered its fighting tribes on the news of the conquest, and the roll's own word has Malik resolving to march against the Messenger of Allah. The Muslims could meet the confederacy in its own hills, or wait for it in Mecca's streets. The Prophet ﷺ marched out to meet it.

The conduct of the day is the Qur'an's own subject. The verse on Hunayn is a rebuke to the victors, naming the pride of numbers as the cause of the rout. The aftermath the follow-on carries, the families held as captives and then returned, completes the verdict with mercy.

Prevention lay with the confederacy. It gathered on the news of the conquest, and Malik resolved to march against the Messenger of Allah, on the roll's word. That resolve made the battle a question of ground, and the ground was Hunayn.
