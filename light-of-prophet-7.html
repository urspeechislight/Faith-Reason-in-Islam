Hadith of the Light

Part 7: The Light Through the Generations to Hashim

Bihar al-Anwar, Volume 15, pages 49 to 55. Continued from Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib ﵇.

Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib ﵇ continued:

The light of the Prophet ﷺ passed from Adam to Seth, the first inheritor. From Seth it passed to his son Anush, and from Anush to Qaynan, and from Qaynan to Mahlail, and from Mahlail to Yarid, and from Yarid to Akhnukh, who is Idris ﵇, the one whom God raised to a high place.

From Idris ﵇ the light passed to Matushalakh, and from Matushalakh to Lamak, and from Lamak to Noah ﵇. And when the Flood came and wiped the earth clean, the light rode with Noah ﷺ in the Ark. It was carried through the waters, safe in the body of the one whom God had chosen to save.

After the Flood, the light passed to Sam, the son of Noah. From Sam to Arfakhshad, from Arfakhshad to Abir, from Abir to Qali, from Qali to Argho, from Argho to Sharigh, from Sharigh to Takhur, and from Takhur to Tarakh. And Tarakh was the father of Abraham ﵇, the friend of the Merciful.

Into Abraham ﵇ the light entered, and from Abraham it passed to his firstborn son, Ishmael ﵇, the one whom Abraham was commanded to sacrifice and whom God ransomed with a great offering. Ishmael ﵇ carried the light to Mecca, to the valley where his father had left him and his mother as a trust to God. And there, in the land of the Kaaba, the light found its home.

From Ishmael ﵇ the light passed through a long line of fathers and sons: Qaydhar, al-Humaysa, Nabt, Yashhub, Udud, Adnan, Ma'add, Nizar, Mudar, Ilyas, Mudrika, Khuzayma, Kinana, Qusayy, Luayy, Ghalib, and Fihr. Each one carried the light and passed it on, and none of them ever bowed to an idol.

From Fihr the light passed to Abd Manaf. And Abd Manaf, on his deathbed, gathered his sons and spoke to them of the trust that had been placed in their line. He said:

"This light that has been carried through our fathers is a trust from God. It can only be placed in a pure womb. Never let it enter a womb that has been defiled by idolatry or impurity. Choose your wives from the purest of women, women of upright character and untainted ancestry, so that the light remains unstained until it reaches the one for whom it was created."

This instruction became the foundation of the doctrine of the pure wombs. Every ancestor of the Prophet ﷺ, from Adam to Abdullah, was a believer. None of them worshipped an idol. None of them was born of a union that God had not sanctioned. The vessel that would carry the light had to be clean, and it was clean at every link in the chain.

From Abd Manaf the light passed to his son Amr al-Ula. Amr al-Ula was a man of standing in Mecca, held in honour by his tribe, the Quraysh. He carried the light, and by this point in the chain the light had begun to show.

Earlier bearers had carried the light in their bodies, and no ordinary eye could see it. But now, as the chain drew closer to its destination, the light pressed outward. When Amr al-Ula approached the Kaaba, the walls of the Sacred House would glow. When he stood in prayer at night, his face threw a beam of light into the sky that the people of Mecca could see from their rooftops. The closer the light came to the Prophet's birth, the harder it became to contain.

One year, a severe famine struck Mecca. The people were starving. Amr al-Ula, who was a man of great wealth and greater generosity, took his stores of bread and crumbled them into broth and meat, feeding every pilgrim who came to the Sacred House until the famine passed. He crumbled and he fed, and the people said he had become Hashim, the one who crumbles. The name stuck. He was called Hashim from that day forward, and his birth name, Amr al-Ula, fell out of use.

Hashim was the first of his line to hold the offices of rifada and siqaya in Mecca: the duty of feeding the pilgrims and the duty of providing them with water. He organised the caravan trade, sending merchant expeditions north to Gaza and south to Yemen, bringing wealth into Mecca and establishing the Quraysh as a mercantile power. He was honoured by kings and common folk alike.

He married Salma, the daughter of Amr of the tribe of Najjar, a woman of pure lineage and upright character, and from her he conceived a son. But Hashim died in Gaza, far from Mecca, before the child was born. His body was buried there, in the city where he had traded, and the city is still called Ghazzat Hashim for his sake.

The child was born after his father's death. His name was Shayba al-Hamd, and the light passed into him. He would grow up to be known by another name, Abd al-Muttalib, the grandfather of the Prophet ﷺ. The chain was drawing close to its end. Only two links remained: Abd al-Muttalib, and then Abdullah, and then the light would reach the one for whom the heavens and the earth had been made.

Commentary

Light Becoming Visible

Earlier bearers of the light carried it without ordinary eyes being able to see it. By the time the line reaches Hashim, the light is showing. The Kaaba lights up when he approaches it. His face throws a beam into the sky. The closer it gets to the Prophet's birth, the harder it becomes to hide.

The Pure Womb Pledge

Abd Manaf's deathbed instruction is foundational in Imami teaching on the Prophet's lineage. The light could only be placed in pure wombs, women of upright character and untainted ancestry. None of the Prophet's ancestors ever fell into idolatry. Every link in the chain, from Adam to Abdullah, was a bearer of faith.

How Hashim Got His Name

Hashim's birth name was Amr al-Ula. The name Hashim comes from the Arabic verb hashama, meaning to crumble or to break into pieces. During a year of famine in Mecca, he took his bread, crumbled it into broth, and fed the pilgrims until the famine broke. The act defined him so completely that his birth name was forgotten. His descendants, the Banu Hashim, carry the name to this day.

Hashim's Death in Gaza

The historical record places Hashim's death in Gaza in Palestine. The city is still called Ghazzat Hashim for this reason. The Sayyid al-Hashim Mosque in Gaza City marks his burial place. His son Shayba, born after his death, would grow up to be Abd al-Muttalib, the grandfather of the Prophet ﷺ.
