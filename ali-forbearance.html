Historical Analysis

Why Imam Ali Did Not Fight for the Khilafa

Forbearance as guardianship: the evidence from sermons, hadith, and the shared historical record

Background

Ali ibn Abi Talib ﵇ was the first to believe in the Prophet and the man charged at every turn of the mission. Yet when the khilafa was settled upon another at the Saqifa, he did not rise with the sword. His own testimony in the sermon known as the Shaqshaqiyya declares the mantle was his, and in the same breath explains that he lowered a curtain over it.

Premise 1: The Claim Was Never Dropped

Ali ﵇ proclaimed his right to the khilafa openly and never ceased to protest it. The Shaqshaqiyya sermon, delivered from the pulpit, names the usurpation and the usurper in the hearing of the world.

Premise 2: The Helpers Were Not There

When Ali ﵇ called the people of Badr and the foremost Companions to his aid, only four answered: Salman, Abu Dharr, al-Miqdad, and al-Zubayr. Had he found forty, he would have fought.

Premise 3: A Charge Governed the Choice

The Prophet ﷺ had charged Ali ﵇ with a conditional command: fight if you find helpers for the religion; hold back your hand if you do not. His patience was obedience to that charge.

Conclusion: Guardianship of the Faith

Ali ﵇ weighed his claim against the survival of Islam itself and found the faith the heavier of the two. A war over the mantle of the Prophet ﷺ would have burned the religion to its roots before it could be won.

Why did Ali ﵇, the first to believe in the Prophet ﷺ, not rise with the sword when the khilafa was settled upon another at the Saqifa? His own sermon sharpens the puzzle: he declared the mantle his by right, and then described lowering a curtain over it.

The Puzzle Stated

Introduction

The Shaqshaqiyya sermon is the most celebrated address in Nahj al-Balagha. In it, Ali ﵇ declared that the man who donned the mantle of the caliphate knew full well that Ali's place in it was the pivot of the millstone, and yet that he lowered a curtain between himself and the office, caught between two choices: to strike with a hand cut off from helpers, or to endure a blind darkness in which the old would wither and the young grow grey.

Example

The sermon's opening passage sets the contradiction before the listener in a single breath:

أما والله لقد تقمصها فلان وإنه ليعلم أن محلي منها محل القطب من الرحى . ينحدر عني السيل ولا يرقى إلي الطير . فسدلت دونها ثوبا وطويت عنها كشحا . وطفقت أرتأي بين أن أصول بيد جذاء أو أصبر على طخية عمياء يهرم فيها الكبير . ويشيب فيها الصغير . ويكدح فيها مؤمن حتى يلقى ربه فرأيت أن الصبر على هاتا أحجى فصبرت وفي العين قذى . وفي الحلق شجا أرى تراثي نهبا

Amma wallāhi laqad taqammaṣahā fulānun wa-innahu la-ya'lamu anna maḥallī minhā maḥall al-quṭbi min al-raḥā. Yanḥidaru 'annī al-saylu wa-lā yarqā ilayya al-ṭā'ir. Fasudaltu dūnahā thawban wa-ṭuwaytu 'anhā kashḥan, wa-ṭufiqu arta'ī bayna an aṣūla bi-yadin jadhā'an aw aṣbira 'alā ṭakhiyatin 'amyā'a yahramu fīhā al-kabīru wa-yashību fīhā al-ṣaghīru, wa-yakdaḥu fīhā mu'minun ḥattā yalqā rabbahu, fara'aytu anna al-ṣabra 'alā hātā aḥjā, fa-ṣabartu wa-fī al-'ayni qadhan wa-fī al-ḥalqi shajan arā turāthī nahaban.

"By God, so-and-so wrapped himself in the caliphate, though he knew full well that my place in it is the place of the pivot in the millstone: the torrent descends from me, and no bird soars up to me. So I drew a curtain between myself and it, and turned my side away from it, and I began to weigh my choice: to strike with a severed hand, or to endure a blinding darkness in which the old man grows feeble, the young man's hair turns white, and a believer toils until he meets his Lord. And I saw that patience under these conditions was the wiser course. So I was patient, though there was dust in my eye and a bone lodged in my throat, as I watched my inheritance plundered."

- Sermons of Imam 'Ali (d.40 AH), Nahj al-Balagha, vol.01, p36
Implication

Ali ﵇ claimed the right in public, in prose that has outlived empires. The sermon names the usurper, names the station, names the act of lowering the curtain, and names the cost of the patience: a thorn in the eye and a bone in the throat. What remains is to account for the patience.

Conclusion

He named the usurpation openly, and he named the cost of enduring it.

What Would Falsify the Thesis

Introduction

The thesis would fall if the record showed that Ali ﵇ ever conceded the khilafa to another as a matter of right, or that he refrained from claiming it when a claim cost him nothing, or that his own explanation pointed to incapacity alone.

Implication

What the record must show is the opposite conjunction: unbroken protest of the right joined to an unbroken refusal of civil war, with the fear of the people's apostasy named as the reason the hand stayed sheathed.

The Imams of Ali's ﵇ House preserved their own account of those days. In that account, the reason for the stayed hand is named without ambiguity, placed on Ali's ﵇ own tongue at the most charged hour of that year.

The Confrontation at al-Baqi

Introduction

After Fatima's ﵍ death in Jumada al-Akhira of the eleventh year, Ali ﵇ buried her secretly at night and concealed her grave among forty fresh ones. When the community resolved to dig up the graves to find hers, Ali ﵇ came out to al-Baqi in anger, wearing the yellow cloak he put on in calamity, leaning on his sword Dhu al-Fiqar. When Umar pressed the matter, Ali seized him, threw him to the ground, and spoke the sentence on which this whole study turns.

Example

The report is carried in al-Tabari's Dala'il al-Imama, preserved by al-Majlisi in Bihar al-Anwar:

يا ابن السوداء أما حقي فقد تركته مخافة أن يرتد الناس عن دينهم ، وأما قبر فاطمة فوالذي نفس علي بيده ، لئن رمت وأصحابك شيئا من ذلك لأسقين الأرض من دمائكم ، فإن شئت فأعرض يا عمر

Yā ibna al-sawdā', ammā ḥaqqī faqad taraktuhū makhāfatan an yartadda al-nāsu 'an dīnihim, wa-ammā qabru Fāṭimata fa-walladhī nafsu 'Alīyyin bi-yadihi, la'in rumta wa-aṣḥābuka shay'an min dhālika la-asqiyanna al-arḍa min dimā'ikum, fa-in shi'ta fa-a'riḍ yā 'Umar.

"O son of the black woman! As for my own right, I let it go out of fear that the people would apostatize from their religion. But as for the grave of Fatima, by the One in whose hand is the soul of Ali ﵇, if you and your companions attempt any such thing, I will water the earth with your blood. If you have any sense, Umar, turn back."

- al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.43, p173
Implication

The restraint and its reason are uttered in one breath, and the drawn sword belongs to a different cause entirely. He had chosen endurance for the sake of the religion. For the sanctity of his wife's ﵍ grave, the same man was ready in an instant to water the earth with blood.

Conclusion

The religion was the measure of both the patience and the threat.

The Foretold Trial

Introduction

The House also preserved the Prophet's own warning that a fitna was coming after him. Sulaym ibn Qays relates that Ali ﵇ reported hearing the Messenger of God ﷺ declare a warning about the trials that would follow, naming them by name.

Example

Sulaym ibn Qays records the report in his book, the earliest surviving layer of the tradition:

اتَّقُوا فِتْنَةَ الْأُخَيْنِسِ ، اتَّقُوا فِتْنَةَ سَعْدٍ ، فَإِنَّهُ يَدْعُو إِلَى خِذْلَانِ الْحَقِّ وَأَهْلِهِ

Ittaqū fitnata al-Ukhaynis, ittaqū fitnata Sa'd, fa-innahu yad'ū ilā khidhlāni al-ḥaqqi wa-ahlih.

"Beware the trial of al-Ukhaynis; beware the trial of Sa'd, for he calls to the abandonment of the truth and of its people."

- Sulaym ibn Qays al-Hilali (d.76 AH), Kitab Sulaym ibn Qays al-Hilali, vol.02, p339
Implication

Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas took refuge in God from ever fighting Ali ﵇ and recited what he knew of the man: the station of Aaron at Tabuk, the one door left open upon the mosque, the banner of Khaybar, the hand taken at Ghadir Khumm. The warning and the catalogue traveled together through the tradition.

Conclusion

Ali ﵇ stood at the crossing of two certainties: he held the declared right, and he watched the foretold trial gather. To strike would have been to ignite the very fire the Prophet ﷺ had named.

The earliest book-layer of the tradition answers plainly: the patience was executed under an older command.

The Conditional Command

Introduction

Sulaym ibn Qays records Ali ﵇ recounting a private conversation with the Prophet ﷺ about what was coming. Ali asked what he should do when the community turned away. The Prophet's answer was a conditional charge, governed by a circumstance.

Example

The fullest recension of the report is preserved in al-Tabarsi's Mustadrak al-Wasa'il:

إن وجدت أعوانا فانبذ إليهم وجاهدهم ، وإن لم تجد أعوانا فكف يدك واحقن دمك ، حتى تجد على إقامة الدين وكتاب الله وسنتي أعوانا

In wajadta a'wānan fanbith ilayhim wa-jāhidhum, wa-in lam tajid a'wānan fakfuf yadaka wa-aqin damaka, ḥattā tajida 'alā iqāmati al-dīni wa-kitābi Allāhi wa-sunnatī a'wānan.

"If you find supporters, fling yourself against them and fight them; but if you find no supporters, hold back your hand and spare your blood, until you find supporters for the upholding of the religion, the Book of God, and my sunna."

- al-Tabarsi (d.1320 AH), Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.11, p75
Implication

The Prophet commanded patience under a condition. The sword was deferred, contingent on the existence of men willing to stand with him for the religion itself.

Conclusion

When Uthman was killed and Ali ﵇ finally found supporters, the same man who had held back for twenty-five years drew his sword without hesitation.

The Station of Harun

Introduction

The Prophet ﷺ named Ali's ﵇ station outright: he stood as Harun stood to Musa. And the Quran records what Harun did when he found the Children of Israel worshipping the Calf in his brother's absence. The analogy is the key to the whole charge.

Implication

When Musa returned and found his people worshipping the Calf, he seized his brother in anger. Harun answered: "Son of my mother, the people deemed me weak and were near to killing me; I feared you would say: You have divided the Children of Israel." Harun's forbearance was the refusal to split the community while its prophet was absent. On the Prophet's own analogy, Ali's ﵇ patience was Harun's patience: obedience to a standing charge.

Conclusion

The Imam ﵇ also did not attend the Saqifa because he was occupied with the burial of the Prophet ﷺ. The Imam stands as the Ka'ba stands: it is for the people to come to it.

The Shaqshaqiyya gives us Ali's ﵇ own verdict on his forbearance, delivered from the pulpit in his own voice.

A Thorn in the Eye

Introduction

The sermon's most haunting passage describes the patience itself. Ali ﵇ said he was patient, and the metaphors he chose are wounds: a mote in the eye and a bone in the throat.

Example

Al-Shirazi's edition of Nahj al-Balagha preserves the passage:

وطفقت أرتئي بين أن أصول بيد جذّاء أو أصبر على طخية عمياء ، يهرم فيها الكبير ، ويشيب فيها الصّغير ، ويكدح فيها مؤمن حتّى يلقى ربّه فرأيت أنّ الصّبر على هاتا أحجى ، فصبرت وفي العين قذى ، وفي الحلق شجا ، أرى تراثي نهبا ، حتّى مضى الأوّل لسبيله ، فأدلى بها إلى فلان بعده

Wa-ṭufiqu arta'ī bayna an aṣūla bi-yadin jadhā'in aw aṣbira 'alā ṭakhiyatin 'amyā'a, yahramu fīhā al-kabīru wa-yashību fīhā al-ṣaghīru, wa-yakdaḥu fīhā mu'minun ḥattā yalqā rabbahu, fara'aytu anna al-ṣabra 'alā hātā aḥjā, fa-ṣabartu wa-fī al-'ayni qadhan wa-fī al-ḥalqi shajan, arā turāthī nahaban, ḥattā maḍā al-awwalu li-sabīlihi fa-adlā bihā ilā fulānin ba'dahu.

"I began to weigh my choices: either I would strike out with a severing hand, or I would endure a blind darkness in which the old man grows decrepit, the young man turns gray, and the believer toils on until he meets his Lord. And I saw that patience in the face of all this was the wiser course. So I was patient, though there was a mote in my eye and a bone caught in my throat, watching my inheritance plundered, until the first of them went the way of all flesh and handed it over, after him, to a certain man."

- al-Shirazi (d.1422 AH), Nahj al-Balagha, vol.01, p62
Implication

The metaphors are deliberately physical: a wound carried in the two most sensitive places a man has: the eye that must keep watching and the throat that must keep swallowing.

Conclusion

The images insist that he felt every inch of the wrong, and bore it anyway.

The Rider of the Unbroken Camel

Introduction

Ali ﵇ compared the caliphate itself to an unbroken she-camel. The image captures the double bind: whatever the rightful holder does, the beast will hurt him or hurt those around him.

Example

Al-Gharawi gathers the proverb in his collection of the sermon's figures:

فصاحبها كراكب الصّعبة ، إن أشنق لها خرم ، وإن أسلس لها تقحّم

Fa-ṣāḥibuhā ka-rākibi al-ṣa'ba, in ashnaqa lahā kharama, wa-in aslasa lahā taqaḥḥama.

"Its companion is like the rider of an unbroken she-camel: if he draws her rein tight, he splits her nose; if he lets it go slack, she plunges headlong into ruin."

- Muhammad al-Gharawi, al-Amthal wa-l-Hikam al-Mustakhraja min Nahj al-Balagha, p230
Implication

If he took the office by force, they would call him a man of the world, to say nothing of the blood shed. If he held back in patience, they would call him incapable. His condition was exactly as he portrayed it.

Conclusion

Patience, in his telling, was the most painful of the duties that knowledge and covenant laid upon him.

The man who declared his right usurped was the same man to whom the holders of that office returned again and again, in private perplexity and in open court, whenever the law outran their knowledge of it.

The Woman Spared from Stoning

Introduction

A woman came to Umar and confessed to fornication. Umar ordered her stoned. Ali ﵇ intervened with a deduction from two verses of the Quran, the kind of reasoning that only mastery of the Book could produce on the spot.

Example

The case is recorded in Wasa'il al-Shi'a among the strange rulings of that era:

إن الله تعالى يقول (وحمله وفصاله ثلاثون شهرا) ، وقال تعالى (وفصاله في عامين) فالحمل ستة أشهر والفصال في عامين

Inna Allāha ta'ālā yaqūlu: (wa-ḥamluhu wa-fiṣāluhu thalāthūna shahran), wa-qāla ta'ālā: (wa-fiṣāluhu fī 'āmayn) fa-al-ḥamlu sittatu ashhurin wa-al-fiṣālu fī 'āmayn.

"God Almighty says, 'His gestation and weaning are thirty months,' and He also says, 'and his weaning is within two years.' The gestation is therefore six months, and the weaning falls within two years."

- al-Hurr al-'Amili (d.1104 AH), Wasa'il al-Shi'a, vol.01, p20
Implication

God says bearing and weaning together are thirty months; He says elsewhere the weaning is within two years. Subtract and six months remain for the bearing: a child delivered at six months can live. The ruling was reversed. Umar said: "Were it not for Ali ﵇, Umar would have perished."

Conclusion

The protest was against a usurpation of right, never against the law, the lives, or the faith of Muslims.

The Verse of the Trial

Introduction

The Quran warns of a fitna whose fire does not respect the line between guilty and innocent. Ibn al-Arabi, the Maliki jurist of Seville, treats the verse as a problem of legal theology and asks the simplest question: what is the fitna the believers are told to fear?

Example

The verse is from Surat al-Anfal:

وَاتَّقُوا فِتْنَةً لا تُصِيبَنَّ الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا مِنْكُمْ خَاصَّةً وَاعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ شَدِيدُ الْعِقابِ

Wa-ttaqū fitnatan lā tuṣībanna alladhīna ẓalamū minkum khāṣṣatan, wa-'lamū anna Allāha shadīdu al-'iqāb.

"And fear a trial which will by no means strike only those among you who did wrong; and know that God is severe in punishment."

- Ibn al-'Arabi (d.543 AH), Ahkam al-Qur'an, vol.02, p328

Ibn al-Arabi answers that the fitna is acts of abomination: when people see the wrongdoer and do not take hold of his hands, God may engulf them all. The punishment falls on the field, not the faction.

Implication

A war over the succession in 11 AH would not have punished the usurpers; it would have punished Islam, in a peninsula where the tribes were already weighing apostasy.

Conclusion

Once the sword is loose inside a community, the punishment falls on the field, not the faction.

The Verse of the Promise

Introduction

Against the warning stands the promise: God has pledged succession in the earth to those who believe and do righteous deeds. The commentators of the House read this verse as a specific designation.

Example

Al-Kulayni records the report in al-Kafi, the earliest comprehensive collection of the school's hadith:

سألت أبا عبد الله عن قول الله : وعد الله الذين آمنوا منكم وعملوا الصالحات ليستخلفنهم في الأرض كما استخلف الذين من قبلهم قال : هم الأئمة

Sa'altu Abā 'Abdi Allāhi 'an qawli Allāhi: "wa'ada Allāhu alladhīna āmanū minkum wa-'amilū al-ṣāliḥāti la-yastakhlifannahum fī al-arḍi kamā istakhlafa alladhīna min qablihim" qāla: hum al-a'imma.

"I asked Abu Abdullah ﵇ about the words of God: 'God has promised those among you who believe and do righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession upon the earth, just as He granted succession to those who came before them.' The Imam ﵇ answered, 'They are the Imams ﵈.'"

- al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, vol.01, p242
Implication

Asked about the verse, Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ identified its people by name: "They are the Imams ﵈." The succession in the earth had been pledged by God Himself to the household, and what God pledges no council can annul.

Conclusion

Ali's ﵇ forbearance, read through these lenses, was a Quranic act: the patience of a man who had read the end of the story in the Book and was willing to wait for it.

The raw facts were never the property of one side. Long before the argument over meaning hardened, the record itself had been written into books that neither camp could disown.

The Testimony of Miqdad

Introduction

During the shura that selected Uthman, Miqdad looked upon what had befallen the Prophet's ﷺ household and spoke. His words are preserved by al-Naqdi in al-Anwar al-'Alawiyya, carrying the report through Sulaym ibn Qays.

Example

Miqdad's testimony names the right, the abandonment, and the condition in a single breath:

والله ما رأيت مثل ما أتى على أهل هذا البيت بعد نبيهم ، اني لا عجب من قريش حين تركوا رجلا ما فيهم أعدل وأعلم بالله منه ، أما والله لو وجد أعوانا لقاتلهم كقتالي إياهم مع رسول الله يوم بدر

Wallāhi mā ra'aytu mithla mā atā 'alā ahli hādhā al-bayti ba'da nabiyyihim, innī lā u'jibu min Qurayshin ḥīna tarakū rajulan mā fīhim a'dala wa-a'lama bi-Allāhi minhu, amma wallāhi law wajada a'wānan la-qātalahum ka-qitālī iyyāhum ma'a rasūli Allāhi yawma Badr.

"By God, I have never seen anything like what has befallen the people of this House since their Prophet. I feel no astonishment at Quraysh for casting aside a man than whom there is no one among them more just or more knowing of God. By God, if I could find supporters, I would fight them as I fought them beside the Messenger of God ﷺ on the day of Badr."

- al-Shaykh Ja'far al-Naqdi (d.1370 AH), al-Anwar al-'Alawiyya, p334
Implication

The sentence carries the whole argument in a single breath: the right was clear, the abandonment was real, and the restraint was arithmetic. There were no supporters.

Conclusion

Miqdad swore: had he found supporters, he would have fought them as he once fought at the side of the Messenger of God on the day of Badr.

The Boundary of Forbearance

Introduction

The forbearance had a boundary, and the boundary is the final proof that it was obedience to a charge. Ibn Abi al-Hadid, the Mu'tazili commentator on Nahj al-Balagha, poses the obvious objection: if restraint preserved Islam at the Saqifa, why did Ali not extend the same restraint to the people of the Camel and to Muawiya?

Example

Ibn Abi al-Hadid poses the question and answers it in his own voice:

فإن قلت : فهلا سلم إلى معاوية وإلى أصحاب الجمل ، وأغضى على اغتصاب حقه حفظا للاسلام من الفتنة ؟ قلت : إن الجور الداخل عليه من أصحاب الجمل ومن معاوية وأهل الشام ، لم يكن مقصورا عليه خاصة ، بل كان يعم الاسلام والمسلمين جميعا

Fa-in qulta: fa-halā salima ilā Mu'āwiyata wa-ilā aṣḥābi al-jamali wa-aghḍā 'alā ghtaṣābi ḥaqqihi ḥifẓan li-al-islāmi min al-fitnati? Qultu: inna al-jawra al-dākhila 'alayhi min aṣḥābi al-jamali wa-min Mu'āwiyata wa-ahli al-shāmi lam yakun maqṣūran 'alayhi khāṣṣatan, bal kāna ya'ummu al-islāma wa-al-muslimīna jamī'an.

"If you ask, Why then did Ali ﵇ not yield to Mu'awiya and to the companions of the Camel, and why did he not close his eyes to the usurpation of his right, in order to preserve Islam from civil strife? I answer: The injustice that was brought upon him by the companions of the Camel, and by Mu'awiya and the people of Syria, was not confined to him alone; it encompassed Islam and the Muslims all together."

- Ibn Abi al-Hadid (d.656 AH), Sharh Nahj al-Balagha, vol.06, p167
Implication

The earlier injustice fell on Ali ﵇ alone; a wrong borne by one man can be swallowed for the sake of the whole. The wrong at Jamal and Siffin was a general assault on the religion and the community; and against that, forbearance had no warrant and the sword did.

Conclusion

The same rule governed both the stillness and the war.

Glossary of Key Terms

Saqifa: The portico of the Banu Sa'ida where the community gathered to settle the succession after the Prophet's ﷺ death.

Khilafa: The caliphate; the office of successor to the Prophet ﷺ in temporal and religious leadership.

Shaqshaqiyya: The most celebrated sermon in Nahj al-Balagha, named for Ali's ﵇ own comparison of its speech to a camel's foam that wells up and then subsides.

Fitna: Trial, civil strife; a testing that engulfs both the wrongdoers and the innocent.

Imam: The divinely appointed successor to the Prophet ﷺ in the teaching of the People of the House.

Final Verdict

His silence preserved the religion his sword would have shattered. The community that could not yet bear his rule could still be carried by his restraint, and the faith that survived Saqifa survived because the one man entitled to burn the house down refused to strike the match. His patience was the composure of the appointed, who knew whose promise would have the last word. And when the wrong ceased to be his alone and became the community's, the same fidelity that had held his hand now drew his sword.
