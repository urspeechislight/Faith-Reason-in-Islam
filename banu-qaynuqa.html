The Expedition Against Banu Qaynuqa

After Badr the Jewish tribe of Qaynuqa tore up its covenant with Medina, a Muslim and a Jew died over an assaulted woman in their market, and a fifteen night siege ended with the tribe spared, exiled, and disarmed at the plea of Ibn Ubayy. Part 6 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Covenant and the Market

Badr changed the balance inside Medina as much as outside it. The Jewish tribe of Qaynuqa lived inside Medina as goldsmiths and armorers. They were bound by a written covenant to the Prophet ﷺ. They saw the Muslim victory at Badr and broke the covenant. The Prophet ﷺ gathered them and warned them before anything else. They answered with defiance, and called themselves the men of war.

Then a woman was assaulted in their market. A Muslim killed her assailant, and the tribe killed him in return. The covenant was dead. The siege began in mid-Shawwal, on al-Waqidi's dating, some two months after Badr.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"Abd Allah ibn Jafar told me from al-Harith ibn al-Fadil from Ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi. He said. When the Messenger of Allah arrived in Medina, all the Jews made peace with him, and he wrote a document between himself and them. The Messenger of Allah joined each group to its allies, made a covenant of security with them, and laid conditions on them, and among the conditions was that they would not back an enemy against him. When the Messenger of Allah won at Badr and came back to Medina, the Jews turned on him, and they cut the covenant between themselves and the Messenger of Allah. The Messenger of Allah sent for them and gathered them, then said, 'Jews, accept Islam. By Allah, you know that I am the Messenger of Allah before Allah brings down on you something like what He brought on Quraysh.' They said, 'Muhammad, do not let what you met deceive you. You defeated a people with no experience of war. By Allah, we are the men of war, and if you fight us you will learn that you have never fought the likes of us.' While they stood in this open hostility and tearing up of the covenant, a woman of the Arabs from outside, married to a man of the Ansar, the native Muslims of Medina, came to the market of Banu Qaynuqa and sat at a goldsmith's shop of theirs about some jewelry of hers. A man of the Jews of Qaynuqa came and sat behind her without her knowing, and pinned the skirt of her dress to her back with a thorn. When the woman stood, she was exposed, and they laughed at her."

- al-Waqidi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.180, from Ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi

"In their transgression and open disbelief, a woman married to a man of the Ansar came to the market of Banu Qaynuqa and sat at one of their goldsmiths' about jewelry she had. A man of Banu Qaynuqa came and sat behind her without her knowing, and fastened the skirt of her dress to her back with a thorn. When she stood, she was uncovered and exposed, and they laughed at her. A Muslim man rose, followed him, and killed him. The Jews banded together against the Muslim and killed him, and they cast the covenant away against the Messenger of Allah. The verse came down about them, 'And if you fear treachery from a people, throw it back to them on equal terms. Allah does not love the treacherous.' It is also related that the verse came down about Banu Qurayza."

- al-Baladhuri, Ansab al-Ashraf, vol.1, p.342

The Prophet ﷺ marched on their stronghold and besieged it for fifteen nights, with Hamza carrying the banner, in the report preserved by the book of narrators the two communities share.

كان حمزة أول من قاتل المشركين بأمر النبي صلّى اللّه عليه واله ، حيث واجههم بثلاثين فارسا ، وكان يحمل اللواء بيده ، وفي غزوة قينقاع أيضا كان يحمل لواء الرسول صلّى اللّه عليه واله .

"He marched against them, and they had fortified themselves in their stronghold. He besieged them for fifteen nights. Then they came down to his judgment, and he ordered them bound. He put al-Mundhir ibn Qudama al-Sulami in charge of binding them and tying their hands."

- al-Baladhuri, Ansab al-Ashraf, vol.1, p.342, the siege

Then Ibn Ubayy intervened. He was the chief of the hypocrites, the Muslims of wavering loyalty whom the Qur'an names, and Qaynuqa were his allies. He grabbed the armor of the Prophet ﷺ himself and demanded mercy for them. They were the men who had shielded Ibn Ubayy in Bu'ath, the last great battle of Medina's tribes before Islam.▼ commentary

Commentary

The Sources for Qaynuqa

Source spine: al-Waqidi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.180, from Ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi

Same line, other carriers: al-Baladhuri, Ansab al-Ashraf, vol.1, p.342. al-Yusufi al-Gharawi, Mawsu'at al-Tarikh al-Islami, vol.2, p.205, quoting al-Waqidi

Shia lines: al-Ruwat al-Mushtaraka, vol.1, p.275, on Hamza and the banner. Ibn Jarir al-Tabari, al-Mustarshad, p.130, on the deputy

Scripture: Qur'an 8:58, Uthmani text, with a variant that ties it to Qurayza instead

The story rests on one transmission line. Al-Waqidi carries it from Ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi. Al-Baladhuri transmits its core, and al-Yusufi al-Gharawi quotes al-Waqidi directly.

Dependence is not corroboration, and all of these carriers go back to that one line. The Shia carriers join the line on every point they touch.

A Covenant Broken in the Market

Covenant condition: no aid to an enemy of the Prophet ﷺ

Breach: after Badr, open hostility and tearing up of the covenant

Warning: the Prophet ﷺ assembled them and invited them before any move

Defiance: their answer claimed the men-of-war mantle

Flashpoint: the assault on the woman, the killing of her assailant, the killing of the Muslim

The sequence matters. Before any sword moved, the Prophet ﷺ gathered the tribe and warned them in plain words. They answered with a threat. The market assault then cost one life on each side.

The tribe as a body cast off the covenant. Under the rule the Qur'an itself had laid down, treachery is answered by open, equal repudiation. That is how the action against them began, an open siege of a fortress, with no ambush and no massacre.

The Siege and the Intercession

Length: fifteen nights

Commander: the Prophet ﷺ. Banner, Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib. Deputy over Medina, Abu Lubaba

Surrender: the tribe came down to his judgment and was bound

Intercession: Ibn Ubayy, citing his four hundred armored and three hundred unarmored allies

Outcome: execution waived, exile to Adhri'at in Syria, wealth and arms taken

The intercession is the turning point of the story. Ibn Ubayy seized the collar of the Prophet's armor, and the Prophet ﷺ, though his face changed with anger, accepted the plea and spared the men. The tribe walked out of Medina with its families, and most of them soon perished in the Syrian border town where they settled. No companion is reported to have fled in this expedition, and the Muslims suffered no reported dead beyond the man killed in the market.

What the Expedition Left Behind

Tribe: spared, exiled, disarmed

Arms taken: a bow called al-Katum, which broke at Uhud, in al-Waqidi's line, among the wealth taken

Wealth: taken as forfeit, they were goldsmiths with no lands

Medina: one of the three Jewish tribes neutralized inside the city

The expulsion removed an armed, hostile enclave from inside the walls of Medina two months after Badr. Mecca was arming for revenge at the time. Strategically the gain was unity inside Medina, with Mecca already arming for revenge. The bow called al-Katum, taken here and broken later at Uhud, shows the scale of armament a hostile Qaynuqa held inside the city.

Was the Expedition Against Qaynuqa Justified

The case rests on the covenant. Qaynuqa had signed a written pact with conditions. The tribe broke it publicly after Badr, answered the Prophet's warning with a threat, and sheltered the killing of a Muslim. The pact was the tribe's own word, set down in writing, and it was broken in wartime.

The response stayed within limits at every step. The warning came first. The repudiation was open, on equal terms, as the Qur'an's own rule demands. The siege cost the tribe its stronghold and its arms.

The death sentence the surrender exposed them to was commuted at the plea of an ally, against the Prophet's visible anger. The penalty was the loss of the covenant's protection. It was the forfeit of arms and wealth, and removal from the city.

The tribe walked out alive. They left behind no lands, for they were craftsmen, and a craft travels with its owner.

The expedition could have been prevented. The Prophet ﷺ tried once, in the assembly where he invited them and warned them. Their answer closed that door.

The deeper prevention lay in Qaynuqa honoring the covenant it had signed. It also lay in not killing the man who answered the market assault. Both choices were theirs.
