Ibn Taymiyya on the Record of His Own School

The Sunni historians, the traveler who sat in his mosque, his own collected works, and the judges of Egypt and Damascus record the creed he taught in his own works, the grave-visitation rulings, imprisonments across twenty-three years, and a death in the citadel. A documentary investigation from the school's own sources.

← Faith & Reason in Islam Overview 1. The Talent 2. The Minbar 3. The Creed 4. The Ruling 5. The Trials 6. The Verdicts Conclusion

Taqi al-Din Ahmad ibn Taymiyya (661-728 AH, years of the Muslim calendar) is remembered today as a renewer of the Hanbali school, one of the four Sunni schools of law. His own tradition tells a fuller story. A biographical dictionary, a travel diary, his own collected works, and the later verdicts of the Shafi'i school document it. The talent is conceded, in the words of his own contemporaries and students. His collected works teach where God is. The creed incidents repeatedly put him before councils of judges. The grave-visitation ruling sent him to his final prison. A proclamation was made in Damascus in 705, and in 707 in Cairo a creed from his own hand was produced against him. Later authorities of the Shafi'i school and one Hanbali of his own line also recorded verdicts against him.

Every quotation below is cited to a Sunni source. Where a report survives only as later authors quote it, that later author is named. Where the record conflicts, the conflict is stated.

The Talent Conceded

Source: al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.178

Speaker: Abu Hayyan al-Andalusi, a leading grammarian of the age

Claim: he praised Ibn Taymiyya in verse, then broke with him permanently over one discourtesy about Sibawayh

Abu Hayyan of Granada met Ibn Taymiyya in Cairo and praised him in verse. Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani preserves the poem and the break that followed in al-Durar al-Kamina, a biographical dictionary of the eighth century AH.

لما اتانا تقي الدين لاح لنا داع إلى اللّه فرد ما له وزر على محياه من سيما الأولى صحبوا خير البرية نور دونه القمر حبر تسربل منه دهره حبرا بحر تقاذف من أمواجه الدرر قام ابن تيمية في نصر شرعتنا مقام سيدتيم إذ عصت مضر واظهر الحق إذ آثاره اندرست واخمد الشر إذ طارت له شرر كنا نحدث عن حبر يجئ بها (1) * أنت الامام الذي قد كان ينتظر ثم دار بينهما كلام فجرى ذكر سيبويه فاغلظ ابن تيمية القول في سيبويه فنافره أبو حيان وقطعه بسببه ثم عاد ذا ماله وصير ذلك ذنبا لا يغفر قال وحج ابن المحب سنة 34 فسمع من أبى حيان انا شيد فقرأ عليه هذه الأبيات فقال قد كشطتها من ديواني ولا اذكره بخير فسأله عن السبب

"When Taqi al-Din came to us, there appeared to us a caller to God, singular and bearing no burden. On his face were the marks of those who accompanied the best of created beings, a light before which the moon is dim. A learned scholar whose age clothed him in learning, a sea whose waves cast forth pearls. Ibn Taymiyya rose to support our law as Sayyid Tamim rose when Mudar rebelled. He made the truth manifest after its traces had been effaced, and he extinguished evil after its sparks had flown. We used to speak of a learned scholar who would come with it. You are the imam who had been awaited. Then words passed between them, and Sibawayh was mentioned. Ibn Taymiyya spoke harshly about Sibawayh, so Abu Hayyan broke with him and cut him off because of it. He then went back on all that he had said and made it an unforgivable sin. He said that Ibn al-Muhibb performed the pilgrimage in the year 734 and heard from Abu Hayyan that he had composed these verses. He recited them to him, and Abu Hayyan said, "I have erased them from my collected poems, and I do not mention him favorably." He asked him about the reason."

- Abu Hayyan's praise, in Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.178

Words passed between the two men, the grammarian Sibawayh was mentioned, and Ibn Taymiyya spoke harshly of him. Abu Hayyan broke with him over that single discourtesy, crossed the verses out of his collected poems, and said he would not mention him favorably.

The Minbar in Damascus

Source: Rihlat Ibn Batuta, vol.1, pp.316-317

Speaker: Ibn Batuta, who states he was present in Damascus

Claim: Ibn Taymiyya illustrated the divine descent from the minbar step, a Maliki jurist who objected was beaten by the crowd, and the Maliki and Shafi'i jurists' protest over the objector's punishment reached the sultan

وكان بدمشق من كبار الفقهاء الحنابلة تقي الدين ابن تيمية (221) كبير الشأن يتكلم في الفنون إلا أن في عقله شيئا ! وكان أهل دمشق يعظمونه أشد التعظيم ويعظهم على المنبر ، وتكلم مرة بأمر أنكره الفقهاء ورفعوه إلى الملك الناصر فأمر بإشخاصه إلى القاهرة وجمع القضاة والفقهاء بمجلس الملك الناصر وتكلم شرف الدين الزواوي المالكي (222) ، وقال : إن هذا الرجل قال : كذا ، وعدّد ما أنكر على ابن تيمية ، وأحضر العقود بذلك ووضعها بين يدي قاضي القضاة وقال قاضي القضاة لابن تيمية : ما تقول ؟ قال : لا إلاه إلا الله ، فأعاد عليه فأجاب بمثل قوله ، فأمر الملك الناصر بسجنه فسجن أعواما ، وصنف في السجن كتابا

"In Damascus there was among the leading Hanbali jurists Taqi al-Din Ibn Taymiyya, a man of great standing who spoke on various disciplines, except that there was something wrong with his mind. The people of Damascus held him in the highest reverence, and he preached to them from the minbar. On one occasion he spoke about a matter that the jurists condemned and brought before al-Malik al-Nasir. He ordered that Ibn Taymiyya be brought to Cairo. The judges and jurists were assembled in the council of al-Malik al-Nasir, and Sharaf al-Din al-Zawawi the Maliki spoke and said, "This man said such and such," and listed what had been condemned in Ibn Taymiyya. He produced the documents concerning this and placed them before the Chief Judge. The Chief Judge said to Ibn Taymiyya, "What do you say?" He said, "There is no god but God." He repeated the question, and Ibn Taymiyya answered in the same way. Al-Malik al-Nasir ordered him imprisoned, so he was imprisoned for years. While in prison, he composed a book."

- Ibn Batuta, Rihlat Ibn Batuta (Tuhfat al-Nuzzar), vol.1, p.316

Ibn Taymiyya was a leading Hanbali jurist of Damascus, a man of great standing who spoke on the sciences, and a man in whom the traveler found something wrong with his mind. The report of the sermon that follows is Ibn Batuta's own, from a Friday he says he attended in the congregational mosque.

لاقه إلى أن وقع منه مثل ذلك ثانية ، وكنت إذ ذاك بدمشق (223) فحضرته يوم الجمعة وهو يعظ الناس على منبر الجامع ويذكّرهم فكان من جملة كلامه أن قال : إن الله ينزل إلى سماء الدنيا كنزولي هذا ، ونزل درجة من درج المنبر ، فعارضه فقيه مالكي يعرف بابن الزهراء وأنكر ما تكلم به ، فقامت العامة إلى هذا الفقيه وضربوه بالأيدي والنعال ضربا كثيرا حتى سقطت عمامته وظهر على رأسه شاشية حرير ، فأنكروا عليه لباسها واحتملوه إلى دار عز الدين بن مسلّم قاضي الحنابلة فأمر بسجنه وعزره بعد ذلك ، فأنكر فقهاء المالكية والشافعية ما كان من تعزيره ورفعوا الأمر إلى ملك الأمراء سيف الدين تنكيز

"until the like of that occurred from him a second time. At that time I was in Damascus, and I attended him on Friday while he was preaching and reminding the people from the minbar of the congregational mosque. Among his words was his statement, "God descends to the lowest heaven as I descend in this way," and he descended one step of the minbar. A Maliki jurist known as Ibn al-Zahra objected to him and condemned what he had said. The common people rose against this jurist and struck him greatly with their hands and sandals until his turban fell off and a silk cap appeared on his head. They condemned him for wearing it and carried him to the house of Izz al-Din Ibn Muslim, the Hanbali judge. He ordered him imprisoned and afterward disciplined him. The Maliki and Shafi'i jurists condemned his having been disciplined and referred the matter to the commander of the emirs, Sayf al-Din Tankiz."

- Ibn Batuta, Rihlat Ibn Batuta (Tuhfat al-Nuzzar), vol.1, p.317

Preaching on the descent of God, Ibn Taymiyya descended one step of the pulpit to illustrate the manner of the descent. When the Maliki jurist Ibn al-Zahra objected from the congregation, the crowd beat him with hands and sandals until his turban fell, before any judge acted. The Hanbali judge of Damascus then imprisoned the objector and had him disciplined. The Maliki and Shafi'i jurists carried the protest to Tankiz, the commander of the emirs. The report carries no date of its own.

The Creed in His Collected Works

Source: Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.5, pp.41, 138, 165; Majmu' al-Rasa'il wa-l-Masa'il, vol.1, p.69

Speaker: Ibn Taymiyya, in his own collected works

Claim: God is above His heavens, on His Throne, separate from creation and in heaven, and the old formula denies only the how, not the reality of the attribute

The sermon Ibn Batuta heard does not stand alone. His collected works carry both the creed and the visitation ruling. Where God is, and what the verses that describe God's attributes mean, he answers in his own prose.

وأصل ضلال هؤلاء أنهم لم يعرفوا مباينة الله سبحانه للمخلوقات وعلوه عليها ، وعلموا أنه موجود فظنوا أن وجوده لا يخرج عن وجودها ، بمنزلة من رأى شعاع الشمس فظن أنه الشمس نفسها ، ولما ظهرت الجهمية المنكرة لمباينة الله وعلوه على خلقه افترق الناس في هذا الباب على أربعة أقوال ، فالسلف والأئمة يقولون : إن الله فوق سماواته على عرشه بائن من خلقه (1) كما دل على ذلك الكتاب والسنة

"The root of these people's error is that they did not know God's separateness from created things and His exaltedness above them. They knew that He exists, so they assumed that His existence did not lie outside their existence, like someone who sees the sun's rays and assumes that they are the sun itself. When the Jahmiyyah emerged, denying God's separateness from and exaltedness above His creation, people divided in this matter into four views. The early generations and the imams say that God is above His heavens, upon His Throne, separate from His creation, as the Book and the Sunnah indicate."

- Ibn Taymiyya, Majmu' al-Rasa'il wa-l-Masa'il, vol.1, p.69

People divided into four camps when the Jahmiyya emerged, an early group that denied that God is above His creation, and he names the position he counts his own. The Salaf, the earliest generations of Muslims, and the imams hold that God is above His heavens, upon His Throne, separate from His creation, on the authority of the Book and the Sunnah. That doctrine is what the later Shafi'i verdicts itemize under corporeality and direction.

وَحَدِيثُ { الْجَارِيَةِ لَمَّا سَأَلَهَا : أَيْنَ اللَّهُ ؟ قَالَتْ : فِي السَّمَاءِ . فَأَمَرَ بِعِتْقِهَا وَعَلَّلَ ذَلِكَ بِإِيمَانِهَا } . وَأَمْثَالُهُ كَثِيرَةٌ . وَأَمَّا الَّذِي يَدُلُّ عَلَيْهِ مِنْ " الْإِجْمَاعِ " فَفِي الصَّحِيحِ عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ - ﵁ - قَالَ : كَانَتْ زَيْنَبُ تَفْتَخِرُ عَلَى أَزْوَاجِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ تَقُولُ زَوَّجَكُنَّ أَهَالِيكُنَّ وَزَوَّجَنِي اللَّهُ مِنْ فَوْقِ سَبْعِ سَمَاوَاتِهِ .

"The hadith of the slave girl, when he asked her, "Where is God?" She said, "In heaven." He then ordered that she be freed and gave her faith as the reason for that. There are many similar reports. As for what this indicates from consensus, it is recorded in the Sahih from Anas ibn Malik, who said, "Zaynab would boast to the wives of the Prophet, saying, 'Your families married you off, while God married me from above His seven heavens.'""

- Ibn Taymiyya, Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.5, p.138

The proofs he reaches for are two texts about heaven. The Prophet ﷺ asks a slave girl where God is, she answers that He is in heaven, and he accepts the answer as faith itself. Zaynab ﵍ boasts before the other wives that God married her from above His seven heavens. From the second report Ibn Taymiyya derives a consensus.

وَتَارَةً يُخْبِرُ بِأَنَّهُ فِي " السَّمَاءِ " كَقَوْلِهِ تَعَالَى : { أَأَمِنْتُمْ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ أَنْ يَخْسِفَ بِكُمُ الْأَرْضَ فَإِذَا هِيَ تَمُورُ } { أَمْ أَمِنْتُمْ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ أَنْ يُرْسِلَ عَلَيْكُمْ حَاصِباً } . فَذَكَرَ السَّمَاءَ دُونَ الْأَرْضِ وَلَمْ يُعَلِّقْ بِذَلِكَ أُلُوهِيَّةً أَوْ غَيْرَهَا كَمَا ذَكَرَ فِي قَوْله تَعَالَى { وَهُوَ الَّذِي فِي السَّمَاءِ إلَهٌ وَفِي الْأَرْضِ إلَهٌ } وَقَالَ تَعَالَى { وَهُوَ اللَّهُ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَفِي الْأَرْضِ } . وَكَذَلِكَ قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ { أَلَا تَأْمَنُونِي وَأَنَا أَمِينُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ ؟ } { وَقَالَ لِلْجَارِيَةِ : أَيْنَ اللَّهُ ؟ قَالَتْ فِي السَّمَاءِ . قَالَ : أَعْتِقْهَا فَإِنَّهَا مُؤْمِنَةٌ } .

"At times, He states that He is in heaven, as in His saying, Most High is He, "Do you feel secure that He who is in heaven will cause the earth to swallow you, and then it will shake?" "Or do you feel secure that He who is in heaven will send against you a violent storm of stones?" Thus He mentioned heaven rather than earth, and He did not attach divinity or anything else to that, as He mentioned in His saying, Most High is He, "He is the God in heaven and the God on earth." And He said, Most High is He, "He is Allah in the heavens and on earth." Likewise, the Prophet said, "Do you not trust me, when I am the trustworthy one of Him who is in heaven?" And he said to the slave girl, "Where is Allah?" She said, "In heaven." He said, "Free her, for she is a believer.""

- Ibn Taymiyya, Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.5, p.165

The Qur'an itself, on his reading, informs that God is in heaven. The verses that say He who is in heaven he takes as words naming where God is. He sets them beside the verses He is the God in heaven and on earth, and the Prophet's ﷺ description of himself as the trustworthy one of Him who is in heaven. He gathers what God and the Messenger say about where God is, and the texts he cites name heaven.

فَقَوْلُ رَبِيعَةَ وَمَالِكٍ : الِاسْتِوَاءُ غَيْرُ مَجْهُولٍ وَالْكَيْفُ غَيْرُ مَعْقُولٍ وَالْإِيمَانُ بِهِ وَاجِبٌ مُوَافِقٌ لِقَوْلِ الْبَاقِينَ : أَمِرُّوهَا كَمَا جَاءَتْ بِلَا كَيْفٍ فَإِنَّمَا نَفَوْا عِلْمَ الْكَيْفِيَّةِ وَلَمْ يَنْفُوا حَقِيقَةَ الصِّفَةِ .

"The statement of Rabi'ah and Malik, "Istiwa is not unknown, its how is not comprehensible, and belief in it is obligatory," accords with the statement of the others, "Pass them on as they have come, without asking how." They only denied knowledge of the modality, and they did not deny the reality of the attribute."

- Ibn Taymiyya, Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.5, p.41

The old formula of the Salaf, pass the reports on as they came without asking how, was widely read among the kalam theologians as a refusal to say what the words mean. Ibn Taymiyya reads it the other way. The Salaf denied knowledge of the modality, and they did not deny the reality of the attribute. That is the reading at issue in Cairo in 707. A creed in his own handwriting was produced against him, denying that the establishment over the Throne, the istiwa of the Qur'anic verse, is according to its apparent sense.

The Ruling on Visiting Graves

Source: Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.27, pp.27, 253

Speaker: Ibn Taymiyya, naming authorities across the page break

Claim: travel to visit the graves of the prophets is disobedience, and the traveler does not shorten his prayer on that journey

The ruling that put Ibn Taymiyya in his final prison was his own, recorded in his collected fatwas.

بِأَنَّ الْمُسَافِرَ لِزِيَارَةِ قُبُورِ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ ﵈ وَغَيْرِهَا لَا يَقْصُرُ الصَّلَاةَ فِي هَذَا السَّفَرِ ؛ لِأَنَّهُ مَعْصِيَةٌ لِكَوْنِهِ مُعْتَقِداً أَنَّهُ طَاعَةٌ وَلَيْسَ بِطَاعَةِ وَالتَّقَرُّبُ إلَى اللَّهِ ﷿ بِمَا لَيْسَ بِطَاعَةِ هُوَ مَعْصِيَةٌ ؛ وَلِأَنَّهُ نَهَى عَنْ ذَلِكَ وَالنَّهْيُ يَقْتَضِي التَّحْرِيمَ . وَرَخَّصَ بَعْضُ الْمُتَأَخِّرِينَ فِي السَّفَرِ لِزِيَارَةِ الْقُبُورِ كَمَا ذَكَرَ أَبُو

"A traveler who travels to visit the graves of the prophets, peace be upon them, and others does not shorten the prayer during this journey, because it is a sin. This is because he believes it to be an act of obedience, when it is not an act of obedience. Seeking nearness to God, Mighty and Majestic, through what is not an act of obedience is a sin. It is also because he prohibited that, and a prohibition entails forbiddance. Some later scholars permitted traveling to visit graves, as Abu mentioned"

- Ibn Taymiyya, Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.27, p.27

A traveler who sets out to visit the graves of the prophets believes he is performing an act of obedience, and the act is not one. Seeking nearness to God through what is not obedience is disobedience, and the prohibition entails forbiddance. He notes that some later scholars permitted the travel, and the Arabic excerpt breaks off inside the name of the first of them. The next page completes the name, Abu Hamid, the author of the Ihya, followed by two more. He also answers the summary form of the charge himself.

فَحَكَوْا عَنْ الْمُجِيبِ أَنَّهُ يَنْهَى عَنْ زِيَارَةِ قَبْرِ الرَّسُولِ وَالسَّفَرِ إلَيْهِ وَيُحَرِّمُ ذَلِكَ وَيُحَرِّمُ قَصْرَ الصَّلَاةِ فِيهِ بِحَيْثُ جَعَلُوهُ يَنْهَى عَمَّا يَفْعَلُهُ الْحُجَّاجُ مِن السَّفَرِ إلَى مَسْجِدِهِ وَأَنَّ مَنْ سَافَرَ إلَى هُنَاكَ لَا يَقْصُرُ الصَّلَاةَ . وَهَذَا كُلُّهُ افْتِرَاءٌ وَبُهْتَان

"They attributed to the respondent the view that he forbids visiting the Messenger's grave and traveling to it, declares that forbidden, and declares it forbidden to shorten the prayer on that journey. Thus they made him forbid what pilgrims do when traveling to his mosque, and hold that whoever travels there may not shorten the prayer. All of this is fabrication and slander."

- Ibn Taymiyya, Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.27, p.253

The denial and the ruling sit together without contradiction, on his own terms. The ruling forbids the journey taken to visit, and the denial rejects the claim that he forbids the visit itself. Both texts are his, and the dossier Tankiz sent the sultan survives in the Rihla.

فكتب إلى الملك الناصر بذلك وكتب عقدا شرعيا على ابن تيمية بأمور منكرة منها : أن المطلّق بالثلاث في كلمة واحدة لا تلزمه إلا طلقة واحدة ، (225) ومنها أن المسافر الذي ينوي بسفره زيارة القبر الشريف زاده الله طيبا لا يقصر الصلاة ، وسوى ذلك مما يشبهه ، وبعث العقد إلى الملك الناصر فأمر بسجن ابن تيمية بالقلعة فسجن بها حتى مات في السجن .

"He wrote to al-Malik al-Nasir about this and drew up a legal document against Ibn Taymiyya concerning objectionable matters, among them that a man who pronounces a threefold divorce in one utterance incurs only one divorce, and among them that a traveler whose purpose in traveling is to visit the Noble Grave, may God increase it in goodness, does not shorten the prayer, along with other similar matters. He sent the document to al-Malik al-Nasir, who ordered Ibn Taymiyya imprisoned in the Citadel. He remained imprisoned there until he died in prison."

- Ibn Batuta, Rihlat Ibn Batuta, vol.1, p.317

The dossier named the rulings as errors. The threefold divorce counted as one. The pilgrim who intends the visit of the grave does not shorten his prayer. The order that followed confined Ibn Taymiyya to the citadel of Damascus, where he remained until he died.

Taqi al-Din al-Hisni, a Shafi'i scholar of Damascus who wrote his book against the visitation rulings, records what the ruling did to the student who preached it.

فاتفق أن ابن قيم الجوزية سافر إلى القدس الشريف ، ورقى على منبر في الحرم ووعظ ، وقال في أثناء وعظه ، بعد أن ذكر المسألة ، وقال : ها أنا راجع ، ولا أزور الخليل . ثم جاء إلى نابلس ، وعمل له مجلس وعظ ، وذكر المسألة بعينها حتى قال : فلا يزور قبر النبي ﵌ . فقام إليه الناس وأرادوا قتله ، فحماه منهم والي نابلس ، وكتب أهل القدس وأهل نابلس إلى دمشق يعرفون صورة ما وقع منه ، فطلبه القاضي المالكي ، فتردد ، وصعد إلى الصالحية إلى القاضي شمس الدين بن مسلم الحنبلي ، وأسلم على يديه ! فقبل توبته ، وحكم بإسلامه ، وحقن دمه ، ولم يعزره لأجل ابن تيمية . ولما كان يوم الجمعة رابع شعبان ، جلس القاضي جلال الدين بعد العصر بالمدرسة العادلية ، وأحضر جماعة من جماعة ابن تيمية كانوا معتقلين في سجن الشرع ، فادعى على إسماعيل بن كثير صاحب التاريخ أنه قال : إن التوراة والأنجيل ما بدلا ، وأنهما بحالهما كما أنزلا ، وشهدوا عليه

"It happened that Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyyah traveled to the noble Jerusalem. He climbed onto a pulpit in the Sanctuary and preached. During his sermon, after mentioning the issue, he said, "Here I am returning, and I will not visit al-Khalil." Then he came to Nablus, where a preaching gathering was arranged for him. He mentioned the very same issue, until he said, "One should not visit the Prophet's grave." The people rose against him and wanted to kill him, but the governor of Nablus protected him from them. The people of Jerusalem and the people of Nablus wrote to Damascus, reporting what had happened from him. The Maliki judge summoned him, but he hesitated. He went up to al-Salihiyyah to Judge Shams al-Din ibn Muslim the Hanbali and embraced Islam at his hands. He accepted his repentance, ruled that he was Muslim, and spared his blood. He did not discipline him on account of Ibn Taymiyyah. Then, on Friday, the fourth of Sha'ban, Judge Jalal al-Din sat after the afternoon prayer at the Adiliyyah School. He brought in a group from Ibn Taymiyyah's group who had been detained in the legal prison. He brought a claim against Isma'il ibn Kathir, the author of the history, that he had said, "The Torah and the Gospel were not altered, and they remain as they were when they were sent down." They testified against him."

- Taqi al-Din al-Hisni, daf' al-shubah 'an al-Rasul, p.213

Ibn Qayyim escaped with his life by submitting a renewed profession of Islam before a Hanbali judge. The historian Ibn Shakir al-Kutubi, whom al-Hisni introduces as one of Ibn Taymiyya's followers, records a follower leveling a charge of associationism inside the movement.

( تاريخ ابن تيمية كما نقله المؤرخ ابن شاكر ) وأزيد على ذلك ما ذكره صاحب ( عيون التواريخ ) ، وهو ابن شاكر ، ويعرف بصلاح الدين الكتبي وبالتريكي ، وكان من أتباع ابن تيمية ، وضرب الضرب البليغ ، لكونه قال لمؤذن في مأذنة العروس وقت السحر : أشركت ، حين قال : ألا يا رسول الله أنت وسيلتي * إلى الله في غفران ذنبي وزلتي وأرادوا ضرب عنقه ، ثم جددوا إسلامه . وإنما أذكر ما قاله لأنه أبلغ في حق ابن تيمية في إقامة الحجة عليه ، مع أنه أهمل أشياء من خبثه ولؤمه ، لما فيها من المبالغة في إهانة قدوته . والعجب أن ابن تيمية ذكرها ، وهو سكت عنها : كلام ابن تيمية في الاستواء ووثوب الناس عليه فمن ذلك ما أخبر به أبو الحسن علي الدمشقي - في صحن الجامع الأموي - عن أبيه ، قال : كنا جلوسا في مجلس ابن تيمية ، فذكر

"The history of Ibn Taymiyya as transmitted by the historian Ibn Shakir. I add to this what the author of Uyun al-Tawarikh mentioned. He was Ibn Shakir, known as Salah al-Din al-Kutubi and al-Turki. He was one of Ibn Taymiyya's followers, and he was severely beaten because he said to a muezzin in the Minaret of the Bride at dawn, "You have committed associationism," when the muezzin said, "O Messenger of God, you are my means to God for the forgiveness of my sin and my lapse." They intended to strike off his neck, then they renewed his profession of Islam. I mention what he said only because it is more forceful regarding Ibn Taymiyya in establishing the proof against him, even though he omitted things of his malice and baseness because they contained an excessive disparagement of his exemplar. It is remarkable that Ibn Taymiyya mentioned them while he remained silent about them. Ibn Taymiyya's words concerning establishment and the people's rushing upon him. Among this is what Abu al-Hasan Ali al-Dimashqi reported in the courtyard of the Umayyad Mosque from his father. He said, "We were sitting in Ibn Taymiyya's gathering when he mentioned""

- Taqi al-Din al-Hisni, daf' al-shubah 'an al-Rasul, p.88, relaying Ibn Shakir al-Kutubi

A muezzin called on the Prophet as his means to God, in lines he recited from the minaret before dawn. Ibn Shakir told the muezzin that he had committed associationism, the sin of calling on other than God. A crowd then moved to behead Ibn Shakir, and his Islam was renewed for him. Al-Hisni, transmitting the historian roughly a century after the events, says the beating was severe and remarks that the follower softened the record out of regard for his teacher.

Twenty-Three Years of Trials

Source: al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, pp.172-176

Chain: Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, from the court records and the historians of Cairo and Damascus

Claim: proceedings in 705, 707, 719, 720, and 726, and the citadel death of 728

The chronology that follows is Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani's. It opens in Damascus with the imprisonment of the hadith master al-Mizzi, whom Ibn Taymiyya went to the prison and brought out with his own hand.

فبلغ ابن تيمية فتوجه إلى الحبس فأخرجه بيده فبلغ القاضي فطلع إلى القلعة فوافاه ابن تيمية فتشاجرا بحضرة النائب واشتط ابن تيمية على القاضي لكون نائبه جلال الدين آذى أصحابه في غيبة النائب فامر النائب من ينادى ان من تكلم في العقائد فعل كذا به وقصد بذلك تسكين الفتنة ثم عقد لهم مجلس في سلخ رجب وجرى فيه بين

"This reached Ibn Taymiyyah, so he went to the prison and brought him out with his own hand. This reached the judge, so he went up to the Citadel. Ibn Taymiyyah met him there, and they quarreled in the presence of the deputy. Ibn Taymiyyah was excessive against the judge because his deputy, Jalal al-Din, had harmed his companions during the deputy's absence. So the deputy ordered someone to announce that whoever spoke about matters of creed, such-and-such would be done to him. By this he intended to calm the disturbance. Then he convened a session for them at the end of Rajab, and there took place between"

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.172

The judge of Damascus took the matter to the citadel. Ibn Taymiyya quarreled with him in the presence of the governor's deputy. The deputy had it proclaimed that whoever spoke on the creed would be punished, to still the disturbance. The council of Rajab 705 followed. In Ramadan came the summons to Cairo.

وعقد مجلس في ثالث عشر (3) منه بعد صلاة الجمعة فادعى على ابن تيمية عند المالكي فقال هذا عدوى ولم يجب عن الدعوى فكرر

"A council was convened on the thirteenth of that month after the Friday prayer. A claim was brought against Ibn Taymiyya before the Maliki judge. He said, "This is my enemy," and did not answer the claim. It was repeated"

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.172

Before the Maliki judge of Egypt the claim against him was read, and he would not answer it.

فأقيم من المجلس وحبس في برج * ثم بلغ الملكي ان الناس يترددون اليه فقال يجب التضييق عليه ان لم يقتل والا فقد ثبت كفره فنقلوه ليلة عيد الفطر إلى الجب وعاد القاضي الشافعي إلى ولايته ونودي بدمشق من اعتقد عقيدة ابن تيمية حل دمه وماله خصوصا الحنابلة فنودي بذلك وقرئ المرسوم وقرأها ابن الشهاب محمود في الجامع ثم جمعوا الحنابلة من الصالحية وغيرها واشهدوا على أنفسهم انهم على معتقد الامام الشافعي

"He was removed from the council and imprisoned in a tower. Then the Maliki was told that people were visiting him, and he said, "He must be kept under tighter confinement if he is not killed. Otherwise, his unbelief has been established." They moved him on the night of Eid al-Fitr to the pit. The Shafi'i judge returned to his office. A proclamation was made in Damascus that whoever held Ibn Taymiyya's creed, especially the Hanbalis, had made his blood and property lawful. This was proclaimed, the decree was read, and Ibn al-Shihab Mahmud read it in the congregational mosque. Then they gathered the Hanbalis from al-Salihiyya and elsewhere and made them testify against themselves that they followed the creed of Imam al-Shafi'i."

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.173

The Maliki judge stated the holding, that his unbelief was established and that he must be confined if not killed. A proclamation ran over Damascus that whoever held Ibn Taymiyya's creed had forfeited his blood and property. The Hanbalis of al-Salihiyya, a quarter of Damascus, were assembled to testify against themselves that they held the creed of al-Shafi'i. Two years later, in Cairo, a deposition was drawn against him and a creed in his own handwriting was produced.

فكتب عليه محضر بأنه قال انا اشعرى ثم وجد خطه بما نصه الذي اعتقد ان القرآن معنى قائم بذات اللّه وهو صفة من صفات ذاته القديمة وهو غير مخلوق وليس بحرف ولا صوت وان قوله الرحمن على العرش استوى ليس على ظاهره ولا اعلم كنه المراد به بل لا يعلمه الا اللّه والقول في النزول كالقول في الاستواء وكتبه أحمد بن تيمية ثم اشهدوا عليه انه تاب مما ينافي ذلك مختار أو ذلك في خامس عشرى ربيع الأول سنة 707 وشهد عليه بذلك جمع جم من العلماء وغيرهم وسكن الحال وافرج عنه وسكن القاهرة ثم اجتمع جمع من الصوفية عند تاج الدين ابن عطاء فطلعوا في العشر الأوسط من شوال إلى القلعة وشكوا من ابن تيمية انه يتكلم في حق مشايخ الطريق وأنه قال لا يستغاث بالنبي ﷺ فاقتضى الحال ان امر بتسييره إلى الشام فتوجه على خيل البريد . . . (1) وكل ذلك والقاضي زين الدين ابن مخلوف مشتغل بنفسه بالمرض وقد اشرف على الموت وبلغه سفر ابن تيمية فراسل النائب فرده من بلبيس وادعى عليه عند ابن جماعة وشهد عليه شرف الدين ابن الصابوني وقيل إن علاء الدين القونوى أيضا

"A document was written against him stating that he had said, "I am an Ash'ari." Then his handwriting was found containing the following text. "What I believe is that the Qur'an is a meaning subsisting in the essence of God. It is one of the attributes of His eternal essence. It is uncreated, and it is neither letter nor sound. His saying, 'The Most Merciful established Himself over the Throne,' is not according to its apparent sense, and I do not know the true nature of what is meant by it. Rather, no one knows it except God. The statement concerning descent is like the statement concerning establishment." It was written by Ahmad ibn Taymiyya. They then made him testify that he had repented from what contradicted this voluntarily. That was on the fifteenth of Rabi al-Awwal in the year 707. A large group of scholars and others testified to this against him. The matter became quiet, he was released, and he resided in Cairo. Then a group of Sufis gathered with Taj al-Din Ibn Ata. During the middle ten days of Shawwal, they went up to the Citadel and complained about Ibn Taymiyya, saying that he spoke concerning the shaykhs of the path and that he had said, "The Prophet is not to be appealed to for rescue." The circumstances required that he be ordered to be sent to Syria, so he set out on the postal mounts. . . . All this took place while Judge Zayn al-Din Ibn Makhluf was occupied with his illness and had come close to death. News of Ibn Taymiyya's departure reached him, so he sent a message to the deputy, who brought him back from Bilbays. He brought a claim against him before Ibn Jama'a, and Sharaf al-Din Ibn al-Sabuni testified against him. It was also said that Ala al-Din al-Qunawi did so."

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.174

The creed in his handwriting affirms that the Qur'an is neither letter nor sound. It affirms that the establishment over the Throne is not according to its apparent sense, and that the statement concerning descent is like the statement concerning establishment. It was the Ash'ari creed, the theology of the kalam school, and it marks how far the concession went. On the fifteenth of Rabi al-Awwal 707 the witnesses certified that he repented from whatever contradicted it, willingly by the record's own word. Later that same year the Sufis of Cairo complained to the citadel. They said he spoke against the masters of the path and taught that no aid is sought from the Prophet. He was sent back toward Syria.

ثم قاموا عليه في شهر رمضان سنة 719 بسبب مسألة الطلاق واكد عليه المنع من الفتيا ثم عقد له مجلس آخر في رجب سنة عشرين ثم حبس بالقلعة ثم اخرج في عاشوراء سنة 721 ثم قاموا عليه مرة أخرى في شعبان سنة 726 بسبب مسألة الزيارة واعتقل بالقلعة فلم يزل بها إلى أن مات في ليلة الاثنين العشرين من ذي القعدة سنة 728 قال الصلاح الصفدي كان كثيرا ما ينشد تموت النفوس باوصابها ولم تدر عوادها مابها

"Then they proceeded against him in Ramadan of the year 719 because of the question of divorce, and the prohibition against his issuing legal opinions was made more stringent. Then another council was convened for him in Rajab of the year 720, and he was imprisoned in the Citadel. He was then released on Ashura of the year 721. Then they proceeded against him once more in Shaban of the year 726 because of the question of visitation, and he was detained in the Citadel. He remained there until he died on the night of Monday, the twentieth of Dhu al-Qada, in the year 728. Salah al-Din al-Safadi said that he often recited, "Souls die from their pains, while those who visit them do not know what is in them.""

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.175

Proceedings over the divorce ruling in Ramadan 719 tightened the ban on his legal opinions. Another council in Rajab 720 sent him to the citadel, and Ashura 721 brought release. The visitation ruling brought proceedings again in Shaban 726, the citadel again, and this time no release. He died there on the night of Monday, the twentieth of Dhu al-Qa'da 728. Safadi's couplet, which Ibn Hajar sets at the close of the chronology, was one Ibn Taymiyya himself often recited.

قال ورثاه محمود بن علي الدقوقى ومجير الدين ابن الخياط وصفى الدين عبد المؤمن البغدادي وجمال الدين ابن الأثير وتقى الدين محمد ابن سليمان الجعبرى وعلاء الدين بن غانم وشهاب الدين ابن فضل اللّه وزين الدين ابن الوردي وجمع جم

"He said that he was elegized by Mahmud ibn Ali al-Duquqi, Mujir al-Din ibn al-Khayyat, Safi al-Din Abd al-Mumin al-Baghdadi, Jamal al-Din ibn al-Athir, Taqi al-Din Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Jabari, Ala al-Din ibn Ghanim, Shihab al-Din ibn Fadl Allah, and Zayn al-Din ibn al-Wardi, and a great multitude."

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.176

Eight poets are named, and the entry adds a great multitude beyond them. Ibn Hajar also transmits a letter on the mood of Egypt.

ان جميع من بمصر من القضاة والشيوخ والفقراء والعلماء والعوام يحطون على ابن تيمية الا الحنفي فإنه يتعصب له والا الشافعي فإنه ساكت عنه وكان من أعظم القائمين عليه الشيخ نصر المنبجى لأنه كان بلغ ابن تيمية انه يتعصب لابن العربي فكتب اليه كتابا يعاتبه على ذلك فما اعجبه لكونه بالغ في الحط على ابن العربي وتكفيره فصار هو يحط على ابن تيمية ويغرى به بيبرس الجاشنكير وكان بيبرس يفرط في محبة نصر ( ويعظمه وقام القاضي زين الدين ابن مخلوف قاضى المالكية مع الشيخ نصر ) (2) وبالغ في أذية الحنابلة واتفق ان قاضى الحنابلة شرف الدين الحراني كان قليل البضاعة في العلم فبادر إلى اجابتهم في المعتقد واستكتبوه خطه بذلك واتفق ان قاضى الحنفية بدمشق وهو شمس الدين ان الحريري انتصر لابن تيمية وكتب في حقه محضرا بالثناء عليه بال

"All those in Egypt among the judges, shaykhs, poor Sufis, scholars, and common people spoke against Ibn Taymiyya, except for the Hanafi, who was partisan toward him, and the Shafi'i, who remained silent about him. Among those most active against him was Shaykh Nasr al-Manbiji. Ibn Taymiyya had learned that he was partisan toward Ibn al-Arabi, so he wrote him a letter reproaching him for that. It did not please Nasr because Ibn Taymiyya had gone to extremes in disparaging Ibn al-Arabi and declaring him an unbeliever. Nasr therefore began to disparage Ibn Taymiyya and incite Baybars al-Jashankir against him. Baybars was excessive in his love for Nasr and in honoring him. Judge Zayn al-Din Ibn Makhluf, the Maliki judge, joined Shaykh Nasr and went to extremes in harming the Hanbalis. It happened that Sharaf al-Din al-Harrani, the Hanbali judge, had little learning, so he hastened to answer them concerning the creed, and they had him write out his statement on it in his own hand. It also happened that the Hanafi judge of Damascus, Shams al-Din al-Hariri, defended Ibn Taymiyya and wrote a document in praise of him with"

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.173

In the letter Ibn Hajar transmits, all of Egypt spoke against him, the judges, the shaykhs, the Sufis, the scholars, and the common people. The Hanafi judge stood excepted as his partisan and the Shafi'i as silent. Damascus differed. Its Hanafi judge Shams al-Din al-Hariri defended him and wrote a document in his praise.

The Verdicts of the Later School

Source: Ibn Hajar al-Haytami, al-Fatawa al-Hadithiyya, pp.114-116, relayed in later works; al-Hisni, daf' al-shubah; Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali, relayed by al-Hisni

Claim: the later authorities itemized his contraventions and recorded verdicts from condemnation to declared unbelief

The later Shafi'i authority Ibn Hajar al-Haytami of Mecca (d. 974 AH), a different Ibn Hajar from the author of al-Durar al-Kamina, itemized the contraventions in his collected verdicts. The list survives in relay and is quoted here as the relayers preserve it.

وأن العالم قديم بالنوع ولم يزل مع الله مخلوقا دائما فجعله موجبا بالذات لا فاعلا بالاختيار تعالى الله عن ذلك ، وقوله بالجسمية ، والجهة ، والانتقال ، وأنه بقدر العرش لا أصغر ولا أكبر ، تعالى الله عن هذا الافتراء الشنيع والكفر البواح الصريح

"And that the world is eternal in kind and has always been a created thing with God, so he made Him a necessary agent by His essence, not an agent by choice. Exalted is God above that. And his statement affirming corporeality, direction, movement, and that He is the size of the Throne, neither smaller nor larger. Exalted is God above this vile fabrication and manifest, explicit unbelief."

- Ibn Hajar al-Haytami, al-Fatawa al-Hadithiyya, p.116, relayed in Hasan ibn Ali al-Saqqaf, Tahnitat al-Siddiq al-Mahbub, p.49

The items are the doctrine of the eternity of the world in kind and the affirmations of corporeality, direction, and transfer for God, claims that He has a body, occupies a place above, and moves. The list also holds the affirmation that God is the size of the Throne, neither smaller nor larger. The same authority's summary verdict survives in the edition of al-Subki's Shifa' al-Saqam, and the quotation names him al-Makki, which is the same Ibn Hajar al-Haytami.

المكي في الفتاوى الحديثية ، ما نصه : ابن تيمية عبد خذله الله وأضله وأعماه وأصمه وأذله ، وبذلك صرح الأئمة الذين بينوا فساد أحواله ، وكذب أقواله ، ومن أراد ذلك فعليه بمطالعة كلام الإمام المجتهد المتفق على إمامته وبلاغه رتبة الاجتهاد أبي الحسن السبكي ، وولده التاج (5) . قال الإمام محمد بخيت المطيعي شيخ الإسلام : ولما تظاهر قوم في هذا العصر بتقليد ابن تيمية في عقائده الكاسدة وتعضيد أقواله الفاسدة . . . وجدنا كتاب الإمام الجليل

"Al-Makki wrote in al-Fatawa al-Hadithiyya as follows. Ibn Taymiyya was a servant whom God forsook, led astray, blinded, deafened, and humiliated. The imams who explained the corruption of his condition and the falsity of his statements explicitly said this. Whoever wishes to know this should consult the words of the imam, mujtahid, and universally acknowledged imam, Abu al-Hasan al-Subki, who attained the rank of ijtihad, and of his son Taj."

- Ibn Hajar al-Haytami, al-Fatawa al-Hadithiyya, quoted in the edition of al-Subki, Shifa' al-Saqam, p.38

Al-Hisni of Damascus, a century closer to the events than al-Haytami, wrote his book to answer the visitation rulings, and he writes as an open partisan of that cause. His verdict is his own position, not a finding the record establishes.

ابن تيمية من أعظم الكذبة والفجار) وإذا تقرر ذلك ، ففي ذكري ما أتبرع به - مع غاية الاقتصار - تتحقق أن ابن تيمية من أعظم الكذبة والفجار ، وقد انكشف لك ذلك كما انكشف ضوء النهار : فمن ذلك ما ذكره القاضي أبو الطيب - وهو من أئمة الشافعية - قال : ويستحب أن يزور قبر النبي ﵌ بعد أن يحج ويعتمر . وكيف يزور من غير سفر ، سواء كان راكبا أو ماشيا ؟ ! وقال المحاملي في كتابه (التجريد) ويستحب للحاج إذا فرغ من مكة أن يزور قبر النبي ﵌ انتهى . وقال الحليمي في كتابه (المنهاج) ، عند ذكر تعظيم النبي ﵌ ، وذكر جملة ، ثم

"Ibn Taymiyyah is among the greatest liars and wicked people. Once that is established, then by mentioning what I will contribute, with the utmost brevity, you will realize that Ibn Taymiyyah is among the greatest liars and wicked people. This has become clear to you as the light of day becomes clear. Among this is what Judge Abu al-Tayyib, one of the leading Shafi'i scholars, mentioned. He said, "It is recommended that one visit the Prophet's grave after performing the pilgrimage and the lesser pilgrimage." How could one visit without traveling, whether riding or walking? Al-Mahamili said in his book al-Tajrid, "It is recommended for a pilgrim, once he has finished in Mecca, to visit the Prophet's grave." End quote. Al-Halimi said in his book al-Minhaj, when discussing the veneration of the Prophet, and he mentioned a number of matters, then"

- Taqi al-Din al-Hisni, daf' al-shubah 'an al-Rasul, p.184

He builds it from a run of Shafi'i authorities on the visitation, from al-Qadi Abu al-Tayyib to al-Mahamili to al-Halimi.

وكان الشيخ زين الدين ابن رجب الحنبلي ممن يعتقد كفر ابن تيمية

"Shaykh Zayn al-Din Ibn Rajab the Hanbali was among those who believed Ibn Taymiyya to be an unbeliever."

- Taqi al-Din al-Hisni, daf' al-shubah 'an al-Rasul, p.214, on Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali

Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali, of Ibn Taymiyya's own school two generations later, is recorded by al-Hisni among those who believed him an unbeliever, and the report survives only in al-Hisni's relay. The entry continues on the following page.

وكان يقول بأعلى صوته في بعض المجالس : معذور السبكي ، يعني في تكفيره

"In some gatherings, he would say at the top of his voice, "Al-Subki is excused," meaning in his declaring him an unbeliever."

- Taqi al-Din al-Hisni, daf' al-shubah 'an al-Rasul, p.215, continuing the entry on Ibn Rajab

There Ibn Rajab is reported excusing al-Subki, the Shafi'i imam cited in the verdict above, for declaring him an unbeliever. Against all of this stands the obituary of al-Dhahabi, his own student, which Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani transmits.

وانا لا اعتقد فيه عصمة بل انا مخالف له في مسائل أصلية وفرعية فإنه كان مع سعة علمه وفرط شجاعته وسيلان ذهنه وتعظيمه لحرمان (1) الدين بشرا من البشر تعتريه حدة في البحث وغضب وشظف للخصم تذرع (2) له عداوة في النفوس والا لولا طف خصومه لكان كلمة اجماع فان كبارهم خاضعون لعلومه معترفون بشنوفه (3) مقرون بنذور (4) خطائه وانه بحر لا ساحل له وكنز لا نظير له ولكن ينقمون عليه اخلافا (5) وافعالا وكل أحد يؤخذ من قوله ويترك * قال وكان محافظا على الصلاة والصوم معظما للشرائع ظاهرا وباطنا لا يؤتى من سوء فهم فان له الذكاء المفرط ولا من قلة علم فإنه بحر زخار ولا كان متلاعبا بالدين ولا ينفرد بمسائله بالتشهى ولا يطلق لسانه بما اتفق بل يحتج بالقرآن والحديث والقياس ويبرهن ويناظر أسوة من تقدمه من الأئمة فله اجر على خطائه واجران على اصابته إلى أن قال تمرض أياما بالقلعة بمرض جد إلى أن مات ليلة الاثنين

"I do not believe him infallible. Rather, I disagree with him on fundamental and subsidiary questions. For despite the breadth of his learning, the extent of his courage, the quick flow of his mind, and his reverence for the sanctity of religion, he was a human being. Sharpness in debate, anger, and harshness toward an opponent would come over him and create enmity for him in people's hearts. Were it not for the petulance of his opponents, he would have been a point of consensus. Their leading figures submit to his learning, acknowledge his distinctions, admit the sins of his errors, and acknowledge that he is a sea without shore and a treasure without equal. But they resent in him beliefs and actions. Every person's statement may be accepted or rejected. He said that he was constant in prayer and fasting and reverent toward the sacred laws inwardly and outwardly. He was not to be faulted for poor understanding, for he possessed extraordinary intelligence, nor for lack of knowledge, for he was a surging sea. He was not one who toyed with religion, did not single out his legal questions according to personal desire, and did not let his tongue loose with whatever occurred to him. Rather, he argued from the Qur'an, hadith, and analogy, gave proof, and debated, following the example of the imams before him. He receives one reward for his error and two rewards for his correctness. Then he said that he was ill for days in the Citadel with a severe illness until he died on the night of Monday."

- al-Dhahabi, obituary of Ibn Taymiyya, in Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.177

Al-Dhahabi concedes the learning, the memory, the courage, and the devotion, and refuses him infallibility. He disagrees with him on fundamental and subsidiary questions. He names the sharpness in debate, the anger, and the harshness toward opponents as the traits that bred enmity toward him, and adds that his opponents' petulance kept him from being a point of consensus. He defends him from the charge of trifling with religion and of lone caprice in his rulings, and closes with days of severe illness and death in the citadel. The obituary is the assessment of a student who knew him, and the enmity it names is the same enmity the record shows reaching the rulers.

What the Record Supports

He taught, in his own collected works, that God is above His heavens, upon His Throne, separate from creation and in heaven, and that the attribute is real with only the how unknown. He ruled, in the same collection, that travel to visit the graves of the prophets is disobedience. He was summoned before the judges of Egypt in 705, and it was proclaimed in Damascus that whoever held his creed forfeited blood and property. In 707 the creed from his own hand, denying the apparent sense, was produced against him in Cairo. He was imprisoned repeatedly across twenty-three years, the last confinement in 726 for the visitation ruling, and he died in the citadel in 728. The grammarian Abu Hayyan praised him in verse and then broke with him. The later authorities of the Shafi'i school itemized his contraventions and recorded verdicts up to declared unbelief, in relay, and a Hanbali of his own line is recorded, in a single relay, holding that view.

The record also carries its qualifications. The minbar report is a traveler's eyewitness account, undated in the text of the Rihla. The 707 document exists, and its witnesses certified a repentance the record itself calls voluntary, so the motive question stays open. Ibn Taymiyya himself denounced as slander the summary form of the visitation charge, and his own volume holds both the ruling and the denial. The proclamation of 705 was issued amid the war of the judges of Cairo. And al-Dhahabi's obituary is the counterweight, a student's defense that concedes the sharpness and rejects the charge of trifling with religion.

The record also shows Shaykh Nasr al-Manbiji inciting the ruler Baybars against him over the Ibn al-Arabi letter, and the judges of Cairo fighting their own war through his case. The councils that tried him were councils of his own tradition's judges, the confinement orders were the sultan's and the governor's, and the verdicts are the verdicts of the school's own later authorities. The career is documented by the people who wrote the history of the tradition he belonged to.
