Ibn Taymiyya on the Record of His Own School

The Sunni historians, the traveler who attended his sermons, his own fatwa collection, and the judges of Egypt and Damascus record the creed incidents, the grave-visitation rulings, imprisonments across twenty-three years, and a death in the citadel. A documentary investigation from the school's own sources.

← Faith & Reason in Islam Overview 1. The Talent 2. The Minbar 3. The Ruling 4. The Trials 5. The Verdicts Conclusion

Taqi al-Din Ahmad ibn Taymiyya (661-728 AH) is remembered today as a renewer of the Hanbali school and the ancestor of a movement. His own tradition tells a fuller story. The biographical dictionaries, travel diaries, court records, and fatwa collections document it. The writers are the scholars who sat in his circles, judged his cases, and buried him. The talent is conceded first, in the words of his own contemporaries and students. Then the record turns. The creed incidents repeatedly put him before councils of judges. The grave-visitation ruling sent him to his final prison. There is a signed recantation of 707 and a proclamation over Damascus. Later authorities of the Shafi'i school and one Hanbali of his own line also recorded verdicts against him.

Every quotation below is cited to a Sunni source. Where a report survives only in a relay, the relay is named. Where the record conflicts, the conflict is stated.

The Talent Conceded

Source: al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.178

Speaker: Abu Hayyan al-Andalusi, the era's foremost grammarian

Claim: he praised Ibn Taymiyya in improvised verse, then broke with him permanently over one discourtesy about Sibawayh

Abu Hayyan of Granada met Ibn Taymiyya in Cairo and praised him in improvised verse. Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani preserves both the poem and what followed it in the authoritative biographical dictionary of the eighth century.

لما اتانا تقي الدين لاح لنا داع إلى اللّه فرد ما له وزر على محياه من سيما الأولى صحبوا خير البرية نور دونه القمر حبر تسربل منه دهره حبرا بحر تقاذف من أمواجه الدرر قام ابن تيمية في نصر شرعتنا مقام سيدتيم إذ عصت مضر واظهر الحق إذ آثاره اندرست واخمد الشر إذ طارت له شرر كنا نحدث عن حبر يجئ بها (1) * أنت الامام الذي قد كان ينتظر ثم دار بينهما كلام فجرى ذكر سيبويه فاغلظ ابن تيمية القول في سيبويه فنافره أبو حيان وقطعه بسببه ثم عاد ذا ماله وصير ذلك ذنبا لا يغفر قال وحج ابن المحب سنة 34 فسمع من أبى حيان انا شيد فقرأ عليه هذه الأبيات فقال قد كشطتها من ديواني ولا اذكره بخير فسأله عن السبب

"When Taqi al-Din came to us, there appeared to us a caller to God, singular and bearing no burden. On his face were the marks of those who accompanied the best of created beings, a light before which the moon is dim. A learned scholar whose age clothed him in learning, a sea whose waves cast forth pearls. Ibn Taymiyya rose to support our law as Sayyid Tamim rose when Mudar rebelled. He made the truth manifest after its traces had been effaced, and he extinguished evil after its sparks had flown. We used to speak of a learned scholar who would come with it. You are the imam who had been awaited. Then words passed between them, and Sibawayh was mentioned. Ibn Taymiyya spoke harshly about Sibawayh, so Abu Hayyan broke with him and cut him off because of it. He then went back on all that he had said and made it an unforgivable sin. He said that Ibn al-Muhibb performed the pilgrimage in the year 734 and heard from Abu Hayyan that he had composed these verses. He recited them to him, and Abu Hayyan said, "I have erased them from my collected poems, and I do not mention him favorably." He asked him about the reason."

- Abu Hayyan's praise, in Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.178

The poem is the measure of the man at his best. The same entry preserves the sequel. Words passed between the two men, the grammarian Sibawayh was mentioned, and Ibn Taymiyya spoke harshly of him. Abu Hayyan broke with him over that single discourtesy, crossed the verses out of his collected poems, and said he would not mention him favorably again. A mind that could win Abu Hayyan's verse could also lose him with a sentence.

The Minbar in Damascus

Source: Rihlat Ibn Batuta, vol.1, pp.316-317

Speaker: Ibn Batuta, who states he was present in Damascus

Claim: Ibn Taymiyya illustrated the divine descent from the minbar step, a Maliki jurist who objected was beaten by the crowd, and the judges' protest reached the sultan

The traveler Ibn Batuta passed through Damascus and left a portrait in his Rihla.

وكان بدمشق من كبار الفقهاء الحنابلة تقي الدين ابن تيمية (221) كبير الشأن يتكلم في الفنون إلا أن في عقله شيئا ! وكان أهل دمشق يعظمونه أشد التعظيم ويعظهم على المنبر ، وتكلم مرة بأمر أنكره الفقهاء ورفعوه إلى الملك الناصر فأمر بإشخاصه إلى القاهرة وجمع القضاة والفقهاء بمجلس الملك الناصر وتكلم شرف الدين الزواوي المالكي (222) ، وقال : إن هذا الرجل قال : كذا ، وعدّد ما أنكر على ابن تيمية ، وأحضر العقود بذلك ووضعها بين يدي قاضي القضاة وقال قاضي القضاة لابن تيمية : ما تقول ؟ قال : لا إلاه إلا الله ، فأعاد عليه فأجاب بمثل قوله ، فأمر الملك الناصر بسجنه فسجن أعواما ، وصنف في السجن كتابا

"In Damascus there was among the leading Hanbali jurists Taqi al-Din Ibn Taymiyya, a man of great standing who spoke on various disciplines, except that there was something wrong with his mind. The people of Damascus held him in the highest reverence, and he preached to them from the minbar. On one occasion he spoke about a matter that the jurists condemned and brought before al-Malik al-Nasir. He ordered that Ibn Taymiyya be brought to Cairo. The judges and jurists were assembled in the council of al-Malik al-Nasir, and Sharaf al-Din al-Zawawi the Maliki spoke and said, "This man said such and such," and listed what had been condemned in Ibn Taymiyya. He produced the documents concerning this and placed them before the Chief Judge. The Chief Judge said to Ibn Taymiyya, "What do you say?" He said, "There is no god but God." He repeated the question, and Ibn Taymiyya answered in the same way. Al-Malik al-Nasir ordered him imprisoned, so he was imprisoned for years. While in prison, he composed a book."

- Ibn Batuta, Rihlat Ibn Batuta (Tuhfat al-Nuzzar), vol.1, p.316

The portrait is double-edged, and it says so itself. Ibn Taymiyya was among the leading Hanbali jurists of Damascus, a man of great standing who spoke on the sciences. There was something wrong with his mind. Ibn Batuta then records what he says he witnessed on a Friday.

لاقه إلى أن وقع منه مثل ذلك ثانية ، وكنت إذ ذاك بدمشق (223) فحضرته يوم الجمعة وهو يعظ الناس على منبر الجامع ويذكّرهم فكان من جملة كلامه أن قال : إن الله ينزل إلى سماء الدنيا كنزولي هذا ، ونزل درجة من درج المنبر ، فعارضه فقيه مالكي يعرف بابن الزهراء وأنكر ما تكلم به ، فقامت العامة إلى هذا الفقيه وضربوه بالأيدي والنعال ضربا كثيرا حتى سقطت عمامته وظهر على رأسه شاشية حرير ، فأنكروا عليه لباسها واحتملوه إلى دار عز الدين بن مسلّم قاضي الحنابلة فأمر بسجنه وعزره بعد ذلك ، فأنكر فقهاء المالكية والشافعية ما كان من تعزيره ورفعوا الأمر إلى ملك الأمراء سيف الدين تنكيز (224) وكان من خيار الأمراء وصلحائهم ، فكتب إلى الملك الناصر بذلك وكتب عقدا شرعيا على ابن تيمية بأمور منكرة منها : أن المطلّق بالثلاث في كلمة واحدة لا تلزمه إلا طلقة واحدة ، (225) ومنها أن المسافر الذي ينوي بسفره زيارة القبر الشريف زاده الله طيبا لا يقصر الصلاة ، وسوى ذلك مما يشبهه ، وبعث العقد إلى الملك الناصر فأمر بسجن ابن تيمية بالقلعة فسجن بها حتى مات في السجن . ذكر مدارس دمشق إعلم أن للشافع

"until the like of that occurred from him a second time. At that time I was in Damascus, and I attended him on Friday while he was preaching and reminding the people from the minbar of the congregational mosque. Among his words was his statement, "God descends to the lowest heaven as I descend in this way," and he descended one step of the minbar. A Maliki jurist known as Ibn al-Zahra objected to him and condemned what he had said. The common people rose against this jurist and struck him greatly with their hands and sandals until his turban fell off and a silk cap appeared on his head. They condemned him for wearing it and carried him to the house of Izz al-Din Ibn Muslim, the Hanbali judge. He ordered him imprisoned and afterward disciplined him. The Maliki and Shafi'i jurists condemned his having been disciplined and referred the matter to the commander of the emirs, Sayf al-Din Tankiz, who was among the best and most righteous of the emirs. He wrote to al-Malik al-Nasir about this and drew up a legal document against Ibn Taymiyya concerning objectionable matters, among them that a man who pronounces a threefold divorce in one utterance incurs only one divorce, and among them that a traveler whose purpose in traveling is to visit the Noble Grave, may God increase it in goodness, does not shorten the prayer, along with other similar matters. He sent the document to al-Malik al-Nasir, who ordered Ibn Taymiyya imprisoned in the Citadel. He remained imprisoned there until he died in prison. Mention of the schools of Damascus Know that the Shafi'is"

- Ibn Batuta, Rihlat Ibn Batuta (Tuhfat al-Nuzzar), vol.1, p.317

On this report, Ibn Taymiyya, preaching on the descent of God, descended one step of the minbar to illustrate the manner of the descent. The Maliki jurist Ibn al-Zahra objected from the congregation. The crowd beat the objector with hands and sandals until his turban fell. The Hanbali judge of Damascus then imprisoned the objector and had him disciplined. The Maliki and Shafi'i jurists carried the protest to the governor of Syria, Tankiz. The modern editors of the Rihla note a chronological difficulty in the report. One commentator suggested the scribe embellished it. The report stands as the traveler recorded it, with that doubt attached.

The Ruling on Visiting Graves

Source: Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.27, p.27

Speaker: Ibn Taymiyya, citing Ibn Aqil and a group of the scholars

Claim: travel to visit the graves of the prophets is disobedience, and the traveler does not shorten his prayer on that journey

The ruling that put Ibn Taymiyya in his final prison was his own, recorded in his own collected fatwas.

بِأَنَّ الْمُسَافِرَ لِزِيَارَةِ قُبُورِ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ عَلَيْهِمْ السَّلَامُ وَغَيْرِهَا لَا يَقْصُرُ الصَّلَاةَ فِي هَذَا السَّفَرِ ؛ لِأَنَّهُ مَعْصِيَةٌ لِكَوْنِهِ مُعْتَقِداً أَنَّهُ طَاعَةٌ وَلَيْسَ بِطَاعَةِ وَالتَّقَرُّبُ إلَى اللَّهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ بِمَا لَيْسَ بِطَاعَةِ هُوَ مَعْصِيَةٌ ؛ وَلِأَنَّهُ نَهَى عَنْ ذَلِكَ وَالنَّهْيُ يَقْتَضِي التَّحْرِيمَ . وَرَخَّصَ بَعْضُ الْمُتَأَخِّرِينَ فِي السَّفَرِ لِزِيَارَةِ الْقُبُورِ كَمَا ذَكَرَ أَبُو

"A traveler who travels to visit the graves of the prophets, peace be upon them, and others does not shorten the prayer during this journey, because it is a sin. This is because he believes it to be an act of obedience, when it is not an act of obedience. Seeking nearness to God, Mighty and Majestic, through what is not an act of obedience is a sin. It is also because he prohibited that, and a prohibition entails forbiddance. Some later scholars permitted traveling to visit graves, as Abu mentioned"

- Ibn Taymiyya, Majmu' al-Fatawa, vol.27, p.27

The reasoning is stated plainly. A traveler who sets out to visit the graves of the prophets believes he is performing an act of obedience, and the act is not one. Seeking nearness to God through what is not obedience is disobedience, and the prohibition entails forbiddance. Ibn Taymiyya cites earlier authorities for the ruling and notes that some later scholars permitted the travel. The Arabic excerpt breaks off inside the name of Ibn Aqil, the authority the fatwa is citing. Elsewhere in the same volume, in the passage at vol.27, p.253, he denounces as slander the summary claim that he forbids visiting the Prophet's grave and traveling to it. Both texts are his. On this record the operative ruling is the first, and the governor's dossier itemized it when the final imprisonment came.

فكتب إلى الملك الناصر بذلك وكتب عقدا شرعيا على ابن تيمية بأمور منكرة منها : أن المطلّق بالثلاث في كلمة واحدة لا تلزمه إلا طلقة واحدة ، (225) ومنها أن المسافر الذي ينوي بسفره زيارة القبر الشريف زاده الله طيبا لا يقصر الصلاة ، وسوى ذلك مما يشبهه ، وبعث العقد إلى الملك الناصر فأمر بسجن ابن تيمية بالقلعة فسجن بها حتى مات في السجن . ذكر مدارس دمشق إعلم أن للشافعية بدمشق جملة من المدارس أعظمها العادلية (226) ، وبها يحكم قاضي القضاة ، وتقابلها المدرسة الظاهرية (227) وبها قبر الملك الظاهر ، وبها جلوس نواب

"He wrote to al-Malik al-Nasir about this and drew up a legal document against Ibn Taymiyya concerning objectionable matters, among them that a man who pronounces a threefold divorce in one utterance incurs only one divorce, and among them that a traveler whose purpose in traveling is to visit the Noble Grave, may God increase it in goodness, does not shorten the prayer, along with other similar matters. He sent the document to al-Malik al-Nasir, who ordered Ibn Taymiyya imprisoned in the Citadel. He remained imprisoned there until he died in prison. Mention of the schools of Damascus Know that the Shafi'is in Damascus have a number of schools, the greatest of which is the Adiliyya. The Chief Judge gives judgment there. Opposite it is the Zahariyya school, in which is the grave of al-Malik al-Zahir, and there is the seat of the deputies"

- Ibn Batuta, Rihlat Ibn Batuta, vol.1, p.317

The dossier Tankiz, governor of Syria, sent to Sultan al-Nasir named the rulings as errors. The threefold divorce counted as one. The pilgrim who intends the visit of the grave does not shorten his prayer. The order that followed confined Ibn Taymiyya to the citadel of Damascus, where he remained until he died. His student Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya carried the same ruling to Jerusalem and Nablus, and the crowd there tried to kill him.

فاتفق أن ابن قيم الجوزية سافر إلى القدس الشريف ، ورقى على منبر في الحرم ووعظ ، وقال في أثناء وعظه ، بعد أن ذكر المسألة ، وقال : ها أنا راجع ، ولا أزور الخليل . ثم جاء إلى نابلس ، وعمل له مجلس وعظ ، وذكر المسألة بعينها حتى قال : فلا يزور قبر النبي ﵌ . فقام إليه الناس وأرادوا قتله ، فحماه منهم والي نابلس ، وكتب أهل القدس وأهل نابلس إلى دمشق يعرفون صورة ما وقع منه ، فطلبه القاضي المالكي ، فتردد ، وصعد إلى الصالحية إلى القاضي شمس الدين بن مسلم الحنبلي ، وأسلم على يديه ! فقبل توبته ، وحكم بإسلامه ، وحقن دمه ، ولم يعزره لأجل ابن تيمية . ولما كان يوم الجمعة رابع شعبان ، جلس القاضي جلال الدين بعد العصر بالمدرسة العادلية ، وأحضر جماعة من جماعة ابن تيمية كانوا معتقلين في سجن الشرع ، فادعى على إسماعيل بن كثير صاحب التاريخ أنه قال : إن التوراة والأنجيل ما بدلا ، وأنهما بحالهما كما أنزلا ، وشهدوا عليه

"It happened that Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyyah traveled to the noble Jerusalem. He climbed onto a pulpit in the Sanctuary and preached. During his sermon, after mentioning the issue, he said, "Here I am returning, and I will not visit al-Khalil." Then he came to Nablus, where a preaching gathering was arranged for him. He mentioned the very same issue, until he said, "One should not visit the Prophet's grave." The people rose against him and wanted to kill him, but the governor of Nablus protected him from them. The people of Jerusalem and the people of Nablus wrote to Damascus, reporting what had happened from him. The Maliki judge summoned him, but he hesitated. He went up to al-Salihiyyah to Judge Shams al-Din ibn Muslim the Hanbali and embraced Islam at his hands. He accepted his repentance, ruled that he was Muslim, and spared his blood. He did not discipline him on account of Ibn Taymiyyah. Then, on Friday, the fourth of Sha'ban, Judge Jalal al-Din sat after the afternoon prayer at the Adiliyyah School. He brought in a group from Ibn Taymiyyah's group who had been detained in the legal prison. He brought a claim against Isma'il ibn Kathir, the author of the history, that he had said, "The Torah and the Gospel were not altered, and they remain as they were when they were sent down." They testified against him."

- Taqi al-Din al-Hisni, daf' al-shubah 'an al-Rasul, p.213

Ibn Qayyim escaped with his life by submitting a renewed profession of Islam before a Hanbali judge. The historian Ibn Shakir al-Kutubi, whom al-Hisni introduces as one of Ibn Taymiyya's followers, records the same temper inside the movement itself.

( تاريخ ابن تيمية كما نقله المؤرخ ابن شاكر ) وأزيد على ذلك ما ذكره صاحب ( عيون التواريخ ) ، وهو ابن شاكر ، ويعرف بصلاح الدين الكتبي وبالتريكي ، وكان من أتباع ابن تيمية ، وضرب الضرب البليغ ، لكونه قال لمؤذن في مأذنة العروس وقت السحر : أشركت ، حين قال : ألا يا رسول الله أنت وسيلتي * إلى الله في غفران ذنبي وزلتي وأرادوا ضرب عنقه ، ثم جددوا إسلامه . وإنما أذكر ما قاله لأنه أبلغ في حق ابن تيمية في إقامة الحجة عليه ، مع أنه أهمل أشياء من خبثه ولؤمه ، لما فيها من المبالغة في إهانة قدوته . والعجب أن ابن تيمية ذكرها ، وهو سكت عنها : كلام ابن تيمية في الاستواء ووثوب الناس عليه فمن ذلك ما أخبر به أبو الحسن علي الدمشقي - في صحن الجامع الأموي - عن أبيه ، قال : كنا جلوسا في مجلس ابن تيمية ، فذكر

"The history of Ibn Taymiyya as transmitted by the historian Ibn Shakir. I add to this what the author of Uyun al-Tawarikh mentioned. He was Ibn Shakir, known as Salah al-Din al-Kutubi and al-Turki. He was one of Ibn Taymiyya's followers, and he was severely beaten because he said to a muezzin in the Minaret of the Bride at dawn, "You have committed associationism," when the muezzin said, "O Messenger of God, you are my means to God for the forgiveness of my sin and my lapse." They intended to strike off his neck, then they renewed his profession of Islam. I mention what he said only because it is more forceful regarding Ibn Taymiyya in establishing the proof against him, even though he omitted things of his malice and baseness because they contained an excessive disparagement of his exemplar. It is remarkable that Ibn Taymiyya mentioned them while he remained silent about them. Ibn Taymiyya's words concerning establishment and the people's rushing upon him. Among this is what Abu al-Hasan Ali al-Dimashqi reported in the courtyard of the Umayyad Mosque from his father. He said, "We were sitting in Ibn Taymiyya's gathering when he mentioned""

- Taqi al-Din al-Hisni, daf' al-shubah 'an al-Rasul, p.88, relaying Ibn Shakir al-Kutubi

A muezzin called on the Prophet as his means to God, in the verse of the dawn call. Ibn Shakir told the muezzin that he had committed associationism, the sin of calling on other than God. A crowd then moved to behead Ibn Shakir, and his Islam was renewed for him. Al-Hisni, transmitting the historian roughly a century after the events, says the beating was severe and remarks that the follower softened the record out of regard for his teacher.

Twenty-Three Years of Trials

Source: al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, pp.172-175

Chain: Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, from the court records and the historians of Cairo and Damascus

Claim: proceedings in 705, 707, 719, 720, and 726, and the citadel death of 728

The chronology is Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani's, and it begins with an act of force. The hadith master al-Mizzi was imprisoned in Damascus, and the record of al-Durar opens Ibn Taymiyya's chain of trials with what he did about it. Ibn Taymiyya went to the prison and brought him out with his own hand.

فبلغ ابن تيمية فتوجه إلى الحبس فأخرجه بيده فبلغ القاضي فطلع إلى القلعة فوافاه ابن تيمية فتشاجرا بحضرة النائب واشتط ابن تيمية على القاضي لكون نائبه جلال الدين آذى أصحابه في غيبة النائب فامر النائب من ينادى ان من تكلم في العقائد فعل كذا به وقصد بذلك تسكين الفتنة ثم عقد لهم مجلس في سلخ رجب وجرى فيه بين

"This reached Ibn Taymiyyah, so he went to the prison and brought him out with his own hand. This reached the judge, so he went up to the Citadel. Ibn Taymiyyah met him there, and they quarreled in the presence of the deputy. Ibn Taymiyyah was excessive against the judge because his deputy, Jalal al-Din, had harmed his companions during the deputy's absence. So the deputy ordered someone to announce that whoever spoke about matters of creed, such-and-such would be done to him. By this he intended to calm the disturbance. Then he convened a session for them at the end of Rajab, and there took place between"

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.172

The judge of Damascus took the matter to the citadel. Ibn Taymiyya quarreled with him in the presence of the governor's deputy. The deputy had it proclaimed that whoever spoke on the creed would be punished, to still the disturbance. The council of Rajab 705 followed. In Ramadan came the summons to Cairo.

وعقد مجلس في ثالث عشر (3) منه بعد صلاة الجمعة فادعى على ابن تيمية عند المالكي فقال هذا عدوى ولم يجب عن الدعوى فكرر

"A council was convened on the thirteenth of that month after the Friday prayer. A claim was brought against Ibn Taymiyya before the Maliki judge. He said, "This is my enemy," and did not answer the claim. It was repeated"

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Durar al-Kamina, vol.1, p.172

Before the Maliki judge of Egypt the claim against him was read, and he would not answer it. The ruling and proclamation that followed are Ibn Hajar's to transmit.

What the Record Supports

The documented facts are these. The leading linguist of the age praised him and then broke with him. He ruled, in his own collected fatwas, that travel to visit the graves of the prophets is disobedience. His student Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya carried the ruling to Jerusalem and Nablus, and a crowd in Nablus tried to kill him for it. He was summoned before the judges of Egypt in 705 and 707. He signed a creed recanting the apparent-sense reading of the establishment and the descent. It was proclaimed over Damascus that whoever held his creed forfeited blood and property. He was imprisoned repeatedly across twenty-three years, the last confinement in 726 for the visitation ruling, and he died in the citadel in 728. His own student and obituarist conceded the learning and named the harshness. The later authorities of the Shafi'i school itemized his contraventions, from the eternity of the world to the attribution of body and direction to God. They recorded verdicts up to declared unbelief. A Hanbali of his own line is recorded holding that view.

The record also carries its qualifications. The minbar report is a traveler's eyewitness account with a chronological doubt attached by its own editors. The 707 recantation exists, whatever its motive. Ibn Taymiyya himself denounced as slander the summary form of the visitation charge, and his own volume holds both the ruling and the denial. The proclamation of 705 was politics as much as creed, issued in the war of the judges of Cairo. And al-Dhahabi's obituary stands as the counterweight to the verdicts, a student's defense that concedes the faults and refuses the worst. The same entry's roll of elegists keeps the mourning on the record.

The politics around the trials were real. The same record that confines him shows Shaykh Nasr al-Manbiji inciting the rulers against him over the Ibn al-Arabi letter. It shows the judges of Cairo fighting their own war through his case. Both things are in the sources. The councils that tried him were councils of his own tradition's judges. The confinement orders were the sultan's and the governor's, issued on those councils' findings, and the verdicts are the verdicts of the school's own later authorities. The career is documented, in the main, by the people who wrote the history of the tradition he belonged to.
