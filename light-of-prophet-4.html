Hadith of the Light

Part 4: The Creation of Adam

Bihar al-Anwar, Volume 15, pages 33 to 41. Continued from Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib ﵇.

Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib ﵇ continued:

When God Almighty resolved to create Adam, He commanded Gabriel to go to the earth and bring Him a handful of clay. Gabriel descended and reached for the earth, but the earth spoke and said:

"I seek refuge in God from you. God has commanded you to take from me. Who is this that God wants to make from my clay?"

Gabriel returned to God and said: "My Lord, the earth seeks refuge in You from me, and I did not want to take from it by force." And God, who is merciful to those who show mercy, left the earth alone.

Then He sent Michael. The same thing happened. The earth sought refuge in God, and Michael returned empty-handed.

Then He sent the Angel of Death, Azrael. The earth sought refuge in God from him, as it had from the others. But Azrael said:

"The earth is seeking refuge in God from me, and God has commanded me to take from it. And the one who obeys God is more deserving than the one who seeks refuge without cause."

So Azrael took the handful. And for this reason God entrusted him with the task of seizing the spirits of the descendants of Adam until the Day of Resurrection. He was the one who took from the earth what the earth did not want to give, and so he became the one who takes from the children of Adam what they do not want to give.

But Iblis had been watching. He had seen the angels descend and turn back. When Azrael took the clay, Iblis went to the earth and whispered into it:

"What is this that has been taken from you? You will rue the day. A creature is being fashioned from your clay who will bring corruption and bloodshed upon your surface. Their descendants will fill you with sin and disobedience. Mark my words."

And the earth trembled. From that day the seed of doubt was planted, and it would grow in the children of Adam.

The handful that Azrael took was drawn from every part of the earth. From its peaks and its valleys, from its white sands and its black soils, from its red earths and its yellow clays, from the rough and the smooth, the hard and the soft. Every colour and every texture that human beings would one day carry was gathered into that single handful.

And there was a white handful, brighter than the rest, taken from the very spot where the grave of the Prophet ﷺ would one day rest in Medina. The earth that would receive his body at the end of his life was the same earth from which his clay was first taken. The end met the beginning in that handful.

God Almighty kneaded the clay of Adam with seven waters. The water of the primordial sea, the water of the rivers of Paradise, the water of life, the water of the heavenly spring, the water of mercy, the water of wisdom, and the water of light.

Into the clay God kneaded the virtues. He mixed in forbearance, so that Adam could be patient with what was to come. He mixed in humility, so that Adam would know his smallness before his Creator. He mixed in mercy, so that Adam would feel for his descendants. He mixed in knowledge, so that Adam could teach the names. He mixed in longing, so that Adam would yearn for the light of Muhammad ﷺ that was placed within him.

Then God left the clay to dry for forty years. It lay there, shaped but lifeless, and the angels passed by it and marvelled. Iblis passed by it and looked into its hollows, and he tapped it and it rang hollow. And he said:

"This creature is hollow. It was not made to last. I have been given power over it."

When the body of Adam was complete, God commanded the spirit to enter it. The spirit looked at the body and hesitated. It did not want to enter.

God said to it:

"Enter unwillingly, and leave unwillingly."

So the spirit entered. It began at the head, and as it reached the nose, Adam sneezed. The angels around him said: "Say: Praise be to God." And Adam sneezed again, and said:

"Praise be to God, the Lord of all the worlds."

And God replied:

"May God have mercy on you, O Adam."

Adam opened his eyes. He looked around Paradise and saw its wonders. He moved to stand, but the spirit had not yet reached his legs. He moved too fast, too eager, and God said:

"Man was created from haste. I will show you My signs, so do not ask Me to hasten."

Commentary

The First Temptation Comes Before Adam Exists

Iblis arrives on the scene before there is even a human being. His method is already in place. He plants a thought in the earth herself. The long story of Iblis whispering doubts begins at the very first step of human creation.

Why People Differ in Colour and Character

The handful is drawn from every part of the earth, the high and the low, the white, black, red, rough and soft. Human variety is rooted right here, in the very first scoop of clay. The Quran makes a similar point in Surat al-Rum (30:22), where the differences of colours and languages are described as signs of God.

The White Handful and the Spot of the Future Grave

The original handful came from the patch of earth that would one day hold the Prophet's grave in Medina. The earth that received his body at the end was the same earth from which his clay was first taken. The end meets the beginning.

"Enter Unwillingly, Leave Unwillingly"

The spirit hesitates at the threshold of the body. God's reply has a poignancy to it. The spirit enters reluctantly and will leave reluctantly. Life is bracketed by two moments of resistance, one at birth and one at death.

The First Word Spoken

The first word spoken by a human being was al-hamdu lillah (Praise be to God), said after the first sneeze. The exchange became the prototype for what later Muslims say when someone sneezes near them. The response "may God have mercy on you" echoes God's own reply to Adam in this narration.
