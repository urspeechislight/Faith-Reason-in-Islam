Battle Narration

The Expedition of al-Sawiq

After Badr, Abu Sufyan swore an oath of revenge, struck a farm at al-Arid by night, killed two men, and the Prophet ﷺ chased him for five days while the raiders dumped their flour sacks to run faster. The march took its name from the abandoned flour. Part 7 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

A Vow Kept with Murder

Revenge began as a vow. After Badr, in Dhu al-Hijja of the second year after the Hijra, Abu Sufyan forswore oil until he had taken blood for the men of Quraysh. Al-Askari's line adds perfume and women to the vow. He came out of Mecca with two hundred riders, or forty by another count, to keep it. By night he called on the Nadir, a Jewish tribe of Medina, was refused by Huyayy ibn Akhtab, and was hosted by Salam ibn Mishkam, who fed him, gave him wine, and briefed him. Before dawn his men struck al-Arid on the edge of Medina, killed Ma'bad ibn Amr of the Ansar, the native Muslims of Medina, and his hired hand, and burned palms and two houses. Then they ran.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Al-Waqidi carries the raid from the vow to the pursuit.

حدثني محمد بن عبد الله عن الزهري وإسحاق بن حازم عن محمد بن كعب قالا : لما رجع المشركون إلى مكة من بدر حرم أبو سفيان الدهن حتى يثأر من محمد وأصحابه بمن أصيب من قومه . فخرج في مائتي راكب في حديث الزهري وفي حديث ابن كعب في أربعين راكباً حتى سلكوا النجدية . فجاءوا بني النضير ليلاً فطرقوا حيى بن أخطب ليستخبروه من أخبار النبي صلى الله عليه وسلّم وأصحابه فأبى أن يفتح لهم وطرقوا سلام بن مشكم ففتح لهم فقراهم وسقى أبا سفيان خمراً وأخبره من أخبار النبي صلى الله عليه وسلّم وأصحابه . فلما كان بالسحر خرج فمر بالعريض فيجد رجلاً من الأنصار مع أجير له في حرثه فقتله وقتل أجيره وحرق بيتين بالعريض وحرق حرثاً لهم ورأى أن يمينه قد حلت ثم ذهب هارباً وخاف الطلب فبلغ رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلّم فندب أصحابه فخرجوا في أثره

"Muhammad ibn Abd Allah told me from al-Zuhri and Ishaq ibn Hazim from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, and they said. When the idolaters came back to Mecca from Badr, Abu Sufyan forswore oil until he took revenge for his people killed at Badr from Muhammad and his companions. He went out with two hundred riders in al-Zuhri's account, and with forty in Ibn Ka'b's, until they took the Najd road. They came to Banu al-Nadir by night and knocked at Huyayy ibn Akhtab's, to ask him for news of the Prophet and his companions, and he refused to open to them. They knocked at Salam ibn Mishkam's, and he opened, hosted them, gave Abu Sufyan wine, and told him the news of the Prophet and his companions. Near dawn he went out and passed by al-Arid, where he found a man of the Ansar with a hired hand in his field, and killed him and his hired hand, and burned two houses at al-Arid and burned a field of theirs. He counted his oath fulfilled, fled, and feared pursuit. The news reached the Messenger of Allah, who called his companions, and they went out on his track."

- al-Waqidi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.185, from al-Zuhri and Muhammad ibn Ka'b

The Halabi Sira names the dead man and the deputy.▼ commentary

فبعث رجالا من قريش فأتوا ناحية من المدينة فحرقوا نخلا منها ووجدوا رجلا من الأنصار . قال في الإمتاع : وهذا الأنصاري هو معبد بن عمرو وحليفا لهم فقتلوهما ثم انصرفوا راجعين ، فعلم بهم الناس ، فخرج رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم في طلبهم في مائتين من المهاجرين والأنصار : أي واستعمل صلى اللّه عليه وسلم على المدينة بشير بن عبد المنذر . وكان خروجه لخمس خلون من ذي الحجة

"Abu Sufyan sent men of Quraysh, who came to a corner of Medina, burned some of its palms, and found a man of the Ansar. Imta' al-Asma' says this Ansari was Ma'bad ibn Amr with an ally of his, and they killed the two of them, then turned back. The people learned of them, and the Messenger of Allah went out after them with two hundred of the Emigrants and the Ansar. He put Bashir ibn Abd al-Mundhir in charge of Medina, and his going out was on the fifth of Dhu al-Hijja."

- al-Halabi, al-Sira al-Halabiyya, vol.2, p.288, quoting Imta' al-Asma'

The raiders, feeling the pursuit behind them, lightened their loads.▼ commentary

وجعل أبو سفيان وأصحابه يخففون للهرب أي لأجله ، فجعلوا يلقون جرب السويق أي وهو قمح أو شعير يقلى ثم يطحن ليسفّ ، تارة بماء ، وتارة بسمن ، وتارة بعسل وسمن وهو عامة أزوادهم ، فيأخذه المسلمون ولم يلحقوا بهم ، وانصرف

"Abu Sufyan and his companions lightened themselves for flight. They kept throwing down the pouches of sawiq, which is wheat or barley roasted and then ground, eaten sometimes with water, sometimes with clarified butter, and sometimes with honey and butter, and it was most of their provisions. The Muslims kept picking it up and did not catch them, and they turned back."

- al-Halabi, al-Sira al-Halabiyya, vol.2, p.288, on the sawiq

"And his companions threw down their provisions to lighten themselves for escape, and most of what they threw down was sawiq. The Muslims came on a great heap of sawiq, and so it was named the expedition of al-Sawiq. His absence lasted five days."

- Musta'dhab al-Akhbar, p.247

استخلف أيضا أبا لبابة في غزوة السويق ، وكان خروجه (صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم) في طلب أبي سفيان حين اقبل في مائتي راكب ليبر بنذره ان لا يمس الطيب والنساء حتى يثأر لأهل بدر ، وانتهوا إلى عريض ، فبلغهم خروج النبي صلى الله عليه وآله ، فجعلوا يلقون جرب السويق تخففا فسميت غزوة السويق (3) .

"He also put Abu Lubaba in charge in the expedition of al-Sawiq. His going out was in pursuit of Abu Sufyan, who had come with two hundred riders to fulfill his vow, to touch no perfume and no women until he avenged the people of Badr. They reached al-Arid, and word of the Prophet's march reached them, so they threw down the pouches of sawiq to lighten themselves, and it was named the expedition of al-Sawiq."

- al-Askari, Ma'alim al-Madarisayn, vol.1, p.207

Commentary

The Sources for al-Sawiq

Source spine: al-Waqidi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.185, from al-Zuhri and Muhammad ibn Ka'b

Other carriers: al-Halabi, al-Sira al-Halabiyya, vol.2, p.288, quoting al-Maqrizi's Imta' al-Asma'. Musta'dhab al-Akhbar, p.247

Shia line: al-Askari, Ma'alim al-Madarisayn, vol.1, p.207

The carriers agree on the whole arc, the vow, the night with the Nadir, the killings at al-Arid, the pursuit, the dumped provisions, and the five day absence. They divide on the size of Abu Sufyan's band and on the deputy left over Medina. They also divide on the vow's terms, oil alone or perfume and women, and on the second victim, a hired hand in one line and an ally in the other.

A Vow Kept with Murder

Vow: no oil until Badr was avenged, per al-Waqidi, with perfume and women added in al-Askari's line

Force: two hundred riders, or forty by the other count

Collaboration: hosted and briefed by Salam ibn Mishkam of the Nadir, after Huyayy ibn Akhtab refused them

The crime: Ma'bad ibn Amr of the Ansar and his hired hand killed at al-Arid, palms and two houses burned

End: the oath counted fulfilled, and flight

The raid was small and its meaning was large. It was Mecca's first answer to Badr, and it showed the war had moved inside Medina's own fence. The Nadir connection matters most in hindsight. A chief of that tribe fed and briefed the raiders by night, a fact the sources record plainly. The account came due two years later, in the expedition against the Nadir, part 13 of this series.

The Flight and the Flour

Pursuit: the Prophet ﷺ, with two hundred Emigrants, the Meccan Muslims, and Ansar, five days out

Name: the raiders dumped their pouches of sawiq, roasted grain flour, their staple food

Haul: the Muslims collected the dumped flour, and the march took its name from it

Contact: none, the raiders escaped

The Muslims came home with a heap of flour and no prisoners. The tactical outcome was a failed pursuit. The strategic outcome was sober. Medina had answered a night raid within hours, with the Prophet ﷺ himself in the field, and the chase in the saddle the same day. The only dead were the two men murdered at al-Arid.

The Two Deputies

Bashir ibn Abd al-Mundhir: deputy over Medina, per the Halabi Sira

Abu Lubaba: deputy over Medina, per al-Askari

The deputy line splits between Bashir ibn Abd al-Mundhir and Abu Lubaba, and the split runs through both communities, with the Shia al-Askari on Abu Lubaba. Nothing else in the story turns on the name, and the sources give no way to force a settlement.

Was the Expedition of al-Sawiq Justified

The march was a hot pursuit of murderers. Abu Sufyan's band killed two men on Medina's own ground and burned property as they left, and the state's answer was to chase them. No ambush was laid, no neutral party was touched, and the pursuit ended when the trail ended.

The name the march carries says the rest. The raiders bought their escape with their own food, and the Muslims brought that food home. The tactical ledger showed a heap of dumped flour and no prisoners. The strategic one showed a city that answered night murder with an immediate mounted pursuit, twice in one year after the Kurz affair.

Could it have been prevented? The prevention was in Mecca's hands, in the shape of a vow Abu Sufyan could have left unsworn, or kept without murder. No talks appear in the record, and a raid already in flight left nothing to negotiate with.
