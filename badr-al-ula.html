Battle Narration

The First Badr and the Pursuit of Kurz ibn Jabir

A raider stole the grazing camels of Medina, and the Prophet ﷺ rode after him as far as the valley of Safwan near Badr and lost his trail. From Ibn Ishaq and al-Waqidi, with the Shia reading of A'lam al-Hidaya. Part 3 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Stolen Herds

The third march began with a theft. Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri, a raider of the Fihr clan of Quraysh, swept down on the grazing herds of Medina while they pastured at al-Juma' and its surroundings, and drove off the camels. The Prophet ﷺ rode out in pursuit at once, with Zayd ibn Haritha left in charge of Medina. The trail ran to the valley of Safwan, near Badr, and there it went cold. Kurz escaped, and the Muslims came home without a fight and without their camels.▼ commentary

Ibn Ishaq tells the pursuit, and Ibn Sayyid al-Nas adds the notes of Ibn Hisham and Ibn Sa'd. Ibn Ishaq ties the march to the return from al-Ushayra, a march this series places after this one, and the dating notes sit at the heart of that tie.▼ commentary

قال ابن إسحاق : فلم يقم رسول الله ﵌ بالمدينة حين قدم من غزوة العشيرة إلا ليالي قلائل لا تبلغ العشر حتى أغار كرز بن جابر الفهري على سرح (1) المدينة فخرج رسول الله ﵌ في طلبه حتى بلغ واديا يقال له سفوان من ناحية بدر وفاته كرز بن جابر فلم يدركه واستعمل على المدينة فيما قال ابن هشام زيد بن حارثة ، وذكر ابن سعد أنها في ربيع الأول على رأس ثلاثة عشر شهرا من الهجرة وحمل اللواء فيها علي بن أبي طالب قال والسرح ما رعوا من نعمهم .

"Ibn Ishaq said. The Messenger of Allah did not stay in Medina after returning from the expedition of al-Ushayra more than a few nights, fewer than ten, before Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri raided the grazing herds of Medina. The Messenger of Allah went out in pursuit until he reached a valley called Safwan near Badr. Kurz ibn Jabir escaped, and he did not catch him. Ibn Hisham said he had put Zayd ibn Haritha in charge of Medina. Ibn Sa'd said it was in Rabi' al-Awwal, thirteen months after the Hijra, and that Ali ibn Abi Talib carried the banner. The grazing herds are the camels they let graze."

- Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.1, p.301, carrying Ibn Ishaq with Ibn Hisham's and Ibn Sa'd's notes

Al-Waqidi's chronicle compresses the same event to three lines.

ثم غزا في ربيع الأول على رأس ثلاثة عشر شهراً في طلب كرز بن جابر الفهري أغار على سرح المدينة وكان يرعى بالجماء ونواحيها حتى بلغ بدراً ولم يدركه

"Then he marched in Rabi' al-Awwal, thirteen months after the Hijra, in pursuit of Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri. Kurz had raided the grazing herds of Medina, which were pasturing at al-Juma' and its surroundings. He reached Badr and did not catch him."

- al-Waqidi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.16

Al-Maqrizi carries both lines together, Ibn Sa'd's dating and al-Waqidi's detail.

غزوة بدر الأولى ثم غزا في ربيع الأول على رأس ثلاثة عشر شهرا ، في طلب كرز بن جابر الفهري ، أغار على سرح المدينة ، وكان يرعى بالجماء ونواحيها ، حتى بلغ بدرا ولم يدركه] (1) .

وذكر الواقدي أن غارة كرز في ربيع الأول بعد بواط ، أغار كرز بن جابر الفهري على سرح المدينة ، فخرج رسول الله ﵌ في طلبه ، حتى بلغ واديا يقال له : سفوان من ناحية بدر ، وفاته كرز بن جابر ، فلم يدركه ، وهي غزوة بدر الأولى ، ثم رجع إلى المدينة (2) ، [وكان قد استخلف عليها زيد بن حارثة ﵁] .

"The first expedition of Badr. Then he marched in Rabi' al-Awwal, thirteen months after the Hijra, in pursuit of Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri, who had raided the grazing herds of Medina while they pastured at al-Juma' and its surroundings, until he reached Badr and did not catch him. Al-Waqidi held that Kurz's raid was in Rabi' al-Awwal, after Buwat. Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri raided the grazing herds of Medina. The Messenger of Allah went out in pursuit until he reached a valley called Safwan near Badr. Kurz ibn Jabir escaped, and he did not catch him. This is the first expedition of Badr. Then he returned to Medina. He had put Zayd ibn Haritha in charge of it."

- al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.8, p.336, carrying Ibn Sa'd and al-Waqidi

Ibn Abd al-Barr's digest adds the detachment of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, sent out in the same period with eight men.▼ commentary

ولما انصرف رسول الله ﵌ من العشيرة لم يقم بالمدينة إلا عشر ليال أو نحوها حتى أغار كرز بن جابر الفهري على سرح المدينة فخرج رسول الله ﵌ في طلبه حتى بلغ واديا يقال له سفوان في ناحية بدر وفاته كرز فرجع إلى المدينة

بعث سعد بن أبي وقاص وقد كان رسول الله ﵌ بعث في حين خروجه لطلب كرز بن جابر سعد بن أبي وقاص في ثمانية رهط من المهاجرين فبلغ إلى الخرار ثم رجع إلى المدينة ولم يلق حربا وقيل إنما بعثه رسول الله ﵌ في طلب كرز بن جابر الفهري

"When the Messenger of Allah returned from al-Ushayra, he stayed in Medina only ten nights or so before Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri raided the grazing herds of Medina. The Messenger of Allah went out in pursuit until he reached a valley called Safwan near Badr. Kurz escaped, and he returned to Medina. He had sent Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas. When the Messenger of Allah went out in pursuit of Kurz ibn Jabir, he had sent Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas with eight men of the Emigrants. They reached al-Kharar, then returned to Medina and met no war. Some said the Messenger of Allah sent him in pursuit of Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri."

- Ibn Abd al-Barr, al-Durar, p.73

Al-Bayhaqi's Dala'il carries the aftermath. After the return, the Prophet ﷺ stayed in Medina through Jumada, Rajab, and Sha'ban.

ثم لم يقم رسول الله ﵌ بالمدينة حين رجع من العشيرة كمل عشر ليال حتى أغار كرز بن جابر الفهري على سرح المدينة فخرج رسول الله ﵌ في طلبه حتى بلغ واديا يقال له سفوان من ناحية بدر وهي غزوة بدر الأولى وفاته كرز فلم يدركه فرجع رسول الله ﵌ فأقام جمادي ورجبا وشعبان وقد كان بعث بين ذلك سعدا في ثمانية رهط فرجع

"The Messenger of Allah did not stay in Medina a full ten nights after returning from al-Ushayra before Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri raided the grazing herds of Medina. The Messenger of Allah went out in pursuit until he reached a valley called Safwan near Badr. This is the first expedition of Badr. Kurz escaped, and he did not catch him. The Messenger of Allah returned and stayed through Jumada, Rajab, and Sha'ban. In between he had sent Sa'd with eight men, and he returned."

- al-Bayhaqi, Dala'il al-Nubuwwa, vol.3, p.15

The modern Shia work A'lam al-Hidaya reads the pursuit as a measured answer to aggression, held within the customs of truce and the sacred months.▼ commentary

وتحرك النبيّ ( صلّى اللّه عليه واله ) لردّ الاعتبار ومعاقبة المعتدي حين أغار كرز بن جابر الفهري على أطراف المدينة لسلب الإبل والمواشي فخرج النبي ( صلّى اللّه عليه واله ) لملاحقته وخلّف زيد بن حارثة على المدينة (1) .

وانطلق النبيّ ( صلّى اللّه عليه واله ) في حركته العسكرية من مفهوم الجهاد والتضحية من أجل الدين بدلا عن مفهوم العصبية والثأر ، محترما أعراف وتقاليد الصلح والمواعدة وحرمة الأشهر الحرم .

"The Prophet moved to restore standing and punish the aggressor when Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri raided the outskirts of Medina to seize camels and livestock. The Prophet went out to pursue him and left Zayd ibn Haritha in charge of Medina. His campaign drew on jihad and sacrifice for the sake of religion. It rose above tribal loyalty and vengeance. He kept faith with the customs of truce and covenant and the sanctity of the sacred months."

- A'lam al-Hidaya, vol.1, p.132

Commentary

The Two Placements of This March

Ibn Ishaq's order: Buwat, then al-Ushayra, then the pursuit of Kurz, ten nights or fewer after returning from al-Ushayra

Al-Waqidi's order, with Ibn Sa'd's dating: Buwat, then the pursuit of Kurz in Rabi' al-Awwal, then al-Ushayra in Jumada al-Ula

This series: follows al-Waqidi, and notes the disagreement here

The two great roots of the chronology disagree on where this march sits. Ibn Ishaq puts it after the march to al-Ushayra, which he dates to Jumada. Al-Waqidi and the line he shares with Ibn Sa'd put it immediately after Buwat, in Rabi' al-Awwal. The events themselves are not in dispute. Kurz raided the herds, the Prophet ﷺ pursued him to Safwan near Badr, and Kurz escaped. Only the slot in the calendar moves. The series keeps al-Waqidi's order because the series index follows his list, and the reader now holds the other placement too.

Who Rode on the Pursuit

Commander: the Prophet ﷺ in person

Deputy over Medina: Zayd ibn Haritha, per Ibn Hisham and al-Waqidi's line

Banner: Ali ﵇, in the line Ibn Sayyid al-Nas cites from Ibn Sa'd

Related detachment: Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas with eight Emigrants to al-Kharar, sent in the same period

The banner detail deserves its weight. The line cited from Ibn Sa'd gives the banner of this pursuit to Ali ﵇, and no other carrier names a different bearer. Zayd ibn Haritha held Medina. Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas rode out with eight men and reached al-Kharar, then turned back. The digest calls his errand separate, and it also records the other reading, that Sa'd rode in the pursuit itself. No source names a single man who fled, and no man died on either side. The herds were not recovered.

An Answer Above Vengeance

The aggression: Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri stole the grazing camels of Medina

The response: immediate pursuit in force, by the commander of the state in person

The restraint: no innocent party touched on the way, no village punished for the raider

A'lam al-Hidaya frames the march in two moves. The Prophet ﷺ answered the aggressor, and he kept the answer above tribal vengeance, inside the customs of truce and covenant that his faith laid down. The frame fits the plain facts. A thief was chased, and a tribe was not. The march itself fell in Rabi' al-Awwal, an ordinary month, and the pursuit found only the raider's trail. No fight happened, and no harm on the way is recorded anywhere in the six lines above.

Was the Pursuit Justified

The cause was as clean as any march in this series. A raider stole the public camels of Medina, and every state answers theft of that kind with force. The response stayed narrow. One raider and his trail were followed, no ambush was laid on neutral ground, and no blood was shed when the trail failed.

No battle occurred, so no conduct in battle arises. No companions fled. No one was killed, and the camels were lost, and no ransom was paid. The tactical outcome was a failed pursuit. The show of intent still stood. The commander of the state himself rode at once after a raider, and the camels were lost with him in the field. What any tribe made of that, the sources do not say.

Could the confrontation have been prevented? Only by Kurz staying his hand, or by catching him before he scattered into the desert. No negotiation was possible with a theft in motion, and no talks are recorded. The march fell in an ordinary month, and no act on the way that broke a truce appears in any line above.
