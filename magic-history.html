How Magic Began and How It Ends

The complete history of magic in the Twelver Shia corpus, from Iblis stealing words from heaven to the Dajjal and the final judgment. Told in nine parts from the school's own books.

Magic begins as a theft. The satans climbed toward heaven and stole whatever words they could hear. They mixed lies into those words and fed them to the soothsayers of the earth. Iblis made the first idols as images of righteous dead men, and each generation worshipped the image itself. After the Flood, sorcerers multiplied everywhere, and God sent two angels to the prophet of that era carrying both the sorcery and its antidote. From there the history runs forward through every era, to Moses and Pharaoh's sorcerers, to Solomon's forged book, to the Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ own era and the spell in the well, to the Imams who broke illusions by command, to the Dajjal and the last judgment.

The school carries both sides of this history with unusual honesty. It tells the full story of Harut and Marut at Babylon, and it records its own verdicts against the story's plain sense, sometimes inside the very books that preserve it. Every quotation in this series is verbatim corpus text, cited by work, volume, and page.
