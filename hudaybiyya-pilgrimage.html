The Road to the Tree

In the sixth year the Prophet ﷺ saw in a dream that he and his companions entered the House, the ancient sanctuary at Mecca. He marched out in the pilgrim's garb to visit it, fourteen hundred men with him, or sixteen hundred, or eighteen hundred by the counts. Quraysh, the ruling clan of Mecca, barred the road at Hudaybiyya. What followed was a negotiation under threat, a pledge of allegiance under a tree when a murder was rumored. And it ended in a treaty signed with an enemy who refused to write Messenger of Allah as the Prophet's ﷺ name.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi said, 'And there came down on the Pledge of Ridwan, Allah was well pleased with the believers when they pledged to you under the tree. And he laid on them the condition that they would never after that object to anything the Messenger of Allah did, nor oppose him in anything he ordered them. Then Allah Almighty said after the verse of the pleasure, Those who pledge to you pledge only to Allah, the hand of Allah above their hands, so whoever breaks it breaks it only against himself, and whoever fulfills what he has covenanted with Allah, He will give him a great wage. And He was only pleased with them by this condition, that they should keep after it Allah's covenant and His bond and not break His pact and His tie. By this covenant Allah was pleased with them. They set the verse of the condition before the Pledge of Ridwan in the ordering of the Book, and the verse of the pledge came down first, then the verse of the condition upon them in it.'"

- al-Qummi's Tafsir, in Nusus al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil, vol.1, p.391

"Write, in Your name, O Allah. This is what Muhammad the Messenger of Allah made peace upon with Suhayl ibn Amr. Suhayl said, 'If I testified that you were the Messenger of Allah I would not have fought you. Write Muhammad ibn Abd Allah, your name and your father's name.' The Messenger of Allah said, 'Write Muhammad ibn Abd Allah and Suhayl ibn Amr.' And it was written, 'This is what Muhammad ibn Abd Allah has made peace upon with Suhayl ibn Amr, on laying war aside for ten years, in which the people are safe and each withholds from the other, on condition that whoever comes to the Messenger of Allah of their people without his guardian's leave is returned to them, and whoever comes to Quraysh of those with the Messenger of Allah is not returned, and that there shall be no theft and no betrayal.'"

"When the Messenger of Allah had finished the treaty he said to his companions, 'Rise, sacrifice, then shave.' And by Allah, not a man of them rose until he had said it three times. When none of them rose he went in to Umm Salama and told her what he had met from the people. Umm Salama said to him, 'Messenger of Allah, is that what you want? Go out and speak no word to any of them until you sacrifice your camel and call your barber to shave your head.' So he rose and went out and spoke to none of them a word until he had done it, sacrificed his camel and called his barber and he shaved his head. And when they saw that, they rose and sacrificed, and some of them shaved the heads of others, until they were near killing one another in their grief."

Commentary

The Sources for Hudaybiyya

Source spine: the treaty text preserved in Ibn Hibban, al-Thiqat, vol.1, p.302

Other sources: al-Subhani, Hawar ma'a al-Shaykh Salih al-Darwish, vol.2, p.37, on the year and the counts

Shia lines: al-Tabarsi's I'lam al-Wara in Mawsu'at al-Tarikh al-Islami, vol.2, p.619, on the Uthman mission. al-Qummi's Tafsir in Nusus al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil, vol.1, p.391, on the pledge's condition

Sunni lines: the Umm Salama scene with its full chain, Ma'mar from al-Zuhri from Urwa from al-Miswar ibn Makhrama and Marwan ibn al-Hakam, carried in al-Ansari, al-Itra wa-l-Sahaba, vol.2, p.138. The night watch, al-Waqdi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.606

Scripture: Qur'an 48:1, 48:18, and 48:27, Uthmani text

Companion study: the site's debate page on the treaty argues the charge sheet, and the narration here tells the event inside the battles serial

The treaty text is the spine because it is the event. The counts vary between fourteen hundred and eighteen hundred, and the accounts leave the variation standing.

The Pledge and the Shaving

The mission: Uthman sent to Mecca for leave to visit, detained, and the Prophet's ﷺ fear that he had been killed

The watch: the camp guarded through the night in turns, three companions rotating the guard, al-Waqdi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.606

The pledge: the army's pledge of allegiance under the tree on the rumor, the pledge the Qur'an sealed with pleasure, and the condition al-Qummi's line preserves, never to object to the Prophet's ﷺ acts

The deadlock: the treaty signed, the order to sacrifice and shave refused three times

The counsel: Umm Salama's word, act and speak nothing, sacrifice first and be shaved

The ending: the Prophet's ﷺ own act broke the deadlock, and the army's grief-shaving nearly became a disaster

The two halves of the standoff belong together. The pledge was the army's answer to a rumor of murder, its terms preserved in the Shia line as a covenant never to oppose the Prophet's ﷺ judgment. The shaving crisis was the same army's answer to a treaty it tasted as defeat. The Prophet's ﷺ conduct through both is the same. The pledge under the tree closed with Allah's own word of pleasure, and the shaving deadlock closed with the Prophet's ﷺ own act first. Umm Salama's counsel belongs to the Sunni line, and its chain ends at al-Miswar ibn Makhrama and Marwan ibn al-Hakam.

The Treaty Under Duress

Form: a ten year laying down of war, the people safe, each side withholding from the other

Asymmetry: converts coming to the Prophet ﷺ returned to Quraysh, converts going to Quraysh kept

Guarantees: no theft and no betrayal

The name: Suhayl refused Messenger of Allah, and the Prophet ﷺ himself dictated Muhammad ibn Abd Allah

The price: Abu Jandal came to the treaty ground walking in his fetters, the hadith phrase preserved in Ibn al-Athir's lexicon, al-Nihaya, vol.2, p.222, and the debate page on the treaty carries his imprisonment and his return

Read the return clause and the name clause together, because the sources give them together. The truce bought ten years of open roads, and the return clause was the price. The name clause carried the same logic at smaller scale, Suhayl refusing the Prophet's ﷺ title, and the Prophet ﷺ accepting the lesser name.

What the Truce Bought

Tactical: the pilgrimage deferred, the army returned without entering the House

Strategic: ten years of open roads for the faith, and within them the Khaybar campaign, the spread through the tribes, and the Mecca that opened in year eight

The Qur'an's own frame: a victory near at hand, given as reward at the tree itself

The gains were the largest of the whole series, bought with the smallest show of arms. No battle was fought, and none died. The army went home without the visit it came for. The treaty gave it ten years of open roads, and the Qur'an itself names the treaty a clear opening.

Was the Standoff at Hudaybiyya Justified

The march was a pilgrimage, announced as one, garbed as one. The Prophet ﷺ asked Quraysh for leave to visit the House and sent Uthman to carry the request, and Quraysh refused. Their refusal barred the road, and the bar caused the standoff.

The Prophet's ﷺ conduct through the negotiation is the measure of the man. Faced with a clause that returned Muslim converts to their persecutors, he signed. Faced with a refusal of his own title, he gave way. Together those concessions bought with ink what Badr and Uhud had not bought with blood. Whether the return clause was justified to the men it endangered remains the standing question of the treaty. Mecca opened without a battle two years later.

Prevention lay in Quraysh's hands. Quraysh could have granted the leave the Prophet ﷺ asked for. Once it refused, the treaty prevented what the standoff threatened, a battle at the sanctuary's door.
