A Narration on the Greatest Name

The Greatest Name and the Seals of the Prophets

The letters of the Greatest Name were measured out to the prophets and gathered in Muhammad and his family, while the drawn marks sold as the Name today descend from late poetry. From al-Kafi, Tafsir al-Qummi, Muhaj al-Daawat, Bihar al-Anwar, Ithbat al-Huda, and al-Dhari'a.

The school of the Ahl al-Bayt, the Shia school of the Prophet's own family through Ali and Fatimah, ﵈ tells the story of the Greatest Name as a story about words. The small ligatures after each name are honorifics, written blessings such as peace be upon him, never broken characters. The Name is a word of Allah ﷿, and its letters were measured out like everything else He gives. Adam ﵇ received twenty-five of its letters. Noah ﵇ received fifteen. Abraham ﵇ received eight, Moses ﵇ four, and Jesus ﵇ two. Allah gathered all of those letters in Muhammad ﷺ. From there the Name passed to his family ﵈, and the reports count its letters in their house as seventy-two.▼ commentary

The same story touches Solomon ﵇. His court saw one letter of the Name split the earth and carry a throne across it. His name was later forged onto a book of magic he never wrote. The story touches the Quran too. Its separated letters are read in this school as pieces of that same Name, waiting to be gathered in prayer. It touches a sick young man in Kufa whose written prayer was answered in a night. It touches a rock outside Kufa that carried the names of the lawbearing prophets. And it ends, many centuries later, in a place the hadith never went. Market stalls sell seven drawn marks to Shi'a buyers as the Greatest Name that Ali ﵇ is said to have found carved in a rock.

A 2011 study of Islamic talismans by Lloyd D. Graham traced those seven marks to a twelfth-century poem. The poem survives in a manuscript copy of the Diwan, a collected book of poems, attributed to Ali ﵇. The Shi'i corpus itself carries the whole story behind that claim. The story runs from the first prophet to the last days. And it separates two things that the talisman trade joined together, the living Name of prayer and the drawn marks of the later poets.

Seventy-Three Letters from Adam to Muhammad

Al-Kulayni closes the first volume of al-Kafi with the Imams' account of how the Name was shared out. Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇ lists the prophets and their portions. Jesus ﵇ stands lowest in the count with two letters. Adam ﵇ stands highest before Muhammad ﷺ with twenty-five.

Source: al-Kafi, vol.1, p.278

Speaker: Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, reported by an unnamed man among the companions of Abu Abd Allah, from Harun ibn al-Jahm, from Zakariyya ibn Imran al-Qummi.

Claim: Allah's Greatest Name has seventy-three letters. Portions of it were given to earlier prophets, two to Jesus, four to Moses, eight to Abraham, fifteen to Noah, and twenty-five to Adam. Allah gathered all of them for Muhammad, gave him seventy-two letters, and withheld one letter from him.

عن زكريا بن عمران القمي ، عن هارون بن الجهم ، عن رجل من أصحاب أبي عبد الله ﵇ لم أحفظ اسمه قال سمعت أبا عبد الله ﵇ يقول : إن عيسى ابن مريم ﵇ أعطي حرفين كان يعمل بهما وأعطي موسى أربعة أحرف ، وأعطي إبراهيم ثمانية أحرف ، وأعطي نوح خمسة عشر حرفا ، وأعطي آدم خمسه وعشرين حرفا ، وإن الله تعالى جمع ذلك كله لمحمد ﵌ وإن اسم الله الأعظم ثلاثة وسبعون حرفا ، أعطى محمدا ﵌ اثنين وسبعين حرفا وحجب عنه حرف واحد .

Zakariyya ibn Imran al-Qummi reports from Harun ibn al-Jahm, from a man among the companions of Abu Abd Allah whose name he did not remember. He heard Abu Abd Allah, Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, say, 'Jesus, son of Mary, received two letters and worked through them. Moses received four letters. Abraham received eight letters. Noah received fifteen letters.

Adam received twenty-five letters. Allah gathered all of them for Muhammad. Allah's Greatest Name has seventy-three letters. He gave Muhammad seventy-two letters and withheld one letter from him.'

- Imam al-Sadiq, al-Kafi, vol.1, p.278

The gathering is the point of the report, before any arithmetic. The portions add up to fifty-four letters. Muhammad's share is counted at seventy-two letters, so the gathering gave him more than the prophets' portions alone ﷺ. The report then counts the Name at seventy-three letters and the share of Muhammad ﷺ at seventy-two. The last letter of the seventy-three stays with Allah alone. The next report explains who held one letter of the Name in Solomon's court, and why the final letter never reaches creation.▼ commentary

The Throne of Bilqis and the One Withheld Letter

The same chapter of al-Kafi names the man who used a single letter of the Name. His name will return at the center of this story. He is Asaf ibn Barkhiya, the executor who carried out Solomon's orders in his court ﵇. Imam al-Hasan al-Askari ﵇ repeats the count of seventy-three and adds the scene in which one letter of the Name moved the throne of the queen of Saba across the earth.

Source: al-Kafi, vol.1, p.278

Speaker: Imam al-Hasan al-Askari, through Ali ibn Muhammad al-Nawfali, from Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Abd Allah, from al-Mualla ibn Muhammad, from al-Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Ash'ari.

Claim: Allah's Greatest Name has seventy-three letters. Asaf ibn Barkhiya worked through one letter of it, and the school holds seventy-two letters. One letter remains with Allah alone, kept in His knowledge of the unseen.

الحسين بن محمد الأشعري ، عن معلى بن محمد ، عن أحمد بن محمد بن عبد الله ، عن علي بن محمد النوفلي ، عن أبي الحسن صاحب العسكر ﵇ قال : سمعته يقول : اسم الله الأعظم ثلاثة وسبعون حرفا ، كان عند آصف حرف فتكلم به فانخرقت له الأرض فيما بينه وبين سبأ فتناول عرش بلقيس حتى صيره إلى سليمان ، ثم انبسطت الأرض في أقل من طرفه عين ، وعندنا منه اثنان وسبعون حرفا ، وحرف عند الله مستأثر به في علم الغيب .

Al-Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Ash'ari reports from al-Mualla ibn Muhammad, from Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Abd Allah, from Ali ibn Muhammad al-Nawfali, from Abu al-Hasan al-Askari. He said that he heard him say, 'Allah's Greatest Name has seventy-three letters. Asaf had one letter. He spoke it, and the earth split open between him and Saba.

He took the throne of Bilqis and brought it to Solomon. Then the earth closed again in less than the blink of an eye. We have seventy-two letters of it. One letter remains with Allah alone, kept in His knowledge of the unseen.'

- Imam al-Hasan al-Askari, al-Kafi, vol.1, p.278

The report gives the school's reading of the miracle of the throne. The Quran tells that the throne of Bilqis was brought before Solomon ﵇ in the blink of an eye. The Imam names the instrument. One letter of a word, spoken by the right man in the right office. The rest of the count, seventy-two letters, sits with the Imam's own house. The report speaks of inheritance and speech. The two reports of al-Kafi do not say which letter Asaf held, whether one of the seventy-two or the letter kept with Allah, and the school carries both reports without settling the count. Marks and glyphs have no place in either of them.▼ commentary

The House of Glass and the Staff That Held Him Up

Solomon ﵇ himself returns in al-Qummi's story, still inside his own court. The forged books of later ages would hang their lies on exactly this court. Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi relays a report of Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇ in which Solomon ﵇ commands jinn and humans together, and they build him a house of glass.

Source: Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.1, p.80

Speaker: Imam al-Baqir, through Abu Basir, from Aban ibn Uthman, from Ibn Abi Umayr.

Claim: Solomon commanded the jinn and humans to build him a house of glass, and he watched how the devils worked while he leaned on his staff.

فإنه حدثني أبي عن ابن أبي عمير عن أبان بن عثمان عن أبي بصير عن أبي جعفر ﵇ قال إن سليمان بن داود امر الجن والإنس فبنوا له بيتا من قوارير قال فبينما هو متكئ على عصاه ينظر إلى الشياطين كيف يعملون

"My father told me from Ibn Abi Umayr, from Aban ibn Uthman, from Abu Basir, from Abu Ja'far, who said, Sulayman ibn Dawud commanded the jinn and humans to build him a house of glass. While he leaned on his staff, he watched how the devils worked."

- Imam al-Baqir, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.1, p.80

Solomon ﵇ leans on his staff and watches his builders. The report then gives him a death as public as his court. The Quran itself turns on the moment the staff gives way.

Source: Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.1, p.81

Speaker: Imam al-Baqir, through Abu Basir, from Aban ibn Uthman, from Ibn Abi Umayr.

Claim: Solomon died leaning on his staff inside the glass house, and the jinn kept working for a year without knowing he had died. Allah sent termites that ate his staff. When he fell, people learned that the jinn had no knowledge of the unseen.

وينظرون إليه إذا حانت منه التفاتة فإذا هو برجل معه في القبة ، ففزع منه وقال من أنت ؟ قال انا الذي لا اقبل الرشى ولا اهاب الملوك ، انا ملك الموت ، فقبضه وهو متكئ على عصاه فمكثوا سنة يبنون وينظرون إليه ويدانون له ويعملون حتى بعث الله الأرضة فأكلت منساته وهي العصا فلما خر تبينت الانس ان لو كان الجن يعلمون الغيب ما لبثوا سنة في العذاب المهين

They watched him whenever he turned, and they saw a man with him inside the chamber. He feared him and asked, 'Who are you?' The man answered, 'I am the one who accepts no bribes and fears no kings. I am the Angel of Death.' He took his soul while he leaned on his staff. They spent a year building, watching him, obeying him, and working.

Then Allah sent termites, which ate his staff. When he fell, people learned that if the jinn had known the unseen, they would not have remained for a year in humiliating punishment.

- Imam al-Baqir, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.1, p.81

The jinn worked a full year under the eye of a dead king, and the fall of the staff exposed them. The verse of Saba, the chapter of the Quran named after the people of Sheba, descended on exactly this scene. The verse declares that no demon knows anything hidden.

The Devil Writes a Book in Asaf's Name

Al-Qummi's report then reaches the event that made Solomon's name a home for every later forgery. After the king's death, Iblis himself composes a book of magic. He signs it with the name of Asaf ibn Barkhiya, the same executor who once carried a letter of the Name.

Source: Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.1, p.81

Speaker: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi, in his commentary on the magic verses of the Qur'an.

Claim: When Solomon died, Iblis wrote a book of magic, attributed it to Asif ibn Barkhiya, and buried it beneath the throne. The disbelievers said Solomon had overcome them only through it, while the believers declared him Allah's servant and Prophet.

فلما هلك سليمان وضع إبليس السحر وكتبه في كتاب ثم طواه وكتب على ظهره " هذا ما وضع آصف بن برخيا للملك سليمان بن داود من ذخاير كنوز العلم من أراد كذا وكذا فليفعل كذا وكذا " ثم دفنه تحت السرير ثم استثاره لهم فقرأه فقال الكافرون ما كان سليمان ﵇ يغلبنا الا بهذا وقال المؤمنون بل هو عبد الله ونبيه فقال الله جل ذكره " واتبعوا ما تتلوا الشياطين على ملك سليمان وما كفر سليمان ولكن الشياطين كفروا يعلمون الناس السحر وما انزل على الملكين ببابل هاروت وماروت "

When Sulayman died, Iblis set down magic and wrote it in a book. He folded it and wrote on its cover, 'This is what Asif ibn Barkhiya prepared for King Sulayman ibn Dawud from the stored treasures of knowledge. Whoever wants such and such should do such and such.' Then he buried it beneath the throne and brought it out for them. They read it.

The disbelievers said, 'Sulayman overcame us only through this.' The believers said, 'He was Allah's servant and His Prophet.' Then Allah said, 'They followed what the devils recited during Sulayman's kingdom. Sulayman did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved.

They taught people magic and what was sent down to the two angels at Babylon, Harut and Marut.'

- Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.1, p.81

The pattern in this report holds for every object that later ages hung on Solomon's name, seals included. A powerful dead king attracts a forged text. The forgery carries the name of his executor. The Quran answers with a verdict. Solomon ﵇ did not disbelieve. The school reads the magic attributed to him as the work of the accursed, buried under a throne and dug up for the credulous.▼ commentary

The Letters Waiting in the Quran

Where the full Name lives after the prophets is the next question the school answers. Its answer is the Quran itself. Al-Qummi's gloss on the separated letters of Sura Yunus is preserved in al-Bahrani's al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Quran.

Source: al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Quran, vol.3, p.11

Speaker: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi, on the separated letters of the Qur'an.

Claim: Alif Lam Ra is a letter from the separated letters of the Greatest Name in the Qur'an. When the Messenger or the Imam joins its letters and calls upon Allah through it, Allah answers him.

علي بن إبراهيم ، قال : * (الر) * هو حرف من حروف الاسم الأعظم المقطع (1) في القرآن ، فإذا ألفه الرسول أو الإمام فدعا به أجيب .

"Ali ibn Ibrahim said, 'Alif Lam Ra is a letter from the separated letters of the Greatest Name in the Qur'an. When the Messenger or the Imam joins its letters and calls upon Allah through it, Allah answers him.'"

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi, al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Quran, vol.3, p.11

The opened letters that stand at the head of the suras are, in this reading, pieces of the Name scattered through the Book itself. The one who gathers them is a Messenger or an Imam. The gathering is an act of calling upon Allah, a prayer. Al-Kafi shows the same power at work in an older prophet. When Allah ﷿ taught Hizqil, the prophet Ezekiel, the words that raised the dead, Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ told what those words were.

Source: al-Kafi, vol.8, p.223

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq, on the words Allah revealed to the prophet Ezekiel.

Claim: Allah revealed the words of the Greatest Name to Ezekiel. When he spoke them, the dead bones flew toward one another and returned to life.

فأوحى الله ﷿ إليه أن قل كذا وكذا ، فقال الذي أمره الله ﷿ أن يقوله - فقال أبو عبد الله (﵇) : وهو الاسم الأعظم - فلما قال : حزقيل ذلك الكلام نظر إلى العظام يطير بعضها إلى بعض فعادوا أحياءا ينظر بعضهم إلى بعض يسبحون الله عز ذكره ويكبرونه ويهللونه

"Allah revealed to him, 'Say such and such.' He spoke the words Allah commanded him to say. Abu Abd Allah said, 'They were the Greatest Name.' When Hizqil spoke those words, he saw the bones flying toward one another. They returned to life and looked at one another. They glorified Allah, declared His greatness, and proclaimed His oneness."

- Imam al-Sadiq, al-Kafi, vol.8, p.223

Ibn Tawus collected the reports that identify the Name for the worshippers of his own age. Three of them come through Muhaj al-Daawat. The first finds the Name in the opening words of the Book.

Source: Muhaj al-Daawat, p.328

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq, through Muawiyah ibn Ammar, from the book The Merit of Supplication of Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Saffar.

Claim: The Basmala, in the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate, is Allah's Greatest Name.

فمن الروايات فيه بإسنادنا إلى محمد بن الحسن الصفار من كتاب فضل الدعاء بإسناده إلى معاوية بن عمار عن الصادق ( ع ) أنه قال بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمنِ الرَّحِيمِ اسم الله الأكبر أو قال الأعظم

"Among the reports is one transmitted through our chain to Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Saffar, from his book The Merit of Supplication, through his chain from Muawiyah ibn Ammar, from al-Sadiq. Al-Sadiq said, 'In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate, is Allah's greatest name, or the Imam said, His Greatest Name.'"

- Imam al-Sadiq, Muhaj al-Daawat, p.328

The second report locates the Name in the Mother of the Book, the preserved source of the Quran's verses.

Source: Muhaj al-Daawat, p.328

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq, reported by al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Hamzah, from his father.

Claim: Allah's Greatest Name is separated into letters in the Mother of the Book.

عن الحسن بن علي بن أبي حمزة عن أبيه عن أبي عبد الله ( ع ) قال اسم الله الأعظم مقطع في أم الكتاب

"Al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Hamzah reports from his father, from Abu Abd Allah, 'Allah's Greatest Name is separated into letters in the Mother of the Book.'"

- Imam al-Sadiq, Muhaj al-Daawat, p.328

The third report is the Imam's practical instruction. It turns the Name into a lesson a companion can carry home.

Source: Muhaj al-Daawat, p.328

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq, reported by Umar ibn Tawbah.

Claim: The Greatest Name is recited through al-Fatihah, the sura of Sincerity, the Verse of the Throne, and the verse of We sent it down, followed by facing the qiblah and asking for whatever one wishes.

عن عمر بن توبة عن أبي عبد الله ( ع ) أنه قال لبعض أصحابه ألا أعلمك اسم الله الأكبر الأعظم قال بلى قال اقرأ الحمد وقل هو الله أحد وآية الكرسي وإنا أنزلناه ثم استقبل القبلة فادع بما أحببت

"Umar ibn Tawbah reports that Abu Abd Allah said to one of his companions, 'Shall I teach you Allah's greatest and most supreme name?' The man answered, 'Yes.' Abu Abd Allah said, 'Recite al-Fatihah, the sura of Sincerity, the Verse of the Throne, and We sent it down. Then face the qiblah and ask for whatever you wish.'"

- Imam al-Sadiq, Muhaj al-Daawat, p.328

The answer is consistent across every relay. The Name is gathered from recitation, and it is spent in asking. Nothing in any of these reports teaches a drawing, a shape, or a carved figure.

A Book Under a Sick Man's Hands

The reports also show the Name at work in ordinary houses. Al-Majlisi carries in Bihar al-Anwar a story related by al-Husayn ibn Ali ﵈. In the story, a young man burning with fever is given a written prayer, and the written words answer before the night is out.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar, vol.92, p.404

Speaker: A fevered young man, relating to Imam Ali what happened when he called upon Allah through the book he had been given.

Claim: The young man called upon Allah through the right of the book many times during the night, and during the second call he received an answer. He returned the next morning cured and said that he had called upon Allah by His Greatest Name.

وأخذ الفتى الكتاب ومضى ، فلما كان من غد ما أصبحنا حينا حتى أتى الفتى إلينا سليما معافا ، والكتاب بيده ، وهو يقول : هذا والله الاسم الأعظم استجيب لي ورب الكعبة ، قال له علي صلوات الله عليه : حدثني ، قال : [لما] هدأت العيون بالرقاد ، واستحلك جلباب الليل (1) رفعت يدي بالكتاب ، ودعوت الله بحقه مرارا ، فأجبت في الثانية : حسبك ، فقد دعوت الله باسمه الأعظم

The young man took the book and left. The next day, soon after morning came, he returned to them safe and healthy, holding the book. The young man said, 'By Allah, this is the Greatest Name.

My prayer was answered, by the Lord of the Ka'bah.' Ali asked him, 'Tell me what happened.' The young man answered, 'When eyes grew quiet in sleep and night covered the world, I raised my hands with the book and called upon Allah through its right many times. During the second call, I received an answer, 'That is enough.

You have called upon Allah by His Greatest Name.''

- al-Husayn ibn Ali relating, Bihar al-Anwar, vol.92, p.404

The young man calls the written prayer itself the Greatest Name, and the report lets the phrase stand. What the young man held was a prayer taught by the house of the Prophet ﷺ, recited with raised hands, and answered from within. The writing is a servant of the words, and the words are speech to Allah.▼ commentary

The Rock of the Prophets at Nukhayla

Outside Kufa the story steps from prayer into the open field. Al-Hurr al-Amili preserves a report of Ali ﵇ through Ammar ibn Yasir. Fifty Jews meet the Imam at al-Nukhayla, an estate two farsakhs, about ten kilometers, from the city, and put a test to him from their own books. Their books describe a rock, and the rock carries names.

Source: Ithbat al-Huda, vol.3, p.449

Speaker: Imam Ali, reported by Ammar ibn Yasir.

Claim: Fifty Jews of an estate near Kufah tested Ali over a rock said to bear the names of seven prophets. Ali called on the wind to scatter the mountain of sand, turned over the rock a thousand men could not move, and the names of the prophets appeared. The Jews accepted Islam.

54 - وعن عمار بن ياسر عن علي عليه السّلام في حديث : أنه عبر في ضيعة على فرسخين من الكوفة فخرج منها خمسون رجلا من اليهود ، فقالوا : لنا صخرة مذكورة في كتبنا عليها اسم سبعة من الأنبياء فإن كنت إماما فأظهر لنا الصخرة ، فقال :

اتبعوني فإذا هو بجبل رمل ، فقال : أيتها الريح انسفي الرمل بإذن اللّه ، فما كانت إلا ساعة حتى نسفت جبل الرمل وبانت الصخرة ، فقال عليه السّلام : هذه صخرتكم ، قالوا عليها أسماء الأنبياء ، وليس نرى عليها شيئا ؟ قال : اقلبوها تجدوها ، قال :

فاعصوصب عليها ألف رجل فلم يقدروا على تحريكها ثم قال : إليكم عنها فمد يده إليها فقلبها فوجدوا أسماء الأنبياء فأسلموا

Ammar ibn Yasir reports from Ali in a longer account. Ali passed through an estate two farsakhs from Kufah. Fifty Jews came out from it and said, 'We have a rock mentioned in our books. It bears the names of seven prophets. If you are an Imam, show us the rock.' Ali said, 'Follow me.' They came to a mountain of sand.

Ali said, 'O wind, scatter the sand by Allah's permission.' Within a short time, the wind scattered the mountain of sand and the rock appeared.

Ali said, 'This is your rock.' They said, 'The names of the prophets are on it, yet we see nothing on it.' Ali said, 'Turn it over and you will find them.' A thousand men gathered around it and could not move it. Then Ali said, 'Step away from it.' He reached out, turned it over, and they found the names of the prophets.

Then they accepted Islam.

- Ali through ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir, Ithbat al-Huda, vol.3, p.449

The fuller relay in Ibn Tawus's al-Yaqin names the instrument of the miracle. It is the same instrument that carried the throne of Bilqis. Ali ﵇ addresses the wind in the name of the Greatest Name.

Source: al-Yaqin, p.284

Speaker: Imam Ali, in the account of Abd Allah ibn Khalid.

Claim: Ali called on the wind to scatter the sand from the rock through the right of Allah's Greatest Name.

فقال علي ﵇ : اتبعوني .

قال عبد الله بن خالد : فسار القوم خلف أمير المؤمنين إلى أن استبطن فيهم البر ، وإذا بجبل من رمل عظيم ، فقال ﵇ : أيتها الريح ، انسفي الرمل عن الصخرة بحق اسم الله الأعظم .

"Ali said, 'Follow me.' Abd Allah ibn Khalid said, 'The people walked behind the Commander of the Faithful until they entered the open desert. There stood a great mountain of sand.' Ali said, 'O wind, scatter the sand from the rock through the right of Allah's Greatest Name.'"

- Ali, al-Yaqin, p.284

And al-Majlisi's relay in Bihar al-Anwar records what the flipped face carried. The names stood in a row, and the Jews drew their testimony from them.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar, vol.41, p.260

Speaker: The Jews of Nukhayla, on the rock that Ali uncovered.

Claim: The rock bore the names of six prophets who brought sacred laws, Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad, and the Jews bore witness to Allah, to Muhammad's message, and to Ali's guardianship.

فوجدوا عليها اسم ستة من الأنبياء ﵈ أصحاب الشرائع : آدم ونوح وإبراهيم وموسى وعيسى ومحمد عليهم الصلاة والسلام ، فقال (1) النفر اليهود ؟ نشهد أن لا إله إلا الله وأن محمدا رسول الله ﵌ وأنك أمير المؤمنين وسيد الوصيين وحجة الله في أرضه

The inscriptions on this rock are names that read, and the relays count them. One relay says seven. The relay just quoted says six and lists them. The difference between the relays is itself part of the record. The wind and the rock both answer the Name in the mouth of the Imam, and the Jews read the prophets' names and submit.▼ commentary

Solomon's Seal at the End of Days

The names on the rock were read, and the Jews submitted. One more seal belongs to the school's story before the market takes up the word at its stalls. This one belongs to the last days. Fayd al-Kashani preserves a sermon of Ali ﵇ that walks through the death of the Dajjal, the false messiah, and then names the next sign. The beast of the earth will rise from al-Safa carrying two objects. The first of them is the seal of Solomon ﵇.

The seal of Solomon ﵇, when the school gives it work, stamps the truth of a face before the assembly of the last day. The seal here marks and reveals, and the words it prints are words again.▼ commentary

When the Name Took Talisman Form

The beast of the last day stamps faces with words. The market will engrave marks without words. Between the hadith and the market stalls stands one scholar of the school explaining how the vocabulary of the talisman entered its prayer books. Ibn Tawus comments on a protective prayer whose lines address the Name directly. He suggests that the light the prayer speaks of may be the light of the Greatest Name, written into that very prayer in the form of a talisman.

Source: Muhaj al-Daawat, p.54

Speaker: Ibn Tawus, reading the words of the amulet he transmits.

Claim: The words, Allah refused anything except to complete your light, may mean the light of the Greatest Name itself, written in the amulet in the form of a talisman.

أقول وأما قوله فأبى الله إلا أن يتم نورك لعله يعني نورك أيها الاسم الأعظم المكتوب في هذا الحرز بصورة الطلسم

"I say that his words, 'Allah refused anything except to complete your light,' may mean your light, O Greatest Name, written in this amulet in the form of a talisman."

- Ibn Ṭāwūs, Muhaj al-Daawat, p.54

Ibn Tawus stands near the end of a development that had begun before him. Five centuries after Ali ﵇, the talisman started producing poems of its own that claim the Imam as their finder. Ibn Tawus himself still keeps the talisman inside a prayer of the Imams, a written protection to be recited. The record of the reversal sits in the school's own bibliographies. Al-Tihrani's al-Dhari'a describes the famous talisman among the people of invocation, and the mim-rhyme poem said to explain it, and then the rock.

Source: al-Dhari'a, vol.3, p.204

Speaker: The author of Riyad al-Ulama, relaying a chain from Shaykh Abu Ali al-Tabrisi, from al-Ra'is Abu al-Badr.

Claim: Ali saw the talisman of the Greatest Name in a rock and explained it through the poem with the mim rhyme.

The author of al-Riyad included part of this poem with the mim rhyme. He reported it from Shaykh Abu Ali al-Tabrisi through his chain from al-Ra'is Abu al-Badr, who said, 'I heard from a trustworthy person that the Commander of the Faithful Ali ibn Abi Talib saw this talisman in a rock.

He said that it was the Greatest Name and explained it through this poem with the mim rhyme.'

- al-Tihrani, al-Dhari'a, vol.3, p.204

The poem itself describes drawn marks. The seal carries three rods above it, with a lance-like stroke at their head.

Claim: The Diwan is a collection of poems attributed to Imam Ali whose rhymes run in the order of the alphabet's letters. Qutb al-Din al-Kaydari gathered it, and he learned from the exegete Abu Ali al-Tabrisi, who died in 548 AH.

A collection of poems attributed to Imam Ali, the Commander of the Faithful. Its rhymes run in the order of the alphabet's letters. Qutb al-Din al-Kaydari gathered it. He also wrote a commentary on Nahj al-Balagha called Gardens of Truths. He finished that commentary in 576 AH.

His full name is Shaykh Abu al-Hasan Muhammad ibn al-Husayn ibn al-Hasan al-Bayhaqi al-Naysaburi. He learned from the exegete Abu Ali al-Fadl ibn al-Hasan al-Tabrisi. Al-Tabrisi died in 548 AH.

- al-Tihrani, al-Dhari'a, vol.2, p.431

The 2011 study by Lloyd D. Graham assembles the manuscript evidence around this poem. The copy in the British Museum, Add. 7534, carries a drawing of seven marks beside the verses. The marks run from a seal and three rods, through a mutilated mim, a ladder, a second seal, a curved ha, and four strokes, ending in a run of shapes. The canonical series that later replaced it was fixed in the books of the Egyptian magician al-Buni, who died in 622 AH. That series runs from a many-pointed star through three rods, a broken mim, a ladder, a plain ha, a split ha, and an inverted waw. The first mark is repeated at the end. The Diwan drawing is the earliest known ancestor of the canonical series. The market plaques of today carry descendants of the same marks. The school's bibliography supplies what the manuscripts cannot, the date of the collection and the name of the compiler.▼ commentary

Claim: The marks of the talisman are five has, a line above a line, a cross with four dots around it, seven hamzas, then a ha, a waw, a sad, and a mim in the middle.

The word "attributed" on the printed page is the bibliography's verdict carried into the open. The poem's inventory needs no deciphering today. Graham's study documents the same inventory. Five has in a row, a cross with dots at its quarters, a run of seven marks, and the letters waw, ha, sad, and mim. All of these are engraved on hand-crafted brass plaques sold new in Iran. They appear as well on the yellow onyx stones called sharaf al-shams, the dignity of the sun, cut on the night the moon stands in Scorpio. In the healing design the study calls the Pleiades Square, the cross carries seven dots for the seven stars. The run of seven marks beside it reads as seven repeated ayns. The glyph inventory of the market is the poem's inventory. Both descend from the marks that the mim-rhyme poem first drew beside the Name.▼ commentary

What the Hand Keeps Today

Against all of this the school keeps its own small objects, and its reports bless them plainly. The ring with its stone is the first. A Zaydi collection of the school preserves the famous report through a full chain of the Imams. The chain runs from al-Sadiq ﵇ through al-Baqir ﵇, al-Sajjad ﵇, and the Martyr al-Husayn ﵇ to Ali ﵇ and the Prophet ﷺ, with Jibril ﵇ as the speaker.

Source: Majmu Rasail al-Mansur billah, vol.1, p.454

Speaker: The Prophet Muhammad, from Jibril, relayed through Ali to al-Sajjad, al-Baqir, and al-Sadiq to al-A'mash.

Claim: Jibril directed the wearing of agate rings, the first stone to bear witness that Allah is one, that Muhammad is His Prophet, that Ali is the heir, that his two sons are Imams, and that his followers will enter Paradise.

فمن ذلك ما رويناه في باب تختموا بالعقيق ، رويناه عنه ، ورفعه إلى كثير بن زيد قال : دخل الأعمش على المنصور وهو جالس للمظالم ؛ فلما بصر به قال له :

يا سليمان تصدر . فقال : أنا صدر حيث جلست ، ثم قال : حدثني الصادق ، قال :

حدثني الباقر ، قال : حدثني السجاد ، قال : حدثني الشهيد ، قال : حدثني التقي وهو الوصي أمير المؤمنين علي بن أبي طالب ﵈ قال : حدثني النبي صلى اللّه عليه وآله وسلم قال :

«أتاني جبريل ﵇ آنفا فقال : تختموا بالعقيق فإنه أول حجر شهد للّه بالوحدانية ولي بالنبوة ولعلي بالوصية ولولديه بالإمامة ولشيعته بالجنة»

Among the reports we have transmitted on wearing an agate ring is this report. It reaches Kathir ibn Zayd, who said, al-A'mash entered upon al-Mansur while he was hearing grievances.

When al-Mansur saw him, al-Mansur said, 'Sulayman, sit at the front.' al-A'mash replied, 'I sit at the front wherever I sit.' Then al-A'mash said, 'al-Sadiq told me, al-Baqir told him, al-Sajjad told him, the Martyr told him, and the Pious One, the heir and Commander of the Faithful Ali ibn Abi Talib, told him that the Prophet said, Jibril came to me just now and said, Wear agate rings.

The stone the school blesses does no work of its own. It bears witness, the report says, to oneness, to prophethood, and to the executorship. The believer who wears it wears a testimony.

The stars that the talisman trade pressed into its dots have their own place in the school. When the school looks at the Pleiades, it hears an argument for the Maker. Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ teaches Mufaddal to read the seasonal stars as designed signs.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p.117

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq, on the stars that appear and disappear.

Claim: The stars appear and disappear at different times so that people can benefit from the signs each one gives on its own, and know what follows their rising and their setting.

فكر في هذه النجوم التي تظهر في بعض السنة وتحتجب في بعضها كمثل الثريا والجوزاء والشعريين وسهيل فإنها لو كانت بأسرها تظهر في وقت واحد لم تكن لواحد فيها على حياله دلالات يعرفها الناس ويهتدون بها لبعض أمورهم كمعرفتهم الآن بما يكون من طلوع الثور والجوزاء إذا طلعت ، واحتجابها إذا احتجبت فصار ظهور كل واحد واحتجابه في وقت غير وقت الآخر لينتفع الناس بما يدل عليه كل واحد منها على حدته ،

Think about these stars that appear during part of the year and disappear during another part, such as the Pleiades, Orion, Sirius and Procyon, and Suhayl. If they all appeared at the same time, none of them would give people its own signs by which they could learn and guide themselves in some of their affairs.

Now people know what follows the rising of Taurus and Orion, and what follows when they disappear. Each one appears and disappears at a different time from the others, so people can benefit from the signs that each one gives on its own.

- Imam al-Sadiq, Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p.117

The Pleiades that the healing square pressed into its seven dots stand in the school's own record as part of the ordering of the sky. Each star arrives on its own schedule so that travelers and farmers can read it. The star cluster is a signpost with an appointment, and the Imam draws the lesson of design from its punctuality.▼ commentary

Commentary

The Sources That Carry This Story

Source: al-Kafi, Tafsir al-Qummi, al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Quran, Muhaj al-Daawat, al-Yaqin, Bihar al-Anwar, Ithbat al-Huda, Ilm al-Yaqin, Riyad al-Ulama, al-Dhari'a, Majmu Rasail al-Mansur billah

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329 AH), al-Bahrani (d.1107 AH), Ibn Tawus (d.664 AH), al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), al-Hurr al-Amili (d.1104 AH), Fayd al-Kashani (d.1091 AH), Mirza Abd Allah Afandi, al-Tihrani (d.1389 AH)

Source: al-Kafi vol.1 p.278, vol.8 p.223. Tafsir al-Qummi vol.1 pp.80-81. al-Burhan vol.3 p.11. Muhaj al-Daawat p.54, p.328. Bihar al-Anwar vol.3 p.117, vol.41 p.260, vol.92 p.404. Ithbat al-Huda vol.3 p.449. Ilm al-Yaqin vol.2 p.260. Riyad al-Ulama vol.5 p.419. al-Dhari'a vol.2 p.431, vol.3 p.204. Majmu Rasail al-Mansur billah vol.1 p.454

Source: Lloyd D. Graham, Repeat-Letter Ciphers in Islamic Talismans, Epigraphic Society Occasional Papers 29 (2011), pp.70-91

The hadith core of this story sits in books of the 3rd and 4th centuries AH. The school carries these reports with chains of mixed strength. The al-Kafi report of the prophets' letters itself rests on a narrator whose name the relater forgot. The reports are carried here as the school carries them, without grading the chains. The talisman poems enter the written record in the 6th century AH, and the school's bibliographies mark them as attributed material. Every Arabic quotation above is carried verbatim from these books as they stand in the corpus on titan. The modern study is cited only where the manuscripts and the market stalls are described.

Letters of a Word, Not Marks on a Stone

Source: al-Kafi, vol.1, p.278. al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Quran, vol.3, p.11

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ and Imam al-Askari ﵇. Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi in relay

Claim: the Greatest Name has seventy-three letters, measured to the prophets, gathered in Muhammad ﷺ except one, and its letters lie scattered in the Quran's separated openings

The two reports of al-Kafi and the gloss of al-Qummi fix the school's grammar for the Name. It is counted in letters of speech. Prophets receive letters. Asaf speaks a letter. The dead rise at the words of Hizqil. The Imam gathers the scattered pieces from the Quran and calls. Al-Qummi's gloss names the mechanism precisely. The separated letters that open the suras are pieces of the Name, and the gathering of them belongs to a Messenger or an Imam inside an act of prayer.

The written prayer of the fevered young man belongs to this same world, and the report itself calls his book the Greatest Name because the book carries the taught words. The hadith reports themselves say nothing of glyphs. The first meeting of the two worlds appears only in the later prayer books, in the line of Ibn Tawus quoted in the narration above. A drawing of seven marks is a fixed shape. The Name in these reports is a word measured out, withheld in part, gathered from the Book, and spent in asking. The marketplace reading of the seals arrives from the opposite direction, from poems describing drawn shapes.

Why Solomon Stands Cleared

Source: Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.1, p.81

Speaker: Imam al-Baqir ﵇, through Abu Basir, in Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi's relay

Claim: Iblis wrote the book of magic after Solomon's death and signed it in Asaf's name, and the verse of al-Baqara came down clearing Solomon of it

The report gives the school a working theory of forged attributions, and it is worth reading slowly. The forged book carries a famous name on its cover. It names the treasure it claims to open. It instructs the reader in powers. And it is dug up for an audience ready to believe. The Quran's verdict lands on the whole procedure. Solomon ﵇ did not disbelieve.

The one who worked by the Name in the court of Solomon ﵇ was Asaf. The book signed with Asaf's name was the devil's. Every seal and talisman later attributed to Solomon ﵇, and every glyph series later attributed to Ali ﵇, walks the path this verse already judged. The school's own Solomon story ends with his seal in the hands of the beast of the last day. That seal stamps the truth of every face, and the truth is what it reveals.

Six Names or Seven

Source: Ithbat al-Huda, vol.3, p.449. Bihar al-Anwar, vol.41, p.260. al-Yaqin, p.284

Speaker: Ali ﵇, through Ammar ibn Yasir and through Abd Allah ibn Khalid

Claim: the rock at Nukhayla carried the names of the lawbearing prophets, six in the Bihar relay, seven in the relay of al-Hurr al-Amili

The Nukhayla story is the school's genuine rock story, and its details are the mirror image of the talisman legend. The Jews bring the claim from their books. The Imam uncovers the rock in the name of the Greatest Name. The wind obeys as it obeyed for the throne of Bilqis. And the revealed inscription is a list of readable names, Adam ﵇ at the head and Muhammad ﷺ at the seal.

One relay of al-Hurr al-Amili says seven prophets. The Bihar relay says six and lists them. Both counts circulate in the same books without friction. The story answers the question the talisman legend borrows for itself, whether Ali ﵇ found something holy written on a rock. Its answer keeps the inscription inside the world of words, names that read, names that end in testimony.

When the Poems Were Collected

Source: al-Dhari'a, vol.2, p.431, vol.3, p.204. Riyad al-Ulama, vol.5, p.419. Asrar al-Huruf, p.91

Source: Qutb al-Din al-Kaydari, the compiler of the Diwan carrying the seal poems, finished his Nahj al-Balagha commentary in 576 AH

Claim: the legend that Ali ﵇ saw the talisman in a rock and named it the Greatest Name enters the record through Abu Ali al-Tabrisi, relaying one Abu al-Badr, who heard it from an unnamed trustworthy source

The chain of the rock legend is short and late, and the school's greatest bibliographer records it without granting it the standing of hadith. Five centuries separate the compiler from Ali ﵇. The poem's own wording, three rods beside a seal, describes a drawing that had already left the world of words. The Shaykh Abu Ali al-Tabrisi who carries the rock legend is in all likelihood the same exegete under whom the Diwan's compiler studied. If so, the legend and the collection that preserves it grew inside one twelfth-century circle.

The ta-rhyme poem then converts the marks into a recipe. Five has, a stroke over a stroke, a cross with four dots, seven hamzas, waw, ha, sad, mim, promising healing to whoever carries the engraving. Graham's study of the surviving talismans shows what the plaques and the sharaf al-shams stones of the present market actually carry. The inventory is exactly this one, alongside the seven seals of the al-Buni tradition described above. The study traces each series back to the prototype drawn beside the mim-rhyme poem in the British Museum copy of the Diwan. It also shows the seven-dot cross standing for the seven stars of the Pleiades inside the healing square of al-Buni's tradition. And it argues one step further. The cipher marks are themselves letters worn down by copying, five has and seven ayns flattened into ornament, faded writing at the bottom of their history. The bibliographic verdict and the manuscript evidence meet at one point. The glyphs are the afterlife of late poetry, and the poetry is the afterlife of a longing for the Name that the hadith had already placed somewhere else entirely.

The Stones and Stars the School Blesses

Source: Majmu Rasail al-Mansur billah, vol.1, p.454. Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p.117

Speaker: the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ through Jibril ﵇. Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ to Mufaddal

Claim: the agate ring is the first stone to testify to Allah's oneness, the Prophet's ﷺ prophethood, and Ali's ﵇ executorship, and the seasonal stars, the Pleiades among them, are designed signs on appointed schedules

The school blesses a stone and reads the sky, and neither blessing is a spell. The agate of the ring witnesses, and the report makes its testimony a sentence of creed. Oneness, prophethood, executorship, imamate, and a promise for the plain people who follow. The Pleiades of the talisman square appear in the Imam's teaching as evidence for a Maker who schedules His signs. Each star keeps its own clock, so people can read the year from the sky.

Where the market engraves the seven stars into a cipher of dots, the school points at the same cluster and draws the lesson of purpose from its order. The difference between the two uses of the same heavens is the whole distance this story has traveled.

Where the Name Rests

The reports of the school end the argument themselves. The Greatest Name rests where they put it, in letters measured to the prophets, gathered from the Quran, held by the house of the Prophet ﷺ, and spent in calling upon Allah. It raises the dead in one report, sweeps a mountain of sand in another, and answers a fevered young man before dawn.

The carved glyphs sold under the Imam's name are the later poets' shadow of that Name. They were gathered in a Diwan compiled in the sixth century AH. Verses inventory the marks. The school's own bibliographers record them as attributed works. The longing the glyphs carry is real, and the school answers it with a stone that witnesses and a sky that keeps its appointments.
