The Expedition of Dhat Amr

Ghatafan gathered at Dhat Amr to raid the edges of Medina, the Prophet ﷺ marched with four hundred and fifty men, the gathering fled to the heights, and the war chief who reached the sleeping Prophet with a drawn sword left as a Muslim. Part 9 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Gathering and the Tent

Ghatafan tried again. After the scattering at al-Kudr, a gathering of Tha'laba and Muharib collected at the water of Dhat Amr in Najd, the high plateau east of Medina. The gathering meant to raid the edges of the city, and the man who gathered them was Du'thur ibn al-Harith al-Muharibi. The Prophet ﷺ marched out with four hundred and fifty men and horses, stayed the month of Safar in Najd, and the tribes fled to the mountain tops. On that ground stands one of the strangest scenes in the whole sira, the Prophet's biography.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Ibn Ishaq's frame, carried by Ibn Kathir, with the note of Ibn Hisham that names his deputy over Medina.

قال ابن إسحاق : فلما رجع رسول الله ﵌ من غزوة السويق أقام بالمدينة بقية ذي الحجة أو قريبا منها ، ثم غزا نجدا يريد غطفان ، وهي غزوة ذي أمر .

قال ابن هشام : واستعمل على المدينة عثمان بن عفان .

"Ibn Ishaq said. When the Messenger of Allah came back from the expedition of al-Sawiq, he stayed in Medina for the rest of Dhu al-Hijja or most of it. Then he marched on Najd, with Ghatafan in view. This is the expedition of Dhat Amr. Ibn Hisham said. He put Uthman ibn Affan in charge of Medina. Ibn Ishaq said. He stayed in Najd the whole of Safar or most of it, then went back and met no hostility."

فأقام بنجد صفرا كله أو قريبا من ذلك ثم رجع ولم يلق كيدا

- Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Kathir, al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, vol.4, p.3, with Ibn Hisham's deputy note

Al-Bayhaqi's Dala'il gives the cause and the size of the force.

قالوا بلغ رسول الله ﵌ أن جمعا من غطفان من بني ثعلبة بن محارب بذي أمر قد تجمعوا يريدون أن يصيبوا من أطراف رسول الله ﵌ معهم رجل منهم يقال له دعثور بن الحارث بن محارب فندب رسول الله ﵌ المسلمين فخرج في أربعمائة رجل وخمسين رجلا ومعهم

"They said. Word reached the Messenger of Allah that a group of Ghatafan, of Banu Tha'laba ibn Muharib, had gathered at Dhat Amr, meaning to strike at his outskirts. With them was a man of theirs called Du'thur ibn al-Harith ibn Muharib. The Messenger of Allah called the Muslims out, and he went out with four hundred and fifty men, and they had horses with them."

- Ibn Ishaq via al-Bayhaqi, Dala'il al-Nubuwwa, vol.3, p.170

Al-Baladhuri's entry adds the guide who converted on the road.▼ commentary

ثم غزاة بنى غطفان ،

بذى أمر ، بنجد . وكانت في شهر ربيع الأول سنة ثلاث . وكان سببها أن جمعا من بنى ثعلبة بن سعد بن ذبيان بن بغيض ابن ريث بن غطفان ، وبنى محارب بن خصفة بن قيس تجمعوا يريدون أن يصيبوا من أطراف رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم . وكان الذي جمعهم دعثور ابن الحارث المحاربي . وبلغ رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم خبرهم ، فخرج في أربع مائة وخمسين . وصار إلى ذي القصّة ، فلقى بها رجلا من بنى ثعلبة . فقال له المسلمون : أين تريد ؟ فقال : أريد يثرب لأرتاد لنفسي وأنظر . فدعاه رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم إلى الإسلام . فأسلم ، وأخبر أن المشركين تجمعوا

"Then the expedition of Banu Ghatafan at Dhat Amr in Najd. It fell in Rabi' al-Awwal of the third year. Its cause was that a gathering of Banu Tha'laba ibn Sad ibn Dhubyan ibn Baghid ibn Rayth ibn Ghatafan, and Banu Muharib ibn Khasafa ibn Qays, had collected, meaning to strike at the outskirts of the Messenger of Allah, and the one who gathered them was Du'thur ibn al-Harith al-Muharibi. Word of them reached the Messenger of Allah, and he went out with four hundred and fifty. He came to Dhat al-Qassa and met there a man of Banu Tha'laba. The Muslims asked him where he was going, and he said, 'I am for Yathrib, to scout for myself and look around.' The Messenger of Allah called him to Islam, and he became Muslim and told them the idolaters had gathered."

- al-Baladhuri, Ansab al-Ashraf, vol.1, p.365

The rain, the tree, and the watching enemy.

وهربت منه الأعراب فوق ذرى من الجبال ونزل رسول الله ﵌ ذا أمر وعسكر به فأصابهم مطر كثير فذهب رسول الله ﵌ لحاجته فأصابه ذلك المطر فبل ثوبه وقد جعل رسول الله ﵌ وادي ذي أمر بينه وبين أصحابه ثم نزع ثيابه فنشرها لتجف وألقاها على شجرة ثم اضطجع تحتها والأعراب ينظرون إلى كل ما يفعل رسول الله ﵌

"The Bedouins fled from him to the mountain peaks. The Messenger of Allah came down at Dhat Amr and camped there, and heavy rain fell on them. The Messenger of Allah went off to see to a need, and the rain soaked his garment. He had put the valley of Dhat Amr between himself and his companions. He took off his garments, spread them to dry, hung them on a tree, and lay down beneath it, while the Bedouins watched everything the Messenger of Allah did."

- Ibn Ishaq via al-Bayhaqi, Dala'il al-Nubuwwa, vol.3, p.170, the rain and the tree

The Shia abridgement al-Mustafa min Sira al-Mustafa carries the whole scene to its end.

فأسلم ، ودلهم على الطريق إليهم ؛ فسمعوا بمسير الرسول (﵌) فهربوا في رؤوس الجبال .

ويذكر هنا : أنه أصاب الرسول (﵌) مطر كثير ؛ فنزع (﵌) ثوبيه ، ونشرهما على شجرة ، واضطجع بمرأى من المشركين . واشتغل المسلمون في شؤونهم ، فنزل إليه دعثور «زعيم المشركين الغطفانيين» حتى وقف على رأسه ، ثم قال : «من يمنعك مني اليوم ؟» فقال (﵌) : «الله» . ودفع جبرئيل في صدره ، فوقع على ظهره ، ووقع السيف من يده ، فأخذ النبي (﵌) السيف ، وقال له : «من يمنعك مني ؟» قال : «لا أحد ، أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله ، وأشهد أن محمداً رسول الله» . فأعطاه (﵌) سيفه ؛ فرجع إلى قومه ، وجعل يدعوهم للإسلام

"He became Muslim and guided them on the road to them. The enemy heard of the Messenger's march and fled to the mountain tops. It is told here that heavy rain caught the Messenger. He took off his two garments, spread them on a tree, and lay down within sight of the idolaters. The Muslims were busy with their affairs, and Du'thur, the chief of the Ghatafan idolaters, came down to him and stood over his head. He said, 'Who will protect you from me today?' The Messenger said, 'Allah.' Jibril pushed him in the chest, and he fell on his back, and the sword fell from his hand. The Prophet took the sword and said to him, 'And who will protect you from me?' He said, 'No one. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.' He gave him his sword back, and the man went back to his people and called them to Islam."

- al-Amili, al-Mustafa min Sira al-Mustafa, vol.2, p.78

The tafsir of al-Qubaysi al-Amili carries the scene whole and closes it with the verse it brought down.

فأصابه مطر فبل ثوبه فنشره على شجرة واضطجع تحته والاعراب ينظرون اليه فجاء سيدهم دعثور بن الحرث حتى وقف على رأسه بالسيف مشهورا ، فقال يا محمد من يمنعك مني اليوم ، فقال ص : الله ، فدفع جبرائيل في صدره فوقع السيف من يده ، فأخذه رسول الله ص وقام على رأسه . فقال : من يمنعك مني قال لا أحد وانا أشهد أن لا إله الا الله وان محمدا رسول الله فنزلت الآية

"Rain caught him and soaked his garment. He spread it on a tree and lay beneath it, while the Bedouins watched him. Their chief Du'thur ibn al-Harith came and stood over his head with the sword drawn, and said, 'Muhammad, who will protect you from me today?' He said, 'Allah.' Jibril pushed him in the chest, and the sword fell from his hand. The Messenger of Allah took it and stood over his head and said, 'And who will protect you from me?' He said, 'No one, and I testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.' And the verse came down."

- al-Qubaysi al-Amili, Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi, vol.2, p.401

The Qur'an set the event into a verse, in the tafsir line above.▼ commentary

يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱذْكُرُوا۟ نِعْمَتَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذْ هَمَّ قَوْمٌ أَن يَبْسُطُوٓا۟ إِلَيْكُمْ أَيْدِيَهُمْ فَكَفَّ أَيْدِيَهُمْ عَنكُمْ ۖ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ۚ وَعَلَى ٱللَّهِ فَلْيَتَوَكَّلِ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ

yā ayyuhā lladhīna āmanū dhkurū niʿmata llāhi ʿalaykum idh hamma qawmun an yabsuṭū ilaykum aydiyahum fa-kaffa aydiyahum ʿankum wa-ttaqū llāha wa-ʿalā llāhi fa-l-yatawakkali l-mu'minūn

"You who believe, remember Allah's favor toward you, when a people intended to stretch out their hands against you, and He held their hands back from you. So be mindful of Allah, and in Allah let the believers put their trust."

- Qur'an 5:11, Uthmani text

Commentary

The Sources for Dhat Amr

Source spine: Ibn Ishaq, carried by Ibn Kathir, al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, vol.4, p.3, and al-Bayhaqi, Dala'il al-Nubuwwa, vol.3, p.170

Other carriers: al-Baladhuri, Ansab al-Ashraf, vol.1, p.365, who dates it to Rabi' al-Awwal

Shia lines: al-Amili, al-Mustafa min Sira al-Mustafa, vol.2, p.78. al-Qubaysi al-Amili, Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi, vol.2, p.401, who closes the scene with the verse

Scripture: Qur'an 5:11, Uthmani text

Deputy: Uthman ibn Affan, on the Ibn Hisham note Ibn Kathir carries

The carriers agree on the frame, the Ghatafan gathering under Du'thur, the march of four hundred and fifty, and the flight to the heights. The tent scene's opening, the rain and the tree, is al-Bayhaqi's, and the sword's turn belongs to the Shia lines, al-Amili and al-Qubaysi al-Amili, whose telling closes with the verse. The month divides the dating, Safar in Ibn Ishaq's frame against Rabi' al-Awwal in al-Baladhuri's, and both readings stay in view.

The Sword Over the Sleeping Prophet

The attempt: Du'thur, the war chief of the gathering, stood over the sleeping Prophet ﷺ with a drawn sword

The answer: the Prophet ﷺ said one word, Allah

The result: the attacker fell on his back, the sword changed hands, and the chief testified and took his own sword back

Aftermath: he went home and called his people to Islam

Read the scene as the sources give it. The Muslims were busy with their affairs, the enemy was watching from the heights, and the one man who reached the Prophet ﷺ came away with a faith, having come for a murder. The Qur'an's own record of the event keeps the reading exact, hands intended to stretch out, and hands held back. The war band had already fled to the heights, and the army went home after its month in Najd. No battle happened, and no one was killed on either side.

What the March Achieved

Deterrence: the second Ghatafan gathering in two months scattered without a fight

Guide gained: a man of Tha'laba met on the road converted and led the army

Notable convert: Du'thur, the enemy war chief, by the tent scene

Cost: no battle, no dead, a month of campaigning

The tactical outcome was another bloodless dispersal. The strategic outcome ran deeper than al-Kudr. The army had now shown it would come back, month after month, and hold the Najd plateau through a full month of Safar. The tribes learned that gathering against Medina brought the state's army to their own waters.

Was the Expedition of Dhat Amr Justified

Like al-Kudr, the march answered a war gathering, and here two carriers state the intent in the same words, a gathering meaning to strike at the edges of Medina. The response was again proportional, a march to the enemy's own ground, a month of presence, no pursuit of the fleeing, and the record names no plunder.

The tent scene shows the character of the encounter. The enemy chief stood armed over an unarmed sleeping man, and the Prophet ﷺ sent him home with his sword and a new faith. The verse the tafsir line ties to the event names restraint of hands as the favor remembered, hands intended to stretch out, and hands held back.

Could it have been prevented? The gathering was the tribes' choice, twice repeated in two months. The march scattered the gathering it answered, and no talks are recorded. The one man who came to kill went home with his own sword, calling his people to Islam.
