The Battle of the Trench

The exiled Nadir bought their revenge, rousing Quraysh and the desert tribes into a ten thousand man confederacy, and the Muslims answered with a trench, a duel won by Ali ﵇, and a siege that the east wind ended after twenty nights. Part 18 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Confederacy and the Ditch

The exiled Nadir bought their revenge. Their men went out from Khaybar to Mecca, then to Ghatafan, Sulaym, and Asad, and roused them all to war. The confederacy that gathered against Medina numbered ten thousand, Quraysh under Abu Sufyan with the desert tribes behind it.

The Muslims could not meet ten thousand men in the open field. They answered it with a trench, on the advice of Salman the Persian, a fortification the Arabs had never seen.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Al-Mazandarani's commentary on al-Kafi carries the confederacy whole.

وقصدهم قتله (﵇) حين طلب منهم الجزية فخرجوا إلى خيبر ثم اجتمعت منهم ومن غيرهم من اليهود فخرج بعضهم إلى مكة لاستنفار القريش ومن يحذو حذوهم ودان بمقالتهم إلى حرب الرسول (﵌) وبعضهم إلى غطفان وبعضهم إلى سليم وبعضهم إلى بني أسد وبعضهم إلى غير هؤلاء من قبايل العرب وحرضوهم على المحاربة واستنفروهم فأجمعت قريش السير إلى المدينة مع أربعة آلاف وأميرهم أبو سفيان بن حرب بن أمية ولحق بهم غطفان وأميرهم عيينة بن حصن الفزاري ومنهم بنو أشجع قبيلة بن غطفان وأميرهم طلحة الأزوي بنو فزارة وبنو أسد وبنو سليم وبنو عمرو وغيرهم وأميرهم عامر بن الطفيل إلى غير هؤلاء حتى بلغوا عشرة آلاف واتصل خبرهم برسول الله (﵌) فأمر بحفر الخندق حول المدينة وكان أمراً لم تعهده العرب وإنما كان من عمل فارس والروم

"Their aim was to kill him when he asked them for the tribute, so they went out to Khaybar, and then they gathered, men of theirs and other Jews, and some of them went to Mecca to rouse Quraysh and whoever followed their word to war against the Messenger, and some to Ghatafan, and some to Sulaym, and some to Banu Asad, and some to other tribes of the Arabs, and they pressed them to make war and roused them. So Quraysh resolved on the march to Medina with four thousand, their commander Abu Sufyan ibn Harb ibn Umayya, and Ghatafan joined them under Uyayna ibn Hisn al-Fazari, and of them Banu Ashja under Talha al-Azwi, and Fazara, and Asad, and Sulaym, and Amir ibn al-Tufayl, until they came to ten thousand. Word of them reached the Messenger of Allah, and he ordered the trench dug around Medina, a thing the Arabs had never known, for it was a work of the Persians and the Romans."

- al-Mazandarani, Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol.12, p.395, the confederacy

وبنو قريظة عاهدوا رسول الله (﵌) على أن لا يلحقه منهم ضرر فلما حاصروا دخلهم بنو النضير وحملوهم على نقض العهد فساءت الظنون ورسول الله (﵌) يبشر ويعدهم بالنصر من عند الله تعالى والأحزاب يطلبون من الخندق مضيقاً للمرور ولم يجدوه مع أن سلمة بن أسلم مع مائتي نفر وزيد بن حارثة مع ثلاثمائة نفر كانوا يحرسون الخندق وعند ذلك برز عمرو بن عبد ود وكان شجاعاً معروفاً في العرب ومعه عكرمة بن أبي جهل وطايفة أخرى فطلب عمرو مبارزاً فخرج أمير المؤمنين (﵇) فقتله وانهزم عكرمة وأصحابه وألقى الله الرعب في قلوب المشركين ويئسوا من الظفر ، ثم أن الله سبحانه أرسل ريح الصبا فهدمت خيامهم وقطعت حبالهم وأكفأت قدورهم ولم يمكنهم معها قرار . وقد قيل إن الله تعالى بعث مع الرياح ملائكة تشددوها فخافوا حتى أزمعوا الرحلة بعد بضع وعشرين ليلة فانصرفوا

"The Qurayza had made a pact with the Messenger of Allah that no harm would come to him from them, and when the siege closed in, the Nadir entered in on them and pressed them to break the pact, and expectations darkened, while the Messenger of Allah gave good news and promised them victory from Allah. The confederates looked for a narrow place to cross the trench and found none, while Salma ibn Aslam with two hundred men and Zayd ibn Haritha with three hundred guarded it. Then Amr ibn Abd Wudd came out, a rider of known courage among the Arabs, with Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl and a party, and Amr called for a duel, and Ali, the Commander of the Faithful, went out to him and killed him, and Ikrima and his companions fled, and Allah cast terror into the hearts of the idolaters, and they despaired of victory. Then Allah sent the east wind, and it threw down their tents, cut their ropes, and overturned their pots, and they could not stand firm under it, and it is said Allah sent angels with the winds to drive them on. They were afraid, and resolved on departure after a little over twenty nights, and they went away disappointed."

- al-Mazandarani, Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol.12, p.395, the duel and the wind

The Ismaili judge al-Nu'man carries the digging and the labor of it.▼ commentary

وانتهى أمرهم إلى رسول الله صلوات الله عليه وآله فأمر بحفر الخندق على المدينة وما والاها مما يحتاج إلى حياطته والتفسح فيه ، فبادر المسلمون إلى ذلك ، وكان من بعضهم فيه تقصير فعمل فيه رسول الله صلوات الله عليه وآله بيده ، وكان علي صلوات الله عليه وشيعته أكثر الناس عناء ، وفيه عملا ، وكان في ذلك من الاخبار ما يطول ذكره . فلم تأت جموع الأحزاب إلا بعد أن فرغ رسول الله صلوات الله عليه وآله منه فأناخوا حول المدينة من كل جانب وخرج إليهم اليهود وبعض من كان منهم قد حالف رسول الله صلوات الله عليه وآله حلفه - وهم بنو قريظة - وصاروا مع الأحزاب إلبا على رسول الله صلوات الله عليه وآله ، فأرجف المنافقون من أهل المدينة لذلك .

"The matter reached the Messenger of Allah, and he ordered the trench dug around Medina and whatever of it needed guarding and elbow room. The Muslims rushed to it, though some of them fell short in the work, and the Messenger of Allah labored on it with his own hands, and Ali, peace be upon him, and his partisans bore the most hardship of all men, untiring in it, and there were reports in that too long to tell. The hosts of the confederates came only after the Messenger of Allah had finished it, and they camped around the city from every side. The Jews went out to them, and with them some of those who had been the allies of the Messenger of Allah, the Qurayza, and they turned with the confederates against the Messenger of Allah, and the hypocrites of Medina spread alarm over it."

- al-Nu'man al-Maghribi, Sharh al-Akhbar, vol.1, p.292

And the order to hold.

وأمر رسول الله صلوات الله عليه وآله المسلمين بالثبات في مكانهم ولزوم خندقهم وبادخال النساء والولدان والضعفاء من الرجال في أطام المدينة وحصونها لتسكن أنفسهم ووعدهم نصر الله عز وجل إياهم . ونظر المشركون إلى الخندق فتهيبوا القدوم عليه ولم يكونوا قبل ذلك رأوا مثله ، وقالوا إن هذه لمكيدة ما كانت العرب تعرفها

"The Messenger of Allah ordered the Muslims to hold their ground, keep to the trench, and bring the women, the children, and the weak men into the forts and strongholds of Medina to still their hearts, and he promised them the victory of Allah. The idolaters looked at the trench and dreaded the coming to it, and they had never seen the like of it, and they said, 'This is a stratagem the Arabs never knew.'"

- al-Nu'man al-Maghribi, Sharh al-Akhbar, vol.1, p.292, the hold order

Abu al-Fida fixes the date and preserves the signs of the digging.▼ commentary

زوة الأحزاب وكانت في شوال من هذه السنة وبلغ رسول الله ﵌ تحزب قبائل العرب فأمر بحفر الخندق حول المدينة قيل : إِنه كان بإِشارة سلمان الفارسي وهو أول مشهد شهده مع رسول الله ﵌ وظهرت للنبي ﵌ في حفر الخندق عدة معجزات .

اشتدت عليهم كدية أي صخرة فدعا النبي ﵌ وسلم بماء وتفل فيه ونضحه عليها فانهالت تحت المساحي ومنها أن ابنة بشير بن سعد الأنصاري وهي أخت النعمان بن بشير بعثتها أمها بقليل تمر غذاء أبيها بشير وخالها عبد الله بن رواحة فمرت برسول الله ﵌ فدعاها وقال : " هاتي ما معك يا بنية " قالت : فصببت ذلك التمر في كفي رسول الله ﵌ فما امتلأتا ثم دعا رسول اللّه ﵌ بثوب وبدد ذلك التمر عليه ثم قال لإِنسان : اصرخ في أهل الخندق أنْ هلموا إِلى الغداء فجعلوا يأكلون منه وجعل يزيد حتى صدر أهل الخندق عنه

"The expedition of the Confederates. It fell in Shawwal of that year. Word reached the Messenger of Allah that the tribes of the Arabs had formed a confederacy, and he ordered the trench dug around Medina, and it is said it was on the advice of Salman the Persian, and it was the first battle he saw with the Messenger of Allah. A number of signs appeared to the Prophet in the digging of the trench. A hard boulder stopped them, so the Prophet called for water, spat into it, and sprinkled it on the rock, and it crumbled under the spades. And among them, the daughter of Bashir ibn Sa'd of the Ansar, sister of al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, whose mother sent her with a handful of dates as food for her father and her uncle Abd Allah ibn Rawaha. She passed by the Messenger of Allah, and he called her and said, 'Give me what you carry, daughter.' She said, 'I poured the dates into the hands of the Messenger of Allah, and they did not fill them. Then the Messenger of Allah called for a cloth, spread the dates on it, and said to a man, cry out to the people of the trench, come to the meal.' And they kept eating of it, and it kept growing, until the people of the trench turned away from it filled."

- Abu al-Fida, al-Mukhtasar fi Akhbar al-Bashar, vol.1, p.144

Commentary

The Sources for the Trench

Shia lines: al-Mazandarani, Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol.12, p.395, on the confederacy, the duel, and the wind. al-Nu'man al-Maghribi, Sharh al-Akhbar, vol.1, p.292, on the digging and the hold order

Sunni line: Abu al-Fida, al-Mukhtasar fi Akhbar al-Bashar, vol.1, p.144, on the date, Salman, and the signs

Scripture: Qur'an 33:9 and 33:10, Uthmani text

Dating: Shawwal of year five by Abu al-Fida and al-Mazandarani's main line

The layers agree on the frame. The Nadir's instigation, the ten thousand, the trench, the Qurayza treachery, the duel of Amr ibn Abd Wudd, and the wind that broke the siege.

The Digging and the Stratagem

Confederacy: ten thousand, Quraysh under Abu Sufyan, Ghatafan under Uyayna ibn Hisn, with Ashja, Fazara, Asad, Sulaym, and Amir ibn al-Tufayl

The trench: Salman's advice, a Persian and Roman work new to the Arabs, finished before the hosts arrived

The labor: the Prophet ﷺ dug with his own hands, and Ali ﵇ and his partisans bore the most hardship of all

The hold: women and children in the forts, the army in the trench, under orders to hold

The stratagem worked as designed. The confederate cavalry circled the trench and found no crossing, and the works keep the enemy's own verdict on it, a stratagem the Arabs never knew. The diggers' signs belong to the same ground. The boulder crumbled, and a handful of dates fed the trench, and Abu al-Fida sets them down as the army remembered them.

The Duel and the Wind

The duel: Amr ibn Abd Wudd, a champion of known courage, fell to Ali ﵇ at the trench, and Ikrima's party fled

The treachery: the Qurayza broke their pact under Nadir pressure, inside the walls, while the army held the trench

The guard: Salma ibn Aslam with two hundred, Zayd ibn Haritha with three hundred

The wind: the east wind threw down tents, cut ropes, overturned pots, and the confederates marched home after some twenty nights

Casualties: the works quoted here name Amr alone; the Qurayza affair belongs to the next part

The confederacy sent Amr out to force a crossing, and his death broke the deadlock instead. The terror the sources name then entered their councils. The wound inside the walls ran deeper than anything outside them.

The Qurayza break was an armed fifth column inside the walls while every fighting man stood at the trench. It darkened the hour, and the Qur'an's verse carries what the army felt, the eyes rolling and the hearts in the throats. The wind closed it, and the hypocrites' alarm inside the city was loud enough for the works to record.

What the Siege Decided

Tactical: the confederacy withdrew without forcing the trench

Strategic: the offensive coalition of Mecca and the desert broke, and never assembled again in that shape

Cost: hunger, cold, and about twenty nights of mobilization, and Amr dead by Ali's ﵇ hand

The strategic result is the clearest of the series. The coalition that marched to end Medina never fought it again in that shape, and within three years Mecca opened its gates. The trench was bought with shovels, and it held.

Was the Battle of the Trench Justified

The battle was a siege forced on Medina by a confederacy the exiled Nadir worked to assemble. The sources name the mover, the exiled Nadir, working Mecca and the desert into the war. Medina itself had marched out twice that year, to the appointment at Badr and to Dumat al-Jandal, and both marches had ended without a battle. The trench was pure defense, a wall dug in the ground, and the army behind it was under orders to hold.

The one sally the sources record, Ali's ﵇ duel, was fought at the trench line against the champion who came out calling for single combat. The Qurayza treachery that darkened the siege belongs to the next part of this series, and its judgment is treated there on its own record. The wind the sources credit with the final breaking of the siege is theirs to credit.

The siege could have been prevented only by the men who assembled the confederacy. They gathered it before Medina knew of it, and their grievance was an exile they had earned by attempted murder. A city facing the choice between a trench and annihilation chooses the trench, and this trench held.
