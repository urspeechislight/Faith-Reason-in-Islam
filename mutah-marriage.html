Qur'an and Hadith Analysis

Who Forbade Mut’ah?

The Sunni canon dates the mut’ah prohibition to six different occasions, keeps the practice alive through two caliphates, and preserves the ban as Umar’s own pulpit declaration in the first person. From the lexicons, the Sahih collections, the Musnad, and the jurists’ own harmonizations.

The Argument Under Examination

The Charge

The Sunni polemic holds that mut’ah, temporary marriage, was a pre-Islamic practice the Qur’an never licensed, that the Prophet ﷺ abolished it, and that the Shia retention of it is an innovation. The Shia position holds that 4:24 legislates it, that the Prophet ﷺ permitted it without ever revoking it, and that the ban came from Umar.

The Method

Everything turns on three questions. What the term means, what the verse says, and what the Sunni canon’s own reports narrate about the dates. Each report below carries its own date inside it, and every quotation is cited from the classical corpus.

Premise 1, the Term Is Fixed

The lexicons define the root m-t-’ as benefit joined to a span. The derived stem of that root is the verb of Qur’an 4:24. The sister institution, the pilgrimage mut’ah, uses the same stem with a defined frame (Qur'an 2:196). The term cannot mean an open-ended marriage.

Premise 2, the First Readers Named the Term

Ibn Mas’ud, Ubayy ibn Ka’b, and Ibn Abbas read the clause with the words to a named term inside the recitation itself. Al-Qurtubi records that the early and late generations did not differ that the mut’ah is a marriage to a term. The Sunni jurists fixed the meaning before any hadith of prohibition is cited.

Premise 3, the Practice Ran Past the Prophet’s Death

Sound Sunni chains carry the practice through the Prophet’s ﷺ era, Abu Bakr’s caliphate, and into Umar’s. The ban’s occasion is a mut’ah affair in Kufa. Kufa was founded in 17 AH, years after the Prophet ﷺ died.

Premise 4, the Ban Speaks in Umar’s Voice

The pulpit reports say the two mut’as stood on the Prophet’s ﷺ day. Then, “I forbid them”. Stoning follows, unless four witnesses prove a later prophetic license. The canon names Umar as the man who “spoke by his own opinion”.

Conclusion, the Ban Belongs to Umar’s Caliphate

Premises 1 and 2 fix the verse as legislation. Premise 3 breaks the single-prohibition story. A license at the Fath after the Khaybar ban forces two prohibition cycles, and six competing dates have no single original. Premise 4 supplies the actual legislative event in the canon’s own words. The Shia position matches the record. The innovation is the ban.

The Word Before the Law

What the Lexicons Say

Source: Ibn Faris (d.395 AH), Maqayis al-Lugha, vol.5, p293

Claim: the root m-t-ʿ means benefit joined to a stretch of time

( متع ) الميم والتاء والعين أصل صحيح يدل على منفعة وامتداد مدة في خير . منه استمتعت بالشيء . والمتعة والمتاع المنفعة في قوله تعالى * ( بيوتا غير مسكونة فيها متاع لكم ) * ومتعت المطلقة بالشيء لأنها تنتفع به . ويقال أمتعت بمالي بمعنى تمتعت . قال : خليطين من شعبين شتى تجاورا قديما وكانا للتفرق أمتعا ورواه الأصمعي بالتفرق

( mataʿa ) al-mīm wa-l-tāʾ wa-l-ʿayn, aṣlun ṣaḥīḥun yadullu ʿalā manfaʿatin wa-imtidādi muddatin fī khayr. Minhu istamtaʿtu bi-l-shayʾ. Wa-l-mutʿatu wa-l-matāʿu al-manfaʿatu fī qawlihi taʿālā, buyūtan ghayra maskūnatin fīhā matāʿun lakum. Wa-mattaʿta al-muṭallaqata bi-l-shayʾ li-annahā tantafiʿu bihi. Wa-yuqālu amtaʿtu bi-mālī bi-maʿnā tamattaʿtu. Qāla, khilayni min shaʿbayni shattā tajāwarā qadīman wa-kānā li-l-tafarruqi umtaʿā, wa-rawāhu al-Aṣmaʿī bi-l-tafarruq.

"Mim, ta, and ayn form one sound root whose meaning is benefit and the extension of a span in good. From it comes istamta'tu bi-l-shay', I enjoyed the thing. The mut'a and the mata' are the benefit in God's word, houses not dwelt in, wherein is mata', provision for you. And one matta'a the divorced woman with a thing because she benefits from it. One says amta'tu bi-mali, meaning tamatta'tu, I enjoyed my wealth. The poet said, two partners from two valleys, unlike, neighbors long, and the two were near to parting; and al-Asma'i transmitted it with li-l-tafarruq."

- Ibn Faris (d.395 AH), Maqayis al-Lugha, vol.5, p293

One root carries the whole question. Ibn Faris defines m-t-ʿ as benefit joined to a span in good. The mutʿa is benefit bounded by time. The derived stem istamtaʿa, the very verb of Qur’an 4:24, is the poet’s word for taking enjoyment of a thing, and Ibn Faris cites the poet’s line inside the entry itself.

So the lexicons fix the meaning before the Qur’an is even opened. The root never names open-ended benefit. It names enjoyment bounded by a span, and the derived stem istimta’a is the verb of exactly that. Dictionaries assert, and assertions want witnesses. The poets are the witnesses, and they used the word for six centuries on either side of the revelation.

The Word in the Poets

Source: al-Ra’i al-Numayri, mukhadram poet, in Nashwan b. Sa’id (d.573 AH), Shams al-Ulum, vol.9, p514; and Ibn Faris, Maqayis al-Lugha, vol.5, p293

Claim: the poets used the root for enjoyment that runs toward its end

خليطين من شعبين شتى تجاورا * قديماً وكانا للتفرق أمتعا

Two partners from two valleys, unlike, neighbors long, whose enjoyment of each other ran only toward parting.

- al-Ra’i al-Numayri, in Nashwan b. Sa’id (d.573 AH), Shams al-Ulum, vol.9, p514

قال أبو زيد : يقال : أمتعت بالشيء بمعنى تمتّعت ، يقال : أمتعت بأهلي ، قال الراعي : خليطين من شعبين شتى تجاورا * قديماً وكانا للتفرق أمتعا أي : لم يكن يمتِّع أحدهما بصاحبه إِلا التفرقُ ، وكان الأصمعي يرويه : بالتفرق .

Abu Zayd said, one says amta’tu bi-l-shay’ in the sense of tamatta’tu, one says amta’tu with my family. Al-Ra’i said, two partners from two valleys, unlike, neighbors long, whose enjoyment of each other ran only toward parting. That is, neither of the two enjoyed the other except toward parting. And al-Asma’i transmitted it with li-l-tafarruq.

- Nashwan b. Sa’id (d.573 AH), Shams al-Ulum, vol.9, p514

وعن هرمى مصر الغداة أمتّعا * بشرخ شباب أم هما هرمان

And of the gray-haired one of Egypt, did the two enjoy the prime of youth, or are they two gray ones?

- al-Isfahani (d.597 AH), Kharidat al-Qasr, vol.20, p572

نتمتع أبكار الزمان بأيده * وجئنا بوهن بعد ما خرف الدهر

We enjoy the young days of time with our hands, and old age came upon us after time grew gray.

- al-Ma’arri (d.449 AH), in Ibn Mammati (d.606 AH), Lata’if al-Dhakhira, p238

The poets confirm the lexicons at every point. Al-Ra’i’s two partners enjoy each other with parting already inside the enjoyment, the exact bounded sense Ibn Faris defined. Al-Isfahani’s gray-haired man enjoyed the prime of youth, sharh al-shabab, the very phrase whose passing the line mourns. Al-Ma’arri, four centuries into Islam, still uses the verb for enjoying days that end. From the mukhadram poet to the eleventh century the root never loses its boundary. Enjoyment with the end built into it, that is what the word meant on the street and in the poem. The next question is whether the Qur’an legislates with the word as it stood, or strips the boundary from it. The verse decides.

The Verse and Its First Readers

The Verse Itself

Verse: Qur’an 4:24, the clause of dispute

Reading: Uthmani text, the segment the dispute turns on

فَمَا ٱسْتَمْتْعْتُم بِهِۦ مِنْهُنَّ فَـَٔاتُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةًۭ

Fa-mā istamtaʿtum bihi minhunna fa-ʾātūhunna ujūrahunna farīḍah.

"So what you have enjoyed of them, give them their wages as an obligation."

- The Qur'an, al-Nisaa 4:24 (segment, Uthmani)

Here is the payoff of the lexical and poetic chain. The verb of the clause, istamta’tum, is the derived stem the lexicons fixed and the poets used for enjoyment running toward its end. The Qur’an did not strip the boundary from the word. It legislates with the boundary inside it. Enjoyment taken, wages owed as an obligation, and the span the word itself carries. The first readers of the Qur’an understood the clause exactly that way, and they did not leave it to inference. They read the term into their recitation.

The First Readers Added the Term

Source: al-Shaybani (d.700 AH), Nahj al-Bayan, vol.2, p138; al-Tusi (d.460 AH), al-Tibyan, vol.3, p165

Readers: Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas’ud, Ubayy ibn Ka’b, Sa’id ibn Jubayr, and Aisha in relay

Claim: the clause was read as Mut’ah to a named term

وقال ابن عبّاس - رحمه اللّه - وعبد اللّه بن مسعود وجابر بن عبد اللّه الأنصاريّ وأبيّ بن كعب ، وهو المرويّ عن عائشة : أنّها المتعة إلى أجل مسمّى . وهو المرويّ عن أبي جعفر وأبى عبد اللّه - عليهما السّلام - . والدّليل على ذلك ، أنّ لفظ الاستماع إذا أطلق لا يفيد في عرف الشّرع إلّا العقد المؤجّل . ويدلّ عليه قوله - تعالى - : [ فآتوهنّ أجورهن ] ؛ يعني : على العقد بأجل معيّن . وقرأ ابن مسعود : « فما استمتعتم به منهنّ إلى أجل مسمّى . » وهو مسطور في مصحف أبيّ - أيضا - (5) . وروي عن عليّ - عليه السّلام - أنّه قال في خلافته : لولا أنّ عمر نهى عن المتعة ما زنى إلّا شفا (6) - بالفاء المعجمة ؛ يريد : ما زنى إلّا قليل من الرّجال .

"Ibn Abbas, Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, Jabir ibn Abd Allah al-Ansari, and Ubayy ibn Ka'b said, and it is transmitted from Aisha, that it is the mut'a to a named term. And it is transmitted from Abu Ja'far and Abu Abd Allah, peace upon them both. The proof is that the word istimta', when unqualified, yields in the law's usage only the deferred contract, as His word 'give them their wages' shows, meaning, upon the contract to a fixed term. And Ibn Mas'ud read, 'what you have enjoyed of them to a named term'; and it is inscribed in the codex of Ubayy as well. And it is transmitted from Ali, peace upon him, that he said in his caliphate, were it not that Umar forbade the mut'a, none but a few men would fornicate; he means, none but a few of the men."

- Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Shaybani (d.700 AH), Nahj al-Bayan, vol.2, p138

والقول الثاني : أنها المتعة إلى أجل مسمى من غير نكاح ، قال ابن عباس كان في قراءة أبيّ : فما استمتعتم به منهن إلى أجل مسمى ، وكان ابن عباس كذلك يقرأ ، وسعيد بن جبير ، وهذا قول السدي ، وقال الحكم : قال عليّ : لولا أن عمر نهى عن المتعة ما زنى إلا شقي ، وهذا قول لا يثبت ، والمحكي عن ابن عباس خلافه ، وأنه تاب من المتعة وربا النقد .

"The second position is that it is the mut'a to a named term without a permanent marriage. Ibn Abbas said, in the reading of Ubayy it was 'what you have enjoyed of them to a named term', and Ibn Abbas used to read it so, and Sa'id ibn Jubayr; and this is the statement of al-Suddi. And al-Hakam said, Ali said, were it not that Umar forbade the mut'a, none but the wretched would fornicate. And this statement does not stand, and what is transmitted from Ibn Abbas is contrary to it, namely that he repented of the mut'a and raised the cash price."

- al-Mawardi (d.450 AH), al-Nukat wa-l-Uyun, vol.1, p471

وقوله : ( فما استمتعتم به منهن ) قال الحسن ، ومجاهد ، وابن زيد : هو النكاح ، وقال ابن عباس ، والسدي : هو المتعة إلى أجل مسمى ، وهو مذهبنا ، لان لفظ الاستمتاع إذا أطلق لا يستفاد به في الشرع إلا العقد المؤجل ، ألا ترى أنهم يقولون : فلان يقول بالمتعة ، وفلان لا يقول بها ، ولا يريدون إلا العقد المخصوص

"As for His word 'what you have enjoyed of them', al-Hasan, Mujahid, and Ibn Zayd said it is the marriage; Ibn Abbas and al-Suddi said it is the mut'a to a named term, and this is our school's position, because the word istimta', when unqualified, yields in the law only the deferred contract. Do you not see that they say, so-and-so holds the mut'a, and so-and-so does not hold it, and they mean nothing by it but that specific contract?"

- al-Tusi (d.460 AH), al-Tibyan, vol.3, p165

Ibn Mas’ud read the clause with the words “to a named term” inside his recitation, and the gloss stood in the codex of Ubayy. Al-Tusi records the split of the early exegetes and his school’s reason, that istimta’ unqualified yields in the law only the deferred contract, the very contract people meant when they said a man “holds Mut’ah”.

The gloss was no private reading. It stood in the codex of Ubayy, in the recitation of Ibn Mas’ud, in the fatwa of Ibn Abbas, and in the transmission from Aisha. The term the lexicons fixed and the poets used was carried into the verse by the generation that heard it revealed. Al-Mawardi answers Ali’s ﵇ statement with a wave, this does not stand, and offers Ibn Abbas’s repentance in its place. The repentance report runs against the mass of Ibn Abbas material. He defended Mut’ah to Ibn al-Zubayr’s face. His school kept the position. His own words survive in the Mustadrak, quoted below. Mawardi prints the report and leaves the field. The reading stood among the first readers, so the question moves from text to men. Did the school’s own jurists fix that reading as the meaning of the verse?

The Jurists Fixed the Meaning

Source: al-Qurtubi (d.671 AH), al-Jami li-Ahkam al-Qur’an, on 4:24; Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Tamhid, book of marriage

Claim: Mut’ah is a marriage to a term, and the early and late generations did not differ on its form

لم يختلف العلماء من السلف والخلف أن المتعة نكاح إلى أجل لا ميراث فيه ، والفرقة تقع عند انقضاء الأجل من غير طلاق

"The scholars of the early and the later generations did not differ that the mut'a is a marriage to a term, with no inheritance in it, and the separation occurs at the expiry of the term without divorce."

- al-Qurtubi (d.671 AH), al-Jami li-Ahkam al-Qur'an, on 4:24, as relayed in al-Milani, al-Mut'a, and al-Ja'far al-Amili, Zawaj al-Mut'a, vol.3, p174

أجمعوا على أن المتعة نكاح لا إشهاد فيه ، وأنه نكاح إلى أجل ، تقع الفرقة بلا طلاق ولا ميراث بينهما

"They agreed that the mut'a is a marriage with no witnessing in it, and that it is a marriage to a term, in which the separation occurs without divorce and without inheritance between the two."

- Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Tamhid, book of marriage, as relayed in al-Milani, Rasala fi-l-Mut'atayn, p23, and al-Kunturi, Tashyid al-Mata'in, vol.8, p284

With al-Qurtubi and Ibn Abd al-Barr the lexical argument closes. The word means a term-bound marriage, the first readers read it so, and the Sunni juridical tradition itself records no difference on the form, a marriage to a term, separation without divorce, no inheritance. The term is fixed three times over, by the lexicons, by the poets, and now by the jurists. What remains is the record of practice and prohibition. The Imami canon speaks first, in its own voice, on the same verse.

The Imami Canon on the Same Verse

Never Forbidden on Campaign

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p449, hadith 3

Speaker: Imam Ja’far al-Sadiq ﵇

Claim: the verse came down with the term named

العياشي في تفسيره عن محمد بن مسلم ، عن أبي جعفر ﵇ قال : قال : جابر بن عبد الله عن رسول الله ﵌ : انهم غزوا معه فأحل لهم المتعة ولم يحرمها

"From Muhammad ibn Muslim, from Abu Ja'far, peace be upon him, who said, Jabir ibn Abd Allah related from the Messenger of God, God bless him and his family, they went on campaign with him, and he made the mut'a lawful for them, and he did not forbid it. And Ali ﵇ used to say, were it not for what Ibn al-Khattab preceded me in, meaning Umar, none but the wretched would fornicate."

- Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, al-Furu', vol.5, p449, hadith 3; parallel in the Dar al-Hadith ed., vol.11, p8, hadith 9923

The Wasa’il relay carries the Companion witness inside the Shia chain. Jabir ibn Abd Allah reports from the Prophet ﷺ that on campaign he made Mut’ah lawful and did not forbid it, the same Jaber whose Sunni report carries the practice into two caliphates. Ali ﵇ names the man who later changed the practice, and Ibn Abbas kept the reading with the named term in it. A reader who rejects the Imami canon will weigh this as Shia-chain material. Its force is agreement, two canons, one Companion, one date for the change. That objection, the fact of Umar’s ban, was already put to the Imams themselves.

The Imam Answers Umar’s Ban

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p449, hadith 4

Speakers: Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇ with Abd Allah ibn Umayr al-Laythi

Claim: God made Mut’ah lawful in His Book, and it stays lawful to the Day of Resurrection

عن عمر بن اذينة ، عن زرارة قال : جاء عبد الله بن عمير الليثي إلى أبي جعفر (﵇) فقال له : ما تقول في متعة النساء ؟ فقال : أحلها الله في كتابه وعلى لسان نبيه (﵌) فهي حلال إلى يوم القيامة فقال : يا أبا جعفر مثلك يقول هذا وقد حرمها عمر ونهى عنها ؟

"Ali ibn Ibrahim transmits, from his father, from Ibn Abi Umayr, from Umar ibn Udhayna, from Zurara, who said, Abd Allah ibn Umayr al-Laythi came to Abu Ja'far, peace be upon him, and said to him, what do you say of the mut'a of women? He said, 'God has made it lawful in His Book and through the words of His Prophet ﷺ, so it will remain lawful until the Day of Resurrection.' He replied, 'Abu Ja'far, how can someone like you say that when Umar prohibited it and forbade people from practicing it?'"

- Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇ with Abd Allah ibn Umayr, in al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, al-Furu', vol.5, p449, hadith 4; parallel in the Dar al-Hadith ed., vol.11, p8, hadith 9924

The Kufan challenger puts the fact of Umar’s ban to the Imam as an objection. The Imam has already given the source of the ruling, the Book and the tongue of the Prophet ﷺ. The exchange stops at the objection in the transmitted text, and the objection itself concedes the point at issue, that the ban was Umar’s act. What the Imami canon adds next is stronger than a defense. It gives the Imam’s own explanation of what the verse means, a marriage to a fixed term.

The Verse Came Down With the Term

Source: al-Hurr al-Amili, Wasa’il al-Shi’a, vol.14, p440, from al-Ayyashi’s Tafsir

Speakers: Jabir ibn Abd Allah from the Prophet ﷺ; Ali ﵇; Ibn Abbas

Claim: the Prophet made Mut’ah lawful on campaign and never forbade it

عَلِيُّ بْنُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ ، عَنِ ابْنِ أَبِي عُمَيْرٍ ، عَمَّنْ ذَكَرَهُ : عَنْ أَبِي عَبْدِ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ ، قَالَ : « إِنَّمَا نَزَلَتْ « فَمَا اسْتَمْتَعْتُمْ بِهِ مِنْهُنَّ » إِلى أَجَلٍ مُسَمًّى « فَآتُوهُنَّ أُجُورَهُنَّ فَرِيضَةً »

Ali ibn Ibrahim transmits, from his father, from Ibn Abi Umayr, from the one he named, from Abu Abd Allah ﵇, who said, it was revealed thus, 'what you have enjoyed of them to a named term, give them their wages as an obligation.'

- Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi (Dar al-Hadith ed.), vol.11, p8, hadith 9923

The Kafi report is a tafsir narration, not a claim of different letters. The Imam explains what the revealed verb means, that the enjoyment the verse legislates is a marriage to a fixed, named term. The Qur’an’s text is one text. The meaning is what the report fixes. The school’s reason for accepting this explanation is not the report alone. The Sunni record arrives at the same meaning from the same verse, Ibn Mas’ud teaching the clause with the term explained in his reading, and the gloss noted beside the verse in the codex of Ubayy. Two canons, one meaning. A reported explanation can still be disputed, so the tradition closes with the strongest witness it has, the founder of a Sunni law school hearing the verse from the Imam.

Abu Hanifa Hears the Verse

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p449, hadith 5

Speakers: Imam Ja’far al-Sadiq ﵇ with Abu Hanifa

Claim: the eponym of the Hanafi school concedes he had not registered the verse

"Ali ibn Ibrahim transmits, from his father, from Ibn Abi Umayr, from Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn Ribat, from Hariz, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Abd Allah, who said, I heard Abu Hanifa ask Abu Abd Allah ﵇ about the mut'a. He said, 'which of the two mut'as do you ask of?' He said, I asked you of the mut'a of pilgrimage, so tell me of the mut'a of women, is it right? He said, 'glory be to God, have you not read God's Book, what you have enjoyed of them, give them their wages as an obligation?' Abu Hanifa said, by God, it is as though it were a verse I had never read."

- Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇ with Abu Hanifa, in al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, al-Furu', vol.5, p449, hadith 5; parallel in the Dar al-Hadith ed., vol.11, p11, and in al-Bahrani (d.1107 AH), al-Burhan, vol.2, p59

Abu Hanifa asks which of the two mut’as is meant, the pilgrimage one or the women’s one. The Imam answers with the verse alone, and Abu Hanifa’s reply is preserved, that it is as though he had never read it. The lexical chain, the poetry, the codices, and now a law-school founder, all converge on one clause. With the verse fixed as legislation, everything turns on the practice and its dates. The Sunni canon keeps that record in its own voice, and each report carries its date inside it.

The Sunni Canon’s Own Record

The Practice Began on Campaign

Source: Bukhari and Muslim, book of marriage; wording in Ibn Hajar, al-Daraya, vol.2, p59

Speaker: Abd Allah ibn Mas’ud, Companion

Claim: the Prophet ﷺ licensed the term-bound marriage on campaign, and Ibn Mas’ud closed with the verse forbidding prohibition

كنا نغزو مع رسول الله ليس لنا نساء فقلنا ألا نستخصي فنهانا عن ذلك ثم رخص لنا أن تنكح المرأة بالثوب إلى أجل ثم قرأ عبد الله «يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تحرموا طيبات ما أحل الله لكم» الآية

"We campaigned with the Messenger of God ﷺ and had no wives. We said, shall we castrate ourselves? And he forbade us that. Then he gave us license to marry the woman for a garment, to a term. Then Abd Allah recited, O you who believe, do not forbid the good things God has made lawful to you, and do not transgress; God does not love the transgressors (Qur'an 5,87)."

- Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, in Bukhari and Muslim, book of marriage; wording as relayed in Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Daraya fi Takhrij Ahadith al-Hidaya, vol.2, p59

The Companion whose codex carried the gloss also carried the practice report. The men on campaign asked about castration and were forbidden it, then given the license to marry a woman for a garment to a term. Ibn Mas’ud closes with Qur’an 5:87, the command not to forbid the good things God made lawful. The recitation is the report’s own argument against the later ban. The verse legislated the term, the practice took the term at its word, and the canon then records the first report of a prohibition, at Khaybar.

The First Ban at Khaybar

Source: Ahmad, Musnad, vol.1, p79; graded by al-Tirmidhi, Sunan, vol.3, p421

Speaker: Ali ﵇ to Ibn Abbas

Chain: Sufyan ibn Uyayna from al-Zuhri from the two sons of Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya from their father from Ali ﵇, all trustworthy

Grading: hasan sahih in al-Tirmidhi’s judgment

Claim: the Prophet ﷺ forbade Mut’ah marriage and donkey flesh in the season of Khaybar

حدثنا عبد الله حدثني أبي ثنا سفيان عن الزهري عن الحسن وعبد الله ابني محمد بن علي عن أبيهما وكان حسن أرضاهما في أنفسنا ان عليا قال لابن عباس ﵁ ان رسول الله ﵌ نهى عن نكاح المتعة وعن لحوم الحمر الأهلية زمن خيبر

"Sufyan told us, from al-Zuhri, from al-Hasan and Abd Allah, the two sons of Muhammad ibn Ali, from their father, and al-Hasan was the more pleasing of the two to us, Ali said to Ibn Abbas, the Messenger of God ﷺ forbade the mut'a marriage and the flesh of domestic donkeys in the season of Khaybar."

- Ali ibn Abi Talib ﵇, in Ahmad (d.241 AH), Musnad (Risala ed.), vol.2, p30, no. 592; graded by al-Tirmidhi (d.279 AH), Sunan, vol.3, p421, hasan sahih

Khaybar fell in Safar 7 AH. This report carries a prohibition narrated from Ali ﵇, sound in chain by the canon’s grading. The Shia school does not accept that the Prophet ﷺ forbade Mut’ah, and the contradiction is stated plainly here rather than hidden. The school reads the Khaybar report against the mass of practice reports, Jabir’s three eras, Ibn Mas’ud’s campaign license, and Imran’s testimony that no prohibition came until the Prophet’s ﷺ death. One report outnumbered inside its own canon loses to the many. The page dates the Khaybar report because the Sunni harmonizers need it as their first ban. And if that ban stood, the canon’s own next reports make no sense, because they license the practice again, a year later, at the Fath.

The License at the Fath and Awtas

Speakers: Salama ibn al-Akwa’; Sabra al-Juhani

Claim: the license was given at the Fath and Awtas, Ramadan to Shawwal 8 AH, bounded at three days and revoked before the army left Mecca

رَخَّصَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﵌ عَامَ أَوْطَاسٍ فِي الْمُتْعَةِ ثَلاثًا ثُمَّ نَهَى عَنْهَا

"The Messenger of God ﷺ gave license in the year of Awtas to the mut'a for three, then he forbade it."

- Salama ibn al-Akwa', in al-Nasa'i (d.303 AH), al-Sunan al-Kubra, book of marriage; parallel in Muslim, Sahih, and Ahmad, Musnad, vol.27

خرجنا مع رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يوم الفتح فأقمنا خمس عشرة من بين ليلة ويوم قال قال فاذن لنا رسول الله ﵌ في المتعة قال وخرجت أنا وابن عم لي في أسل مكة أو قال في أعلى مكة فلقينا فتاة من بنى عامر بن صعصعة كأنها الكبرة العنطنطة قال وأنا قريب من الدمامة وعلى برد جديد غض وعلى ابن عمى برد خلق قال فقلنا لها هل لك أن يستمتع منك أحدنا قالت وهل يصلح ذلك قال قلنا نعم قال فجعلت تنظر إلى ابن عمى فقلت لها ان بردى هذا جديد غض وبرد ابن عمى هذا خلق مح قالت ابن عمك هذا بالاس به قال فاستمتع منها فلم تخرج من مكة حتى حرمها رسول الله ﵌

"Abd Allah ibn Bakr al-Sahmi told us, Hatim ibn Isma'il told us, Abd al-Aziz ibn Abi Hazim told us, from his father, from al-Rabi' ibn Sabra al-Juhani, from his father, who said, we went out with the Messenger of God ﷺ in the year of the Fath, and we stayed fifteen, between night and day. The Messenger of God ﷺ gave us license in the mut'a. My cousin and I went out in lower Mecca, or he said in upper Mecca, and we met a young woman of Banu Amir ibn Sa'sa'a, like a young she-camel, tall and full. I was near to ugliness, wearing a fresh new cloak, and my cousin wore a worn cloak. We said to her, would you let one of us enjoy you? She said, is that proper? We said, yes. She kept looking at my cousin. I said to her, my cloak is fresh and new, and my cousin's is worn threadbare. She said, your cousin's cloak is fine. He enjoyed her, and we did not leave Mecca until the Messenger of God ﷺ forbade it."

- Sabra al-Juhani, in Ahmad (d.241 AH), Musnad, vol.3, p405; parallel in the Risala ed., vol.24, p63, and in Muslim, Sahih, from al-Rabi' ibn Sabra from his father

اسْتَمْتَعْتُ عَلَى عَهْدِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﵌ بِامْرَأَةٍ مِنْ بَنِي عَامِرٍ بِبُرْدَيْنِ أَحْمَرَيْنِ ثُمَّ نَهَانَا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﵌ عَنْ مُتْعَةِ النِّسَاءِ

"I contracted mut'a in the era of the Messenger of God ﷺ with a woman of Banu Amir for two red cloaks. Then the Messenger of God ﷺ forbade us the mut'a of women."

- Sabra al-Juhani, in al-Tabarani (d.360 AH), al-Mu'jam al-Kabir, vol.7, p112

Sabra’s full report carries its own dates. The army reached Mecca in Ramadan 8 AH, stayed fifteen days, and the license came at the start of it. Salama’s report dates the license to Awtas, bounded at three days, Shawwal 8 AH, days after the Fath. A license after a standing prohibition is the record of a re-license, since a standing ban needs no license. The verse’s term never lapsed, and neither did the practice. The canon now carries that practice across the caliphates that followed.

The Practice Ran Through Two Caliphates

Claim: Mut’ah was practiced in the era of the Prophet ﷺ, of Abu Bakr, and of Umar, until Umar forbade it

"We practiced mut'a for a handful of dates and flour, for days at a time, in the era of the Messenger of God ﷺ, and of Abu Bakr, and of Umar, until Umar forbade it in the affair of Amr ibn Hurayth."

- Jabir ibn Abd Allah, in Muslim (d.261 AH), Sahih Muslim, book of marriage; wording relayed in al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.30, p598, and Ibn Hajar (d.852 AH), Fath al-Bari, vol.9, p141

استمتعت من النساء على عهد رسول الله «﵌» ، وزمن أبي بكر ، ثم زمن عمر حتى كان من شأن عمرو بن حريث الذي كان ، فقال عمر ﵁ : إنا كنا نستمتع ونفي ، وإني أراكم تستمتعون ولا تفون ، فانكحوا ولا تستمتعوا

"I contracted mut'a in the era of the Messenger of God ﷺ, and the time of Abu Bakr, then the time of Umar, until the affair of Amr ibn Hurayth occurred. Umar said, we used to contract mut'a and keep faith, and I see you contracting mut'a and not keeping faith. So marry, and do not contract mut'a."

- Jabir ibn Abd Allah, in Umar ibn Shabba (d.262 AH), Ta'rikh al-Madina, as relayed in al-Ja'far al-Amili, Zawaj al-Mut'a, vol.2, p127

Jabir’s word on the chain Muslim transmits runs through three eras in one sentence, the Prophet’s ﷺ, Abu Bakr’s, and Umar’s, until the Kufa affair. Ibn Shabba’s version adds Umar’s own stated reason, a moral judgment applied as law. A practice that survives its supposed prohibition, through two caliphates, on a chain the canon grades sound, was never stopped by that prohibition. And Jabir was not alone. The canon and the jurists name the men and women who kept the practice.

Who Kept Practicing After the Prohibition Reports

And from Shuba ibn Muslim, who said, we entered upon Asma bint Abi Bakr and asked her about the mut’a. She said, we did it in the era of the Messenger of God ﷺ.

- in al-Hurr al-Amili, Wasa’il al-Shi’a, vol.21, p12, hadith 26385; from Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi, al-Musnad, and al-Nasa’i, al-Sunan al-Kubra, vol.3, p326

حدثنا عبد الله ، حدثني أبي ، ثنا يحيى بن سعيد ، أنبأنا سليمان يعني التميمي ، حدثني غنيم قال : سألت سعد بن أبي وقاص عن المتعة ؟ قال : “فعلناها وهذا كافر بالعرض يعني معاوية” أخرجه أحمد “المسند” (1 / 181)

Ghunaym asked Sa’d ibn Abi Waqqas about the mut’a. He said, “we did it, and this one was a disbeliever in the Lord of the Ka’ba, meaning Mu’awiya.” Extracted by Ahmad in the Musnad, vol.1, p181. And Ibn Abi Shayba transmitted it by this chain and said in his version, meaning Mu’awiya. Extracted by Muslim, vol.1, p402, chapter of the lawfulness of tamattu’.

- Sa’d ibn Abi Waqqas, in Muslim, Sahih, book of marriage, relayed in al-Ansari, al-Usra wa-l-Sahaba fi-l-Sunna, vol.2, p82

تمتع رسول الله ﵌ وأبو بكر وعمر وعثمان كذلك وأول من نهى عنهما معاوية

The Messenger of God ﷺ practiced the mut’a, and Abu Bakr, and Umar, and Uthman likewise, and the first to forbid them was Mu’awiya.

- Ibn Abbas, in Ahmad, Musnad, vol.1, p313 and p314, two chains, relayed in al-Ansari, al-Usra wa-l-Sahaba fi-l-Sunna, vol.2, p82

وفيه ان معاوية بن أبي سفيان استمتع مقدمه الطائف على ثقيف بمولاة ابن الحضرمي يقال لها : معانة قال جابر : ثم أدركت معانة خلافة معاوية حية ، فكان معاوية يرسل إليها بجائزة كل عام حتى ماتت

And in it, Mu’awiya ibn Abi Sufyan contracted mut’a upon his arrival at Ta’if against Thaqif, with a client-woman of Ibn al-Hadrami called Ma’ana. Jabir said, Ma’ana then lived on into Mu’awiya’s caliphate, and Mu’awiya used to send her a gift every year until she died.

- in al-Askari (d.1428 AH), Ma’alim al-Madrasatayn, vol.2, p253

أ . عبد الرزّاق ، عن ابن جريج قال : أخبرني عبد اللّه بن عثمان بن خثيم ، أنّ محمد بن الأسود بن خلف ، أخبره أنّ عمرو بن حوشب استمتع بجارية بكر من بني عامر بن لؤي فحملت ، فذكر ذلك لعمر ، فسألها ، فقالت : استمتع منها عمرو بن حوشب ، فسأله ، فاعترف ، فقال عمر : من أشهدت ؟ قال : لا أدري أقال : أمّها ، أو أختها ، أو أخاها وأمّها ، فقام عمر على المنبر فقال : ما بال رجال يعملون بالمتعة ولا يشهدون عدولا ، ولم يبيّنها إلاّ حددته .

Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abd Allah ibn Uthman ibn Khuthaym, that Muhammad ibn al-Aswad ibn Khalf informed him that Amr ibn Hawshab contracted mut’a with a virgin slave-girl of Banu Amir ibn Lu’ayy and she conceived. It was mentioned to Umar, and he questioned her, and she said, Amr ibn Hawshab contracted mut’a with me. He questioned him and he confessed. Umar said, whom did you call to witness? He said, I do not know, perhaps her mother, or her sister, or her brother and her mother. Umar stood on the pulpit and said, what is with men who practice the mut’a and call no just witnesses? Whoever does not make it plain, I will have him punished.

- Amr ibn Hawshab, in al-Tabrisi, Dirasat Fiqiyya, p33, from Abd al-Razzaq, al-Musannaf

قال انا قد نكحناها ذلك النكاح للمتعة سنده صحيح قال واخبرني ان سعيدا ، قال له هي احل من شرب الماء للمتعه سنده صحيح

He said, we have married her by that marriage, the mut’a. And he informed me that Sa’id said to him, it is more lawful than drinking water, the mut’a. Its chain is sound.

- Sa’id ibn Jubayr, in al-Ja’far al-Amili, Zawaj al-Mut’a, vol.2, p154, from Abd al-Razzaq; grading in the source

Ibn Hazm names nine Companions who stood on Mut’ah’s lawfulness after the Prophet’s ﷺ death, and his list is not a Shia roster. Mu’awiya is on it, the future caliph. Sa’d ibn Abi Waqqas answers inside the Sahih itself, we did it, while Mu’awiya was still a disbeliever, which dates the practice past the Prophet’s ﷺ death by Sa’d’s own dating of Mu’awiya’s Islam. Ibn Abbas goes further in the Musnad, the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman all practiced it, and the first to forbid the two mut’as was Mu’awiya. The practice ran on past the Companions into the Successors, Sa’id ibn Jubayr in Mecca and Ibn Jurayj’s ninety contracts. A practice that widespread needed a public occasion to end it, and the canon preserves that occasion with names.

The Kufa Affair

Speakers: Jabir; Urwa; Khawla bint Hakim

Claim: the ban was triggered by mut’a contracts discovered in Kufa and Medina during Umar’s caliphate

أَخْبَرَنِي أَبُو الزُّبَيْرِ عَنْ جَابِرٍ قَالَ : قَدِمَ عَمْرُو بْنُ حُرَيْثٍ الْكُوفَةَ فَاسْتَمْتَعَ بِمَوْلاةٍ فَأُتِيَ بِهَا عُمَرُ وَهِيَ حُبْلَى فَسَأَلَهُ فَاعْتَرَفَ قَالَ : فَذَلِكَ حِينَ نَهَى عَنْهَا عُمَرُ

"Abu al-Zubayr informed me, from Jabir, who said, Amr ibn Hurayth came to Kufa and contracted mut'a with a client-woman, and she was brought to Umar pregnant, and he questioned him, and he confessed. That, he said, is when Umar forbade it."

- Jabir ibn Abd Allah, in Abd al-Razzaq (d.211 AH), al-Musannaf, as relayed in Ibn Hajar, Fath al-Bari, vol.9, p141, and al-Amini (d.1390 AH), Nazra fi Kitab al-Washi'a, p53

الموطّأ : حدّثني مالك ، عن ابن شهاب ، عن عروة بن الزبير ، أنّ خولة (6) بنت حكيم دخلت على عمر بن الخطّاب ، فقالت : إنّ ربيعة بن أميّة استمتع بامرأة فحملت منه ، فخرج عمر بن الخطّاب فزعاً يجرّ رداءه ، فقال : هذه المتعة ، ولو كنت تقدّمت فيها لرجمت .

"Malik told me, from Ibn Shihab, from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, that Khawla bint Hakim entered upon Umar ibn al-Khattab and said, Rabi'a ibn Umayya contracted mut'a with a woman and she conceived from him. Umar went out alarmed, dragging his cloak, and said, the mut'a! Had I preceded in it, I would have stoned."

- Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, in Malik (d.179 AH), al-Muwatta, book of marriage, chapter of mut'a; relayed in al-Tabrisi, Dirasat Fiqiyya, p32

عبد الرزّاق : عن عروة : أنّ ربيعة بن أميّة بن خلف تزوّج مولّدة من مولّدات المدينة بشهادة امرأتين : إحداهما : خولة بنت حكيم وكانت امرأة صالحة فلم يفجأهم إلاّ الوليدة قد حملت ، فذكرت ذلك خولة لعمر بن الخطّاب ، فقام يجرُّ صنفة ردائه من الغضب حتى صعد المنبر ، فقال : إنّه بلغني أنّ ربيعة بن أميّة تزوّج مولّدة من مولّدات المدينة بشهادة امرأتين ، وإنّي لو كنت تقدّمت في هذا لرجمت .

"From Urwa, Rabi'a ibn Umayya ibn Khalf married a native-born woman of the natives of Medina upon the testimony of two women, one of them Khawla bint Hakim, a righteous woman. Then the girl was found pregnant, and Khawla mentioned it to Umar, and he rose dragging the hem of his cloak in anger until he mounted the pulpit and said, it has reached me that Rabi'a ibn Umayya has married a native-born woman of Medina upon the testimony of two women. Had I preceded in this, I would have stoned."

- Abd al-Razzaq (d.211 AH), al-Musannaf, as relayed in al-Tabrisi, Dirasat Fiqiyya, p32

The occasion survives with names. Amr ibn Hurayth contracted Mut’ah in Kufa and the pregnancy was carried to Umar. Rabi’a ibn Umayya’s case brought Khawla to the caliph, who went out dragging his cloak. Abd al-Razzaq’s parallel carries the pulpit threat. Kufa was founded in 17 AH, so the affair sits years after the Prophet’s ﷺ death, inside Umar’s reign, in a city that did not exist in the Prophet’s ﷺ lifetime. And in Ibn Hawshab’s case Umar asks about witnesses, not fornication, which concedes the standing of the practice he is ending. The private occasions called for a public ruling, and the pulpit declarations supply it.

The Pulpit Declarations

Speaker: Umar ibn al-Khattab, from the pulpit

Claim: the two mut’as stood on the Prophet’s ﷺ day and “I forbid them”

تمتعنا مع رسول الله ﵌ فلما قام عمر قال : إن الله كان يحل لرسوله ما شاء بما شاء ، فأتموا الحج والعمرة وأبتوا نكاح هذه النساء ، فلن أوتي برجل نكح امرأة إلى أجل إلا رجمته بالحجارة

"We practiced mut'a with the Messenger of God ﷺ. Then when Umar stood he said, God used to make lawful for His Messenger what He willed, as He willed, and the Qur'an has come down in its stations. So complete the hajj and the umra for God as God has commanded you, and attack the marriage of these women, for I shall not be brought a man who has married a woman to a term but that I stone him with the stones."

- Umar ibn al-Khattab, in Muslim (d.261 AH), Sahih, book of marriage, the report of Abu Nadra; wording relayed in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol.30, p599

متعتان كانتا على عهد رسول الله (ص) انهى عنهما وأعاقب عليهما : متعة النساء ، ومتعة الحج

"Two mut'as were upon the day of the Messenger of God ﷺ; I forbid them, and I punish for them, the mut'a of women and the mut'a of pilgrimage."

- Umar ibn al-Khattab, in al-Muttaqi al-Hindi (d.975 AH), Kanz al-Ummal, no.45715, from Abu Salih, scribe of al-Layth, and al-Tahawi; parallel in al-Nuri (d.1320 AH), Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p483

أيها الناس ثلاث كن على عهد رسول الله (﵌) وأنا أنهى عنهن وأحرمهن

"Three things were upon the day of the Messenger of God ﷺ; I forbid them and punish for them, the mut'a of pilgrimage, the mut'a of women, and 'come to the best of works' in the call to prayer."

- Umar ibn al-Khattab, in al-Nuri (d.1320 AH), Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p483, no.17359, from Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, from Abu Qilaba; parallel from Abd al-Wahhab, from Ayyub

إن رسول ص أذن لنا في المتعة ثلاثا ثم حرمها ، والله لا أعلم أحدا تمتع وهو محصن إلا رجمته بالحجارة إلا أن يأتيني بأربعة يشهدون ان رسول الله ص أحلها بعد إذ حرمها ، ولا أجد رجلا من المسلمين متمتعا إلا جلدته مائة جلدة إلا أن يأتيني بأربعة شهداء ان رسول الله ص أحلها بعد إذ حرمها

"The Messenger of God ﷺ gave us license in the mut'a three times, then forbade it. By God, I know of no married man who practices it but that I will stone him with the stones, unless he brings me four witnesses that the Messenger of God ﷺ made it lawful after he had forbidden it."

- Umar ibn al-Khattab, in Ibn Majah (d.273 AH), Sunan; graded in Ibn Hajar (d.852 AH), Talkhis al-Habir, vol.3, p333, "with a sound chain"; relayed in al-San'ani, Subul al-Salam, vol.3, p126, and al-Muttaqi al-Hindi, Kanz al-Ummal, no.45714

The sermon Muslim transmits names the license as the Prophet’s ﷺ and issues the ban in the first person, with stoning attached. The two-mut’a version joins the women’s Mut’ah to the pilgrimage Mut’ah, and the three-fold version adds the call to prayer clause. The Ibn Majah report, graded sound by Ibn Hajar, conditions enforcement on four witnesses testifying that the Prophet ﷺ re-licensed it after his own prohibition. A legislator revoking his own law needs no such witnesses. A caliph overriding a prophetic permission needs exactly that clause. Faced with a first-person ban and reports of prohibition at Khaybar, Tabuk, the Fath, Awtas, the Farewell Pilgrimage, and the dawn of Islam, the school’s own scholars had to reconcile the dates, and their reconciliation is the next exhibit.

The Chronology and the Harmonizers

Two Prohibition Cycles

Source: al-Nawawi (d.676 AH), Sharh Sahih Muslim, relayed in Subul al-Salam, vol.3, p126

Claim: prohibition and license each occurred twice, per the school’s own harmonizer

قال النووي : الصواب أن تحريمها وإباحتها وقعا مرتين فكانت مباحة قبل خيبر ثم حرمت فيها ثم أبيحت عام الفتح وهو عام أوطاس ثم حرمت تحريما مؤبدا

"Al-Nawawi said, the correct and chosen view is that the prohibition and the license each occurred twice. It was lawful before Khaybar, then forbidden on the day of Khaybar, then licensed on the day of the conquest of Mecca, which is the day of Awtas for their connection, then forbidden that day after three days, a forbiddance made perpetual to the Day of Resurrection, and the prohibition continued."

- al-Nawawi (d.676 AH), Sharh Sahih Muslim, on the hadith of Sabra and Salama; relayed in al-San'ani (d.1182 AH), Subul al-Salam, vol.3, p126, and al-Munawi (d.1031 AH), Fayd al-Qadir, vol.6, p416, and al-Qastallani, Irshad al-Sari

The school’s own harmonizer concedes the structure the reports force on him. Lawful before Khaybar, forbidden at Khaybar, licensed again at the Fath, forbidden after three days. The synthesis exists because the reports cannot be read as one prohibition. Its author is the commentator of the Sahih itself, and the later commentaries quote him as the correct view. A double cycle needs two prohibitions, and the tradition’s chroniclers offer far more than two dates to choose from.

Six Dates for One Prohibition

Source: al-Munawi (d.1031 AH), Fayd al-Qadir, vol.6, p416

Claim: the tradition offers Khaybar, the compensatory umra, the Fath, Awtas, Tabuk, and the Farewell Pilgrimage as the date of the one prohibition

ثُمَّ حُرِّمَ ثُمَّ أُبِيحَ ثُمَّ حُرِّمَ فَإِنَّهُ كَانَ جَائِزًا فِي صَدْرِ الدِّينِ ثُمَّ نُسِخَ فِي خَيْبَرَ أَوْ عُمْرَةِ الْقَضَاءِ أَوِ الْفَتْحِ أَوْ أَوْطَاسَ أَوْ تَبُوكَ أَوْ حَجَّةِ الْوَدَاعِ

"Then forbidden, then licensed, then forbidden. It was permitted at the head of the religion, then abrogated at Khaybar, or the compensatory umra, or the Fath, or Awtas, or Tabuk, or the Farewell Pilgrimage."

- al-Munawi (d.1031 AH), Fayd al-Qadir, vol.6, p416

One prohibition, six candidate dates from the tradition’s own chroniclers. A community that knew when its Prophet ﷺ forbade a thing carried one date, as it does for the qibla and for the prohibition of usury at the Farewell Pilgrimage. On Mut’ah it carries a new date in every chain, because each chain is anchoring a ban that had no single original occasion. Six dates that cannot all be true point to a prohibition with a real author and a real date somewhere else, and the canon names that author inside its own reports.

The Canon Names the Man

Source: Imran ibn al-Husayn in the Musnad, relayed in Miftah al-Sa’ada, vol.3, p221; al-Razi on 4:24; Mawardi, al-Nukat wa-l-Uyun, vol.1, p471; Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.14, p481

Claim: “a man said by his own opinion” is Umar, and the verse’s mut’a was never abrogated

نزلت هذه المتعة في كتاب اللّه لم تنزل بعدها آية فتنسخها وأمرنا بها رسول اللّه ( ص ) وتمتّعنا بها ومات ولم ينهنا عنها ثمّ قال رجل برأيه ما شاء انتهى ما ذكره في البحار

"The verse of mut'a came down in God's Book, and we acted upon it with the Messenger of God ﷺ, and no verse came down abrogating it, and the Prophet ﷺ did not forbid it until he died. Then a man said in it what he wanted by his own opinion."

- Imran ibn al-Husayn, in Ahmad (d.241 AH), Musnad, as relayed in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, and al-Qayni, Miftah al-Sa'ada, vol.3, p221; wording in al-Razi (d.606 AH), Mafatih al-Ghayb, on 4:24

وحكى الفخر الرّازى في تفسير آية المتعة عن محمّد ابن جرير الطّبرى قال قال علىّ ابن أبي طالب لولا انّ عمر نهى عن المتعة ما زنى الّا شقىّ

"Al-Razi relayed, in his commentary on the verse of mut'a, from Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari, who said, Ali ibn Abi Talib said, were it not that Umar forbade the mut'a, none but the wretched would fornicate."

- al-Razi (d.606 AH), Mafatih al-Ghayb, on 4:24, relaying al-Tabari; as cited in al-Qayni, Miftah al-Sa'ada, vol.3, p221

هشام ، عن ابن جريح قال : قال عطاء : سمعت ابن عباس يقول : رحم الله عمر ، ما كانت المتعة إلا رحمة من الله رحم بها أمة محمد (﵌) ، ولولا نهيه عنها ما احتاج أحد إلى الزنى إلا شقي ، قال عطاء : والله لكأني اسمع قوله الآن : إلا شقي ، قال عطاء : فهي التي في سورة النساء (فما استمتعتم به منهن فآتوهن أجورهن) قال : إلى كذا وكذا من الاجل ، على كذا وكذا ، وليس بيننا وراثة ، فإن بدا لهما أن يتراضيا بعد الاجل فنعم ، وإن تفرقا فنعم ، وليس بنكاح

"May God have mercy on Umar. The mut'a was nothing but a mercy from God, with which He mercied the nation of Muhammad ﷺ, and were it not for his forbidding of it, no one would need fornication except the wretched. Ata said, by God, it is as though I hear him say it now, except the wretched. Ata said, it is that which is in sura al-Nisa, what you have enjoyed of them, give them their wages. He said, to such-and-such a term, upon such-and-such a wage, and there is no inheritance between us. If the two of them wish to renew after the term, well and good, and if they separate, well and good, and it is not a marriage."

- Ibn Abbas, in al-Nuri (d.1320 AH), Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p481, no.17355, from Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn al-Mundhir, from Ata; parallel in Ibn al-Athir, al-Nihaya, on the entry shafa

Imran’s report runs to the Prophet’s ﷺ death with no abrogating verse and no prohibition, then a man speaks by his own opinion. Al-Razi names the man on the clause, Umar. Inside the Sunni tafsir of the verse itself, al-Mawardi relays al-Hakam’s report of Ali’s ﵇ words and can only answer that it does not stand. Ibn Abbas prays mercy on Umar while naming his prohibition as the thing that removed a mercy from the nation, and Ata swears he still hears the words. Lexicon, poetry, recitation, practice, and now the canon’s own exegetes, all point to the same man. The only defense left is that the reports themselves are too contradictory to prove anything, and that defense indicts its own witnesses.

The Reports Contradict

Source: al-Jawhari (d.1266 AH), Jawahir al-Kalam, vol.30, p147

Claim: the abrogation reports contradict one another to a degree from which one learns they are fabricated

على أن الأخبار التي رووها في النسخ متناقضة على وجه يعلم منه أنها موضوعة ، فإنهم رووا أنها أبيحت عام الفتح وأن النبي ﵌ لم يخرج من مكة حتى حرمها (1) وأنها أبيحت عام أوطاس ثلاثة أيام (2) أو يوما أو ليلة وأنها أبيحت في حجة الوداع ، ثم نهى النبي ﵌ عنها (3) وأنها أبيحت أول الاسلام حتى نزلت (إلا على أزواجهم) (4) الآية (5) وأنها نسخت يوم خيبر ويوم تبوك (6) وأين حجة الوداع والفتح وخيبر وتبوك عن أول الاسلام ، كما أنه أين هو وأين عام أوطاس

"The reports they transmit of the abrogation contradict one another to a degree from which one learns they are fabricated. For they relate that it was licensed in the year of the Fath and that the Prophet ﷺ did not leave Mecca until he forbade it; that it was licensed in the year of Awtas for three days, or a day, or a night; that it was licensed in the Farewell Pilgrimage and the Prophet ﷺ then forbade it; that it was licensed at the dawn of Islam until the verse 'except their spouses' came down; and that it was abrogated on the day of Khaybar and the day of Tabuk. And where is the Farewell Pilgrimage and the Fath and Khaybar and Tabuk against the dawn of Islam, and where is it, and where is the year of Awtas?"

- al-Jawhari (d.1266 AH), Jawahir al-Kalam, vol.30, p147

The Shia jurists read the six dates the way any court reads six mutually exclusive accounts of one event. Where the dates cannot all be true, the event they date is the thing in doubt. The Fath, Awtas, Khaybar, Tabuk, the Farewell Pilgrimage, and the dawn of Islam sit years apart, and a revelation-based law does not schedule its own prohibition six ways. The whole thread gathers here. The word carried the term, the poets confirmed it, the verse legislated it, the first readers recited it, the jurists recorded it, the Companions practiced it through two caliphates, and the pulpit declaration ended it in the first person. That is the record, and the verdict states what it proves.

6. The Four Schools

The Consensus and Its Odd Man

Source: al-Suyuti (d.911 AH), Jawahir al-Uqud, vol.2, p22; Ibn Qudama (d.620 AH), al-Mughni, vol.7, p571

Claim: the four schools forbid Mut’ah, and one of them preserves a minority reading that the contract itself stands

وأجمعوا على أن نكاح المتعة باطل ، لا خلاف بينهم في ذلك . وصفته : أن يتزوج امرأة إلى مدة . فيقول : تزوجتك إلى شهر أو سنة . ونحو ذلك . وهو باطل منسوخ بإجماع العلماء بأسرهم قديما وحديثا . وورد جواز ذلك عن ابن عباس . والصحيح عنه : القول ببطلانه . ولكن حكى زفر عن الحنفية : أن الشرط يسقط ، ويصح النكاح على التأبيد إذا كان بلفظ التزويج . وإن كان بلفظ المتعة فهو موافق للجماعة .

And they agreed that the Mut’ah marriage is void, no difference among them on that. Its form is that a man marries a woman for a period, saying, I marry you for a month, or a year, or the like. It is void, abrogated by the consensus of the scholars, early and late. Its allowance was transmitted from Ibn Abbas, and the sound position from him is that it is void. But Zafar related from the Hanafis that the condition falls and the marriage stands as permanent, if the wording was that of marriage. If the wording was that of Mut’ah, he agrees with the group.

- al-Suyuti (d.911 AH), Jawahir al-Uqud, vol.2, p22

The four schools agree on the prohibition. Abu Hanifa, Malik, al-Shafi’i, and Ahmad all hold the Mut’ah marriage forbidden. That is the record, and it belongs on the page. But al-Suyuti’s inventory preserves the fissure inside the consensus. Zafar, the Hanafi master, held that the contract itself is valid and only the term-condition falls, so a marriage contracted with a named term stands as a permanent marriage. The contract could not stand if the term made it a different species of nothing. The school’s own minority reading concedes the point at issue, that what 4:24 legislated was a real contract of marriage.

Ibn Qudama Names the School

Source: Ibn Qudama (d.620 AH), al-Mughni, vol.7, p571

Claim: the Hanbali school’s own manual dates each school’s prohibition and concedes the practice’s early legality

(مسألة) قال ﵀ تعالى (ولا يجوز نكاح المتعة) معنى نكاح المتعة أن يتزوج المرأة مدة مثل أن يقول زوجتك ابنتي شهرا أو سنة أو إلى انقضاء الموسم أو قدوم الحاج وشبهه سواء كانت المدة معلومة أو مجهولة فهذا نكاح باطل نص عليه أحمد فقال نكاح المتعة حرام . وقال أبو بكر فيها رواية أخرى انها مكروهة غير حرام لأن ابن منصور سأل أحمد عنها فقال يجتنبها أحب إلى ، قال فظاهر هذا الكراهة دون التحريم ، وغير أبي بكر من أصحابنا يمنع هذا ويقول في المسألة رواية واحدة في تحريمها وهذا قول عامة الصحابة والفقهاء ، وممن روي عنه تحريمها عمر وعلي وابن عمر وابن مسعود وابن الزبير ، قال ابن عبد البر وعلى تحريم المتعة مالك وأهل المدينة وأبو حنيفة في أهل الكوفة والأوزاعي في أهل الشام والليث في أهل مصر والشافعي وسائر أصحاب الآثار وقال زفر يصح النكاح ويبطل الشرط وحكي عن ابن عباس انها جائزة وعليه أكثر أصحابه عطاء وطاوس وبه قال ابن جريج وحكي ذلك عن أبي سعيد الخدري وجابر واليه ذهب الشيعة لأنه قد ثبت أن النبي ﵌ أذن فيها

Question. Al-Qadi Abu Ya’la said, and the Mut’ah marriage is not permitted. Its meaning is that a man marries a woman for a period, saying, I marry you my daughter for a month, or a year, or until the season ends, or the pilgrims arrive, or the like, whether the period is known or unknown. This is a void marriage. Ahmad stated it plainly, the Mut’ah marriage is forbidden. Abu Bakr [al-Khallal] related another report from him that it is disliked rather than forbidden, because Ibn Mansur asked Ahmad about it and he said, avoiding it is dearer to me, and the apparent meaning is dislike short of prohibition. The companions other than Abu Bakr reject this and say the school has one report only, on its prohibition. This is the position of the generality of the Companions and the jurists. Those transmitted in prohibition are Umar, Ali, Ibn Umar, Ibn Mas’ud, and Ibn al-Zubayr. Ibn Abd al-Barr said, on the prohibition of Mut’ah are Malik and the people of Medina, Abu Hanifa among the people of Kufa, al-Awza’i among the people of Syria, al-Layth among the people of Egypt, and al-Shafi’i and all the people of the reports. Zafar said, the marriage is valid and the condition falls. It is related from Ibn Abbas that it is permitted, and on this are most of his companions, Ata, Tawus, and Ibn Jurayj held it, and the same is related of Abu Sa’id al-Khudri and Jabir, and to it went the Shia, because it is established that the Prophet ﷺ gave license in it.

- Ibn Qudama (d.620 AH), al-Mughni, vol.7, p571

The Hanbali manual states the whole map in one passage. Ahmad himself is recorded both ways, forbidden plainly, and disliked short of prohibition. Ibn Qudama’s own proof for the prohibition is the list of names, and Ibn Abbas, Jabir, Abu Sa’id, and their schools stand on the other side. His closing reason for the permissive school is the strongest sentence in the chapter, and it comes from the manual of the school that forbids, because it is established that the Prophet ﷺ gave license in it. The four schools inherited a dispute, and each side of it is a Companion position.

The Schools on What the Contract Carries

Source: Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Kafi (the recension of al-Mukhtasar), p230; Malik, al-Mudawwana, vol.6, p203

Claim: the Maliki school treats Mut’ah as a marriage whose consequences the law protects even while voiding it

ونكاح المتعة باطل مفسوخ وهو ان يتوزج الرجل المرأة بشيء مسمى إلى أجل معلوم يوما أو شهرا أو مدة من الزمان معلومة على أن الزوجية تنقضي بانقضاء الأجل والفرقة في ذلك فسخ بغير طلاق قبل الدخول وبعده ويجب في المهر المسمى بالدخول عند مالك فإن لم يسم شيئا أو سمى مالا يكون صداقا عنده وجب فيه صداق المثل ويسقط فيه الحد ويلحق الولد وعليها العدة كاملة

The Mut’ah marriage is void and annulled. It is that a man marries a woman for something named, to a term known, a day, or a month, or a known span of time, on the understanding that the marriage ends at the end of the term. The separation in that is annulment without divorce, before consummation and after it. The named dower becomes due upon consummation according to Malik. If nothing was named, or something was named that is not a dower in his view, the fair dower is due. The fixed punishment falls away in it, the child is attributed, and upon her is the full waiting period.

- Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Kafi, p230, on the doctrine of Malik

على عمتها وكذلك نكاح المتعة عامدا لا يحدون في ذلك ويعاقبون

Upon her aunt, and likewise the Mut’ah marriage, deliberately, they are not given the fixed punishment in that, and they are disciplined.

- Malik (d.179 AH), al-Mudawwana al-Kubra, vol.6, p203

Here is the answer to the question the section exists for. Malik’s school voids the Mut’ah contract and then treats it as a marriage in every consequence. The woman takes her dower. The child is legitimate and attributed. The waiting period is owed in full. The fornication penalty is expressly waived, in the Mudawwana’s own text, because the school classifies the act as a marriage contracted in error of law, an annulled marriage. A thing the law treated as fornication would carry none of this. Malik’s fiqh could only build these consequences on one understanding, that the Mut’ah is a form of nikah, a real marriage, whose defect is legal validity, not its nature. The term, the named wage, the separation without divorce at the term’s end, the absence of inheritance, all of it is exactly what the Shia school legislates, and the Maliki manual describes the form in its own definition before voiding it.

Abu Hanifa’s Own Book on Umar’s Role

Source: al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), al-Mabsut, vol.5, p152; vol.4, p27

Claim: the Hanafi corpus narrates the Prophet’s ﷺ license, affirms the original permission as agreed, and preserves Umar’s prohibition

(باب نكاح المتعة) (قال) بلغنا عن رسول الله ﵌ انه أحل المتعة ثلاثة أيام من الدهر في غزاة غزاها اشتد على الناس فيها العزوبة ثم نهى عنها وتفسير المتعة أن يقول لامرأته أتمتع بك كذا من المدة بكذا من البدل وهذا باطل عندنا جائز عند مالك بن أنس وهو الظاهر من قول ابن عباس ﵁ واستدل بقوله تعالى فما استمتعتم به منهن فآتوهن أجورهن ولأنا اتفقنا على أنه كان مباحا والحكم الثابت يبقي حتى يظهر نسخه ولكن قد ثبت نسخ هذه الإباحة بالآثار المشهورة

Chapter of the Mut’ah marriage. He said, it reached us from the Messenger of God ﷺ that he made Mut’ah lawful three days out of the age, on a campaign of his in which celibacy bore hard upon the people, then he forbade it. The interpretation of Mut’ah is that a man says to a woman, I enjoy you for such a period, for such a compensation. This is void among us, permitted according to Malik ibn Anas, and it is the apparent position of Ibn Abbas. He argued from God’s word, what you have enjoyed of them, give them their wages. And we agree that it was permitted, and a ruling that stands remains until its abrogation appears. But the abrogation of this permission has been established by the well-known reports.

- al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), al-Mabsut, vol.5, p152, chapter of the Mut’ah marriage

وقد صح ان عمر ﵁ نهى الناس عن المتعة فقال متعتان كانتا على عهد رسول الله ﵌ وانا أنهى الناس عنهما متعة النساء ومتعة الحج

And it is established that Umar forbade the people the Mut’ah, saying, two Mut’ahs were upon the day of the Messenger of God ﷺ, and I forbid the people them, the Mut’ah of women and the Mut’ah of pilgrimage.

- al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), al-Mabsut, vol.4, p27

The Hanafi corpus is direct. Al-Sarakhsi’s chapter opens by narrating the Prophetic license, defines the contract exactly as the term-bound marriage, credits Ibn Abbas with the verse as proof, and concedes in so many words, we agree that it was permitted. The school’s case is abrogation, not that the thing was never a marriage. And when al-Sarakhsi explains Umar’s pulpit words on the pilgrimage Mut’ah, the same sermon the page has already quoted, he cites it as established fact in the middle of Hanafi law. The school that helped build the prohibition preserved both its starting point and its legislative author.

What the Four Schools Prove

Claim: the schools’ own books confirm the three facts the page has established

The four schools, read in their own manuals, close the loop the page has been drawing. First, every school describes the Mut’ah as a marriage, contract of offer and acceptance, a dower named, a term named, separation at the term without divorce, no inheritance. That is the fiqh of a nikah, and no school calls it anything else. Second, the schools concede the original permission in their own proofs. Al-Sarakhsi agrees it was permitted. Ibn Qudama’s reason for the permissive Companions is that the Prophet’s ﷺ license is established. The dispute the schools inherit is about abrogation, not about what the thing is. Third, the consequences the schools attach to a contracted Mut’ah, dower, lineage, waiting period, no fornication penalty, are the consequences of a marriage, which is why Zafar could read the contract as standing. The schools forbid what they cannot describe as anything but a term-bound marriage. The verse legislated exactly that, the Companions practiced exactly that, and the school books are the last witnesses to it.

7. Jurisprudence Surrounding Mut’ah

The masa’il below answer the questions people most often ask, taken from the Imami hadith literature itself, al-Kafi’s chapters of Mut’ah and the works that follow them. This is the practical law of a living institution, not a historical curiosity.

The Form of the Contract

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, book of marriage, chapters of Mut’ah

Claim: two elements make the Mut’ah, a named term and a named wage

The masa’il of Mut’ah are the everyday fiqh questions the Imams ﵈ answered across the chapters of al-Kafi. The contract itself needs two things and only two, a term named and a wage named. The offer is simple, the Imam al-Rida ﵇ teaches its form, “O maid of God, I marry you for such-and-such days for such-and-such dirhams.” The separation at the term’s end is automatic, built into the stipulation, and no divorce is required. Everything else in the fiqh follows from that shape.

May a Virgin Contract Mut’ah?

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p455; Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.14, p459

Claim: yes, with two qualifications, and the father’s permission is the contested question

لا بأس أن يتمتع بالبكر ما لم يفض إليها كراهية العيب على أهلها

There is no harm in contracting Mut’ah with a virgin, so long as he does not consummate with her, out of dislike of spoiling her for her family.

- Imam Ja’far al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p455, from Jamil ibn Dirraj

ولا تتمتع بذوات الآباء من الأبكار إلا بأذن آبائهن

And do not contract Mut’ah with a virgin who has a living father, except with her father’s permission.

- al-Saduq, al-Muqni’, p113, in al-Burujerdi, Jami’ Ahadith al-Shi’a, vol.21, p39

A virgin may contract Mut’ah. The Imam’s ﵇ first ruling guards her family’s interest, do not consummate with her, because her value in the marriage market of that society lay in virginity, and the Mut’ah should not spoil it. The second qualification guards the father’s standing, his daughter does not contract without his permission. The jurists weigh these reports against the general Mut’ah permission, and the working rule the manuals settle on is caution, a fathered virgin takes her father’s leave. A woman past virginity, or a fatherless one, contracts for herself.

The Term and the Wage

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p457 and p458; Wasa’il al-Shi’a, vol.21, p50

Claim: no minimum term or wage, and the minimum the Imams named is almost nothing

سَأَلْتُ أَبَا عَبْدِ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ عَنْ أَدْنَى مَهْرِ الْمُتْعَةِ : مَا هُوَ ؟ قَالَ : « كَفٌّ مِنْ طَعَامٍ دَقِيقٍ ، أَوْ سَوِيقٍ ، أَوْ تَمْرٍ »

I asked Abu Abd Allah ﵇, what is the least dower of Mut’ah? He said, ‘a handful of flour, or grain drink, or dates.’

- Imam Ja’far al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p457

No minimum term and no minimum wage. The term is whatever the two name, and one report has a toothpick permitted as the wage. The term may not be left vague, the contract dies without a named span. At the term’s end the couple may renew, and renewal needs a new wage. The man may also gift her the remaining term, releasing her early. Both are free to stipulate conditions, and the working manuals carry the same one, the wife may stipulate that he not take another, or that he not consummate with her.

The Waiting Period

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p458-460; al-Saduq, Man la Yahduruhu al-Faqih, vol.3, p464-465

Claim: a short ‘idda after the term, a full widow’s ‘idda at death

إِنْ كَانَتْ تَحِيضُ فَحَيْضَةٌ ، وَإِنْ كَانَتْ لَا تَحِيضُ فَشَهْرٌ وَنِصْفٌ

If she menstruates, one period. If she does not menstruate, a month and a half.

- Imam Ja’far al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p458, from Zurara

كُلُّ نِكَاحٍ إِذَا مَاتَ عَنْهَا الزَّوْجُ فَعَلَى الْمَرْأَةِ حُرَّةً كَانَتْ أَوْ أَمَةً عَلَى أَيِّ وَجْهٍ كَانَ النِّكَاحُ مِنْهُ مُتْعَةً أَوْ تَزْوِيجًا أَوْ مُلْكَ يَمِينٍ فَالْعِدَّةُ أَرْبَعَةُ أَشْهُرٍ وَعَشْرًا

Every marriage, when the husband dies from her, upon the woman is the waiting period, free or slave, whatever form the marriage took, Mut’ah, permanent marriage, or possession, the waiting period is four months and ten days.

- Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇, in al-Saduq, Man la Yahduruhu al-Faqih, vol.3, p465, from Zurara

Yes, the woman waits. The ‘idda of Mut’ah is one menstruation, or forty-five days for one who does not menstruate, markedly shorter than the three cycles of the divorced permanent wife, because the shorter union carries a shorter doubt. At the husband’s death the full widow’s ‘idda of four months and ten days applies, with no difference between the Mut’ah wife and the permanent one. The woman who was not consummated with waits nothing at all.

Inheritance and the Father’s Duty

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p451 and p460; al-Tusi, al-Istibsar, vol.3, p152; Wasa’il al-Shi’a, vol.21, p69

Claim: no inheritance between the spouses, full rights for the child

ليست من الأربع لأنها لا تطلق ولا ترث ، وإنما هي مستأجرة

She is not one of the four, because she is not divorced and does not inherit. She is as a hired woman.

- Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇, in al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p451, from Muhammad ibn Muslim

قُلْتُ لَهُ : أَرَأَيْتَ إِنْ حَبِلَتْ ؟ قَالَ : هُوَ وَلَدُهُ

I said to him, what if she conceives? He said, it is his child.

- Imam Ja’far al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Tusi, al-Istibsar, vol.3, p152, from Muhammad ibn Muslim

The rule the sources state plainly, no inheritance runs between the Mut’ah spouses, and the child inherits fully from both. The Imam’s ﵇ reason is structural, she does not inherit and is not inherited from because she is like a hired woman, her wage is her contract, her bond ends at its term. The child is another matter. He is his father’s son, and the Imam ﵇ forecloses the escape, even a stipulation against claiming the child is void, and denying him is forbidden. The father owes the child what any father owes, lineage, maintenance, custody’s duties, inheritance on both sides. The mother’s marriage ends, the father’s duty does not.

Everyday Rules

Source: al-Kafi, al-Furu’, vol.5, p450-462; al-Tusi, Tahdhib al-Ahkam, vol.7, p259

Claim: the common masa’il in one view

The rest of the fiqh in brief. The Mut’ah wife is not counted among the four permanent wives, so a man with four may still contract Mut’ah, though some jurists prefer caution and count her. He may not join two sisters in Mut’ah at once, nor contract the aunt and niece together, the prohibited combinations of permanent marriage apply. He may contract Mut’ah while holding a permanent wife. He may look at her before contracting. The People of the Book are permitted, though a believing woman is preferred. He may withdraw, ‘azl, and may stipulate it. She owes no wife’s obedience in his house, no sharing of nights, and he owes her no maintenance or housing, her right is the wage alone, unless he stipulates otherwise. She keeps her own property. He may gift her the remaining term and end the marriage early. The marriage spreads the prohibited degrees exactly as permanent marriage does, her daughter is his stepdaughter, her mother his wife’s mother. A virtuous woman is the standard, the Imam ﵇ said to ask about a woman of unknown character before contracting, and warned that the piety of the women of the first generation is not the piety of the later one.

Glossary of Key Terms

mut’a: a marriage bounded by a named term and a named wage, ending at the term without divorce

istimta’: the derived stem of m-t-ʿ, to take enjoyment of a thing for a span, the verb of Qur’an 4:24

mut’at al-hajj: the sister institution, enjoying the umra up to the hajj, banned by Umar in the same pulpit words

ujr: the wage of Qur’an 4:24, the named payment of the contract

Khaybar: the oasis campaign of Safar 7 AH, the date of the first prohibition report

the Fath: the conquest of Mecca, Ramadan 8 AH

Awtas: the mustering ground of the Hunayn captives, Shawwal 8 AH, where the three-day license was given

tadlis: naming a teacher one did not directly hear from, graded acceptable from trustworthy transmitters only

hasan sahih: al-Tirmidhi’s double grading, sound in chain and acceptable in body

muṣḥaf: a Companion’s written codex of the Qur’an, before the standard collection

Successor: a Muslim of the generation after the Companions

Final Verdict

The word itself settles the first question before any hadith is cited. Mut’ah is not a word the law borrowed and reshaped. The lexicons define the root as benefit joined to a span, and the poets used it that way for six centuries. Al-Ra’i’s two neighbors were li-l-tafarruqi umta’a, their enjoyment of each other running toward parting, with the end inside the enjoyment from the first line. Al-Isfahani’s gray-haired man umatti’a bi-sharakhi shabab, enjoyed the prime of youth, the very phrase whose passing the line mourns. Al-Ma’arri, four centuries into Islam, still used the verb for enjoying days that end. When Qur’an 4:24 legislates with that verb, istamta’tum, the term is already built into the word. A marriage bounded by a named span and a named wage is what the Arabic says.

The Sunni record itself then narrates Mut’ah as lawful from the emigration, banned by report at Khaybar in 7 AH, licensed again at the Fath and Awtas in 8 AH, and banned within days by report. The practice ran on through Abu Bakr’s caliphate into Umar’s. A Kufa affair after 17 AH brought the pulpit declaration. The first readers read the clause with the term inside the recitation, and the Sunni jurists recorded no difference on its form. The prohibition reports give six occasions that cannot all be true. The ban that ends the record survives in the first person, with stoning attached, spoken by the man who made it.

The falsification condition stands. One prophetic report, sound in chain, carrying one date and one final revocation, would end the case. Fourteen centuries of collection have produced six dates, a practice attested into Umar’s caliphate by sound chains, and a caliph’s declaration whose authenticity the canon itself affirms. On this record the Shia position is the record.
