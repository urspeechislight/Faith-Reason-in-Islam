A Christian Charge Weighed Against the Qur'an and the Corpus

The Apostate the Qur'an Leaves Alive

The charge is plain. Islam commands the death of the apostate, so it grants no freedom of religion. The Qur'an answers at the root. It forbids compulsion, leaves the choice to the person, and fixes the cost of leaving in the next world. The corpus reads the death report as a single strand, and the Imams taught that every report must be set against the Book. The attack measures Islam by a freedom its own canon denies.

The Argument Under Examination

Premise 1. The Qur'an Forbids Compulsion

The charge assumes a worldly death for leaving the faith. The Qur'an assumes the opposite. It forbids compulsion in religion and leaves the choice to the person. It sets the price of unbelief in the Hereafter, not in a court. A law that kills at the first exit could not also describe a person who believes, leaves, returns, and leaves again.

Premise 2. 9:11-12 Speak to Treaty-Breakers

The verse critics cite as the death warrant sits inside the bara'a treaties. It answers a coalition that broke its oath and attacked the religion. The same chapter opens by protecting those who kept their word. The school's own tafsir treats the passage as war between parties, not as a statute for a private doubter.

Premise 3. The Death Report Is Single-Strand

The saying "whoever changes his religion, kill him" reaches us through one chain. The Imams taught that every report must be weighed against the Book. The Book already spoke on compulsion and the Hereafter. The early jurists understood the saying as conditioned on an invitation to repent, not as a flat command.

Premise 4. The Recorded Killings Were War

The cases at the Prophet's time fit a wartime frame. Musaylima led an armed following in the ridda wars. The burning report concerns deserters in war, and the Prophet restrained the punishment. The quiet neighbor who changes his mind appears nowhere in the record.

Conclusion. The Book Leaves the Apostate Alive

The Qur'an forbids compulsion and sets the cost of leaving in the next world. Qur'an 9:11-12 answers treaty-breakers, not doubters. The death report is single-strand and condition-bound, and the recorded killings were war. The attack appeals to a freedom its own founding text withholds.

1. The Qur'an Leaves Belief Free

The Qur'an answers the charge at its root. It protects the freedom to believe and the freedom to leave, and it fixes the cost of leaving in the next world rather than by a worldly sword.

لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ قَدْ تَبَيَّنَ الرُّشْدُ مِنَ الْغَيِّ فَمَنْ يَكْفُرْ بِالطَّاغُوتِ وَيُؤْمِنْ بِاللَّهِ فَقَدِ اسْتَمْسَكَ بِالْعُرْوَةِ الْوُثْقَى لَا انْفِصَامَ لَهَا وَاللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ

la ikraha fi al-dini qad tabayyana al-rushdu min al-ghayyi

"There is no compulsion in religion. The right course has become clear from the wrong. Whoever disbelieves in taghut and believes in Allah has grasped the firmest handhold that never breaks."

Qur'an 2:256

وَقُلِ الْحَقُّ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ فَمَنْ شَاءَ فَلْيُؤْمِنْ وَمَنْ شَاءَ فَلْيَكْفُرْ إِنَّا أَعْتَدْنَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ نَارًا أَحَاطَ بِهِمْ سُرَادِقُهَا وَإِنْ يَسْتَغِيثُوا يُغَاثُوا بِمَاءٍ كَالْمُهْلِ يَشْوِي الْوُجُوهَ بِئْسَ الشَّرَابُ وَسَاءَتْ مُرْتَفَقًا

wa qul al-haqqu min rabbikum fa man shaa fa li yu'min wa man shaa fa li yakfur

"Say that the truth is from your Lord. Whoever wills, let him believe, and whoever wills, let him disbelieve. We have prepared for the wrongdoers a fire."

Qur'an 18:29

وَمَنْ يَرْتَدِّدْ مِنْكُمْ عَنْ دِينِهِ فَيَمُتْ وَهُوَ كَافِرٌ فَأُولَئِكَ حَبِطَتْ أَعْمَالُهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ وَأُولَئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ

wa man yartadda min kum an deenihi fa yamut wa huwa kafirun

"Whoever of you reverts from his religion and dies a disbeliever, his deeds have failed in this world and the next, and he is among the companions of the Fire."

Qur'an 2:217

مَنْ كَفَرَ بِاللَّهِ مِنْ بَعْدِ إِيمَانِهِ إِلَّا مَنْ أُكْرِهَ وَقَلْبُهُ مُطْمَئِنٌّ بِالْإِيمَانِ وَلَكِنْ مَنْ شَرَحَ بِالْكُفْرِ صَدْرًا فَعَلَيْهِمْ غَضَبٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ

man kafara bi allahi min ba'di imanihi illa man ukriha

"Whoever disbelieves in Allah after belief, except one forced while his heart is content in faith. Those who open their chests to disbelief incur wrath from Allah and a great punishment."

Qur'an 16:106

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا ثُمَّ آمَنُوا ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا ثُمَّ ازْدَادُوا كُفْرًا لَمْ يَكُنِ اللَّهُ لِيَغْفِرَ لَهُمْ وَلَا لِيَهْدِيَهُمْ سَبِيلًا

inna allatheena amanu thumma kafaru thumma amanu thumma kafaru

"Those who believed, then disbelieved, then believed, then disbelieved, and then increased in disbelief. Allah will not forgive them nor guide them to a way."

Qur'an 4:137

وَلَوْ شَاءَ رَبُّكَ لَآمَنَ مَنْ فِي الْأَرْضِ كُلُّهُمْ جَمِيعًا أَفَأَنْتَ تُكْرِهُ النَّاسَ حَتَّىٰ يَكُونُوا مُؤْمِنِينَ

wa law shaa rabbuka la amana man fi al-ardi kulluhum jamee'an

"If your Lord had willed, all on earth would have believed together. Then would you compel the people until they become believers."

Qur'an 10:99

إِلَّا الَّذِينَ تَابُوا مِنْ بَعْدِ ذَلِكَ وَأَصْلَحُوا فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَحِيمٌ

illa allatheena tabu min ba'di dhalika wa aslahu

"Except those who repent after that and correct themselves. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."

Qur'an 3:89

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا بَعْدَ إِيمَانِهِمْ ثُمَّ ازْدَادُوا كُفْرًا لَنْ تُقْبَلَ تَوْبَتُهُمْ وَأُولَئِكَ هُمُ الضَّالُّونَ

inna allatheena kafaru ba'da imanihim thumma azdadu kufran

"Those who disbelieved after their belief and then increased in disbelief. Their repentance will not be accepted, and they are the astray."

Qur'an 3:90

فَذَكِّرْ إِنَّمَا أَنْتَ مُذَكِّرٌ

fa dhakkir innama anta mudhakkir

"So remind. You are only a reminder."

Qur'an 88:21

لَسْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ بِمُصَيْطِرٍ

lasta alayhim bi musaytir

"You are not a controller over them."

Qur'an 88:22

The verses do not describe a worldly execution. They describe compulsion forbidden, choice left open, deeds lost, and the Hereafter as the arena of account. A death penalty at the first exit would make Qur'an 4:137 impossible, since that verse contemplates a person who believes, leaves, returns, and leaves again.

2. The Bara'a Frame of 9:11-12

The attack reads Qur'an 9:11-12 as the death warrant for any apostate. The chapter places those verses inside the bara'a treaties, where the breaking party is a combatant coalition, not a private doubter.

فَإِنْ تَابُوا وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ فَإِخْوَانُكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَنُفَصِّلُ الْآيَاتِ لِقَوْمٍ يَعْلَمُونَ

fa in tabu wa aqamu al-salata wa atawu al-zakata

"If they repent, establish prayer, and give zakat, then they are your brothers in religion."

Qur'an 9:11

وَإِنْ نَكَثُوا أَيْمَانَهُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ عَهْدِهِمْ وَطَعَنُوا فِي دِينِكُمْ فَقَاتِلُوا أَئِمَّةَ الْكُفْرِ إِنَّهُمْ لَا أَيْمَانَ لَهُمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَنْتَهُونَ

wa in nakathu aymanahum min ba'di ahdihim

"If they break their oaths after their treaty and attack your religion, then fight the leaders of disbelief. They have no oaths, so that they might stop."

Qur'an 9:12

إِلَّا الَّذِينَ عَاهَدْتُمْ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ ثُمَّ لَمْ يَنْقُصُوكُمْ شَيْئًا وَلَمْ يُظَاهِرُوا عَلَيْكُمْ أَحَدًا فَأَتِمُّوا إِلَيْهِمْ عَهْدَهُمْ إِلَى مُدَّتِهِمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُتَّقِينَ

illa allatheena ahadtum min al-mushrikeen

"Except those of the polytheists with whom you made a treaty, who then did not fail you and did not support anyone against you. Fulfill their treaty until its term, for Allah loves the mindful."

Qur'an 9:4

بيّن سبحانه خصال القوم وأنهم اعرضوا عن دين اللّه وصدوا الناس عنه بشيء يسير نالوه من الدنيا

"The Exalted made clear the traits of those people, that they turned away from the religion of Allah and barred people from it for a little of this world that they gained."

al-Tabarsi, Majma' al-Bayan on Qur'an 9:12

The verb "fight" in 9:12 addresses leaders who broke a treaty and attacked the religion. The same chapter opens with the treaty clause of 9:4, which protects those who kept their word. The school's own tafsir treats the passage as war, not as a statute for the quiet apostate.

3. The Single-Strand Death Report

The hadith "whoever changes his religion, kill him" reaches us as a single strand. The Imams taught that every report must be set against the Book, and the Book already spoke.

قال رسول الله ﵌ من بدل دينه فاقتلوه

"The Messenger of Allah said, 'Whoever changes his religion, kill him.'"

Sunan al-Nasa'i, Kitab al-Tahrim

أن ناسا ارتدوا عن الاسلام فحرقهم على بالنار قال ابن عباس لو كنت أنا لم أحرقهم قال رسول الله ﵌ لا تعذبوا بعذاب الله أحدا ولو كنت أنا لقتلتهم قال رسول الله ﵌ من بدل دينه فاقتلوه

"Some people apostatised from Islam, so Ali burned them. Ibn Abbas said, 'If it were me, I would not have burned them.' The Messenger of Allah said, 'Do not punish with the punishment of Allah,' and, 'Were it me, I would have killed them,' and, 'Whoever changes his religion, kill him.'"

Sunan al-Nasa'i, the burning report

قال صاحب الاستذكار لا اعلم بين الصحابة خلافا في استتابة المرتد فكأنهم فهموا من قوله ﵇ من بدل دينه فاقتلوه - أي بعد أن يستتاب -

"The author of al-Istidhkar said, 'I know of no disagreement among the Companions on giving the apostate a chance to repent.' So they understood from his saying 'whoever changes his religion, kill him' to mean after he is invited to repent."

al-Mardini, al-Jawhar al-Naqi

روى عن النبي ﵌ والأئمة ﵈ انهم قالوا : إذا جاءكم عنا حديث فأعرضوه على كتاب الله فما وافق كتاب الله فخذوه وما خالفه فاطرحوه أو ردوه إلينا

"They related from the Prophet and the Imams that they said, 'When a report comes to you from us, set it against the Book of Allah. What agrees with the Book of Allah, take it, and what opposes it, throw it away or return it to us.'"

Wasa'il al-Shi'a, the Imams' book-check rule

The early commentators understood the report as conditioned on an invitation to repent. The recorded killings fit the wartime frame. The well known case is Musaylima and his armed following in the ridda wars, a rebellion with an army, not a neighbor who changed his mind.

4. Imami Fiqh and the Distinctions

The Imami fiqh does rule on irtidad, and it rules with distinctions the attack omits. The school separates the born Muslim from the convert, opens a door of repentance, and spares the woman.

باب أن المرتد عن فطرة قتله مباح لكل من سمعه

"Chapter. The one who apostatises from the natural faith, his killing is permitted to whoever hears of it."

Wasa'il al-Shi'a, Bab al-murtadd an fitra

أن عليا (﵈) ، كان يستتيب الزنادقة ، ولا يستتيب من ولد في الاسلام ، ويقول : إنما نستتيب من دخل في ديننا ثم رجع عنه ، اما من ولد في الاسلام فلا نستتيبه

"Ali used to invite the heretics to repent, but did not invite to repentance one born in Islam. He said, 'We only invite to repentance one who entered our religion and then left it. One born in Islam, we do not invite to repentance.'"

Wasa'il al-Shi'a, from Ali

نصراني أسلم ، ثم ارتد ؟ قال : يستتاب فان رجع ، وإلا فتل

"A Christian accepted Islam, then apostatised. He said, 'He is invited to repent. If he returns, well. Otherwise he is killed.'"

Wasa'il al-Shi'a, the melli case

باب أن المرأة المرتدة لا تقتل ، بل تحبس وتضرب ويضيق عليها

"Chapter. The female apostate is not killed. Rather she is imprisoned, struck, and restricted."

Wasa'il al-Shi'a, Bab al-mar'a al-murtadda

أبي عبد الله (﵇) في المرتدة عن الإسلام قال : لا تقتل وتستخدم خدمة شديدة وتمنع الطعام والشراب إلا ما يمسك نفسها

"Abu Abdullah concerning the female apostate from Islam said, 'She is not killed. She is set to hard service and denied food and drink except what keeps her alive.'"

Wasa'il al-Shi'a, from Abu Abdullah

إذا ارتدت المرأة ، فالحكم فيها أن تحبس حتى تسلم ، أو تموت ، ولا تقتل

"If a woman apostatises, the ruling on her is that she is imprisoned until she submits or dies, and she is not killed."

Da'a'im al-Islam, the woman apostate

وهذه الآية دليل على قبول توبة المرتد الفطري

"This verse is proof of the acceptance of the repentance of the natural-faith apostate."

al-Jawahiri, al-Wadih fi Sharh al-Urwa

The school's own jurists read the Qur'an as opening the repentance door even for the natural-faith apostate. Al-Jawahiri ties the point to Qur'an 2:217. The fiqh is not concealed here. It is stated as it stands. The answer to it is the Book's frame and the reports' conditionality, not a denial that the school ruled.

5. The Attack's Own Canon

The attack measures Islam by a freedom its own founding text denies. Deuteronomy 13 commands death for the apostate and for the one who entices others away.

"If thy brother, the son of thy mother, or thy son, or thy daughter, or the wife of thy bosom, or thy friend, which is as thine own soul, entice thee secretly, saying, Let us go and serve other gods, which thou hast not known, thou, nor thy fathers; Namely, of the gods of the people which are round about you, nigh unto thee, or far off from thee, from the one end of the earth even unto the other end of the earth; Thou shalt not consent unto him, nor hearken unto him; neither shalt thine eye pity him, neither shalt thou spare, neither shalt thou conceal him; but thou shalt surely kill him; thine hand shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterwards the hand of all the people. And thou shalt stone him with stones, that he die; because he hath sought to thrust thee away from the LORD thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage."

Deuteronomy 13:6-10, King James Version, public domain

This is Deuteronomy 13:6-10 from the King James Version, public domain. The attack appeals to a liberty its own scripture withholds from the apostate. The measuring rod it brings condemns its own text first.

Final Verdict

The Qur'an leaves the apostate alive. It forbids compulsion, leaves the choice to the person, and sets the cost of leaving in the Hereafter.

Qur'an 9:11-12 speaks to treaty-breaking combatants in the bara'a frame, not to private belief. The death report is a single strand that the Imams themselves taught to test against the Book, and the early commentators understood it as conditioned on an invitation to repent.

The recorded killings were wartime desertion and armed revolt, not quiet exit. Imami fiqh states its distinctions plainly, with a repentance door and without executing women.

The attack appeals to a freedom its own canon denies. The Qur'an leaves the apostate alive, and the attack's own measuring rod convicts first.
