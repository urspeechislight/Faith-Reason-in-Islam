The 27/24 Challenge

What the critic says

"A man dies. He leaves a wife, his mom, his dad and 2 daughters. Add up the Quran's shares and you get 27/24, which is 112.5%. You can't hand out more than all of the money!"

Where the critic goes wrong

1

The critic treats every share as a fixed amount that must be paid in full.

2

But the Quran gives two kinds of share. Some heirs have a locked seat. Others have a flexible seat.

3

Locked seats are paid first. Flexible seats share what is left. So the total is always exactly 100%.

Your 30-second answer

In this family, the Quran has already put the wife (1/8) and the mother (1/6) at their lowest share, and the father's 1/6 is fixed. Nothing can push them lower. The Quran names no lower share for daughters, so theirs is the one that changes. Pay the locked seats first: 3/24 + 4/24 + 4/24 = 11/24. The daughters get the rest: 13/24. Total: 24/24. Imam Ali gave this exact answer for this exact case: "They get what remains."

Proof from the Quran

For some heirs, the Quran names a lower share for when the shares don't all fit. For daughters and sisters, it names none.

Tap or click a row to read the verse.

Also: the Quran says every share is paid "after any gift written in his will, or any debt" (4:11–12). So the shares were always shares of what is left, not a fixed pile of money.
