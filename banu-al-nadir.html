Battle Narration

The Expedition Against Banu al-Nadir

The Nadir plotted treachery against the Prophet ﷺ at Quraysh's urging, a woman of their own tribe warned the Muslims, and a siege ended in negotiated exile to Khaybar and Syria, carrying what their camels could bear. The event earned the Qur'an a chapter of its own. Part 13 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Plot and the Warning

In Rabi' al-Awwal of the fourth year, the affair of the Nadir came to a head. Quraysh had written to the Jews of Medina. Fight our companion, the letter said, or expect the worst. The occasion report dates the letter after Badr, and the series' chronology puts the expulsion in the months after Uhud.

The Prophet ﷺ went to their ground to seek blood-money for two men a companion of his had killed by mistake. While he sat by their wall, the tribe plotted to drop a rock on him. The plot failed twice over, by a warning from heaven and by a woman of their own house who told her Muslim brother. The next day he came against their forts with squadrons, and the siege ended in negotiated exile.▼ commentary▼ commentary

The occasion itself, in the line al-Suyuti carries from Ibn Ishaq's circle.

انصرف من بئر معونة لقى رجلين كلابيين معهما أمان من رسول الله ﵌ فقتلهما ولم يعلم أن معهما أمانا من رسول الله ﵌ فذهب رسول الله ﵌ إلى بنى النضير ومعه أبو بكر وعمر وعلى فتلقاه بنو النضير فقالوا مرحبا يا أبا القاسم لماذا جئت قال رجل من أصحابي قتل رجلين من بنى كلاب معهما أمان منى طلب منى ديتهما فأريد ان تعينوني قالوا نعم أقعد حتى نجمع لك فقعد تحت الحصن وأبو بكر وعمر وعلى وقد تآمر بنو النضير أن يطرحوا عليه حجرا فجاء جبريل فأخبره بما هموا به فقام بمن معه وأنزل الله يا أيها الذين آمنوا اذكروا نعمة الله عليكم إذ هم قوم الآية

"As he turned back from Bi'r Ma'una, a man of his companions met two men of Banu Kilab who held a covenant from the Messenger of Allah, and killed them, not knowing they held a covenant. The Messenger of Allah went to Banu al-Nadir with Abu Bakr, Umar, and Ali, and the Nadir met him saying, 'Welcome, Abu al-Qasim, why have you come?' He said, 'A man of my companions killed two men who held a covenant from me, and he asked me for their blood-money. I want you to help me with it.' They said, 'Yes, sit until we gather it for you.' He sat by the fortress with Abu Bakr, Umar, and Ali, and the Nadir conspired to throw a rock down on him. Jibril came and told him what they intended, and he rose with his companions. Allah sent down, 'You who believe, remember Allah's favor upon you, when a people intended,' the verse."

- al-Suyuti, al-Durr al-Manthur, vol.2, p.266, carrying the Ibn Ishaq line

The occasions of revelation, the reports of what a verse came down about, carry the frame through a Quraysh letter and a warning from within.

أن كفار قريش كتبوا بعد وقعة بدر إلى اليهود : أنكم أهل الحلقة والحصون ، وأنكم لتقاتلن صاحبنا أو لنفعلن كذا (الى أن قال:) فأرسلت امرأة ناصحة من بني النضير إلى أخيها وهو رجل مسلم من الأنصار ، فأخبرته خبر ما أراد بنو النضير من الغدر برسول الله ، وأقبل أخوها سريعاً حتى أدرك النبي (صلى الله عليه واله) فساره بخبرهم ، فرجع النبي (صلى الله عليه واله)، فلما كان من الغد عدا عليهم بالكتائب فحاصرهم ، فقاتلهم حتى نزلوا على الجلاء ، على ان لهم ما اقلّت الابل الا الحلقة وهي السلاح ، وكانوا يخربون بيوتهم فيأخذون ما وافقهم من خشبها

"After the battle of Badr the idolaters of Quraysh wrote to the Jews, 'You are the people of armor and fortresses. You will fight our companion, or we will do such and such to you.' A woman of Banu al-Nadir who meant well sent word to her brother, a Muslim man of the Ansar, and told him what Banu al-Nadir intended, treachery against the Messenger of Allah. Her brother hurried until he reached the Prophet and told him in secret, and the Prophet turned back. The next day he marched on them with squadrons and besieged them, and he fought them until they came down to exile, with the right to carry away what their camels could bear, except the arms. They were pulling down their houses and taking whatever timber suited them."

- the occasions of revelation, via Maktabat al-Marja', p.554

The Shia line, from al-Qummi's Tafsir, with the negotiation round by round.

سبب ذلك أنه كان بالمدينة ثلاثة أبطن من اليهود : بني النضير وقريظة وقينقاع ، وكان بينهم وبين رسول الله (صلى الله عليه واله) عهد ومدة ، فنقضوا عهدهم ، وكان سبب ذلك بني النضير (الى أن قال:) قالوا : يا محمد : نخرج من بلادك ، فأعطنا مالنا ، فقال : لا ، ولكن تخرجون ولكم ما حملت الإبل ، فلم يقبلوا ذلك ، فبقوا أياماً ، ثم قالوا : نخرج ولنا ماحملت الإبل ، فقال : لا ، ولكن تخرجون ولا يحمل أحد منكم شيئاً ، فمن وجدنا معه شيئاً من ذلك قتلناه ، فخرجوا على ذلك

"Al-Qummi said. The cause was that three Jewish clans lived in Medina, the Nadir, the Qurayza, and the Qaynuqa, and between them and the Messenger of Allah stood a covenant and a term. They broke their covenant, and the beginning of it was the Nadir. They said, 'Muhammad, we will leave your land, so give us our wealth.' He said, 'No, but you may go out with what the camels carry.' They refused that and stayed some days, then said, 'We will go out, and ours is what the camels carry.' He said, 'No, but you will go out, and none of you will carry anything. Whoever we find with anything of it, we will kill him.' So they went out on those terms."

- al-Qummi's Tafsir, via Maktabat al-Marja', p.554

Al-Tabari fixes the terms of the surrender.

الله الذي أخرج الذين جحدوا نبوة محمد ﵌ من أهل الكتاب ، وهم يهود بني النضير من ديارهم ، وذلك خروجهم عن منازلهم ودورهم ، حين صالحوا رسول الله ﵌ على أن يؤمنهم على دمائهم ونسائهم وذراريهم ، وعلى أن لهم ما أقلت الإبل من أموالهم ، ويخلو له دورهم ، وسائر أموالهم ، فأجابهم رسول الله ﵌ إلى ذلك ، فخرجوا من ديارهم ، فمنهم من خرج إلى الشام ، ومنهم من خرج إلى خيبر

"Allah is the one who drove out those who denied the prophethood of Muhammad from among the People of the Book, the Jews of Banu al-Nadir, from their homes. That was their leaving their dwellings and houses, when they made peace with the Messenger of Allah on condition that he secure their blood, their women, and their children, and that they have what their camels could carry of their wealth, and that their houses and the rest of their wealth be left to him. The Messenger of Allah agreed with them on that, and they left their homes, some of them going out to Syria and some of them to Khaybar."

- al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan, vol.28, p.23

هُوَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَخْرَجَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ مِنْ أَهْلِ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ مِن دِيَٰرِهِمْ لِأَوَّلِ ٱلْحَشْرِ ۚ مَا ظَنَنتُمْ أَن يَخْرُجُوا۟ ۖ وَظَنُّوٓا۟ أَنَّهُم مَّانِعَتُهُمْ حُصُونُهُم مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ فَأَتَىٰهُمُ ٱللَّهُ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَمْ يَحْتَسِبُوا۟ ۖ وَقَذَفَ فِى قُلُوبِهِمُ ٱلرُّعْبَ ۚ يُخْرِبُونَ بُيُوتَهُم بِأَيْدِيهِمْ وَأَيْدِى ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ فَٱعْتَبِرُوا۟ يَٰٓأُو۟لِى ٱلْأَبْصَٰرِ

huwa lladhī akhraja lladhīna kafarū min ahli l-kitābi min diyārihim li-awwali l-ḥashri mā ẓanantum an yakhrujū wa-ẓannū annahum māniʿatuhum ḥuṣūnuhum mina llāhi fa-atāhumu llāhu min ḥaythu lam yaḥtasibū wa-qadhafa fī qulūbihimu l-ruʿba yukhribūna buyūtahum bi-aydīhim wa-aydī l-mu'minīna fa-iʿtabirū yā ulī l-abṣār

"It is He who drove out those of the People of the Book who disbelieved, from their homes at the first gathering. You did not think they would leave, and they thought their fortresses would defend them from Allah. But Allah came upon them from where they did not reckon, and He cast terror into their hearts. They wreck their houses with their own hands and the hands of the believers. So take heed, you who have sight."

- Qur'an 59:2, Uthmani text

مَا قَطَعْتُم مِّن لِّينَةٍ أَوْ تَرَكْتُمُوهَا قَآئِمَةً عَلَىٰٓ أُصُولِهَا فَبِإِذْنِ ٱللَّهِ وَلِيُخْزِىَ ٱلْفَٰسِقِينَ

mā qaṭaʿtum min līnatin aw taraktumūhā qā'imatan ʿalā uṣūlihā fa-bi-idhni llāhi wa-li-yukhziya l-fāsiqīn

"Whatever palm trees you cut down, or left standing on their roots, was by Allah's permission, and to disgrace the defiant."

- Qur'an 59:5, Uthmani text

Commentary

The Sources for the Nadir

Shia line: al-Qummi's Tafsir, carried in Maktabat al-Marja', p.554, with the negotiation rounds

Sunni line: the occasions-of-revelation report, carried at the same page

Tafsir line: al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan, vol.28, p.23, on the surrender terms. al-Suyuti, al-Durr al-Manthur, vol.2, p.266, carrying the blood-money occasion

Scripture: Qur'an 59:2 and 59:5, Uthmani text

Dating note: the occasion report dates Quraysh's letter after Badr; the series' chronology, following al-Waqidi's list, puts the expulsion in Rabi' al-Awwal of the fourth year, in the months after Uhud

The two community lines agree on the frame, a plot, a warning from within, a siege, and exile by terms. Al-Qummi's version preserves the negotiation in three rounds, and al-Tabari fixes what the terms were, lives and families secured, camel-borne wealth, houses and lands forfeit. The letter's date is one recorded split, and the final terms are another, the camel-load al-Tabari fixes against the carrying of nothing in al-Qummi's last round. Both splits stay in view.

The Warning and the Siege

Cause: Quraysh's letter, and the Nadir's plan of treachery

The warning: a Nadir woman told her Muslim brother, who reached the Prophet ﷺ in time

The siege: squadrons against the forts, fought until the tribe came down to exile

Terms: lives, women, and children secure, what the camels could carry except arms, houses and lands forfeit

Destination: Khaybar for some, Syria for others

The record is rich in failure points for the plot. A tribe inside the city conspired to kill the Prophet ﷺ at a blood-money visit, was warned off by heaven and by its own blood, and still bargained after the swords were out. The negotiation, in al-Qummi's line, ran three rounds, and the terms hardened each time.

The tribe asked to leave with its wealth, was offered what the camels carry, refused, asked again for the camel-load, and was answered with going out carrying nothing. The tribe pulled down its own houses for the timber, and the Qur'an set even that detail into the chapter.

What the Exile Left Behind

Land: the Nadir groves and forts passed to the state

Precedent: the second of Medina's three Jewish clans removed by treaty terms, after the Qaynuqa

Verse: a chapter of the Qur'an opened by this event inside Medina

The Nadir lands and forts passed to the state, and the chapter the event earned carries its own name for spoils that come without battle, fay'. The strategic outcome was the closing of the internal front before the external war resumed. The tactical outcome was a siege won without storming the walls.

Was the Expedition Against the Nadir Justified

The cause was attempted murder. A treaty tribe inside the city planned to kill the head of the state, at the urging of an enemy that had beaten the state's army at Uhud, on the chronology this series follows. The plot failed by a warning from heaven and by a conscience inside the plotters' own family.

The warning was acted on without arrests. The tribe was besieged and negotiated with through three rounds, and the terms hardened each time it bargained. On al-Tabari's terms it walked out with its lives, its families, and what its camels could carry, minus the arms. On al-Qummi's final round it went out carrying nothing, and that split stands as the record leaves it.

The palm cutting the Qur'an itself addressed, by Allah's permission, kept the pressure on the forts while the gates stayed shut. The execution clause in the final round was a term of the surrender, and no execution under it appears in the record.

The expulsion could have been prevented. The prevention was in the hands of the plotters twice over, in the plot that was never forced on them, and in the first two terms they refused. The Prophet's ﷺ own turning back on the warning day, before any sword moved, is the record of a commander who gave the tribe its last peaceful exit.
