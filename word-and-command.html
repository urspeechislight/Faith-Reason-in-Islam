A Shia Answer to the Logos Argument

If the Word Were Eternal, It Would Be a Second God

The Claim

Christians sometimes argue that the Qur'an itself points toward the doctrine of the divine Word. The Qur'an calls Jesus ﵇ "a Word from Him" and "His Word which He cast into Mary" (Qur'an 3:45, 4:171). Elsewhere Allah creates by saying "Be, and it is" (Qur'an 2:117, 36:82). The argument joins the two. Jesus is God's Word, God creates through His Word, and Jesus therefore shares in the divine act of creation.

A second claim comes from Qur'an 7:54, "To Him belong the creation and the command." If creation is one thing and God's command is another, the argument runs, the command must stand outside the created order. Put together, the two claims begin to look like the Christian Logos, an eternal Word belonging to God and active in the making of all things.

This reading rests on four assumptions the Qur'an does not state. It assumes the "Word" given to Jesus ﵇ is the same thing as the creative "Be." It assumes the "Be" is an entity or instrument God uses. It assumes that separating the command from creation in a verse makes the command an eternal being. And it assumes that "a Word from Him" places Jesus ﵇ inside the divine essence. The title in fact describes how he was created.

The Imami sources answer each assumption. Their starting point is simple. God's speech is not a second eternal reality alongside Him. Speaking, willing, and commanding belong to His acts. Allah existed before any act of speaking, and when He speaks, the speech is something He does. This is why the Commander of the Faithful ﵇ is reported to have said that God's speech is an act He brings about, and that were it eternal, "it would be a second god."

That distinction changes the whole question. When the Qur'an says Allah says to a thing "Be, and it is," the Imams ﵈ describe creation through God's will. He needs no tongue, no sound, and no go-between. He wills the thing, and it comes to be.

Jesus ﵇ therefore does not stand on the side of the one who says "Be." He stands on the side of the thing brought into being by it. The Qur'an says this itself by comparing him with Adam ﵇.

إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ ءَادَمَ ۖ خَلَقَهُۥ مِن تُرَابٍۢ ثُمَّ قَالَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Inna mathala ʿĪsā ʿinda Allāhi ka-mathali Ādama, khalaqahu min turābin thumma qāla lahu kun fa-yakūn.

"Jesus is like Adam before Allah. He created him from dust, then said to him, 'Be,' and he was."

- Qur'an 3:59

Adam ﵇ is not an eternal Word because God said "Be" to him. Neither is Jesus ﵇. The comparison explains the miracle of the birth. The God who created Adam ﵇ without a father or a mother could create Jesus ﵇ without a father.

The Qur'an does call Jesus ﵇ God's Word. The real question is what the title means. On that question the Qur'an, the hadith of the Imams ﵈, and the early Imami theologians agree. Jesus ﵇ is a creature brought into being by God's command. No reading in the tradition makes him an eternal command that became a creature.

One boundary belongs at the start, for readers who accept none of these authorities. The argument runs on the school's own sources, and it carries weight for those who accept those sources as authoritative.

1. What Christianity Means by the Word

The doctrine has real holders, and they fixed its wording over three centuries. This section quotes them in their own words. The question throughout is whether the Qur'an teaches anything like their doctrine when it calls Jesus ﵇ "a Word from Him."

The Word of the Prologue

Source: The Gospel according to John 1:1-3

Editions: Elzevir Textus Receptus (1624), with the King James Version

Claim: the Word was in the beginning, was with God, was God, and all things were made through him

εν αρχη ην ο λογος και ο λογος ην προς τον θεον και θεος ην ο λογος. ουτος ην εν αρχη προς τον θεον. παντα δι αυτου εγενετο και χωρις αυτου εγενετο ουδε εν ο γεγονεν.

En archē ēn ho logos, kai ho logos ēn pros ton theon, kai theos ēn ho logos. Houtos ēn en archē pros ton theon. Panta di' autou egeneto, kai chōris autou egeneto oude hen ho gegonen.

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made."

- John 1:1-3, in the Elzevir Textus Receptus (1624), with the King James Version

The prologue states the claim at its strongest. The Word existed before all things, the Word was God, and all things were made through him. Justin Martyr (d. c. 165) had already taught the same shape a century before the creed, a Word begotten from the Father before all creatures, present as "a certain rational power" at the making of the world.

"God begot before all creatures a Beginning, [who was] a certain rational power [proceeding] from Himself, who is called by the Holy Spirit, now the Glory of the Lord, now the Son, again Wisdom, again an Angel, then God, and then Lord and Logos... just as we see happening among ourselves. For when we give out some word, we beget the word; yet not by abscission, so as to lessen the word [which remains] in us, when we give it out."

- Justin Martyr, Dialogue with Trypho, chapter 61

A generation before Nicaea, Origen (d. c. 254) had already stated the participation claim in its clearest form, that all things were made by God through Christ.

"All souls and all rational natures, whether holy or wicked, were formed or created... because all things were made by God through Christ, as John teaches in a general way in his Gospel, saying, In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by Him, and without Him was nothing made."

- Origen, On First Principles, book I, ch. 7, §1

The council of Nicaea in 325 then fixed the doctrine in law.

"And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only begotten of the Father, that is, of the substance of the Father, God of God, light of light, true God of true God, begotten not made, of the same substance with the Father, through whom all things were made both in heaven and on earth; who for us men and our salvation descended, was incarnate, and was made man, suffered and rose again the third day, ascended into heaven and cometh to judge the living and the dead."

- The creed of the Council of Nicaea, 325

The creed carries the creative clause of the prologue into law. The Son is "true God from true God, begotten not made, of one substance with the Father, through whom all things were made." Above everything else, the church holds one boundary. The Word is eternal, and the Word is begotten, never made. Everything the Imami sources say sits on the other side of that boundary, and section 8 tests each inference it requires.

2. God's Word Is an Act, Not a Second Eternal Reality

One distinction settles the whole question, and it comes from theology before any dictionary. Imami theology divides the attributes of God into two groups. Knowledge and power belong to God's essence, and God has always been knowing and powerful. Speaking, willing, and commanding belong to His acts, and God performs them when He performs them. The hadith of the Imams ﵈ state the division plainly.

Allah Was, and Was Not a Speaker

Source: al-Saduq (d. 381), Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 30, the written answer of Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ to 'Abd al-Rahim al-Qasir, p. 227

Claim: the attributes of speech arise when God acts, and God was before any of them

فإن القرآن كلام الله محدث غير مخلوق وغير أزلي مع الله تعالى ذكره ، وتعالى عن ذلك علوا كبيرا ، كان الله ﷿ ولا شئ غير الله معروف ولا مجهول ، كان ﷿ ولا متكلم ولا مريد ولا متحرك ولا فاعل ﷿ ربنا ،

فجميع هذه الصفات محدثة عند حدوث الفعل منه

"The Qur'an is the speech of God, originated, never fabricated, and never eternal alongside God, who is far above such a claim."

"God existed when nothing else was known or unknown."

"He existed when there was no speaker, no willer, no mover, and no doer besides our Lord. All these attributes are originated upon the origination of the act from Him."

- al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 30, p. 227

Three questions sit inside this report, and separating them removes its apparent puzzle. Did Allah originate the speech? Is the speech eternal with Allah? And why does the report still withhold the word makhlugh from it?

The first two answers are stated in the report itself. The speech is muhdath, brought about after it was not, and the Arabic says plainly that it is not eternal alongside Allah. Allah exists, Allah acts, and the speech occurs. The act is from Him, so the speech depends on Allah as its source. The dependency runs one way. Speech becomes existent through Allah, and Allah never becomes existent through speech.

The third question turns on a word, and al-Saduq's own commentary fixes the sense. The Arabic phrase ghayr makhlugh is literally "not created," and an English ear hears "uncreated," which in theological English means eternal and independent. That is precisely the meaning the report denies in its next breath. Makhlugh here carries the older sense of the root, speech fabricated or made up, and the report is denying that the Qur'an is a fabrication. Al-Saduq says so directly in the commentary he wrote immediately after transmitting this report.

إنما امتنعنا من إطلاق المخلوق عليه لان المخلوق في اللغة قد يكون مكذوبا ، ويقال : كلام مخلوق أي مكذوب قال الله ﵎ : " إنما تعبدون من دون الله أوثانا وتخلقون إفكا " أي كذبا ،

وقال ﷿ حكاية عن منكري التوحيد : " ما سمعنا بهذا في الملة الآخرة إن هذا إلا اختلاق " أي افتعال وكذب ، فمن زعم أن القرآن مخلوق بمعنى أنه مكذوب فقد كذب ،

ومن قال : إنه غير مخلوق بمعنى أنه غير مكذوب فقد صدق وقال الحق والصواب ، ومن زعم أنه غير مخلوق بمعنى أنه غير محدث وغير منزل وغير محفوظ ، فقد أخطأ وقال غير الحق والصواب .

وقد أجمع أهل الاسلام على أن القرآن كلام الله ﷿ على الحقيقة دون المجاز ، وأن من قال غير ذلك فقد قال منكرا وزورا ، ووجدنا القرآن مفصلا وموصلا ، وبعضه غير بعض ، وبعضه قبل بعض ،

كالناسخ التي يتأخر عن المنسوخ ، فلو لم يكن ما هذه صفته حادثا بطلت الدلالة

"We avoid calling it created because the word created can mean fabricated in Arabic. People say, 'Fabricated speech,' meaning false speech. God says, 'You worship idols apart from God and invent lies.' This means falsehood. God also reports the words of those who deny divine unity, 'We never heard of this in the later religion. This is only an invention.' This means fabrication and falsehood."

"Whoever says the Qur'an is created, meaning that it is false, has lied. Whoever says it is uncreated, meaning that it is true, has spoken correctly. Whoever says it is uncreated, meaning that it is unoriginated, unsent down, and unpreserved, has erred."

"The Muslims agree that the Qur'an is truly God's speech, and whoever says otherwise has spoken denial and falsehood. We find that the Qur'an has parts that come before others, as the abrogating verse comes after the abrogated verse. Were a thing with these qualities not originated, the proof would fail."

- al-Saduq's commentary on bab 30, preserved in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 89, p. 121

The distinction explains the startling wording above, that Allah was, and was not a speaker. The report does not mean Allah lacked the power of speech. The power belongs to His eternal perfection. The descriptions of speaker and willer attach to acts, and the acts occur when He performs them. Allah eternally knows, while the statement addressed to Moses had to wait for Moses to exist, because an address needs its addressee. A perfection can be eternal while its particular effectuation occurs in time. Nothing changes in the Agent, and an effect of His agency comes about.

One warning belongs here, because this report is sometimes quoted as proof that God's Word is uncreated and eternal. The same sentence calls the speech originated and not eternal with Allah, and al-Saduq's commentary rules out the unoriginated reading by name. The report draws the line the whole article turns on. God's speech is genuinely His, it comes from Him, and it is no fabrication, yet it is originated and not eternal with Him. Al-Saduq set the same division in his own creed.

وليست الإرادة والمشية والرضا والغضب وما يشبه ذلك من صفات الأفعال بمثابة صفات الذات ، لأنه لا يجوز أن يقال : لم يزل الله مريدا شائيا كما يجوز أن يقال : لم يزل الله قادرا عالما

"Will, choice, pleasure, anger, and similar things are attributes of action. They differ from the essential attributes. One cannot say that God has always willed and chosen, as one may say that God has always been powerful and knowing."

- al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 148

Three early authorities then sealed this position as the school's teaching. Al-Mufid (d. 413) declared it the consensus of the Imamis. Al-Tusi (d. 460) stated it in one line while ruling on oaths.

وأقول : إن كلام الله تعالى محدث وبذلك جاءت الآثار عن آل محمد - ﵈ - وعليه إجماع الإمامية والمعتزلة بأسرها والمرجئة إلا من شذ عنها

"I say that the speech of God is originated, and by this the reports from the family of Muhammad came. All the Imamiyyah, all the Mu'tazilah, and all but a few Murji'ah agree with it."

- al-Mufid, Awa'il al-Maqalat, qawl 19, p. 53

دليلنا : ما تقدم أن اليمين بغير الله لا ينعقد ، وكلام الله غير الله ولا هو صفة من صفاته الذاتية

"Our proof is what came before. An oath by anything other than God does not bind. God's speech is other than God. It is not one of His essential attributes."

- al-Tusi, al-Khilaf, vol. 6, p. 119

The Will Was Created First

Source: al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 11 and bab 55, pp. 148, 338

Claim: the will through which God creates is itself created before the things, then made the means of their making

خلق الله المشية بنفسها ، ثم خلق الأشياء بالمشية

"God created will by itself. Then He created things through will."

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 11, p. 148

المشية والإرادة من صفات الأفعال ، فمن زعم أن الله تعالى لم يزل مريدا شائيا فليس بموحد

"Will and choice are attributes of action. Whoever claims that God has always willed and chosen does not affirm divine unity."

- Imam al-Rida ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 55, p. 338

This matters directly for the Christian argument. In this school, whatever stands between God and the creatures belongs with the creatures. The instrument of creation is therefore a creature, and the first of God's instruments serves the making without sharing in it. The Imam ﵇ goes further. Whoever claims that God's willing never began, he says, denies divine unity. The next question is what the creative "Be!" actually is.

If He Was Not Eternally Willing, What Was He?

Source: the tawqi' of Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, bab 30; Jabir's report from Imam al-Baqir ﵇, relayed in al-Muhsini, Sirat al-Haqq, vol. 2, p. 121

Claim: God's essence was eternally complete in knowledge, truth, and life, while willing and speaking are acts that arise with their objects

The report above says Allah was, and was not a speaker and not a willer. The question presses at once. Was God idle before creation? Was something absent that creation later supplied? The school's answer divides what God is from what God does, and Jabir's report from Imam al-Baqir ﵇ states the eternal side in four words of negation.

إنّ الله ﵎ كان ولا شيء غيره ، نوراً لا ظلام فيه ، وصادقاً لا كذب فيه ، وعالماً لا جهل فيه ، وحيّاً لا موت فيه ، وكذلك هو اليوم ، وكذلك لا يزال أبداً

"Allah existed, and nothing else existed with Him. He was Light, with no darkness in Him. He was Truth, with no falsehood in Him. He was Knowing, with no ignorance in Him. He was Living, with no death in Him. So He is today, and so He remains forever."

- Imam al-Baqir ﵇, from Jabir, relayed in al-Muhsini, Sirat al-Haqq, vol. 2, p. 121

That is what was. Light, truth, knowledge, and life, and these belong to the essence. Nothing was missing, because the perfections named are knowledge and life, and God's knowledge needs no world to be complete. He eternally knew everything He would create, including the moment of its creating. The knowing is eternal. The report places it on the eternal side of the line, where the willing is never placed.

Willing, in the usage of these reports, is the act of bringing a particular thing about. An act of will has an object, and before creatures there was no object, so the name "willer" did not yet describe an act performed. The capacity to will never began. The willing of this world began when this world was brought about. The case is the same as forgiver. The mercy is eternal, while the act of forgiving a particular servant waits for the servant to exist. A name tied to an act needs its object, and God gains the name when the act occurs, without gaining the power.

Suppose the willing were eternal anyway, as an eternal actual willing for this world. Two consequences follow, and both fail. If an eternal actual will carries its effect with it, the world it wills is eternal with it, and the tawqi' stands against this directly, for Allah was, and nothing was with Him. The Qur'an itself gives the heavens and the earth a beginning. If instead the eternal will waited unexecuted, then something obstructed it, and a will eternally blocked is a deficiency. The school refuses both horns for God. The willing arises with its act, and the perfection of the Agent never alters.

So what was happening before creation? Nothing, and there was no time in which anything could happen. Time measures change, and change begins with the first change, the first act of God. The question about what God was doing before creation borrows a "before" that does not exist, since time itself is among the things that came to be. Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (d. 606), the other school's own exegete, states the point in his commentary. Allah was, and there was no time, and time arose with the first new thing (al-Razi, Mafatih al-Ghayb, on Qur'an 25:2).

This is the deepest answer the article gives to the Logos claim. The eternal side of the line holds the essence, light and truth and knowledge and life, and nothing else. Word, will, and command sit on the act side, arising with their objects. God needed no instrument to complete Him and no word to express a perfection He lacked. The eternal Word of the Christian argument is a resident of the wrong side of this line, and the line itself is drawn by the Imams ﵈ report by report.

3. What Does "Be!" Mean?

The Christian argument connects Jesus as God's Word with the Qur'anic formula of creation, "Be, and it is." The question is therefore what that formula means in Imami theology. The sources read it as a picture of God's power, and they deny that any spoken instrument stands inside the creating. Allah does not pronounce sounds. The "Be!" expresses the immediate reach of His will.

A Picture of Power, Not a Spoken Tool

Source: al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 1, p. 504, on Qur'an 2:117; al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol. 1, pp. 157-158

Claim: "Be!" is a likeness for the ease and instant effect of God's will, with no word or sound in the creating

إِنَّمَآ أَمْرُهُۥٓ إِذَآ أَرَادَ شَيْـًٔا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Innamā amruhu idhā arāda shay'an an yaqūla lahu kun fa-yakūn.

"His command, when He wills a thing, is only to say to it, 'Be,' and it is."

- Qur'an 36:82

بَدِيعُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ۖ وَإِذَا قَضَىٰٓ أَمْرًۭا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Badīʿu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ, wa-idhā qaḍā amran fa-innamā yaqūlu lahu kun fa-yakūn.

"He is the Originator of the heavens and the earth. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, 'Be,' and it is."

- Qur'an 2:117

ومعنى قوله : " فإنما يقول له كن فيكون " قيل فيه قولان : أحدهما - انه بمنزلة المثل ومعناه ان منزلة الفعل له في السهولة ،

وانتفاء التعذر كمنزلة ما يقال له كن فيكون كما يقال قال فلان برأسه كذا وقال بيده : إذا حرك رأسه وأومى بيده

"There are two views about the words, 'He only says to it, Be, and it is.' The first view treats them as an example. It means that His act is easy and free from difficulty. It resembles something that is told, 'Be,' and then exists. People similarly say that someone spoke with his head or hand when he moved his head or gestured with his hand."

- al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 1, p. 504

Al-Tusi compares the verse to an Arabic idiom. People say someone "spoke with his hand" when he gestures. The phrase pictures ease, and it posits no utterance. The hadith go further and deny the utterance outright.

الإرادة من الخلق الضمير وما يبدو لهم بعد ذلك من الفعل وأما من الله تعالى فإرادته إحداثه لا غير ذلك لأنه لا يروي ولا يهم ولا يتفكر ، وهذه الصفات منفية عنه وهي صفات الخلق ، فاراده الله ، الفعل ،

لا غير ذلك يقول له : كن فيكون بلا لفظ ولا نطق بلسان ولا همة ولا تفكر ولا كيف لذلك ، كما أنه لا كيف له

"A creature's will is an inward intention, followed by the action that appears to them. God's will is simply His bringing a thing into existence. He does not deliberate, become concerned, or think. These qualities belong to created beings."

"God's action is nothing beyond this. He says to it, 'Be,' and it comes to be, without a spoken word, speech from a tongue, inward concern, thought, or any manner of how. There is no manner of how for Him."

- al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol. 1, pp. 157-158

This is the heart of the matter. If, as these sources state, "Be!" is not a spoken instrument standing between God and creation, then there is no such verbal intermediary for Jesus ﵇ to be identified with. God wills, and the creature comes to be. Imam al-Kazim ﵇ drew exactly this line when a member of the school itself claimed God's speech is eternal.

قاتله الله ، أما علم أن الجسم محدود ، والكلام غير المتكلم معاذ الله وأبرء إلى الله من هذا القول ، لا جسم ولا صورة ولا تحديد ،

وكل شئ سواه مخلوق وإنما تكون الأشياء بإرادته ومشيته من غير كلام ولا تردد في نفس ، ولا نطق بلسان

"May God curse him. Does he not know that a body has limits, and that speech is other than its speaker? God forbid. I declare myself free before God from this statement. He has no body, form, or limit. Everything besides Him is created. Things come to be through His will and wish, without speech, inward hesitation, or speech from a tongue."

- Imam al-Kazim ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 100

The Imam ﵇ rejected the claim in the harshest words preserved from him. Things come to be by God's will and wish, without speech and without a tongue. Mulla Sadra (d. 1050) noticed something else in his commentary. This hadith, he wrote, answers a doubt raised by the very verse the Christian argument leans on, Qur'an 36:82 (Mulla Sadra, Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol. 3, p. 216).

God says and does not utter. His "Be!" strikes no sound and no call is heard. His speech is an act He brings about. And were it eternal, it would be a second god. The Christian assumption, granted in full, lands exactly where the Imam ﵇ said it lands. Within the oneness both faiths confess, it cannot stand.

4. Why Jesus Is Called a Word

With the framework in place, the titling verses become straightforward. The exegetes offer a short list of readings for "a Word from Him," and every reading makes the title describe something God did, not something God is.

"There are two views about the phrase 'a word from Him.' The first says it means the Messiah. He was called a word because God brought him into being through His word, without a father. God said, 'Be,' and he came to be. This is supported by the verse, 'The likeness of Jesus before God is like that of Adam. He created him.' Another view says he was called a word because God announced him in earlier scriptures. When an event happens as someone predicted, that person may say, 'My word has come true.'"

- al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 2, p. 294, on Qur'an 3:45

The early exegetes therefore give several related explanations of the title. The good news the angels brought, the command "Be!" by which he came to be without a father, and the prophecies of him in the earlier scriptures. Each reading names an effect of God's word. None makes Jesus ﵇ the eternal subject of that word. The phrase "cast into Mary" raises one more question, whether the Word existed before the casting. The first reading answers it. What the angels delivered to Mary was the message and the command directed at her, and a word addressed to a woman in time works in time. Al-Jawhari's dictionary had already answered the question in the same century the dispute began.

وعيسى ﵇ كلمة الله سبحانه ، لأنه لما انتفع به في الدين كما انتفع بكلامه سمى به . كما يقال : فلان سيف الله ، وأسد الله

Wa-ʿĪsā kalimat Allāh subḥānahu, li-annahu lammā intafaʿa bihi fī al-dīn kamā intafaʿa bi-kalāmihi sammā bihi. Kamā yuqāl, fulān sayf Allāh wa-asad Allāh.

"Jesus is called the Word of Allah, glorified is He, because people benefited from him in religion as they benefit from His speech. They therefore gave him this name. People also say, 'So-and-so is Allah's sword,' or, 'Allah's lion.'"

- al-Jawhari, al-Sihah, vol. 5, p. 304

Al-Jawhari explains the title in the grammar of praise. People benefit from Jesus ﵇ in religion as they benefit from God's speech, so he carries the name, exactly as a hero carries the name "sword of Allah." The sword of Allah is metal and the lion of Allah is an animal. The epithet itself does not establish a transfer of essence.

Some later Imami thinkers use stronger language, and the argument must meet them at full strength. Al-Tabataba'i (d. 1402) can describe Jesus ﵇ as himself the word of creation, because he came to be with the ordinary chain of causes absent. Qadi Sa'id al-Qummi (d. 1103) can describe him as the command itself, the command cast to Mary ﵍ (al-Tabataba'i, al-Mizan, vol. 3, p. 193; Qadi Sa'id, Sharh Tawhid al-Saduq, vol. 2, p. 733, both quoted in full under Further Evidence). Neither reading means an eternal divine person. Both mean that Jesus came directly through the creative command, without the ordinary chain of causes. Even the strongest Imami reading places Jesus within God's creative act, never within God's eternal essence, and the argument that follows survives after granting it.

Al-Balaghi (d. 1351) set the Quranic title against the prologue of John by name.

ومعنى كون المسيح كلمة الله هو كونه أثرا لقوله تعالى " كن " على خلاف العادة في تناسل البشر ، ولا تحسب أن معنى ذلك يوافق ما في كتب إلهام المتكلف فإن فيها ما نصه : وكان الكلمة الله " يو 1 : 1 "

"The Messiah is called God's word because he came through God's command, 'Be,' outside the usual way people have children. Do not think this agrees with what appears in the books of those who force meanings into scripture. One of those books says, 'The Word was God,' John 1.1."

- al-Balaghi, al-Huda ila Din al-Mustafa, vol. 1, p. 329

Being God's word means being the effect of His "Be." Al-Balaghi sets the two meanings side by side and refuses the comparison. The word that made Jesus ﵇ is the word that made light in the first chapter of the Torah, and the effect of a word is a creature.

5. Jesus and Adam

The Qur'an settles the direction of the title itself. It places Jesus ﵇ beside Adam ﵇, on the receiving side of the creative command. The Qur'an does not say Jesus performed the "Be." It says the "Be" was directed at him, exactly as it was directed at Adam ﵇.

"This verse came down about the delegation from Najran, including al-Aqib, al-Sayyid, and those with them. They asked, 'Have you ever seen a child born without a male?' - al-Tabrisi, Mukhtasar Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 1, p. 216

لما ضرب الله المسيح مثلا بآدم في قوله : (إن مثل عيسى عند الله كمثل آدم خلقه من تراب) أي : من قدر على أن ينشئ آدم من غير أب وأم ، قادر على إنشاء المسيح من غير أب

"When God compared the Messiah to Adam, He said, 'The likeness of Jesus before God is like that of Adam. He created him from dust.' The One who created Adam without a father or mother can create the Messiah without a father."

- al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 9, p. 89, on Qur'an 43:57

The reasoning is simple and decisive. The One who created Adam ﵇ without a father or a mother could create the Messiah without a father. The verse answers a miracle with a greater miracle, and it came down against the claim that the Messiah is the son of God. Extraordinary creation through God's immediate command displays God's power. It says nothing about the creature sharing God's nature, and the verse reads the likeness exactly that way, as a display of power answering a miracle. The Christian may answer that Adam was made through the Word while Jesus ﵇ is the Word. But that distinction is not supplied by Qur'an 3:59, 3:45, or 4:171. It must be imported from the Christian Logos framework, most explicitly from John 1. Section 8 tests this distinction at its own step.

What "from Him" means in Arabic is the next question, and the grammar answers it decisively.

6. What Does "From Him" Mean?

Kalima means a word, an utterance, a statement, an expression. Amr means a command or an affair. The dictionaries give the ordinary range of both words, and no entry carries an eternal divine person. A specialized theological sense can be asserted, but it must be shown from the Qur'anic context. It cannot be smuggled into the word itself, and al-Jawhari's gloss of the title has already shown how the language reads it. The full lexical file, with the entries of Ibn Manzur, Ibn Faris, and the poets, sits under Further Evidence below.

That leaves one pillar of the argument standing, the command of Qur'an 7:54.

7. Creation and Command

"To Him belong the creation and the command" (Qur'an 7:54). The argument reads the second noun as an uncreated something standing beside God, because the verse lists it separately from creation. The exegetes answered this reading seven centuries ago, and the answer is simple. Two ideas can be distinguished without one of them being eternal.

Making and Governing

Source: al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 4, p. 423, on Qur'an 7:54

Claim: the verse separates creation from command because the two words carry two meanings, and whoever infers an eternal speech from the listing has ignored its meaning

A-lā lahu al-khalqu wa-l-amru, tabāraka Allāhu Rabbu al-ʿālamīn.

"Creation and command belong to Him. Blessed is Allah, Lord of all worlds."

- Qur'an 7:54

وقوله " ألا له الخلق والامر " إنما فصل الخلق من الامر ، لان فائدتهما مختلفة " لان له الخلق " يفيد أن له الاختراع ،

" وله الامر " معناه له أن يأمر فيه بما أحب فأفاد الثاني ما لم يفده الأول . فمن استدل بذلك على أن كلام الله قديم ، فقد تجاهل

"In His words, 'Creation and command belong to Him alone,' He separates creation from command because each gives a different meaning. 'Creation belongs to Him' means that He brings things into existence. 'Command belongs to Him' means that He may command within it as He wills. The second statement gives a meaning that the first does not give. Anyone who uses this as proof that God's speech is eternal has ignored its meaning."

- al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 4, p. 423

Creation names the making of things. Command names God's governing of the things He made. Al-Tusi made this argument in the eleventh century, and Muslim theologians of his own day were already reading an eternal speech out of the second noun. He met the inference with the word "ignorance." The Qur'an breaks the same inference by itself.

إِنَّا كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقْنَٰهُ بِقَدَرٍۢ

وَمَآ أَمْرُنَآ إِلَّا وَٰحِدَةٌۭ كَلَمْحٍۭ بِٱلْبَصَرِ

"We created all things by decree. Our command is only one, like a glance of the eye."

- Qur'an 54:49-50

قوله تعالى : وما أمرنا إلا واحدة

"His words, 'Our command is only one,' mean that Our command is one word, swift in bringing things into being, like the blink of an eye. It means, 'Be.'"

- al-Turayhi, Majma' al-Bahrayn, vol. 3, p. 209

The command of Qur'an 54:50 is one word, swift "like a glance of the eye," and al-Turayhi's gloss names the word. It is "Be." In the reading of these exegetes, the command is the willing itself, an event and not a standing object. The hadith then remove speech from the command's working altogether.

إذا أراد الله شيئا كان كما أراد بأمره من غير نطق ، لا ملجأ لعباده مما قضى

"When God wills something, it happens as He wills through His command, without speech. His servants have no refuge from what He has decreed."

- the Commander of the Faithful ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 143

When God wills a thing, it happens as He wills, through His command, without speech. On the school's record, no Word-mediator stands inside the command. And everything the Qur'an ties to the command is a creature.

You did not know what the Book was or what faith was.' He replied, 'It is a creature of God, greater than Gabriel and Michael. It was with the Messenger of God, informing him and guiding him rightly.'"

- al-Kafi, relayed by al-Turayhi, Majma' al-Bahrayn, vol. 2, p. 355, on Qur'an 42:52

Something can be "from the command of my Lord" and still be created, and the Imami tradition says so explicitly. The spirit of Qur'an 42:52 is "a creature of God, greater than Gabriel and Michael." The command of 7:54 is God's making and governing, held in one hand. Being described as "of God's command" does not entail being uncreated, since the spirit of Qur'an 42:52 is explicitly called a creature. That removes the argument's last pillar, and the inference as a whole can now be weighed.

8. Testing the Logos Inference

The preceding sections establish the Qur'anic and Imami understanding of God's Word, His creative command, and the title given to Jesus ﵇. The Christian inference can now be tested at each step it requires.

Not every test names a fallacy. Some expose a missing premise, some test a referent, and one runs a reductio. In a live debate the question always comes first, and the label lands only if the answer fails.

The tests build on each other. If the opponent says the Word is the actual kun, tests three and six apply directly. If he says an eternal Logos stands behind the kun, the debate returns to tests one and eight. If he says the speech is the essence itself, test four applies.

Test One. Which Word Is the Middle Term?

Inference tested. From "Jesus is God's Word" and "God creates by His Word" to "Jesus is the Word through whom God creates."

Evidence established. Sections 4 and 6, and the lexical file under Further Evidence.

The argument needs its middle term, "Word," to carry one referent in both premises. The Qur'an calls Jesus ﵇ a kalima at Qur'an 3:45. The verse of the creating does not call the creative act a kalima at all. It says that when God wills a thing, He yaqulu, "says," to it kun, "Be."

If "Word" names the title given to Jesus ﵇ in the first premise and the act of divine saying in the second, the apparent three-term inference contains an ambiguous middle, in effect a fourth term. The ordinary lexical range does not establish the specialized Nicene meaning of either word. That meaning requires independent proof from context.

The question to ask. "Can you establish from the Qur'an that the kalima applied to Jesus ﵇ is identical with the creative saying represented by kun?"

What the Christian must establish is exactly that identity. The shared English word "Word" does not supply it.

Test Two. Why Would Function Establish Essence?

Inference tested. From "God creates through His Word" to "the Word is divine."

Evidence established. The mashiyya reports of section 2. The participation claim in Origen, quoted in section 1.

The statement "God creates through X" does not entail "X shares God's essence." A causal role does not settle an ontological nature. The inference needs a missing premise in the open.

Anything through which God creates must itself be uncreated and divine.

The Christian must establish that premise, and the Imami sources contain a direct counterexample to it. God creates things through the mashiyya, and the mashiyya itself is created first, as section 2 established. Mediating participation in creation therefore does not transmit divinity on these premises.

The question to ask. "What establishes that mediating creation transmits the divine essence?"

Test Three. Is the Saying Itself Eternal?

Inference tested. The identification of the Qur'anic saying with the eternal Logos.

Evidence established. Qur'an 36:82 and the act-classification of section 2.

The saying the argument cites is indexed to a particular act of willing and a particular thing brought into being. Qur'an 36:82 reads, "When He wills a thing, He says to it, Be." The Imami hadith classify both the willing and the speaking as acts that arise with the act.

The Christian therefore faces a choice between two claims. If he identifies the eternal Logos with this temporally indexed saying, the Imami sources explicitly classify that saying as originated. If he places an eternal Logos behind the temporal saying, the eternal entity is no longer something the wording of Qur'an 36:82 establishes. It has been supplied from Christian theology.

The question to ask. "Are you identifying the eternal Logos with this temporal act of saying, or placing an eternal Logos behind it?"

Test Four. Is God's Speech Literally His Essence?

Inference tested. The claim that God's speech is not separate from His essence, so an eternal speech needs no further proof.

Evidence established. The tawqi', the Jabir report, and al-Saduq's term-by-term commentary, all in section 2.

This test addresses a serious escape route, and it is treated seriously. The claim runs that God's speech is not separate from His essence, and that His speech therefore shares the eternity of the essence. Three different claims are often collapsed inside it.

  • God's essence is eternal.
  • God eternally possesses the power and the knowledge to speak.
  • A particular act of speech, such as the address to Moses or the command "Be," is eternal and identical with God's essence.

The first two claims do not establish the third.

The question to ask. "When you say God's speech is His essence, do you mean His eternal capacity to speak, His eternal knowledge of what He will say, or the actual speech that occurs?" Those three concepts are being treated as interchangeable, and the test refuses the interchange.

Part or Identity

The first precision concerns the word "part." Is speech a constituent part of the essence, or is it literally identical with the whole essence? If it is a part, a composition problem follows, since the essence would be made of parts. If it is literally identical with the essence, it is a whole and never a part, and the two ways of speaking must be kept apart. The opponent must choose one and hold it.

The Transitivity of Identity

Identity carries a formal test. Let E be God's essence. Let S1 be the statement addressed to Moses. Let S2 be the command "Be." If S1 is identical with E, and S2 is identical with E, then S1 is identical with S2, because identity is transitive. But the address to Moses and the command "Be" are different acts of speech. They differ in content, they differ in addressee, and they differ in occurrence. The opponent therefore has to explain what "identical with the essence" means for particular speeches that differ from each other.

A careful opponent will not claim that every temporal utterance is numerically identical with the essence. If he instead distinguishes an eternal internal speech from its temporal expressions, that move is acknowledged here and tested at its own step below.

Capacity Versus Act

Was God eternally capable of addressing Moses? The answer can be yes. Was the particular address to Moses eternally occurring before Moses existed? These are different questions, and the second forces the distinction the whole section turns on. Eternal capacity and eternal knowledge do not entail that every particular act of speech is eternal. The Imami classification established in section 2 says it directly. Knowledge and power belong to the essence, while willing and speaking are attributes of action that arise with the act. The Speaker may be eternal while the act of speaking is originated.

One answer is that the address to Moses was eternally identical with God's essence before Moses existed. The opponent must then explain how an eternal act is eternally directed toward an addressee who did not exist. If the answer is that the address began to occur, then the particular speech is originated and cannot be identical with a beginningless essence. The argument stays on the status of the particular speech act, and concedes that classical theology can distinguish extrinsic relations from intrinsic change.

The Internal-Speech Answer

The sophisticated answer deserves its own hearing. God has one eternal internal speech, identical with or inseparable from His essence, while particular words, commands, and addresses are temporal expressions of that eternal speech. This is stated fairly, and it is internally coherent as far as this article needs to judge.

The fork follows. Is the Qur'anic "Be" identical with that eternal internal speech? If it is not, then the formula "Be, and it is" does not itself establish the eternal Logos, and another premise has entered the argument, which returns the debate to tests one and eight. If it is, then the eternal internal speech carries the different contents, the different addressees, and the different occurrences that S1 and S2 displayed, and the transitivity problem returns inside it.

Knowledge Is Not Speech

One more pressure point belongs here. If speech is identical with the essence, and knowledge is also identical with the essence, then speech and knowledge are numerically identical with each other. The opponent must either accept that or call the distinction conceptual. If the distinction is conceptual, a question follows. Why is speech classified as an essential attribute at all, when the title "speaker" applies through the occurrence of speech, exactly as the hadith of section 2 grade it? The case made here does not settle the classical debate over divine simplicity. It needs only the narrow point. Identifying speech with essence does not prove the particular Qur'anic creative saying is eternal.

What the Terms of the Reports Already Rule Out

The hadith terminology bears on this test directly. The tawqi' of section 2 calls God's speech muhdath, originated, and adds ghayr azali ma'a Allah, not eternal with Allah. It withholds the word makhlugh, and al-Saduq's commentary explains that the withholding means "no fabrication," never "unoriginated." A report that calls the speech originated and not eternal with Allah cannot be quoted as teaching an uncaused, co-eternal Word. The escape route closes on the reports' own terms.

The Christian argument therefore needs more than the move from God's eternity to the eternity of His speech. It must establish that the particular creative saying of Qur'an 36:82 is an eternal divine reality identical with God's essence, and that Jesus ﵇ is identical with that reality. The final question sorts the claims. "Are you identifying Jesus ﵇ with God's eternal essence, with an eternal attribute of speech, with God's power to speak, or with the particular act described by 'Be'? Those are four different claims. Which one does the Qur'an establish?"

Test Five. Is the Addressed Command the Eternal Person?

Inference tested. The referent of the addressed command.

Evidence established. The speech-as-act reports of section 2 and section 3.

The "Be" of the passages is addressed to particular things as they come into existence. The act therefore has a temporal referent and a temporal object. Classical theology can hold that a timeless God gains new relations to temporal creatures without intrinsic change, so the pressure here is not about relations in general. The pressure is about the referent.

If the addressed act is itself the eternal Logos, it conflicts with the Imami classification of speech as an act arising with its object. If the eternal Logos is something distinct behind the addressed act, then the Qur'anic command and the Christian Logos have become two different things, and the argument has quietly changed its object.

The question to ask. "Is the addressed act itself the eternal Logos?"

Test Six. If Kun Itself Is Eternal

Inference tested. A conditional branch, applying only if the opponent claims the articulated kun is eternal.

Evidence established. Al-Himsi's sequence argument, quoted in full under Further Evidence.

This branch applies only on one claim, that the actual articulated kun is eternal. Kaf precedes nun, and ordered elements contain a before and an after. Al-Himsi's argument applies directly to any allegedly eternal articulated word.

Nicene Christianity does not identify the Logos with Arabic letters, and that concession is granted openly. But the concession carries a price. If the eternal Logos is neither the sound, nor the letters, nor the temporal saying of kun, the move from the Qur'anic formula to the Nicene Logos requires another premise. A non-articulated eternal Word is a specialized theological conception, and it must be independently demonstrated.

The question to ask. "If the Logos is none of the things the verse mentions, what establishes its connection to the verse?"

Test Seven. Does Immediate Creation Establish Divinity?

Inference tested. From "created immediately by God's command" to "divine."

Evidence established. Qur'an 3:59 and the exegetes of section 5.

Qur'an 3:59 presents Jesus ﵇ and Adam ﵇ together as recipients of the creative command. Adam's ﵇ immediate creation, without father or mother, does not make Adam ﵇ divine. Extraordinary creation through kun is therefore insufficient by itself to establish divinity.

The Christian can answer that Adam ﵇ was made through the Word while Jesus ﵇ is the Word. That answer is a legitimate part of Christian theology, and it is treated here as an answer, with the question that follows from it.

The question to ask. "Where do Qur'an 3:45, 3:59, or 4:171 establish the distinction between receiving the Word and being the Word?"

The Qur'an supplies immediate creation and the title kalima. Christian Logos theology supplies the further identification, most explicitly in John 1.

Test Eight. Is the Nicene Meaning Assumed Before It Is Proved?

Inference tested. The interpretive premise that defines Qur'anic kalima by prior Christian theology.

Evidence established. Sections 4 and 6, and the doctrine of section 1.

This test carries a scope condition. If the Christian claims only that the Qur'an can be harmonized with Logos theology, no fallacy is committed. The fallacy arises when he claims that the Qur'an independently witnesses to the Nicene Logos while reading kalima with the Johannine meaning already assigned.

The reasoning then runs in a circle. Define Qur'anic kalima as the eternal Logos, using Christian theology. Observe that the Qur'an calls Jesus ﵇ kalima. Conclude that the Qur'an teaches the eternal Logos. The disputed conclusion was built into the interpretive premise.

The Qur'anic passages under discussion do not supply the distinction between being created through the Word and being the eternal Word. Christian Logos theology supplies that distinction, most explicitly in John 1.

The question to ask. "Can you derive the Nicene meaning of kalima from the Qur'anic passages without first importing the Nicene meaning of Logos?"

Test Nine. Does a List Establish an Ontology?

Inference tested. From the noun pair of Qur'an 7:54 to a created and an uncreated category.

Evidence established. Al-Tusi on Qur'an 7:54 and the report on Qur'an 42:52, both in section 7.

From "creation and command are mentioned separately" it does not follow that creation is created and the command uncreated. This is the cleanest non sequitur in the chain. Al-Tusi reads the pair as the making of things and God's governing of them, and he met the eternal-speech inference from this verse with the word "ignorance."

The narrower counterexample settles the point. Something can be described as "from Our command" and still be explicitly identified as a creature, as in the report on Qur'an 42:52. The phrase "from the command" does not logically entail "uncreated."

The question to ask. "Why does a syntactic distinction entail an ontological division into created and uncreated?"

Test Ten. The Reductio and the Burden It Leaves

Inference tested. The eternality of God's speech as a distinct reality.

Evidence established. Khutba 186, quoted in section 3, and the act/essence distinction of section 2.

The Imami argument can be formalized in three lines. If God's speech were eternal as a distinct reality, it would constitute a second god. There is no second god. Therefore God's speech is not eternal as a distinct reality.

That is a valid modus tollens inside the Imami framework, supported by Nahj al-Balagha, khutba 186, and the act/essence distinction. The first premise is an Imami premise, and Christians do not accept it. Their answer holds that Father and Son are distinct persons sharing one divine essence, so an eternal Son is no second god.

That answer may function inside Nicene metaphysics, and it is treated here as coherent within its own framework. It also leaves a new burden. The passages offered as evidence say that God is one. They call Jesus ﵇ a messenger and a Word. They compare his creation with Adam's ﵇. They never present one essence in multiple persons as the explanation of any of those terms.

Two questions end the test. Can Nicene theology construct a coherent account of one essence and multiple persons? And do Qur'an 3:45, 3:59, 4:171, 7:54, and 36:82 establish that account? The case made here needs to win only the second.

Final Verdict

The Qur'an calls Jesus ﵇ the Word of God, and the Imami sources differ over the precise force of that title. Some understand it as the command through which he came to be. Some understand it as the good news concerning him. The later philosophical readings can describe him as the word or the command itself, because ordinary causes were absent from his making. The sources are remarkably consistent about the boundary. God's speech and willing belong to His acts. His word begins when He speaks, and it stands with the creatures. Jesus ﵇ bears the title as a creature brought immediately into being by the command, on the pattern of Adam ﵇. The command standing apart in the verse of the creation is God's governing of the things He made. The argument that makes the word an eternal instrument inverts every grading in the record. The Commander of the Faithful ﵇ named the doctrine of an eternal word for what it is. It is the doctrine of a second god. And Allah Most High knows best.
