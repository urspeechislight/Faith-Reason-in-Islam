A Shia Dossier of a Christian Claim

If the Word Were Eternal, It Would Be a Second God

A Christian catalogue argues that the Quran names Jesus the eternal Word by whom Allah creates, and that the command standing apart in the verse of the creation belongs to an uncreated principle beside Him. The lexicons, the hadith of the Imams ﵈, and the exegetes grade every limb of the claim, and the Commander of the Faithful ﵇ already fixed its premise as the doctrine of a second god. The Argument Under Examination

Background

A Christian polemic holds that the Quran itself supplies the doctrine of the Logos. Its catalogue runs four claims. The Quran calls Jesus a Word from Allah and His Word cast into Maryam ﵍ (Qur'an 3:45, 4:171); Allah creates by His word "Be! and it is" (Qur'an 2:117, 36:82); the word by which He creates is therefore an instrument He uses, and the messenger who is that word shares in the creating; and the verse of the creation separates the command from every created thing, "To Him belongs the creation and the command" (Qur'an 7:54), which the catalogue reads as an uncreated something standing beside Him. The claim deserves the strongest form. It is the oldest argument of its kind; Hisham b. al-Hakam, a partisan of the school itself, drew the inference from the divine speech in the lifetime of Imam al-Kazim ﵇, Muslim theologians of the other school were reading an eternal speech out of the verse of the creation within the same centuries, and the Imams ﵈ and their exegetes graded both moves then and there.

This dossier presents those gradings in order, from the lexicons, through the hadith of the Imams ﵈, to the exegetes and the creeds, and states the Christian doctrine it answers in the words of its own holders. One boundary must be named at the start, for the reader who accepts none of these authorities: the dossier argues inside the school, and its gradings bind those who hold the school's books true.

Premise 1: The lexicons fix the words before the theology begins.

Kalima is a unit of speech, a statement made by a speaker in time; amr is the command of a commander, an act of the one who commands, and an affair among affairs. The partitive "from" of "a word from Him" marks origin and kind, as the grammarians state, and never composition of an essence. No lexicon carries either word in the sense of an eternal divine person, and a doctrine may not rest on a sense the language does not hold.

Premise 2: The school grades the speech of Allah as an originated act.

The hadith of the Imams ﵈ state that Allah was, and was not a speaker; the attributes of speech arise when the act arises, and the will through which the "Be!" operates is itself created before the things and then made the means of their making. Al-Saduq (d. 381) fixed this in his creed, al-Mufid (d. 413) declared it the consensus of the Imamis, and al-Tusi (d. 460) stated it flatly: the speech of Allah is other than Allah and is not an attribute of His essence.

Premise 3: The Imams negated utterance, sound, and instrument from the creating.

The "Be!" of the Qur'an is, on the school's reading, a likeness for the instant efficacy of the will; the hadith state the thing comes to be without utterance, without a tongue, without a sound that resounds. The Commander of the Faithful ﵇ fixed the boundary of the doctrine in one sentence: the speech of Allah is an act He originates, and were it eternal it would be a second god. An instrument of creation, on these gradings, is by definition a creature; the first creature is a minister of the making, never a partner in it.

Premise 4: Jesus ﵇ bears the title as the effect of the word, on the pattern of Adam.

The exegetes close the meaning of "a word from Him" in a fixed set of readings: the good news the angels delivered, the creative command by which he came to be without a father, the prophecy of him in the earlier scriptures. The Qur'an itself pairs him with Adam ﵇, made without father or mother by the same "Be!", and the verse came down against the deification claim of the Christians of Najran. The hadith fix the parallel title "a spirit from Him" as a created spirit Allah placed in Adam ﵇ and Jesus ﵇.

Conclusion: The inference to a divine creative Word fails on every branch, and the school had condemned it in advance.

Premises 1 and 2 remove the word from the essence of Allah and place it among His acts. Premise 3 removes the instrument from the creating altogether, and makes any eternal word a second god by the Imams' ﵈ own formula. Premise 4 reads the title of Jesus ﵇ as the first effect of the command, on the pattern of Adam ﵇, and the command standing apart in the verse of the creation as His governing of the things He made. The Christian catalogue requires the opposite of each grading, and the strongest of its own scriptures for this purpose, the prologue of John, is the text al-Balaghi (d. 1351) already set against it by name.

1. The Words in the Lexicons

Contested words. kalima (a word), amr (a command, an affair), and the "from" of "a word from Him". The catalogue's case hangs on reading kalima as an eternal divine person, amr as an uncreated principle, and "from Him" as a share of the essence. The dictionaries fix the senses the language carried, their record stands independent of the dispute, and the poets show the words at work in the language the Qur'an speaks in.

The argument of the catalogue turns on three Arabic words, and the lexicons close their senses before the Qur'an is opened. Ibn Manzur (d. 711) sets the entries down, Ibn Faris (d. 395) reduces the roots, and al-Jawhari (d. 393) answers the catalogue's own question inside the dictionary itself, in the very century the dispute over the speech of Allah was burning in Baghdad.

The Word in the Dictionaries

Source: Ibn Manzur (d. 711), Lisan al-Arab, vol. 12, p. 523; Ibn Faris (d. 395), Maqayis al-Lugha, vol. 5, p. 131

Claim: kalima is a unit of speech, from a single letter to a whole ode; the entry carries no sense of a divine person

What Lisan al-Arab Records

Ibn Manzur opens the entry of the root with the plainest definition in the language.

ابن سيده : الكلام القَوْل ، معروف ، وقيل : الكلام ما كان مُكْتَفِياً بنفسه وهو الجملة ، والقول ما لم يكن مكتفياً بنفسه ، وهو الجُزْء من الجملة

Ibn Sīdah: al-kalām al-qawl, maʿrūf. It is said that kalām is what stands complete in itself, and that is the sentence, while qawl is what does not stand complete in itself, and that is the part of a sentence.

"Ibn Sidah said: Speech is a known word. Some say speech is a complete expression, which is a sentence. A saying is an incomplete expression, which forms part of a sentence."

- Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol. 12, p. 523

The entry fixes the singular under the plural discussion.

والكَلِمة : اللفظة ، حجازيةٌ ، وجمعها كَلِمٌ ، تذكر وتؤنث . يقال : هو الكَلِمُ وهي الكَلِمُ

Wa-l-kalima: al-lafẓa, ḥijāziyya, wa-jamʿuhā kalim, tudhakkar wa-tuʾannath. Yuqāl: huwa al-kalim wa-hiya al-kalim.

"Kalima means an uttered word. This is a Hijazi usage. Its plural is kalim. It may be masculine or feminine. One may use either gender."

- Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol. 12, p. 523
What Ibn Faris Reduces the Root To

Ibn Faris reduces every word of the root to two original meanings.

الكاف واللام والميم أصلان أحدهما يدل على نطق مفهم والآخر على جراح . فالأول الكلام . تقول كلمته أكلمه تكليما وهو كليمي إذا كلمك أو كلمته . ثم يتسعون فيسمون اللفظة الواحدة المفهمة كلمة والقصة كلمة والقصيدة بطولها كلمة

Al-kāf wa-l-lām wa-l-mīm aṣlān: aḥaduhumā yadullu ʿalā nuṭq mufhim wa-l-ākhar ʿalā jarāḥ. Fa-l-awwal al-kalām... wa-yusammūna al-lafẓa al-wāḥida al-mufhima kalima, wa-l-qaṣṣa kalima, wa-l-qaṣīda bi-ṭūlihā kalima.

"The letters kaf, lam, and mim have two root meanings. One refers to understandable speech. The other refers to wounds. The first is kalam, speech. You say, 'I spoke to him,' and 'he spoke to me.' A person is your kalim when he has spoken to you or you have spoken to him. The usage then expands. A single meaningful utterance is called a kalima. A story is called a kalima. A whole long poem is called a kalima."

- Ibn Faris, Maqayis al-Lugha, vol. 5, p. 131

So the dictionaries close kalima in the domain of speech, from one letter of the alphabet to an ode whole. The entry carries two root senses, speech and the wound, and it carries no sense of an eternal person anywhere in its length. A doctrine may not take the word out of its language by fiat.

Al-Jawhari's Gloss of the Title

Source: al-Jawhari (d. 393), al-Sihah, vol. 5, p. 304

Claim: Jesus ﵇ bears the title as an epithet of benefit, like "sword of Allah" and "lion of Allah"

The lexicographer was confronted with the very title the catalogue leans on, and he glossed it inside the entry of the root.

وعيسى ﵇ كلمة الله سبحانه ، لأنه لما انتفع به في الدين كما انتفع بكلامه سمى به . كما يقال : فلان سيف الله ، وأسد الله

Wa-ʿĪsā kalimat Allāh subḥānahu, li-annahu lammā intafaʿa bihi fī al-dīn kamā intafaʿa bi-kalāmihi sammā bihi. Kamā yuqāl: fulān sayf Allāh wa-asad Allāh.

"Jesus is called the Word of Allah, glorified is He, because people benefited from him in religion as they benefit from His speech. They therefore gave him this name. People also say, 'So-and-so is Allah's sword,' or, 'Allah's lion.'"

- al-Jawhari, al-Sihah, vol. 5, p. 304

The gloss answers the catalogue a thousand years in advance, and it answers in the register of epithets the Arabs already knew. The sword of Allah cuts and is metal; the lion of Allah tears and is an animal; the word of Allah benefits men in their religion and is a creature. Al-Jawhari fixes the title in the grammar of praise, and the grammar of praise never transfers an essence.

The Command in the Dictionaries

Source: Ibn Manzur (d. 711), Lisan al-Arab, vol. 4, pp. 26-27; Ibn Faris (d. 395), Maqayis al-Lugha, vol. 1, p. 139

Claim: amr is the directive of a commander and an affair among affairs; neither sense is a thing that subsists by itself

The Entry of the Root

Ibn Manzur opens the entry with the command and its reception.

أمر : الأَمْرُ : معروف ، نقيض النَّهْيِ . أَمَرَه به وأَمَرَه ؛ الأخيرة عن كراع ؛ وأَمره إِياه ، على حذف الحرف ، يَأْمُرُه أَمْراً وإِماراً فأْتَمَرَ أَي قَبِلَ أَمْرَه

Amr: al-amr maʿrūf, naqīḍ al-nahy. Amara bihi wa-amarahu... yaʾmuruhu amran wa-imāran fa-'tamara ayy qabila amrahu.

"Amr is a known word. It is the opposite of prohibition. You may say, 'He commanded him to do it.' The latter usage comes from Kura'. You may also say, 'He commanded him it,' with the preposition omitted. He commands him with an amr, and the person then accepts his command."

- Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol. 4, p. 26

The second sense follows on the next page.

والأَمْرُ : واحدُ الأُمُور ؛ يقال : أَمْرُ فلانٍ مستقيمٌ وأُمُورُه مستقيمةٌ . والأَمْرُ : الحادثة ، والجمع أُمورٌ ، لا يُكَسَّرُ على غير ذلك . وفي التنزيل العزيز : أَلا إِلى الله تصير الأُمورُ

Wa-l-amr: wāḥid al-umūr... wa-l-amr: al-ḥāditha, wa-l-jamʿ umūr... a-lā ilā Allāh taṣīr al-umūr.

"Amr also means one matter among many matters. People say, 'So-and-so's affair is sound,' and, 'His affairs are sound.' Amr also means an event. Its plural is umur. Allah says: 'To Allah all matters return.'"

- Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol. 4, p. 27
Ibn Faris on the Speech Act

Ibn Faris defines the command as an act of a speaker, and his definition is the whole argument in miniature.

الهمزة والميم والراء أصول خمسة الأمر من الأمور والأمر ضد النهي والأمر النماء والبركة بفتح الميم والمعلم والعجب . فأما الواحد من الأمور فقولهم هذا أمر رضيته وأمر لا أرضاه . وفي المثل أمر ما أتى بك . ومن ذلك في المثل لأمر ما يسود من يسود . والأمر الذي هو نقيض النهي قولك افعل كذا

Al-hamza wa-l-mīm wa-l-rāʾ uṣūl khamsa: al-amr min al-umūr, wa-l-amr ḍidd al-nahy... wa-l-amr alladhī huwa naqīḍ al-nahy qawluka ifʿal kadhā.

"The letters hamza, mim, and ra have five root meanings: a matter among matters, a command, growth and blessing, a landmark, and something astonishing. For a single matter, people say, 'This is a matter I accept.' A proverb says, 'Some matter brought you here.' Amr as the opposite of prohibition appears when you say, 'Do this.'"

- Ibn Faris, Maqayis al-Lugha, vol. 1, p. 139

The command is by lexical definition an utterance of a commander, "your saying, do this", issued and then obeyed or disobeyed. An amir holds the amr; the amr is his act upon hearers, and its other sense is an affair among the affairs of men. The noun pair of the verse of the creation sits in the dictionary between the issuing and the governing, and neither side of the entry is a self-subsisting principle.

The Poets Test the Meaning

Holders: Ru'ba ibn al-'Ajjaj (d. 145), Durayd ibn al-Simma (d. 8 AH)

Claim: kalima is audible, spoken utterance; amr is a directive obeyed or disobeyed

The lexicons cite their own witnesses, and the witnesses carry the words in the senses the dictionaries fixed.

لا يَسْمَعُ الرَّكْبُ به رَجْعَ الكِلَمْ

Lā yasmaʿu al-rukbu bihi rajʿa al-kalim.

"The riders hear no echo of words there."

- Ru'ba ibn al-'Ajjaj (d. 145), cited by Ibn Manzur in Lisan al-Arab, vol. 12, p. 523

Durayd ibn al-Simma, a poet of the Jahiliyya, the age before Islam, who died in the year 8 of the Hijra, mourns his brother and recalls a command the people ignored.

أمرتهم أمري بمنقطع اللوى * فلم يستبينوا النصح إلا ضحى الغد

فلما عصوني كنت منهم وقد أرى * غوايتهم أو أنني غير مهتد

Amartuhum amrī bi-munqaṭiʿi al-Liwā / fa-lam yastabīnū al-nuṣḥa illā ḍuḥā al-ghad. / Fa-lammā ʿaṣawnī kuntu minhum wa-qad arā / ghawāyatahum aw annanī ghayru muhtad.

"I gave them my command at Munqati al-Liwa. They saw the counsel clearly only at dawn of the next day."

"When they disobeyed me, I remained among them. I saw their error, or else I too was one without guidance."

- Durayd ibn al-Simma (d. 8 AH), preserved in al-Qubanchi, Sharh Risalat al-Huquq, vol. 2, p. 311

The kalima of Ru'ba is sound that returns off a hillside. The amr of Durayd is issued, heard, disobeyed, and mourned. Both poets used the words in the exact senses the lexicons record, in the century the Qur'an was speaking Arabic to Arabs, and neither usage carries a person or a principle. The words fixed, the question moves from the dictionary to the school's own grading of the speech of Allah.

The From of a Word From Him

Source: Abu Hayyan (d. 745), al-Bahr al-Muhit, vol. 4, p. 143, on Qur'an 4:171

Claim: the "from" of "a spirit from Him" marks the point of issuing, and a Christian partitive reading of it was raised and answered within the tradition

The Grammarian's Ruling

Abu Hayyan, the grammarian exegete of Andalus, rules on the particle at the end of the titling verse, and his ruling is the catalogue's grammar in reverse.

ومعنى روح منه أي : صادرة ، لأنه ذو روح وجد من غير جزء من ذي روح ، كالنطفة المنفصلة من الأب الحي ، وإنما اخترع اختراعا من عند اللّه وقدرته

"The phrase 'a spirit from Him' means one that came forth from Him. It has a spirit and came into being without coming from any part of a living being. It is like a drop of fluid separated from a living father. Allah created it directly through His power."

- Abu Hayyan, al-Bahr al-Muhit, vol. 4, p. 143

ومن هنا لابتداء الغاية ، وليست للتبعيض كما فهمه بعض النصارى فادعى أنّ عيسى جزء من اللّه تعالى ،

فرد عليه علي بن الحسين بن وافد المروزي حين استدل النصراني بأنّ في القرآن ما يشهد لمذهبه وهو قوله : وروح منه ،

فأجابه ابن وافد بقوله : وَسَخَّرَ لَكُمْ ما فِي السَّماواتِ وَما فِي الْأَرْضِ جَمِيعاً مِنْهُ . وقال : إن كان يجب بهذا أن يكون عيسى جزأ منه وجب أن يكون ما في السماوات وما في الأرض جزأ منه ،

فانقطع النصراني وأسلم

"Min here marks the starting point of origin. It does not mark a part, as some Christians understood it when they claimed that Jesus was a portion of Allah, exalted is He."

"Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn Wafid al-Marwazi answered a Christian who argued that the Qur'an itself witnessed for his belief in the phrase 'a spirit from Him.' Ibn Wafid replied with Allah's words, 'He has made subject to you all that is in the heavens and all that is on the earth, all from Him' (Qur'an 45:13), and said, 'If this requires Jesus to be a part of Him, then everything in the heavens and on earth must also be a part of Him.' The Christian had no answer and became Muslim."

- Abu Hayyan, al-Bahr al-Muhit, vol. 4, p. 143

The particle of the catalogue's title marks issuing, never partition, and the partitive reading was already argued and broken inside the tradition's own record. The lexicons close the words as speech, command, and origin, and the question now stands where the catalogue must win it or lose it: what the school itself grades the speech of Allah to be.

2. The Word Graded Originated

The dictionaries fixed the words as speech and command; the question of being is graded in the hadith of the Imams ﵈, and their gradings run one way. Al-Saduq (d. 381) compiled them in Kitab al-Tawhid, al-Mufid (d. 413) declared them the consensus of the school, and al-Tusi (d. 460) drew the line the catalogue cannot cross.

The Tawqi' That Fixed the Doctrine

Source: al-Saduq (d. 381), Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 30, the written answer of Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ to 'Abd al-Rahim al-Qasir, p. 227

Speaker: Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇

Claim: Allah was, and was not a speaker; the attributes of speech arise when the act arises

The Written Answer

A group in Kufa asked al-Sadiq ﵇ in writing what the school holds of the Qur'an, and his written answer reached al-Saduq's book.

فإن القرآن كلام الله محدث غير مخلوق وغير أزلي مع الله تعالى ذكره ، وتعالى عن ذلك علوا كبيرا ، كان الله ﷿ ولا شئ غير الله معروف ولا مجهول ، كان ﷿ ولا متكلم ولا مريد ولا متحرك ولا فاعل ﷿ ربنا ،

فجميع هذه الصفات محدثة عند حدوث الفعل منه

"The Qur'an is God's speech: originated, not created, and never eternal alongside God, who is far above such a claim."

"God existed when nothing else was known or unknown."

"He existed when there was no speaker, no willer, no mover, and no doer besides our Lord. All these attributes are originated upon the origination of the act from Him."

- al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 30, p. 227

The companion hadith of the same bab refuses both labels that the sects pressed on the question.

يا ابن رسول الله أخبرني عن القرآن أخالق أو مخلوق ؟ فقال : ليس بخالق ولا مخلوق ، ولكنه كلام الله ﷿

"Son of the Messenger of God, tell me about the Qur'an. Does it create, or is it created? The Imam replied, 'It neither creates nor is created. It is God's speech.'"

- Imam al-Rida ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 30, p. 223
Why the Label Was Refused

The refusal of the word "created" was lexical, and al-Saduq states the reason in his commentary on the bab.

إنما امتنعنا من إطلاق المخلوق عليه لان المخلوق في اللغة قد يكون مكذوبا ، ويقال : كلام مخلوق أي مكذوب قال الله ﵎ : " إنما تعبدون من دون الله أوثانا وتخلقون إفكا " أي كذبا ،

وقال ﷿ حكاية عن منكري التوحيد : " ما سمعنا بهذا في الملة الآخرة إن هذا إلا اختلاق " أي افتعال وكذب ، فمن زعم أن القرآن مخلوق بمعنى أنه مكذوب فقد كذب ،

ومن قال : إنه غير مخلوق بمعنى أنه غير مكذوب فقد صدق وقال الحق والصواب ، ومن زعم أنه غير مخلوق بمعنى أنه غير محدث وغير منزل وغير محفوظ ، فقد أخطأ وقال غير الحق والصواب .

وقد أجمع أهل الاسلام على أن القرآن كلام الله ﷿ على الحقيقة دون المجاز ، وأن من قال غير ذلك فقد قال منكرا وزورا ، ووجدنا القرآن مفصلا وموصلا ، وبعضه غير بعض ، وبعضه قبل بعض ،

كالناسخ التي يتأخر عن المنسوخ ، فلو لم يكن ما هذه صفته حادثا بطلت الدلالة

"We avoid calling it created because the word created can mean fabricated in Arabic. People say, 'Fabricated speech,' meaning false speech. God says, 'You worship idols apart from God and invent lies.' This means falsehood. God also reports the words of those who deny divine unity, 'We never heard of this in the later religion. This is only an invention.' This means fabrication and falsehood."

"Whoever says the Qur'an is created, meaning that it is false, has lied. Whoever says it is uncreated, meaning that it is true, has spoken correctly. Whoever says it is uncreated, meaning that it is unoriginated, unsent down, and unpreserved, has erred."

"The Muslims agree that the Qur'an is truly God's speech, and whoever says otherwise has spoken denial and falsehood. We find that the Qur'an has parts that come before others, as the abrogating verse comes after the abrogated verse. Were a thing with these qualities not originated, the proof would fail."

- al-Saduq's commentary on bab 30, preserved in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 89, p. 121

The tawqi', the Imam's signed written answer, fixes the school's formula in one breath: the speech of Allah is real and His, and it arises when the act arises. Allah was, and was not a speaker. Nicaea would later plant its flag on exactly this ground and confess the opposite of every clause, that the Word was in the beginning, was God, and was never without being spoken.

The Consensus and the Flat Statement

Source: al-Mufid (d. 413), Awa'il al-Maqalat, qawl 19; al-Tusi (d. 460), al-Khilaf, vol. 6, p. 119; al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 148

Claim: the speech of Allah is originated by consensus, is other than Allah, and is no attribute of His essence

Al-Mufid's Consensus

Al-Mufid, the teacher of al-Tusi and the systematizer of the school, converts the hadith into a consensus claim. His coalition names the Mu'tazila beside the Imamis, and the charge of import will be pressed; the grading is not theirs to give, for it rests on the tawqi' and the hadith of the Imams ﵈ recorded above, which al-Mufid cites as his proof.

وأقول : إن كلام الله تعالى محدث وبذلك جاءت الآثار عن آل محمد - ﵈ - وعليه إجماع الإمامية والمعتزلة بأسرها والمرجئة إلا من شذ عنها

"I say that the speech of God is originated, and by this the reports from the family of Muhammad came. All the Imamiyyah, all the Mu'tazilah, and all but a few Murji'ah agree with it."

- al-Mufid, Awa'il al-Maqalat, qawl 19, p. 53

وأقول : إن القرآن كلام الله ووحيه ، وأنه محدث كما وصفه الله تعالى وامنع من إطلاق القول عليه بأنه مخلوق

"I say that the Qur'an is God's speech and His revelation, and that it is originated, as God described it. I forbid calling it created."

- al-Mufid, Awa'il al-Maqalat, qawl 19, p. 54
Al-Tusi's Line

Al-Tusi, ruling on oaths in al-Khilaf, states the ontology in passing, and the aside is the sharpest sentence in the corpus.

دليلنا : ما تقدم أن اليمين بغير الله لا ينعقد ، وكلام الله غير الله ولا هو صفة من صفاته الذاتية

"Our proof is what came before: an oath by anything other than God does not bind. God's speech is other than God. It is not one of His essential attributes."

- al-Tusi, al-Khilaf, vol. 6, p. 119
The Taxonomy of the Creed

Al-Saduq's own creed classifies the speech-family of attributes, and the classification bars the word from the essence.

وليست الإرادة والمشية والرضا والغضب وما يشبه ذلك من صفات الأفعال بمثابة صفات الذات ، لأنه لا يجوز أن يقال : لم يزل الله مريدا شائيا كما يجوز أن يقال : لم يزل الله قادرا عالما

"Will, choice, pleasure, anger, and similar things are attributes of action. They differ from the essential attributes. One cannot say that God has always willed and chosen, as one may say that God has always been powerful and knowing."

- al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 148

The answer to the loaded question is therefore fixed on three authorities: the Word of Allah is neither His essence nor an attribute of His essence. It belongs to the attributes of action, and it arises with the act. The catalogue's first inference, that what creates must be divine, loses its premise before it starts, for the school grades the creative word on the side of the acts.

The Will Created First

Source: al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 11 and bab 55, pp. 147-148, 338, 433

Speakers: Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, Imam al-Rida ﵇

Claim: the mashiyya through which the Be! operates is itself created before the things, then made the means of their making

The Grading of the Mashiyya

The school pushed the grading one step behind the word, into the will itself.

خلق الله المشية بنفسها ، ثم خلق الأشياء بالمشية

"God created will by itself. Then He created things through will."

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 11, p. 148

المشية والإرادة من صفات الأفعال ، فمن زعم أن الله تعالى لم يزل مريدا شائيا فليس بموحد

"Will and choice are attributes of action. Whoever claims that God has always willed and chosen does not affirm divine unity."

- Imam al-Rida ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, bab 55, p. 338
The Dialogue With 'Imran

In the debate with 'Imran the Sabian, al-Rida ﵇ was asked how the unknowable God is known at all, and his answer sweeps even the means of knowing into the created.

فبأي شئ عرفناه ؟ قال ﵇ : بغيره ، قال : فأي شئ غيره ؟ قال الرضا ﵇ : مشيته واسمه وصنفه وما أشبه ذلك ، وكل ذلك محدث مخلوق مدبر

"How did we know Him? The Imam replied, 'Through what is other than Him.' The questioner asked, 'What is other than Him?' Al-Rida replied, 'His will, His name, His kind, and similar things. All of these are originated, created, and governed.'"

- Imam al-Rida ﵇ with 'Imran the Sabian, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 433

So whatever stands between Allah and the things stands on the side of the things. The will by which all is made was itself made first, and the Imam grades anyone who calls it eternal a denier of divine unity. The instrument of creation, in this school, is by definition a creature, and the first creature is a minister of the making, never a partner in it. How the Be! itself operates is graded next, and the Imams negated from it every trace of an instrument.

3. The Be! Without Utterance

The word graded an act, the question remains how the act works, and the catalogue stakes its case on the six letters of "Be! and it is". The exegetes read those letters as a likeness, the Imams ﵈ negated the utterance from them outright, and the Commander of the Faithful ﵇ fixed the price of the eternal-word reading in one sentence.

The Likeness Reading

Source: al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 1, p. 504, on Qur'an 2:117; al-Kashani, al-Safi, vol. 4, p. 262, on Qur'an 36:82; al-Mazandarani, Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol. 10, p. 492

Claim: the Be! is a likeness for the instant efficacy of the will, and the instrument reading is the named doubt the exegetes cut off

بَدِيعُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ۖ وَإِذَا قَضَىٰٓ أَمْرًۭا فَإِنَّمَا يَقُولُ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Badīʿu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ, wa-idhā qaḍā amran fa-innamā yaqūlu lahu kun fa-yakūn.

"He is the Originator of the heavens and the earth. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, 'Be,' and it is."

- Qur'an 2:117

Al-Tusi opens the meaning of the verse with the grammar of idiom.

ومعنى قوله : " فإنما يقول له كن فيكون " قيل فيه قولان : أحدهما - انه بمنزلة المثل ومعناه ان منزلة الفعل له في السهولة ،

وانتفاء التعذر كمنزلة ما يقال له كن فيكون كما يقال قال فلان برأسه كذا وقال بيده : إذا حرك رأسه وأومى بيده

"There are two views about the words, 'He only says to it, Be, and it is.' The first view treats them as an example. It means that His act is easy and free from difficulty. It resembles something that is told, 'Be,' and then exists. People similarly say that someone spoke with his head or hand when he moved his head or gestured with his hand."

- al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 1, p. 504

Al-Fayd al-Kashani names the instrument reading as the doubt itself, and cuts it at the root.

وهو تمثيل لتأثير قدرته في مراده بأمر المطاع للمطيع في حصول المأمور من غير امتناع وتوقف وافتقار إلى مزاولة عمل واستعمال آلة قطعا لمادة الشبهة

"This is an example of how His power brings about what He wills. It resembles the command of an obeyed ruler to one who obeys. The commanded thing comes about without refusal, delay, labor, or the use of an instrument. It removes the source of doubt."

- al-Kashani, al-Safi, vol. 4, p. 262

Al-Mazandarani's commentary on al-Kafi closes the same door in a phrase.

ان يقول له كن فيكون) كما نطق به القرآن الكريم وكلمة كن كناية عن التسخير بمجرّد الإرادة لا انّ هناك لفظاً وصوتاً

"He says to it, 'Be,' and it comes to be, as the Noble Qur'an states. The word 'Be' expresses creation through mere will. It does not mean that there is an actual word or sound."

- al-Mazandarani, Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol. 10, p. 492

And the hadith of the treasuries answers the question of means with a definition of the treasury itself.

لمّا صعد موسى عليه السّلام إلى الطور فناجى ربّه عزّ وجلّ قال : يا ربّ أرني خزائنك ! فقال : يا موسى ! انّما خزائني : إذا أردت شيئا أن أقول له « كن » فيكون

"When Moses climbed the Mount and spoke privately with his Lord, he said, 'My Lord, show me Your treasures.' He replied, 'Moses, My treasures are this: when I will something, I say to it, Be, and it comes to be.'"

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, the hadith of the treasuries, preserved in Qadi Sa'id al-Qummi, Sharh Tawhid al-Saduq, vol. 2, p. 455

So the exegetes read the Be! as the Qur'an's own picture of unresisted power, and the picture needs no tool, no work, and no sound. The likeness reading leaves nothing for an instrument to do, and the instrument is the catalogue's entire case.

The Imam's Refutation of the Utterance

Source: al-Kulayni (d. 329), al-Kafi, vol. 1, pp. 157-158, bab al-irada; al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 100

Speakers: Imam al-Kazim ﵇, against Hisham b. al-Hakam's claim that God's speech is uncreated

Claim: things come to be by will and mashiyya, without word, without tongue, without hesitation

إِنَّمَآ أَمْرُهُۥٓ إِذَآ أَرَادَ شَيْـًٔا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Innamā amruhu idhā arāda shay'an an yaqūla lahu kun fa-yakūn.

"His command, when He wills a thing, is only to say to it, 'Be,' and it is."

- Qur'an 36:82
The Hadith of the Will

The hadith of Safwan contrasts the willing of creatures with the willing of Allah.

الإرادة من الخلق الضمير وما يبدو لهم بعد ذلك من الفعل وأما من الله تعالى فإرادته إحداثه لا غير ذلك لأنه لا يروي ولا يهم ولا يتفكر ، وهذه الصفات منفية عنه وهي صفات الخلق ، فاراده الله ، الفعل ،

لا غير ذلك يقول له : كن فيكون بلا لفظ ولا نطق بلسان ولا همة ولا تفكر ولا كيف لذلك ، كما أنه لا كيف له

"A creature's will is an inward intention, followed by the action that appears to them. God's will is simply His bringing a thing into existence. He does not deliberate, become concerned, or think. These qualities belong to created beings."

"God's action is nothing beyond this. He says to it, 'Be,' and it comes to be, without a spoken word, speech from a tongue, inward concern, thought, or any manner of how. There is no manner of how for Him."

- al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol. 1, pp. 157-158
The Cursed Doctrine

Hisham b. al-Hakam reasoned from the uncreatedness of God's knowledge and power to the uncreatedness of His speech, and the Imam's answer is the harshest grading in the file.

قاتله الله ، أما علم أن الجسم محدود ، والكلام غير المتكلم معاذ الله وأبرء إلى الله من هذا القول ، لا جسم ولا صورة ولا تحديد ،

وكل شئ سواه مخلوق وإنما تكون الأشياء بإرادته ومشيته من غير كلام ولا تردد في نفس ، ولا نطق بلسان

"May God curse him. Does he not know that a body has limits, and that speech is other than its speaker? God forbid. I declare myself free before God from this statement. He has no body, form, or limit. Everything besides Him is created. Things come to be through His will and wish, without speech, inward hesitation, or speech from a tongue."

- Imam al-Kazim ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 100

Mulla Sadra, commenting on this hadith three centuries ago, names the exact inference it was uttered to kill.

وقوله انما يكون الأشياء بإرادته ومشيئته من غير كلام ، إشارة إلى دفع شبهة نشأت من قوله تعالى : إِنَّما أَمْرُهُ إِذا أَرادَ شَيْئاً أَنْ يَقُولَ لَهُ كُنْ فَيَكُونُ

"His statement that things come to be through His will and wish without speech answers a doubt raised by the verse, 'His command, when He wills a thing, is to say to it, Be, and it comes to be.'"

- Mulla Sadra, Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol. 3, p. 216

The Imam cursed the doctrine of uncreated speech in the strongest terms the record carries, and the philosopher's note shows the tradition knew precisely where that doctrine leads. The Christian catalogue reads 36:82 exactly as the hadith refuses to let it be read: it hears a word in the creating, and the Imam says the creating has no word in it. The boundary itself was drawn by the Commander of the Faithful ﵇.

The Boundary of the Doctrine

Source: Nahj al-Balagha, khutba 186 (compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi, d. 406; Bonyad edition, p. 310); al-Himsi al-Razi (d. 646), al-Munqadh min al-Taqlid, vol. 1, p. 218

Speaker: The Commander of the Faithful, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib ﵇

Status: attributed; the sermon is a compilation, and the boundary it fixes is carried independently by the hadith of the Imams ﵈

Claim: the speech of Allah is an act He originates, and were it eternal it would be a second god

The Sermon That Settles It

Khutba 186 negates every human mode from Allah's speaking and then states the price of eternity. The compilation of al-Sharif al-Radi carries no chains, and the dossier flags that honestly; the boundary the sermon draws is not its alone, for the tawqi' of al-Sadiq ﵇ had already graded every speech-attribute originated with the act, and Tafsir al-Qummi relays this sermon's formula under the verse of the Be! in the next exhibit below.

لَا بِلِسَانٍ وَلَهَوَاتٍ وَيَسْمَعُ لَا بِخُرُوقٍ وَأَدَوَاتٍ يَقُولُ وَلَا يَلْفِظُ وَيَحْفَظُ وَلَا يَتَحَفَّظُ وَيُرِيدُ وَلَا يُضْمِرُ يُحِبُّ وَيَرْضَى مِنْ غَيْرِ رِقَّةٍ وَيُبْغِضُ وَيَغْضَبُ مِنْ غَيْرِ مَشَقَّةٍ يَقُولُ لِمَا أَرَادَ كوَنْهَُ كُنْ فَيَكُونُ لَا بِصَوْتٍ يَقْرَعُ وَلَا بِنِدَاءٍ يُسْمَعُ وَإِنَّمَا كلَاَمهُُ - سبُحْاَنهَُ - فِعْلٌ مِنْهُ أنَشْأَهَُ وَمثَلَّهَُ لَمْ يَكُنْ مِنْ قَبْلِ ذَلِكَ كَائِناً وَلَوْ كَانَ قَدِيماً لَكَانَ إِلَهاً ثَانِياً

"He speaks without a tongue or palate. He hears without openings or instruments. He speaks without uttering words. He preserves without guarding Himself. He wills without holding an inner intention."

"He says to what He wills to come to be, 'Be,' and it comes to be. This is not a sound that strikes, nor a call that is heard. His speech, exalted is He, is an act that He brought into being and formed. It did not exist before that. If it had been eternal, it would have been a second god."

- Nahj al-Balagha, khutba 186, Bonyad edition, p. 310
The Letters Argument

The Imamī mutakallim al-Himsi al-Razi broke the eternality of the word on its own letters, in an argument aimed at the Karramiyya, who alone taught an eternal uttered word; no orthodox Christian claims a word of letters, and the argument is filed here for the boundary it polices, not as a portrait of Nicene doctrine.

ثم نقول لهم : كلمة « كن » مركبة من الكاف والنون ، والكاف مقدّم على النون ، فكيف يتصوّر في النون تقدّمه الكاف أن يكون قديما ؟ وكيف يمكن أن يكون الكاف قديما ؟ وقد تعقبه النون المحدث ،

وما تقدّمه إلّا بأقلّ قليل الأوقات

"Then we say to them: the word 'Be' consists of kaf and nun, and kaf comes before nun. How can nun, which follows kaf, be eternal? How can kaf be eternal when the created nun follows it only a very short time later?"

- al-Himsi al-Razi, al-Munqadh min al-Taqlid, vol. 1, p. 218

The sermon states the whole verdict of this dossier in one conditional: an eternal word is a second god, and a second god is the end of tawhid. The catalogue's reading of the Be! buys its instrument at exactly that price, and the school had priced it a thousand years before.

Where the Word Is Something Real

Source: al-Saduq, 'Uyun Akhbar al-Rida, vol. 1, p. 154 (the debate with 'Imran); al-Kashani, al-Safi, vol. 4, p. 262; Qadi Sa'id al-Qummi, Sharh al-Arba'in, p. 326

Speaker: Imam al-Rida ﵇ with 'Imran the Sabian

Claim: the letters are the first created thing, and "Be" is His making, never His tool; even the maximal reading leaves the word a creature

The Letters Doctrine

The strongest counterargument the other side can raise from the school's own books is the letters doctrine of the 'Imran debate, and it is stated here in full.

واعلم أن الابداع والمشيئة والإراده معناها واحد وأسماؤها ثلاثة وكان أول ابداعه وارادته ومشيته الحروف التي جعلها أصلا لكل شئ ودليلا على كل مدرك وفاصلا لكل مشكل وبتلك الحروف تفريق كل شئ من اسم حق وباطل أو فعل أو مفعول أو معنى أو غير معنى وعليها اجتمعت الأمور كلها ولم يجعل للحروف في ابداعه لها معنى غير أنفسها تتناهى ولا وجود لها لأنها مبدعة بالابداع والنور في هذا الموضع أول فعل الله الذي هو نور السماوات والأرض والحروف هي المفعول بذلك الفعل وهي الحروف التي عليها مدار الكلام والعبادات كلها من الله ﷿ علمها خلقه وهي ثلاثة وثلاثون حرفا فمنها ثمانية وعشرون حرفا تدل على لغات العربية ومن الثمانية والعشرين اثنان وعشرون حرفا تدل على لغات السريانية والعبرانية ومنها خمسه أحرف متحرفه في سائر اللغات .

من العجم والأقاليم واللغات كلها وهي خمسه أحرف تحرفت من الثمانية والعشرين حرفا من اللغات فصارت الحروف ثلاثة وثلاثين حرفا فاما الخمسة المختلفة فيتجحخ لا يجوز ذكرها أكثر مما ذكرناه ثم جعل الحروف بعد احصائها واحكام عدتها فعلا منه كقوله عز وجل : (كن فيكون) وكن منه صنع وما يكون به المصنوع فالخلق الأول من الله ﷿ الابداع لا وزن له ولا حركه ولا سمع ولا لون ولا حس والخلق الثاني الحروف لا وزن لها ولا لون وهي مسموعه موصوفه

"Know that origination, wish, and will have one meaning, though they have three names. The first thing He originated, willed, and wished was the letters. He made them a foundation for everything, a sign for every object of knowledge, and a means to distinguish every difficulty."

"Through these letters, every matter is divided into the name of truth and falsehood, an act and its object, meaning and what lacks meaning. All matters come together through them. When He originated the letters, He gave them no meaning beyond themselves. They have limits and no independent existence, because origination brought them into being."

"The letters are the objects produced by God's first act. He is the Light of the heavens and the earth. All speech and acts of worship depend on these letters. God taught them to His creation. They are thirty-three letters. Twenty-eight of them signify the languages of the Arabs, and twenty-two of those signify the languages of Syriac and Hebrew. Five letters change across the other foreign languages and regions, so that the letters became thirty-three."

"After counting them and setting their number precisely, He made the letters an act from Himself, as He says, 'Be,' and it comes to be. 'Be' is an act from Him, and through it the made thing comes to be. God's first creation is origination. It has no weight, movement, hearing, color, or sensation. The second creation is the letters. They have no weight or color, and they can be heard and described."

- Imam al-Rida ﵇ with 'Imran the Sabian, in al-Saduq, 'Uyun Akhbar al-Rida, vol. 1, p. 154

Tafsir al-Qummi reads the same formula under 36:82, and the later philosophers refine it away from utterance altogether.

كن منه صنع وما يكون به المصنوع وفي نهج البلاغة إنما كلامه سبحانه فعل منه أنشأه قال يقول ولا يلفظ ويريد ولا يضمر وقال يريد بلا همة

"'Be' is an act from Him, and through it the made thing comes to be. In Nahj al-Balagha it says: His speech, exalted is He, is an act that He brought into being. He speaks without uttering words. He wills without holding an inner intention. It also says that He wills without inward concern."

- Tafsir al-Qummi, as quoted in al-Kashani, al-Safi, vol. 4, p. 262

ومثّل ذلك بكلمة «كن» التي هي الأمر الإيجادي كما يدلّ عليه قوله عليه السّلام : و «كن» منه صنع» أي المبدأ المتوسط في جعل الأشياء وهي مركبة من الكاف والنون لا الملفوظ منهما بل الحقيقة الحرفيّة التي عند اللّه من الأمور الباقية التي لا تنفد

"He illustrated this through the word 'Be,' which is the command that brings things into existence. This is shown by his statement, 'Be is an act from Him.' It means the intermediate source through which things are made. It consists of kaf and nun, referring to their true reality with God among the lasting matters that never end, not to words spoken aloud."

- Qadi Sa'id al-Qummi, Sharh al-Arba'in, p. 326

So even the strand that grants the word real being grants it creaturehood. The letters are "the second creation"; the Be! is called His making, not His tool; and the philosophers who speak of a letter-reality say outright that it is not the spoken kaf and nun and locate it among the abiding affairs of the command, the same created realm this dossier meets again in the verse of the creation. On every reading the school offers, the word by which Allah creates stands with the creatures, and the one who is called that word stands with them too. What that means for Jesus ﵇ is the next question.

4. Jesus and the Word From Him

The word stands with the creatures, and the one the Qur'an calls a Word from Him stands in the same file. The exegetes close his title in a small set of readings, the Qur'an itself pairs him with Adam ﵇, and the Imams ﵈ gloss the parallel title "a spirit from Him" as a created spirit placed in them both.

The Verse and Its Readings

Verse: Qur'an 3:45 and 4:171

Source: al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 3, p. 400; al-Tabrisi (d. 548), Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 2, p. 294

Claim: the exegetes close the title in a fixed set of readings, and no reading is a divine hypostasis

إِذْ قَالَتِ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ يَٰمَرْيَمُ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُبَشِّرُكِ بِكَلِمَةٍۢ مِّنْهُ ٱسْمُهُ ٱلْمَسِيحُ عِيسَى ٱبْنُ مَرْيَمَ وَجِيهًۭا فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا وَٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَمِنَ ٱلْمُقَرَّبِينَ

Idh qālati al-malā'ikatu yā Maryamu inna Allāha yubashshiruki bi-kalimatin minhu, ismuhu al-Masīḥu ʿĪsā ibnu Maryama, wajīhan fī al-dunyā wa-l-ākhirati wa-mina al-muqarrabīn.

"When the angels said, 'Mary, Allah gives you good news of a word from Him. His name is the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary. He will be honored in this world and the next, and he will be among those brought near.'"

- Qur'an 3:45

إِنَّمَا ٱلْمَسِيحُ عِيسَى ٱبْنُ مَرْيَمَ رَسُولُ ٱللَّهِ وَكَلِمَتُهُۥٓ أَلْقَىٰهَآ إِلَىٰ مَرْيَمَ وَرُوحٌۭ مِّنْهُ ۖ فَـَٔامِنُوا۟ بِٱللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِۦ ۖ وَلَا تَقُولُوا۟ ثَلَٰثَةٌ ۚ ٱنتَهُوا۟ خَيْرًۭا لَّكُمْ ۚ إِنَّمَا ٱللَّهُ إِلَٰهٌۭ وَٰحِدٌۭ ۖ سُبْحَٰنَهُۥٓ أَن يَكُونَ لَهُۥ وَلَدٌۭ

Innamā al-Masīḥu ʿĪsā ibnu Maryama rasūlu Allāhi wa-kalimatuhu alqāhā ilā Maryama wa-rūḥun minhu, fa-āminū bi-llāhi wa-rusulihi, wa-lā taqūlū thalātha, intahū khayran lakum, innamā Allāhu ilāhun wāḥidun subḥānahu an yakūna lahu walad.

"The Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, is only Allah's messenger, His word which He delivered to Mary, and a spirit from Him. So believe in Allah and His messengers. Do not say, 'Three.' Stop. It is better for you. Allah is one God. Glory be to Him that He should have a child."

- Qur'an 4:171

Al-Tusi reads the titling of 4:171 first as news delivered, second as the command that made him.

وقوله : (كلمته ألقاها إلى مريم) فإنه يعني بالكلمة الرسالة التي امر الله ملائكته أن يأتي بها بشارة من الله (تعالى) لها التي ذكرت في قوله : " قالت الملائكة يا مريم ان الله يبشرك بكلمة منه " يعني برسالة منه وبشارة من عنده وقال قتادة والحسن : هو قوله : " كن فكان " واختار الطبري الأول

"His statement, 'His word which He cast to Mary,' means the message that God commanded His angels to bring her as good news from Him. This appears in His statement, 'The angels said, Mary, God gives you good news of a word from Him.' It means a message from Him and good news from His presence. Qatadah and al-Hasan said that it means His saying, 'Be,' and it came to be. Al-Tabari chose the first view."

- al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 3, p. 400, on Qur'an 4:171

Al-Tabrisi records the naming rationale that the second reading carries.

بكلمة منه ) فيه قولان أحدهما : إنه المسيح سماه كلمة ، عن ابن عباس وقتادة وجماعة من المفسرين . وإنما سمي بذلك لأنه كان بكلمة من الله من غير والد ،

وهو قوله : ( كن فيكون ) يدل عليه قوله : ( إن مثل عيسى عند الله كمثل آدم خلقه ) الآية . وقيل : سمي بذلك لأن الله بشر به في الكتب السالفة ،

كما يقول الذي يخبرنا بالأمر إذا خرج موافقا لأمره : قد جاء كلامي

"There are two views about the phrase 'a word from Him.' The first says it means the Messiah. He was called a word because God brought him into being through His word, without a father. God said, 'Be,' and he came to be. This is supported by the verse, 'The likeness of Jesus before God is like that of Adam. He created him.' Another view says he was called a word because God announced him in earlier scriptures. When an event happens as someone predicted, that person may say, 'My word has come true.'"

- al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 2, p. 294, on Qur'an 3:45

The strongest objection inside the readings themselves asks why the Qur'an gives Jesus ﵇ alone the possessive, His word, and al-Azhari's entry had already answered it in the previous century's dictionary: the Qur'an itself carries the title.

والقرآنُ كلَامُ اللَّه ، وكَلِمُ اللَّه ، وكَلمَاتُ اللَّه ، وكلمةُ اللَّهُ ، وهو كيفما تَصَرَّفَ ، مَتْلُوَّا ، ومَحْفُوظَاً ، ومَكْتُوباً

Wa-l-Qurʾānu kalāmu Allāh, wa-kalimu Allāh, wa-kalimātu Allāh, wa-kalimatu Allāh, wa-huwa kayfa mā taṣarrafa, matlūwwan wa-maḥfūẓan wa-maktūban.

"The Qur'an is God's speech, God's words, God's plural words, and God's word, however it occurs, recited, memorized, and written."

- al-Azhari (d. 370), Tahdhib al-Lugha, vol. 10, p. 147

The set is closed: the good news, the command "Be!", the prophecy in the earlier scriptures. Each reading makes the title an effect of God's word or a message about him, and none makes him the word's eternal subject. The possessive singles out no hypostasis, for the lexicographers apply the same possessive to the Qur'an in the reader's hands, and al-Jawhari's epithet grammar, the sword of Allah and the lion of Allah, governs both titles alike. The strongest of the three for the catalogue is the second, and the Qur'an itself teaches how to read the second, in the verse of Adam.

The Pattern of Adam

Verse: Qur'an 3:59

Source: al-Tabrisi, Mukhtasar Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 1, p. 216; Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 9, p. 89

Claim: the verse pairs Jesus with Adam to deflate the sonship claim, and the comparison runs in Adam's favor

إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ ءَادَمَ ۖ خَلَقَهُۥ مِن تُرَابٍۢ ثُمَّ قَالَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ

Inna mathala ʿĪsā ʿinda Allāhi ka-mathali Ādama, khalaqahu min turābin thumma qāla lahu kun fa-yakūn.

"Jesus is like Adam before Allah. He created him from dust, then said to him, 'Be,' and he was."

- Qur'an 3:59

The occasion of the verse is the deputation of Najran, and al-Tabrisi records it.

نزلت في وفد نجران ، العاقب والسيد ومن معهما ، قالوا : هل رأيت ولدا من غير ذكر ؟ فنزل : « إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسى عِنْدَ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ آدَمَ . . . » وذلك ردا على قولهم إن المسيح ابن اللّه .

"This verse came down about the delegation from Najran, including al-Aqib, al-Sayyid, and those with them. They asked, 'Have you ever seen a child born without a male?' Then God revealed, 'The likeness of Jesus before God is like that of Adam.' This answered their claim that the Messiah was the son of God."

- al-Tabrisi, Mukhtasar Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 1, p. 216

The same exegete states the direction of the comparison under 43:57.

لما ضرب الله المسيح مثلا بآدم في قوله : (إن مثل عيسى عند الله كمثل آدم خلقه من تراب) أي : من قدر على أن ينشئ آدم من غير أب وأم ، قادر على إنشاء المسيح من غير أب

"When God compared the Messiah to Adam, He said, 'The likeness of Jesus before God is like that of Adam. He created him from dust.' The One who created Adam without a father or mother can create the Messiah without a father."

- al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 9, p. 89, on Qur'an 43:57

The verse is deliberately deflationary. It was revealed against a sonship claim, and it answers the miracle of fatherless birth with the greater case of Adam, who had neither father nor mother, made of dust, by the same "Be!". If the word by which Jesus came to be made him divine, the word by which Adam came to be would make Adam divine first, and the Qur'an calls Adam a servant. The title runs with the parallel title "a spirit from Him", and the hadith fix that one without ambiguity.

The Spirit From Him

Source: al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol. 1, p. 181, bab al-ruh; al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 172

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, to Humran and to Abu Ja'far al-Asamm

Claim: "a spirit from Him" is a created spirit God placed in Adam ﵇ and Jesus ﵇; "of My spirit" means "of My power"

The bab of the spirit in al-Kafi carries the direct question on the titling phrase.

عدة من أصحابنا ، عن أحمد بن محمد بن عيسى ، عن ابن أبي عمير ، عن ابن أذينة ، عن الأحول قال : سألت أبا عبد الله ﵇ عن الروح التي في آدم ﵇ ،

قوله : " فإذا سويته ونفخت فيه من روحي " ؟ : قال : هذه روح مخلوقة والروح التي في عيسى مخلوقة .

2 - عدة من أصحابنا ، عن أحمد بن محمد بن عيسى ، عن الحجال ، عن ثعلبة ، عن حمران قال : سألت أبا عبد الله ﵇ عن قوله الله ﷿ : " وروح منه " قال : هي روح الله مخلوقة خلقها الله في آدم وعيسى

"Al-Ahwal said: I asked Abu Abd Allah, that is Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, about the spirit in Adam and the words, 'When I have formed him and breathed into him of My spirit.' The Imam replied, 'This spirit was created. The spirit in Jesus was also created.'"

"Humran said: I asked Abu Abd Allah about God's words, 'and a spirit from Him.' The Imam replied, 'It is God's created spirit. God created it in Adam and Jesus.'"

- al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol. 1, p. 181

Al-Saduq's Tawhid carries the same grading twice over.

عن أبي جعفر الأصم ، قال : سألت أبا جعفر ﵇ عن الروح التي في آدم ﵇ والتي في عيسى ﵇ ماهما ؟ قال : روحان مخلوقان اختارهما واصطفاهما ، روح آدم ﵇ وروح عيسى ﵇

"Abu Jafar al-Asamm reported: I asked Abu Jafar, that is Imam al-Baqir ﵇, about the spirit in Adam and the spirit in Jesus. What are they? The Imam replied, 'They are two created spirits. God chose and selected them, the spirit of Adam and the spirit of Jesus.'"

- al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 172

في قوله ﷿ : (ونفخت فيه من روحي) قال : من قدرتي

"Regarding God's words, 'I breathed into him of My spirit,' the Imam said, 'It means from My power.'"

- Imam al-Baqir ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 172

The "from Him" that the catalogue needs as a share of essence, the Imam glosses as "of My power". The parallel title is a created thing, chosen, placed in two prophets, and the grammar of "from" in it is the grammar Abu Hayyan fixed, the mark of issuing. The school's own creedal formula on the two titles has survived in a remarkable anecdote.

The Confession of the Two Men

Source: Muhammad b. al-Hasan al-Qummi (d. 728), al-'Aqd al-Nadid wa-l-Durr al-Farid, p. 116

Claim: the fixed confession names Jesus ﵇ the word of Allah cast to Mary and a creature, not a creator, in one breath

The compiler reports an exchange with two Christian men, and the exchange preserves the school's paired formula.

أحدهما : إنّ عيسى عبد الله ورسوله وروح الله وكلمته ألقاها إلى مريم أَمته ، وإنّ عيسى مخلوق ليس بخالق ، فقبلت منه وصدّقته ، وقال [لي] الآخر : إنّ عيسى هو ربّه ، فكذّبته ولعنته .

"One of them told me, 'Jesus is God's servant, His messenger, His spirit, and His word, which He cast to Mary, His servant. Jesus is created and is not a creator.' I accepted this from him and believed him. The other told me, 'Jesus is his Lord.' I called him a liar and cursed him."

- Muhammad b. al-Hasan al-Qummi, al-'Aqd al-Nadid, p. 116

Word, spirit, servant, and creature stand in one sentence, and the deifier is answered with a curse. This is the tradition's own creedal handling of the exact title the catalogue presses, and it was written down a thousand years ago. The modern confrontation with the prologue of John is one century old, and its author knew the catalogue's verse by name.

Against the Prologue by Name

Source: al-Balaghi (d. 1351), al-Huda ila Din al-Mustafa, vol. 1, p. 329; al-Rihla al-Madarsiyya, vol. 2, p. 174

Claim: being God's word means being the effect of His "Be!", and this does not agree with John 1:1

ومعنى كون المسيح كلمة الله هو كونه أثرا لقوله تعالى " كن " على خلاف العادة في تناسل البشر ، ولا تحسب أن معنى ذلك يوافق ما في كتب إلهام المتكلف فإن فيها ما نصه : وكان الكلمة الله " يو 1 : 1 "

"The Messiah is called God's word because he came through God's command, 'Be,' outside the usual way people have children. Do not think this agrees with what appears in the books of those who force meanings into scripture. One of those books says, 'The Word was God,' John 1:1."

- al-Balaghi, al-Huda ila Din al-Mustafa, vol. 1, p. 329

The same author reads the title under 4:171 in the vocabulary of power.

وكلمته ) كلمة القدرة التكوينية وهي قول الله جل شأنه بأنه بأمر قدرته ( كن ) كما يذكر أول توراتكم في خلق السماوات والأرض والنور وآدم أن الله تقدس اسمه يقول بقدرته ( ليكن كذا ) فيكون المخلوق بعد عدمه . وهذه كلمة القدرة والتكوين البديع ( ألقاها إلى مريم ) وأجراها في آلات تناسلها . فالمسيح أثر كلمة القدرة وصنيعة تكوين الله

"His word means the word of creative power. It is God's command, 'Be,' given through His power. Your earliest Torah describes the creation of the heavens, the earth, light, and Adam. It says that God commands through His power, 'Let such a thing be,' and the created thing exists after it had not existed. God cast this word of power and wondrous creation to Mary and brought it about through her means of reproduction. The Messiah is the result of the word of power and the work of God's creation."

- al-Balaghi, al-Rihla al-Madarsiyya, vol. 2, p. 174

Al-Balaghi states the comparison the catalogue wants and refuses it in the same paragraph: the word that made Jesus is the same word that made light at the first verse of the Torah, and the effect of a word is a creature. There remains one register of the tradition that speaks most generously of the title, and honesty requires it in full.

The Philosophical Reading, Stated Fairly

Source: al-Tabataba'i (d. 1402), al-Mizan, vol. 3, p. 193; Qadi Sa'id al-Qummi, Sharh Tawhid al-Saduq, vol. 2, p. 733; al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 8, p. 92

Claim: even the most metaphysical strand reads the word as the creative command under which every existent stands, with Jesus ﵇ its immediate effect

Al-Tabataba'i weighs all three readings of 3:45 and chooses the one the catalogue would most want to borrow.

وربما قيل إن المراد بكلمة منه البشارة نفسها وهي الاخبار بحملها بعيسى وولادته فمعنى قوله يبشرك بكلمة منه يبشرك ببشارة هي أنك ستلدين عيسى من غير مس بشر وفيه أن سياق الذيل أعني قوله اسمه المسيح لا يلائمه وهو ظاهر .

وربما قيل إن المراد به عيسى ﵇ من جهة كونه كلمة الايجاد أعني قوله كن وإنما اختص عيسى ﵇ بذلك مع كون كل إنسان بل كل شئ موجودا بكلمة كن التكوينية لان سائر الافراد من الانسان يجرى ولادتهم على مجرى الأسباب العادية المألوفة في العلوق من ورود ماء الرجل على نطفة الإناث وعمل العوامل المقارنة في ذلك ولذلك يسند العلوق إليه كما يسند سائر المسببات إلى أسبابها

ولما لم يجر علوق عيسى هذا المجرى وفقد بعض الأسباب العادية التدريجية كان وجوده بمجرد كلمة التكوين من غير تخلل الأسباب العادية فكان نفس الكلمة كما يؤيده قوله تعالى " وكلمته ألقاها إلى مريم وروح منه : النساء - 171 وقوله تعالى في آخر هذه الآيات " إن مثل عيسى عند الله كمثل آدم خلقه من تراب ثم قال له كن فيكون الآية " وهذا أحسن الوجوه

"Some have said that the word means the good news itself. It would mean that God gave Mary good news that she would become pregnant with Jesus and give birth to him without a man touching her. The end of the verse does not fit this view, since it says, 'His name is the Messiah.'"

"Some have said that it means Jesus because he came through the word of creation, 'Be.' Every person, and indeed every existing thing, comes through the creative command 'Be.' Jesus received this special description because other people are born through familiar and ordinary causes."

"Jesus did not come into being through this process. Some gradual and ordinary causes were absent. He came to be directly through the word of creation, without ordinary causes coming between. He was therefore the word itself. This is supported by His words, 'His word, which He cast to Mary, and a spirit from Him,' and by His words, 'The likeness of Jesus before God is like that of Adam. He created him from dust, then said to him, Be, and he came to be.' This is the strongest view."

- al-Tabataba'i, al-Mizan, vol. 3, p. 193

Qadi Sa'id al-Qummi carries the same reading into the vocabulary of the command.

فالأمر هي كلمة « كن » وهذه الوسائط أمر اللّه ومن « عالم الأمر » المقابل « للخلق » . ومنها ، ما ورد انّ عيسى - عليه السّلام - « كلمة اللّه » ألقاها إلى مريم ، قيل : وذلك لأنّه وجد بأمره من دون وساطة أب ،

فشابه الإبداعيات كما انّ المبدعات لا واسطة بينها وبين مبدعها . أقول : أو لأنّه - عليه السّلام - نفس أمره تعالى لما عرفت انّ « الكلمة » هي « الأمر » فهو الأمر الملقى إلى مريم ،

ولا يخفى انّ الإلقاء في الآية الكريمة قرينة على ذلك

"The command is the word 'Be.' These means are God's command and belong to the realm of command, which stands opposite the realm of creation. This includes the report that Jesus was God's word, cast to Mary. Some said this was because God created him by His command without a father's mediation. He therefore resembled things God creates directly, since no means stands between them and their Creator. I say that Jesus may be God's command itself. As explained earlier, 'word' means 'command.' He is the command cast to Mary."

- Qadi Sa'id al-Qummi, Sharh Tawhid al-Saduq, vol. 2, p. 733

And where the Qur'an multiplies the words of God beyond counting, al-Tabrisi fixes what they are.

ولو أن ما في الأرض من شجرة أقلام والبحر يمده من بعده سبعة أبحر ما نفدت كلمات الله) أي : لو كان شجر الأرض أقلاما ، وكان البحر مدادا ، ويمده سبعة أبحر مثله أي : تزيده بمائها ، فكتب بتلك الأقلام والبحور ،

لتكسرت تلك الأقلام ، ونفد ماء البحور ، وما نفدت كلمات الله .

وقد ذكرنا تفسير (كلمات الله) في سورة الكهف ، والأولى أن يكون عبارة عن مقدوراته ، ومعلوماته ، لأنها إذا كانت لا تتناهى ، فكذلك الكلمات التي تقع عبارة عنها لا تتناهى

"'If every tree on earth were pens, and the sea were ink, with seven more seas added to it, God's words would not run out.' This means that if the trees of the earth became pens and the sea became ink, and seven similar seas added their water to it, those pens would break and the water of the seas would run out, yet God's words would not run out. We explained God's words in the chapter of the Cave. The stronger view is that they refer to what God can do and what He knows. These have no end, so the words that express them also have no end."

- al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 8, p. 92, on Qur'an 31:27

So the most generous register in the tradition still refuses the catalogue its premise. Al-Tabataba'i calls Jesus the word of creation only after stating that every existent comes by that word, and he grounds the uniqueness in the absence of ordinary causes, never in a share of divinity; Qadi Sa'id calls him the command cast to Mary and means an immediate creature; and the words of God that no sea can ink are His powerables and His knowables. The last pillar of the catalogue is the command standing apart in the verse of the creation, and the exegetes took that question up seven centuries ago.

5. The Command Standing Apart

The verse of the creation lists a pair, "to Him belongs the creation and the command", and the catalogue reads the second noun as an uncreated something beside Him. Al-Tusi explained the listing in 1029, condemned the inference drawn from it, and the hadith make the command His act of willing and governing, with everything "of His command" a creature.

Why the Verse Separates Them

Verse: Qur'an 7:54

Source: al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 4, p. 423; al-Tabrisi, Mukhtasar Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 1, p. 529

Claim: the separation is between two meanings, making and governing, and whoever infers an eternal speech from it has professed ignorance

أَلَا لَهُ ٱلْخَلْقُ وَٱلْأَمْرُ ۗ تَبَارَكَ ٱللَّهُ رَبُّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

A-lā lahu al-khalqu wa-l-amru, tabāraka Allāhu Rabbu al-ʿālamīn.

"Creation and command belong to Him. Blessed is Allah, Lord of all worlds."

- Qur'an 7:54

Al-Tusi's commentary on the verse is the oldest answer this dossier carries to the catalogue's own argument.

وقوله " ألا له الخلق والامر " إنما فصل الخلق من الامر ، لان فائدتهما مختلفة " لان له الخلق " يفيد أن له الاختراع ،

" وله الامر " معناه له أن يأمر فيه بما أحب فأفاد الثاني ما لم يفده الأول . فمن استدل بذلك على أن كلام الله قديم ، فقد تجاهل

"In His words, 'Creation and command belong to Him alone,' He separates creation from command because each gives a different meaning. 'Creation belongs to Him' means that He brings things into existence. 'Command belongs to Him' means that He may command within it as He wills. The second statement gives a meaning that the first does not give. Anyone who uses this as proof that God's speech is eternal has ignored its meaning."

- al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 4, p. 423

Al-Tabrisi reads the verse's imagery of settling on the throne in the same register.

استوى أمره على الملك

"His command settled over the kingdom."

- al-Tabrisi, Mukhtasar Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 1, p. 529, on Qur'an 7:54

The separation in the verse is semantic, between the making of things and the governing of them, and both belong to the One without partner. Muslim partisans of al-Tusi's own day drew the catalogue's inference from this verse, an eternal something read out of the second noun, and he met it with the word "ignorance". The second noun itself is then fixed by the verse of the single command.

The One Command Like a Twinkling

Verse: Qur'an 54:49-50

Source: al-Turayhi (d. 1085), Majma' al-Bahrayn, vol. 3, p. 209; al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 335

Claim: the one command is the "Be!" itself, and the command includes His commanding and abrogating in time

إِنَّا كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلَقْنَٰهُ بِقَدَرٍۢ

وَمَآ أَمْرُنَآ إِلَّا وَٰحِدَةٌۭ كَلَمْحٍۭ بِٱلْبَصَرِ

Innā kulla shay'in khalaqnāhu bi-qadar. Wa-mā amrunā illā wāḥidatun ka-lamḥin bi-l-baṣar.

"We created all things by decree. Our command is only one, like a glance of the eye."

- Qur'an 54:49-50

Al-Turayhi's dictionary of the Qur'an glosses the single command under the entry of its root.

قوله تعالى : وما أمرنا إلا واحدة

أي وما أمرنا إلا كلمة واحدة سريعة التكوين كلمح البصر والمراد كن

"His words, 'Our command is only one,' mean that Our command is one word, swift in bringing things into being, like the blink of an eye. It means, 'Be.'"

- al-Turayhi, Majma' al-Bahrayn, vol. 3, p. 209

Al-Saduq files the command under the doctrine of bada', the school's teaching that Allah may show a matter as one thing and then bring another to pass, where the command names acts of God in time.

وما عظم الله ﷿ بشئ أفضل من الاقرار بأن له الخلق والأمر ، والتقديم ، والتأخير ، وإثبات ما لم يكن ومحو ما قد كان

"God has received no praise greater than this claim, that creation and command are His, that He brings forward and delays, that He makes firm what was not, and erases what was."

- al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 335

The command of 54:50 is the "Be!" of 36:82, one word, one instant, and the same noun names His commanding and forbidding, His advancing and deferring, His abrogating of a law and His changing of a decree. An eternal hypostasis does none of these things; an act of governing does all of them. The hadith go further and strip the word itself out of the command's working.

The Command Works Without Speech

Source: al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, pp. 143, 238; al-Bahrani, al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, vol. 2, p. 559

Speakers: the Commander of the Faithful ﵇; Imam al-Sadiq ﵇; Imam al-Hasan al-'Askari ﵇

Claim: when Allah wills, it is as He wills by His command, without speech; the command is His perpetual activity of commanding, His alone

'Ali's ﵇ answer to a questioner in the Tawhid removes the medium from the amr entirely.

إذا أراد الله شيئا كان كما أراد بأمره من غير نطق ، لا ملجأ لعباده مما قضى

"When God wills something, it happens as He wills through His command, without speech. His servants have no refuge from what He has decreed."

- the Commander of the Faithful ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 143

The hadith of the call to prayer joins the pair of the verse to the mashiyya and the "Be!".

فإذا قال المؤذن (الله أكبر) فإنه يقول : الله الذي له الخلق والأمر ، وبمشيته كان الخلق ، ومنه كان كل شئ للخلق ، وإليه يرجع الخلق ، وهو الأول قبل كل شئ لم يزل ، والآخر بعد كل شئ لا يزال ،

والظاهر فوق كل شئ لا يدرك ، والباطن دون كل شئ لا يحد ، فهو الباقي وكل شئ دونه فان ، والمعنى الثاني (الله أكبر) أي العليم الخبير علم ما كان وما يكون قبل أن يكون ،

والثالث (الله أكبر) أي القادر على كل شئ ، يقدر على ما يشاء ، القوي لقدرته ، المقتدر على خلقه ، القوي لذاته ، قدرته قائمة على الأشياء كلها ، إذا قضى أمرا فإنما يقول له كن فيكون

"When the caller to prayer says, 'God is greatest,' he means: God possesses creation and command. Creation came through His will. Everything that exists for creation came from Him, and creation returns to Him. He is the First, before everything, and He has always been. He is the Last, after everything, and He will always remain. He is the Manifest, above everything, beyond all grasp. He is the Hidden, beneath everything, beyond all limits. He remains, while everything beneath Him passes away."

"The second meaning of 'God is greatest' is that He knows all things. He knew what was and what would be before it came to be. The third meaning is that He has power over all things. His power extends over all things. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, 'Be,' and it comes to be."

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid, p. 238

And Imam al-Hasan al-'Askari ﵇ was asked directly about the command's belonging.

قلت لأبي محمد الحسن العسكري (﵇) : عرفني عن قول الله تعالى : لِلَّه الأَمْرُ مِنْ قَبْلُ ومِنْ بَعْدُ) * .

فقال (﵇) : «لله الأمر من قبل أن يأمر ، ومن بعد أن يأمر بما يشاء» فقلت في نفسي : هذا تأويل قول الله : * (أَلا لَه الْخَلْقُ والأَمْرُ تَبارَكَ اللَّه رَبُّ الْعالَمِينَ) * ،

فأقبل علي وقال : «هو كما أسررت في نفسك

"I said to Abu Muhammad al-Hasan al-Askari, 'Explain to me God's words, Command belongs to God before and after.' The Imam replied, 'Command belongs to God before He commands, and after He commands whatever He wills.' I thought to myself, 'This explains God's words, Creation and command belong to Him alone. Blessed is God, Lord of all worlds.' He turned toward me and said, 'It is as you kept within yourself.'"

- al-Bahrani, al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, vol. 2, p. 559

The amr operates "without speech", and the Imam's gloss makes it God's commanding, before the command and after it, perpetual activity and not a subsisting object. The command is not listed with the things because it is not one more thing; it is His willing and governing of the things. What then of the entities the Qur'an does call "of His command"? The exegetes graded them, and the grade is creation.

Of His Command Means of His Making

Verse: Qur'an 17:85 and 42:52

Source: al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 6, p. 288; al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 6, p. 515; al-Turayhi, Majma' al-Bahrayn, vol. 2, p. 355, relaying al-Kafi

Claim: "the spirit is of my Lord's command" means of His act and His creation, and a thing "of Our command" is a creature above Jibril ﵁

وَيَسْـَٔلُونَكَ عَنِ ٱلرُّوحِ ۖ قُلِ ٱلرُّوحُ مِنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّى وَمَآ أُوتِيتُم مِّنَ ٱلْعِلْمِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًۭا

Wa-yas'alūnaka ʿani al-rūḥ, quli al-rūḥu min amri Rabbī, wa-mā ūtītum mina al-ʿilmi illā qalīlā.

"They ask you about the Spirit. Say, 'The Spirit is by the command of my Lord, and you have been given only a little knowledge.'"

- Qur'an 17:85

Al-Tabrisi records that the verse was revealed to the exact question the catalogue turns on.

وثانيها : إنهم سألوا عن الروح أهي مخلوقة محدثة ، أم ليست كذلك ، فقال سبحانه : * (قل الروح من أمر ربي) * أي : من فعله وخلقه ، وكان هذا جوابا لهم عما سألوه عنه بعينه

"The second view is that they asked whether the spirit was a created and newly made thing. God replied, 'Say: The spirit is from my Lord's command.' This means that it is from His action and creation. This answered exactly what they had asked."

- al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 6, p. 288

Al-Tusi's gloss is four words.

أي من خلق ربي وفعله

"It means: from my Lord's creation and action."

- al-Tusi, al-Tibyan, vol. 6, p. 515, on Qur'an 17:85

And the hadith of the spirit by which the Prophet ﷺ was strengthened fixes the grade of everything "of the command".

روى ثقة الإسلام عن أبي بصير قال : سألت أبا عبد الله ع عن قول الله تعالى : وكذلك أوحينا إليك روحا من أمرنا ما كنت تدري ما الكتاب ولا الإيمان قال : خلق من خلق الله تعالى أعظم من جبرئيل وميكائيل ،

كان مع رسول الله يخبره ويسدده

"The trustworthy authority reports from Abu Basir, who said: I asked Abu Abd Allah, that is Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, about God's words, 'Thus We revealed to you a spirit from Our command. You did not know what the Book was or what faith was.' He replied, 'It is a creature of God, greater than Gabriel and Michael. It was with the Messenger of God, informing him and guiding him rightly.'"

- al-Kafi, relayed by al-Turayhi, Majma' al-Bahrayn, vol. 2, p. 355, on Qur'an 42:52

So the separation the catalogue leans on yields no second principle. The amr is God's commanding and governing, His willing and His "Be!", never a thing among things and never an uncreated object beside Him, and every entity the Qur'an ties to the command is graded a creature, even one above Jibril ﵁. The word of the creating is an act, the act is originated, the one titled by that word is its first effect, and the command standing apart is the making and the governing of all of it in one hand. The dossier now states the doctrine it answers, in the words of its own holders.

What Christians Believe About the Word

The catalogue does not invent its doctrine, and this dossier does not answer an invention. The church fixed the doctrine of the divine Word over three centuries, in the prologue of John, in the fathers before the councils, and in the creeds of Nicaea and Chalcedon, and its holders state it in their own words below.

The Word of the Prologue

Source: The Gospel according to John 1:1-3

Editions: Greek, Elzevir Textus Receptus (1624); Arabic, Smith and Van Dyck (1865); English, King James Version

Claim: the Word was in the beginning, was with God, was God, and all things were made through him

εν αρχη ην ο λογος και ο λογος ην προς τον θεον και θεος ην ο λογος. ουτος ην εν αρχη προς τον θεον. παντα δι αυτου εγενετο και χωρις αυτου εγενετο ουδε εν ο γεγονεν.

فِي الْبَدْءِ كَانَ الْكَلِمَةُ، وَالْكَلِمَةُ كَانَ عِنْدَ اللهِ، وَكَانَ الْكَلِمَةُ اللهَ. هذَا كَانَ فِي الْبَدْءِ عِنْدَ اللهِ. كُلُّ شَيْءٍ بِهِ كَانَ، وَبِغَيْرِهِ لَمْ يَكُنْ شَيْءٌ مِمَّا كَانَ.

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made."

- John 1:1-3, in the Textus Receptus (Elzevir, 1624), the Smith-Van Dyck Arabic Bible (1865), and the King James Version

The prologue states the claim at its strongest, and the strongest is what this dossier answers: a Word that pre-exists all things, that is God, through whom all things were made. Ignatius of Antioch (d. c. 107) had already fixed the paradox of the person in the Epistle to the Ephesians, chapter 7, where the one Physician is "both made and not made; God existing in flesh." The catalogue takes its three steps from exactly this text, and the church agrees with the catalogue about what the text teaches.

The Fathers Before the Councils

Holders: Justin Martyr (d. c. 165), First Apology and the Dialogue with Trypho; Origen (d. c. 254), On First Principles

Claim: the Word is a second divine being, begotten before all creatures, through whom God made all things

Justin's Begotten Word

Justin states the doctrine a full two centuries before the creed fixed it, and he grounds it in an analogy of speech that the school of the Imams ﵈ grades directly.

"God begot before all creatures a Beginning, [who was] a certain rational power [proceeding] from Himself, who is called by the Holy Spirit, now the Glory of the Lord, now the Son, again Wisdom, again an Angel, then God, and then Lord and Logos... just as we see happening among ourselves: for when we give out some word, we beget the word; yet not by abscission, so as to lessen the word [which remains] in us, when we give it out."

- Justin Martyr, Dialogue with Trypho, chapter 61

The analogy is the argument: a speaker begets his word without dividing himself. The hadith of the Imams ﵈ grant the analogy its humanity and refuse its divinity, for the Commander of the Faithful ﵇ states that Allah says and does not utter, and that His speech is an act He originates.

Origen's Creative Instrument

Origen reads the prologue as the church's doctrine of creation through a second.

"All souls and all rational natures, whether holy or wicked, were formed or created... because all things were made by God through Christ, as John teaches in a general way in his Gospel, saying, In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by Him, and without Him was nothing made."

- Origen, On First Principles, book I, ch. 7, §1

Here the word is the instrument between God and the things made, one generation before Nicaea legislated it. The Imami hadith state the opposite boundary for the same office: the things come to be by will and mashiyya, without speech, and the will itself is created first.

The Councils Fix the Doctrine

Holders: the Council of Nicaea (325), reaffirmed at Constantinople (381) and Chalcedon (451)

Claim: the Son is begotten not made, of one substance with the Father, through whom all things were made

"And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only begotten of the Father, that is, of the substance of the Father, God of God, light of light, true God of true God, begotten not made, of the same substance with the Father, through whom all things were made both in heaven and on earth; who for us men and our salvation descended, was incarnate, and was made man, suffered and rose again the third day, ascended into heaven and cometh to judge the living and the dead."

- The creed of the Council of Nicaea, 325

The creed carries the creative clause of the prologue into law, and its anathemas condemn the party that said there was a time when the Son was not. Chalcedon sealed the settlement a century later in the names the creed had fixed, "one and the same Son and only-begotten, God the Word, our Lord Jesus Christ."

The boundary the church polices is eternity: the Word is begotten, never made, and the homoousion answers the count of gods by one substance in two persons, so that the Word is no second deity. The Imams ﵈ police the same boundary from the other side, and their formula is ontological, not a counting of gods: nothing eternal stands beside Allah at all, one substance or two, and al-Sadiq's ﵇ tawqi' grades every speech-attribute originated with the act.

The school is not the party of Arius either, for it makes the word no son and no person at all; it grades the word among the acts, and the first creature among the creatures. One last notice belongs here: the Arabic of John's prologue above renders logos as kalima because the translators of 1865 chose it under the creed's assumption, and their choice is evidence of the doctrine, never of the language.

The Comparison on the Four Tests

Method: ethical, rational, textual fidelity, and economy, argued in both directions

The Ethical Test

The Christian view asks the believer to accept a God who begat a co-eternal Word that is Himself God, and a making of all things that passed through a second Lord. The school of the Imams ﵈ grades that exact shape with the formula of the Commander of the Faithful ﵇: an eternal word beside Allah would be a second god, and the ethics of lordship keep creating, commanding, and governing undivided in one hand.

In the other direction, the Christian answers that a God who creates with no Word keeps Himself unknown; the school replies that nearness is bridged by prophets and by acts, and that an eternal interpreter subtracts nothing from the distance it claims to cross.

The Rational Test

Each view must cohere with its own scripture. The Imami reading coheres with the Qur'an's own explanations, for 36:82 conditions the "Be!" on His willing, and the hadith read the willing itself as the causing. The Logos reading must carry John's prologue into verses that pair Jesus ﵇ with Adam ﵇ and end with a prohibition of the three, and its own canon records three centuries of war over the clause of the Son's beginning before the creed settled it by anathema.

In the other direction, the Christian holds the prologue internally coherent on its own terms, and answers the Adam parallel with John 1:3 itself, that without the Word nothing was made, Adam included, so that making through the Word leaves the Word unmade. The school's reply to that reply is the verse that joins the two questions the prologue keeps apart, for the Qur'an denies the son and brings the Be! in one breath (Qur'an 19:35): the thing that comes to be by the word is a creature, and the sonship and the word-making stand or fall together.

And the grading the catalogue dismisses as theology is the Qur'an's own text under its own description, for al-Saduq's argument for the originatedness of the speech runs on the book's observable features, its parts ordered in time and its abrogating verse arriving after the abrogated, and a book whose suras come one after another in time cannot be eternal beside the God who precedes time.

The Test of Textual Fidelity

The Imami reading takes each word at its attested sense: kalima a word spoken, amr a command of a commander, "from Him" a mark of origin and kind. The Logos reading gives "word" a second meaning the lexicons do not carry, finds an unmentioned person inside "Be! and it is", and reads an eternal entity into the noun pair of 7:54 that the exegetes from al-Tusi onward read as the making and the governing.

The verse the catalogue leans on most, 4:171, closes its own titling with the command to say nothing of a three, and the catalogue's reading makes the verse testify for the doctrine the same verse forbids. The Christian presses the Qur'an's own miracle verses in reply, where Jesus ﵇ forms the bird of clay and it becomes a bird (Qur'an 3:49); the school answers that the same verse conditions the miracle on His leave, and power exercised by leave and permission is borrowed for the moment and presupposes its owner, exactly as al-Balaghi read the title.

The Test of Economy

The Imami reading needs no second meaning for "word", no person in the "Be!", and no hypostasis in "and the command". The Logos reading needs all three, and each is an import from another book.

In the other direction, the Christian counts economy on his side, one eternal Word explaining the titling, the creating, and the miracles at a stroke; the school replies that the explanation explains a text that denies each of its premises where it stands, and the cheapest reading of a text is the one that need not overrule it. Economy does not decide truth by itself; it measures what each reading demands of the text, and on that measure the school's grading is the cheaper by three suppositions.

It is evident that the two readings do not disagree over a word; they disagree over whether the word is a who. The church says a who, begotten not made. The school says an act, originated when Allah wills, and the title of Jesus ﵇ records the manner of his making, never the composition of his maker.

Glossary of Key Terms

Muhdath: originated, brought into being in time; the school's grading of the speech of Allah, as a coin is struck by its striker.

Sifat al-af'al (attributes of action): the attributes that arise when Allah acts, among them willing, speaking, and creating; the school contrasts them with the attributes of essence, such as knowledge and power, which never cease.

Mashiyya: the willing specific to Allah, His act of willing a thing into being; the hadith grade it created first, then the means of the making of things.

Amr: command, and affair; in the verse of the creation the school reads the making of things and the governing of them, both His alone.

Logos: the Word of the prologue of John, which the councils confess as the one Son, true God from true God, through whom all things were made.

Ibda': origination without prior matter, model, or time; the hadith of the letters call it the first making, prior to the letters themselves.

Final Verdict

It is evident that the school grades the word by which Allah creates as an originated act of His will, and the bearer of the title as the first creature of that act. The catalogue that makes the word an eternal instrument inverts every grading in the record, and the Commander of the Faithful ﵇ named the doctrine of an eternal word for what it is, the doctrine of a second god. And Allah Most High knows best.
