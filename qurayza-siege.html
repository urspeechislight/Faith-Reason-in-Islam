The Expedition Against Banu Qurayza

The day the Trench broke, the Prophet ﷺ marched on the Qurayza, whose treachery had opened Medina's inside to the confederates. After a twenty five night siege the tribe came down to the judgment of their own ally Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, and the verdict stood. Part 19 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Command and the Siege

The Qurayza had chosen their side while the trench held. Their pact bound them to harm no one against Medina. When the ten thousand camped outside, the men of the Nadir, the exiled clan who had bought the confederacy, entered their forts and pressed them into the treachery.

The day the wind broke the siege, the Prophet ﷺ was just beginning to lay it down. He was washing his head at Zaynab's house when Jibril came to him with the command, and the army marched the same hour.▼ commentary

"Qatada and others related that when the confederates had gone, the Prophet went to the house of Zaynab daughter of Jahsh to wash his head, and while he washed it Jibril came to him and said, 'May Allah pardon you. The angels have not laid down their weapons for forty nights. Rise against Banu Qurayza, for I have cut their tent pegs and opened their doors and left them in shaking and affliction.' So the Prophet girded himself and went out by the pass of Banu Ghannm, and the people followed him. The Messenger of Allah besieged them and called to them, 'Brothers of monkeys.' They said, 'Abu al-Qasim, you were never a man of foul speech.' They came down to the judgment of Sa'd, and he ruled that their fighting men be killed, and they were six hundred, and their children taken captive, and their lands divided among the Emigrants, the Meccan Muslims."

- Qatada's report in al-Hidaya ila Bulugh al-Nahiya, vol.9, p.231

"He besieged them twenty five nights, until the siege wore them out and Allah cast terror into their hearts. The Prophet said to them, 'Will you come down to my judgment?' They refused. He said, 'Will you come down to the judgment of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, chief of the Aws?' They were content with him. Sa'd judged, 'I judge that the men be killed, the wealth divided, and the children and women taken captive.' The Prophet said, 'You have judged with the judgment of Allah.' Then he had trenches dug in the market of Medina and brought them group by group and struck their necks, and they were between eight hundred and nine hundred."

- al-Darwish, I'rab al-Qur'an, vol.7, p.629

Commentary

The Sources for the Qurayza

Shia line: al-Amili, al-Sahih min Sira al-Nabi al-A'zam, vol.12, p.79, on the surrender, the captives, and the date

Sunni lines: Qatada's report in al-Hidaya ila Bulugh al-Nahiya, vol.9, p.231. al-Nadwi, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, p.363, on the verdict. al-Darwish, I'rab al-Qur'an, vol.7, p.629, on the numbers

Scripture: Qur'an 33:26, Uthmani text

Counting note: the fighting men are six hundred in Qatada's line, eight hundred to nine hundred in al-Darwish's, and one thousand persons in al-Amili's, and the carriers do not force a reconciliation

The layers agree on the frame. The treachery in the Trench, the siege of twenty five nights, the refusal of the Prophet's ﷺ own judgment, the acceptance of Sa'd, the verdict, and the trenches dug in Medina's market.

The Verdict of Sa'd

The judge: Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, chief of the Aws, the Qurayza's own ally tribe in Medina, chosen by the Qurayza themselves after refusing the Prophet's ﷺ judgment

The plea: his tribesmen begged him, deal well with your clients

The answer: the hour has come for Sa'd to spare no blame in the cause of Allah

The verdict: the men killed, the wealth divided, the women and children captive

The seal: you have judged with the judgment of Allah, said over him

The sources give the structure plainly. The accused refused the head of state's judgment and named their own ally, expecting mercy from a client's tie, as the plea shows. The verdict Sa'd gave matches the war law the Torah carries for a city that makes war, as the line above shows, and the page's carriers read it that way. The Prophet's ﷺ word over it, in the carriers' report, named it the judgment of Allah.

The women and children were spared, and al-Amili's line shows them set aside and kept under guard. The lands passed to the state and to the Emigrants, and al-Amili's line adds the bookkeeping, the bound under Muhammad ibn Maslama, the families under Abd Allah ibn Salam.

What the Judgment Left

Tactical: the third and last Jewish clan of Medina removed, the city's inside closed

Strategic: Medina entered the treaty year of Hudaybiyya a single-bodied city, and the Qur'an's verse made the event scripture

Cost: the executions in the market, counted in the hundreds by the carriers, and Sa'd's own death soon after from his trench wound

The strategic gain was total internal unity, the one condition the next year's diplomacy needed. The moral weight of the event stays heavy in the telling, and the reports do not lighten it. The numbers stand in the hundreds. The trenches were dug in the city's own market, and the verdict was sealed in the Prophet's ﷺ word.

What the sources set against the charge of atrocity is the frame. A judged treason inside a besieged city, under a judge the accused themselves named, under the law they shared.

Was the Expedition Against the Qurayza Justified

The case begins where the Trench ended. A treaty tribe opened the city's inside to a ten thousand man confederacy.

The army stood at the trench with families at the enemy's back. The city survived because the wind broke the siege before the Qurayza's decision could mature. That treason, met with a siege and a judgment, is the record this narration carries.

The judgment itself is what the sources record, and it is not the Prophet's ﷺ own. The accused declined his judgment and named their ally. They received a verdict drawn from their own law's war code, delivered by a dying man, wounded in the Trench, whom the carriers show refusing his tribe's plea for mercy. The seal, the judgment of Allah, is the carriers' report of the Prophet's ﷺ word, and the narration carries it as they carry it.

The siege could have been prevented. The prevention belonged to the Qurayza twice. They signed the pact, and the Trench page's record shows their chiefs warning against the treachery before the Nadir's men pressed them into it. The record holds both truths, the Nadir's pressure on the one side and the Qurayza's own choice on the other, and the verdict answered the choice.

The verdict applied the law of the world they had chosen. In that world, a garrison that throws in with a besieging army forfeits its standing when the siege fails.
