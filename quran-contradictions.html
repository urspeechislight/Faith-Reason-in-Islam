A Catalogue of Contradictions Weighed Against the Tafsir

Qur'an 4:82 Is a Challenge to All Scripture, Not a Contradiction Clause

A circulating contradiction catalogue reads the Qur'an with abusive literalism while excusing its own Bible, so Qur'an 4:82 stands as a challenge to every claimed revelation and the catalogue's claimed contradictions dissolve under the context its author refuses to apply. From corpus tafsir of Tabari, al-Burhan, and al-Safi.

The Argument Under Examination

Premise 1. The Verse Is a Challenge, Not a Self-Test

Qur'an 4:82 makes freedom from ikhtilaf the mark of a revelation from Allah. The word means variance and division among people, not a logical self-contradiction, so the verse challenges every claimed scripture to be examined.

Premise 2. The Author Admits the Rigged Method

The catalogue's author writes that he reads the opponent's text rigidly to make it look bad while treating his own Bible with leniency, and that he will make the strongest possible case for contradiction.

Premise 3. The Internal Items Dissolve in Context

The 'First Muslim', 'Seeing Allah', 'Food in Hell', 'Angels of Death', and 'Inheritance' items each collapse once the verse is read by its occasion, its addressee, and its grammar.

Premise 4. The Science Charges Are Readings

The creation, sun, and embryology verses are phenomenological descriptions, not a physics textbook, and the tafsir reads them as such.

Premise 5. The Charge Falls on the Bible He Defends

The Qur'an itself says the People of the Book corrupted and differ over their scripture, so the author's own test condemns his own book first.

Conclusion. Abusive Literalism Produces the Same List for Any Book

Abusive literalism applied to any book yields the same list of alleged contradictions. Read by the rule the author refuses to apply to his Bible, the items resolve, and 4:82's challenge lands on the Bible he defends.

1. What Qur'an 4:82 Actually Says

Contested words. Qur'an 4:82 and the word ikhtilaf across 3:103, 23:53, and 45:17

Tafsir quoted. al-Tabari's Jami' al-Bayan and al-Bahrani's al-Burhan

Claim. the verse makes freedom from ikhtilaf the mark of a revelation, and ikhtilaf means variance among people, not a self-contradiction

What Qur'an 4:82 Says

The catalogue builds its case on one verse. Qur'an 4:82 says the book's freedom from ikhtilaf is the proof that it came from Allah. The word ikhtilaf is the hinge. Read in the Qur'an's own usage, it means variance and division among people, not a logical fault inside a single text.

أَفَلَا يَتَدَبَّرُونَ الْقُرْآَنَ وَلَوْ كَانَ مِنْ عِنْدِ غَيْرِ اللَّهِ لَوَجَدُوا فِيهِ اخْتِلَافًا كَثِيرًا

a-fa-lā yatadabbarūna al-qur'āna wa-law kāna min ʿindi ghayri Allāhi lawajadū fīhi ikhtilāfan kathīran

"Do they not reflect on the Qur'an? Had it been from other than Allah they would have found in it much variance."

Qur'an 4:82

The verse issues a challenge. Test any claimed revelation for ikhtilaf, and the one free of it carries the mark of coming from Allah.

Ikhtilaf Means Variance Among People

Three other verses fix the meaning of ikhtilaf as schism among people, never as a textual defect.

وَاعْتَصِمُوا بِحَبْلِ اللَّهِ جَمِيعًا وَلَا تَفَرَّقُوا

wa-aʿtiṣimū bi-ḥabli Allāhi jamīʿan wa-lā tafarraqū

"Hold fast to the rope of Allah together, and do not divide."

Qur'an 3:103

فَتَقَطَّعُوا أَمْرَهُمْ بَيْنَهُمْ زُبُرًا كُلُّ حِزْبٍ بِمَا لَدَيْهِمْ فَرِحُونَ

fa-taqattaʿū amrahum baynahum zuburan kullu ḥizbin bimā ladaihim fariḥūn

"They split their affair among themselves into factions, each party rejoicing in what it has."

Qur'an 23:53

وَآتَيْنَاهُمْ بَيِّنَاتٍ مِنَ الْأَمْرِ فَمَا اخْتَلَفُوا إِلَّا مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا جَاءَهُمُ الْعِلْمُ بَغْيًا بَيْنَهُمْ

wa-ātaynāhum bayyinātin mina al-amri fa-mā ikhtalafū illā min baʿdi mā jā'ahumu al-ʿilmu baghyan baynahum

"We gave them clear proofs, yet they differed only after knowledge came to them, out of mutual envy."

Qur'an 45:17

The Early Tafsir Reads 4:82 as a Test of Scripture

The early exegetes read the verse the same way. Tabari ties ikhtilaf to the inconsistency of rulings, the very thing the Qur'an does not have, and he sets the speech of Allah apart from the speech of people.

فإن ذلك لو كان من عند غير الله لاختلفت أحكامه ، وتناقضت معانيه ، وأبان بعضه عن فساد بعض

"Had it been from other than Allah, its rulings would differ, its meanings would contradict, and part would show the corruption of part."

Al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan, on Qur'an 4:82

Al-Burhan, citing al-Ihtijaj from Imam Ali ﵇, states the positive side. The Book confirms itself part by part, and there is no ikhtilaf in it.

وذكر أن الكتاب يصدق بعضه بعضا ، وأنه لا اختلاف فيه

"He mentioned that the Book confirms itself part by part, and that there is no ikhtilaf in it."

Al-Bahrani, al-Burhan, citing al-Tabarsi from Imam Ali ﵇, on Qur'an 4:82

The verse, then, is an open invitation to examine every scripture. The catalogue answers it by examining only one.

2. The Author's Own Method Gives Away the Game

The Method the Author States in Plain Words

The author states his method in plain words. He reads the texts of the opponent in the most rigid and literal manner to make them look bad, while he is very lenient with his own book, allowing extra assumptions and metaphorical readings to explain away difficulties. He adds that, to compare like with like, he will make the strongest possible case for something being contradictory.

That is not the discovery of contradictions. It is the manufacture of them through a standard applied to one book and withheld from another. The school returns the same measure the author will not apply to his Bible. Read each verse in its occasion, its addressee, and its grammar, and the list shrinks to items that dissolve.

The iddah calculation at Qur'an 65:4, the honey episode at Qur'an 66:1, and the preservation of the text at quran-preservation each survive the same rigid reading the author reserves for the Qur'an. The method, not the Qur'an, is what the catalogue exposes.

3. The Internal Contradictions Resolve by Context

Contested words. aslama, ru'yah, ta'am, malak al-mawt, and kalalah

Tafsir quoted. al-Kashani's al-Safi relaying al-Ihtijaj

Claim. each item resolves by its occasion, its addressee, and its grammar

The Word Muslim Means One Who Submits

The 'First Muslim' charge lists verses that call different people the first to submit. The word aslama means to submit, and each verse uses it in its own sense. Abraham submits, Moses submits, the Egyptians submit. None of them is claiming a historical title over the others.

قُلْ إِنِّي أُمِرْتُ أَنْ أَكُونَ أَوَّلَ مَنْ أَسْلَمَ وَلَا تَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ

qul innī umirtu an akūna awwala man aslama wa-lā takūnanna mina al-mushrikīn

"Say, I was commanded to be the first of those who submit, and not be of the idolaters."

Qur'an 6:14

وَبِذَلِكَ أُمِرْتُ وَأَنَا أَوَّلُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ

wa-bi-dhālika umirtu wa-anā awwalu al-muslimīn

"By that I was commanded, and I am the first of those who submit."

Qur'an 6:163

رَبَّنَا وَاجْعَلْنَا مُسْلِمَيْنِ لَكَ وَمِنْ ذُرِّيَّتِنَا أُمَّةً مُسْلِمَةً لَكَ

rabbanā wa-ajʿalnā muslimayni laka wa-min dhurriyyatinā ummatan muslimatan laka

"Our Lord, make us both submitters to You, and from our offspring a community submitting to You."

Qur'an 2:128

مَا كَانَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ يَهُودِيًّا وَلَا نَصْرَانِيًّا وَلَكِنْ كَانَ حَنِيفًا مُسْلِمًا

mā kāna ibrāhīmu yahūdiyyan wa-lā naṣrāniyyan wa-lākin kāna ḥanīfan musliman

"Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian, but a hanif, a submitter."

Qur'an 3:67

فَلَمَّا أَفَاقَ قَالَ سُبْحَانَكَ تُبْتُ إِلَيْكَ وَأَنَا أَوَّلُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ

fa-lammā afāqa qāla subḥānaka tubtu ilayka wa-anā awwalu al-mu'minīn

"When he recovered he said, Glory be to You, I turn to You, and I am the first of the believers."

Qur'an 7:143

Al-Safi reads the 'first' in 6:14 as priority in the primordial covenant, not a historical race. His submission precedes the submission of all creation because he answered first in the world of particles.

بل لأنه أول من أجاب في الميثاق في عالم الذر

"Rather, because he was the first to answer in the covenant in the world of particles."

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, on Qur'an 6:14

فإسلامه متقدم على إسلام الخلايق كلهم

"His submission precedes the submission of all creation."

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, on Qur'an 6:14

The Vision Was of Signs, the Essence Beyond Sight

The 'Seeing Allah' charge sets the vision of Qur'an 53 against the denial of vision in 6:103 and 42:51. The two verses speak of different things. The vision in the high chapter is a vision of the signs, while the denial is of encompassing the Essence with the eye.

مَا كَذَبَ الْفُؤَادُ مَا رَأَى

mā kadhaba al-fu'ādu mā ra'ā

"The heart did not deny what it saw."

Qur'an 53:11

لَا تُدْرِكُهُ الْأَبْصَارُ وَهُوَ يُدْرِكُ الْأَبْصَارَ

lā tudrikuhu al-abṣāru wa-huwa yudriku al-abṣāra

"No vision encompasses Him, while He encompasses all vision."

Qur'an 6:103

وَمَا كَانَ لِبَشَرٍ أَنْ يُكَلِّمَهُ اللَّهُ إِلَّا وَحْيًا أَوْ مِنْ وَرَاءِ حِجَابٍ

wa-mā kāna li-basharin an yukallimahu Allāhu illā waḥyan aw min warā'i ḥijābin

"It is not for any human that Allah should speak to him except by revelation or from behind a veil."

Qur'an 42:51

Al-Safi states the distinction plainly. Allah is greater than to be seen with the eye, and the perceptions of hearts do not encompass Him, so how should the eyes' vision.

الله أعظم من أن يرى بالعين

"Allah is greater than to be seen with the eye."

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, on Qur'an 6:103

أوهام القلوب لا تدركه فكيف أبصار العيون

"The perceptions of hearts do not encompass Him, so how should the eyes' vision."

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, on Qur'an 6:103

Each Description Fits a Different Inhabitant

The 'Food in Hell' charge lines up three descriptions and calls them contradictory. The verses describe different inhabitants and different parts of one tree's due. The author lifts the word 'only' out of its context to force an exclusive reading the text does not make.

فَلَيْسَ لَهُ الْيَوْمَ هَاهُنَا حَمِيمٌ

fa-laysa lahu al-yawma hāhunā ḥamīmun

"He has today no friend here."

Qur'an 88:6

وَلَا طَعَامٌ إِلَّا مِنْ غِسْلِينٍ

wa-lā ṭaʿāmun illā min ghislin

"Nor any food except ghislin, a pus."

Qur'an 69:36

أَذَلِكَ خَيْرٌ نُزُلًا أَمْ شَجَرَةُ الزَّقُّومِ

a-dhālika khayrun nuzulan am shajaratu al-zaqqūm

"Is that a better hospitality, or the tree of Zaqqum?"

Qur'an 37:62

Al-Safi treats the Zaqqum verse as one of several descriptions of the Fire's inhabitants' due, not as an exclusive 'only'. The fruit is the food of the people of the Fire among other torments.

أذلك خير نزلا أم شجرة الزقوم شجرة ثمرها نزل أهل النار

"Is that a better hospitality, or the tree of Zaqqum whose fruit is the food of the people of the Fire?"

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, on Qur'an 37:62

The Angel of Death Acts by Command

The 'Angels cause death' charge sets the taking of souls against the verses that deny the idols the power to give life or cause death. The angel of death is Allah's agent, not a rival source of life and death. The denial verses strip the idols of independent creative power, which is a different claim.

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ تَوَفَّاهُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ ظَالِمِي أَنْفُسِهِمْ

inna alladhīna tawaffāhumu al-malā'ikatu ẓālimī anfusihim

"Those whom the angels take in death while wronging themselves."

Qur'an 4:97

وَلَا يَمْلِكُونَ لِأَنفُسِهِمْ ضَرًّا وَلَا نَفْعًا وَلَا يَمْلِكُونَ مَوْتًا وَلَا حَيَاةً وَلَا نُشُورًا

wa-lā yamlikūna li-anfusihim ḍarran wa-lā nafʿan wa-lā yamlikūna mawtan wa-lā ḥayātan wa-lā nushūran

"They do not possess for themselves harm or benefit, nor death, life, or resurrection."

Qur'an 25:3

قُلْ يَتَوَفَّاكُمْ مَلَكُ الْمَوْتِ الَّذِي وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ

qul yatawaffākum malaku al-mawti alladhī wukkila bikum

"Say, the angel of death, who is charged with you, will take you."

Qur'an 32:11

Al-Safi, relaying al-Ihtijaj from Imam Ali ﵇, settles the point. Allah is too grand to do it Himself and does it through His messengers and His angels, because they act by His command.

إن الله تعالى أجل وأعظم من أن يتولى ذلك بنفسه وفعل رسله وملائكته فعله لأنهم بأمره

"Allah, exalted, is too grand to do it Himself; He does it through His messengers and His angels, because they act by His command."

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, citing al-Ihtijaj from Imam Ali ﵇, on Qur'an 4:97

The Inheritance Cases Are Separate Scenarios

The 'Inheritance math' charge adds the shares of unrelated cases and finds a sum above one. The verses describe distinct scenarios. One governs children and parents, another governs spouses and kalalah, and the third governs a childless man with a sister. Fiqh resolves any residual by awl and radd. Mixing the cases is the error, not the text.

يُوصِيكُمُ اللَّهُ فِي أَوْلَادِكُمْ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثْلُ حَظِّ الْأُنْثَيَيْنِ

yūṣīkumu Allāhu fī awlādikum li-l-dhakari mithlu ḥaẓẓi al-unthayayn

"Allah enjoins you concerning your children, the male gets the share of two females."

Qur'an 4:11

وَلَكُمْ نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ أَزْوَاجُكُمْ

wa-lakum niṣfu mā tarakat azwājukum

"You get half of what your wives leave."

Qur'an 4:12

يَسْتَفْتُونَكَ قُلِ اللَّهُ يُفْتِيكُمْ فِي الْكَلَالَةِ إِنِ امْرُؤٌ هَلَكَ لَيْسَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَلَهُ أُخْتٌ فَلَهَا نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ

yastaftūnaka quli Allāhu yuftīkum fī al-kalālati in imru'un halaka laysa lahu waladun wa-lahu ukhtun fa-lahā niṣfu mā taraka

"They ask you for a ruling. Say, Allah instructs you about kalalah, if a man dies with no child but has a sister, she gets half of what he left."

Qur'an 4:176

4. The Science and History Charges Are Readings Not Contradictions

Contested words. ratq, dukhan, mustaqarr, sulala, and nuṭfa

Tafsir quoted. al-Kashani's al-Safi

Claim. the verses describe phenomena for a human audience, not a physics manual

The Creation Verses Speak of Phenomena

The external items fault the Qur'an for not matching a modern science textbook. The charge assumes the revelation was written to be one. The tafsir reads these verses as phenomena described for a human audience, not as a physics manual.

أَوَلَمْ يَرَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَنَّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ كَانَتَا رَتْقًا فَفَتَقْنَاهُمَا

a-wa-lam yarā alladhīna kafarū anna al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍa kānatā ratqan fa-fataqnāhumā

"Did not those who disbelieve see that the heavens and the earth were a joined mass, then We split them?"

Qur'an 21:30

ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى إِلَى السَّمَاءِ وَهِيَ دُخَانٌ

thumma istawā ilā al-samā'i wa-hiya dukhānun

"Then He turned to the sky, and it was smoke."

Qur'an 41:11

فَقَضَاهُنَّ سَبْعَ سَمَوَاتٍ فِي يَوْمَيْنِ

fa-qaḍāhunna sabʿa samāwātin fī yawmayni

"He decreed them as seven heavens in two days."

Qur'an 41:12

أَلَمْ تَرَوْا كَيْفَ خَلَقَ اللَّهُ سَبْعَ سَمَوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا

a-lam taraw kayfa khalaqa Allāhu sabʿa samāwātin ṭibāqan

"Did you not see how Allah created seven heavens in layers?"

Qur'an 71:15

وَجَعَلَ الْقَمَرَ فِيهِنَّ نُورًا وَجَعَلَ الشَّمْسَ سِرَاجًا

wa-jaʿala al-qamara fīhinna nūran wa-jaʿala al-shamsa sirājan

"And made the moon among them a light, and the sun a lamp."

Qur'an 71:16

The joined heavens and earth, the smoke, and the seven heavens are a cosmic structure described from the viewpoint of the witness, not a sequence to be graded against astronomy.

The Sun Runs to Its Decreed Limit

The sun's 'resting place' is read as a literal well by the catalogue. The tafsir reads mustaqarr as the sun's decreed term and course, the limit its movement reaches before the measure of the Knowing turns it back.

وَالشَّمْسُ تَجْرِي لِمُسْتَقَرٍّ لَّهَا ذَلِكَ تَقْدِيرُ الْعَزِيزِ الْعَلِيمِ

wa-al-shamsu tajrī li-mustaqarrin lahā dhālika taqdīru al-ʿazīzi al-ʿalīmi

"The sun runs to a resting-place for it; that is the measuring of the Mighty, the Knowing."

Qur'an 36:38

والشمس تجري لمستقر لها لحد معين ينتهي إليه دورها

"The sun runs to its decreed term, a defined limit that its course reaches."

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, on Qur'an 36:38

ذلك تقدير العزيز العليم

"That is the measuring of the Mighty, the Knowing."

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, on Qur'an 36:38

The word mustaqarr names a term and a course, not a hole in the ground.

The Embryology Words Track Observable Stages

The embryology charge faults words like alaq. The verses track observable stages of formation, from extract of clay to a drop in a firm place. The 'errors' are misreadings of a plain sequence, not contradictions.

وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا الْإِنْسَانَ مِنْ سُلَالَةٍ مِّنْ طِينٍ

wa-la-qad khalaqnā al-insāna min sulālatin min ṭīnin

"We created man from an extract of clay."

Qur'an 23:12

ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَاهُ نُطْفَةً فِي قَرَارٍ مَكِينٍ

thumma jaʿalnāhu nuṭfatan fī qarārin makīn

"Then We made him a drop in a firm resting-place."

Qur'an 23:13

5. The Charge of Contradicting Earlier Revelation Falls on the Bible

Contested. al-Samiri, and the charge of Bible corruption

Sources. al-Kashani's al-Safi, and the Qur'an's own words

Claim. the Qur'an names its sources and says the People of the Book corrupted their scripture

The Qur'an Names al-Samiri

The 'Samiri equals Samaritan' charge assumes the calf maker is the Biblical Samaritan. The Qur'an names him al-Samiri and tells what he did. It draws no equivalence to the Samaritan people of the Bible.

قَالَ فَإِنَّا قَدْ فَتَنَّا قَوْمَكَ مِنْ بَعْدِكَ وَأَضَلَّهُمُ السَّامِرِيُّ

qāla fa-innā qad fatannā qawmaka min baʿdika wa-aḍallahumu al-sāmirī

"[Allah said] We tried your people after you, and al-Samiri led them astray."

Qur'an 20:85

قَالَ فَمَا خَطْبُكَ يَا سَامِرِيُّ

qāla fa-mā khaṭbuka yā sāmirī

"[Moses] said, What is your case, O Samiri?"

Qur'an 20:95

Al-Safi states the mechanism. Al-Samiri led them astray by taking the calf and calling to its worship. The name is a personal name in the story, not a tribal label borrowed from the Bible.

أضلهم السامري باتخاذ العجل والدعاء إلى عبادته

"Al-Samiri led them astray by taking the calf and calling to its worship."

Al-Kashani, al-Safi, on Qur'an 20:85

The Bible the Author Defends Fails His Own Test

The catalogue's last family charges the Qur'an with contradicting earlier revelation. The Qur'an names its own sources, and it says plainly that the People of the Book corrupted and differ over their scripture. The author's own premise, that ikhtilaf proves a text is not from Allah, condemns his own book first.

فَوَيْلٌ لِّلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ الْكِتَابَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ ثُمَّ يَقُولُونَ هَذَا مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ

fa-waylun lilladhīna yaktubūna al-kitāba bi-aydīhim thumma yaqūlūna hādhā min ʿindi Allāh

"Woe to those who write the scripture with their hands then say this is from Allah."

Qur'an 2:79

وَإِنَّ مِنْهُمْ لَفَرِيقًا يَلْوُونَ أَلْسِنَتَهُمْ بِالْكِتَابِ لِتَحْسَبُوهُ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ

wa-inna minhum la-farīqan yalwūna alsunatahum bi-al-kitābi li-taḥsabūhu mina al-kitāb

"Among them is a faction who twist their tongues concerning the Book so you think it is from the Book."

Qur'an 3:78

يُحَرِّفُونَ الْكَلِمَ عَنْ مَوَاضِعِهِ

yuḥarrifūna al-kalama ʿan mawāḍiʿihi

"They displace the words from their places."

Qur'an 5:13

The Qur'an says the People of the Book wrote scripture by hand and claimed it from Allah, twisted their tongues around the Book, and displaced its words from their places. These are the author's own ikhtilaf, stated by the book he defends.

Final Verdict

Qur'an 4:82 is a challenge to every claimed revelation, not a self-contradiction clause the Qur'an fails. The word ikhtilaf means variance and division among people, and the verse, with the early tafsir of Tabari and al-Burhan, invites all scripture to be examined.

The author admits he reads the opponent rigidly while excusing his own Bible, and that he will make the strongest case he can. The internal items dissolve in their occasion and addressee. The 'First Muslim' verses use one word for submission in many senses. The vision verses separate the signs from the Essence. The food verses describe different inhabitants. The angel verses name an agent who acts by command. The inheritance verses are separate cases.

The science charges are readings, not contradictions, and the tafsir reads creation, the sun, and the embryo as phenomena. The charge of contradicting earlier revelation falls first on the Bible, which the Qur'an says was written by hand, twisted, and displaced. The catalogue is a method, not a finding. Applied evenly, its own test condemns the Bible it defends.
