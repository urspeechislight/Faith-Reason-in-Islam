What Qur'an 4:34 Permits When a Wife Turns Away

The claim that Qur'an 4:34 licenses husbands to scourge disobedient wives collapses on the word the charge hangs on, because the lexicons carry the verb's travel, coinage, simile, and sealing senses, the Prophet's ﷺ farewell sermon bounds any strike to one that is not severe, the Imams ﵈ reduce the act to a toothbrush stick, and the verse's own frame sends the dispute to two arbiters while the reports the attack marshals treat beating as a fault. The Argument Under Examination

Background

The attack states its case in one sentence. The Qur'an licenses a husband to beat a disobedient wife, the traditions show the beating in practice, the scholars affirm the right, and the modern world reaps the fruit. The sentence has real sources behind it. Six translations of Qur'an 4:34 render the imperative wa-iḍribūhunna as beat or scourge, Bukhari and Muslim carry reports of struck wives, and the classical commentaries discuss the permission. Everything else in the charge hangs from that one verse, and the verse hangs on one verb. The case against the charge stands on the dictionaries, on the verse's own passage, and on the reports the attack itself quotes, and it rests on five premises.

Premise 1: The Lexicons Widen the Verb

The Arabic lexicons carry the root d-r-b across minting coins, traveling for trade, striking a simile, sealing ears, and the vein's pulse. The Qur'an itself uses the verb in most of these senses, and its imperative form at 29:64 and 36:13 commands parables, with no body in sight. A translator who prints only scourge has chosen the harshest sense and sold it as the only one.

Premise 2: The Farewell Sermon Bounds the Verse

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ read the verse aloud at the farewell hajj and bounded it in the same breath. His words permit a strike that is ghayr mubriḥ, one that is not severe and leaves no injury, and set the permission inside the command to fear Allah regarding women and treat them well. The attack quotes the sermon and drops the bounding clause.

Premise 3: The Imams Reduce the Act to the Symbolic

Imam al-Baqir ﵇ glosses the strike with the siwak, the toothbrush stick. Ibn Abbas gives the same gloss. The jurists codify the bound, no wound, no blood, no lasting harm, no blow to the face. Aisha's own report in Muslim states the Prophet ﷺ never struck anything with his hand, no woman and no servant.

Premise 4: The Verse's Own Frame Is Arbitration

The next verse sends two arbiters when a breach is feared, and the sura gives the wife's nushuz separately and prescribes reconciliation for it. Nushuz in the jurists' definition is the exit of either spouse from owed obedience. The passage describes a process that ends a marital breakdown, and the cap closes it, once she returns, no further course against her.

Premise 5: The Hadith Battery Answers Itself

The green bruise is evidence heard against the husband in a case the Prophet ﷺ never endorses. Abu Jahm is disqualified for marriage because he beats. The standing rule is do not beat Allah's handmaids, and the Umar sequence ends with the beaters named the worst of you. The chest report comes through Aisha's own complaint, and the fist belongs to Abu Bakr acting alone.

Conclusion: The Scourge Reading Survives Only by Dropping Clauses

The lexicons widen the verb, the Prophet's ﷺ sermon bounds it, the Imams ﵈ reduce it to the symbolic, the verse's own structure turns the dispute to arbitration, and the battery's best reports condemn beating. The scourge reading survives only by dropping the clauses that the sources carry in the same sentences.

1. The Verb the Lexicons Widen

What the Root Carries

Contested word: the imperative wa-iḍribūhunna in Qur'an 4:34, from the root d-r-b

Lexicons quoted: Lisan al-Arab of Ibn Manzur (d. 711 AH) and al-Sihah of al-Jawhari (d. ca. 393 AH)

Claim: the dictionaries carry minting, travel, simile, and sealing senses beside the physical one

The attack's appendix on the word beat leans on one dictionary of modern Arabic and then argues that frequency settles meaning. The classical lexicons say more, and they say it at the entry the attack never opens. Ibn Manzur begins the entry d-r-b in Lisan al-Arab with the physical sense and at once crowds it with the derived words that name what strikes and what is struck.

ضرب : الضرب معروف ، والضَّرْبُ مصدر ضَرَبْتُه ؛ وضَرَبَه يَضْرِبُه ضَرْباً وضَرَّبَه . ورجل ضارِبٌ وضَرُوبٌ وضَريبٌ وضَرِبٌ ومِضْرَبٌ ، بكسر الميم : شديدُ الضَّرْب ، أَو كثير الضَّرْب

ḍarab, al-ḍarb maʿrūf, wa-ḍ-ḍarbu maṣdaru ḍarabtuh, wa-ḍarabahu yaḍribuhu ḍarban wa-ḍarrabah, wa-rajulun ḍāribun wa-ḍarūbun wa-ḍarībun wa-ḍaribun wa-miḍrabun, bi-kasri al-mīm, shadīdu ḍ-ḍarbi aw kathīru ḍ-ḍarb

"Striking is well known. Darb is the verbal noun of darabtuhu."

"Darabahu yadribuhu means he struck him, and darrabahu means he struck him repeatedly. A man may be called darib, darub, darib, darib, or midrab."

"Midrab, with a kasra under the mim, means someone who strikes hard or often."

- Lisan al-Arab (Ibn Manzur), vol. 1, p. 543, entry ḍaraba

The entry's next stretch moves the verb clean away from blows. The same root coins money.

وضَرَبَ الدِّرْهمَ يَضْرِبُه ضَرْباً : طَبَعَه . وهذا دِرْهَمٌ ضَرْبُ الأَمير ، ودِرْهَمٌ ضَرْب

wa-ḍaraba ad-dirhama yaḍribuhi ḍarban ṭabaʿah, wa-hādhā dirhamun ḍarbu al-amīr, wa-dirhamun ḍarb

"He struck a dirham means that he stamped it. This is a dirham from the amir's mint, and a dirham of mint."

- Lisan al-Arab (Ibn Manzur), vol. 1, p. 543, entry ḍaraba, minting

A dirham is ḍarb when the mint stamps it. The verb's object there is metal, not flesh. Further down the same entry the scorpion's ḍarb is her sting, the vein's ḍarb and the heart's ḍarb are their pulse, the wound's ḍarb is its throb, and the wave's iḍṭirāb is its heaving against itself. The root names an action landing on a surface, and the surface decides the sense.

To Travel and to Strike a Simile

The travel sense has its own line in the lexicon, and the Qur'an's own words are quoted inside the dictionary as the proof text.

وضَرَبَ في الأَرضِ يَضرِبُ ضَرْباً وضَرَباناً ومَضْرَباً ، بالفتح ، خَرَجَ فيها تاجِراً أَو غازِياً ، وقيل : أَسْرَعَ ، وقيل : ذَهَب فيها ، وقيل : سارَ في ابْتِغاءِ الرزق .

يقال : إِن لي في أَلف درهم لمَضْرَباً أَي ضَرْباً .

والطيرُ الضَّوارِبُ : التي تَطْلُبُ الرِّزْقَ .

وضَرَبْتُ في الأَرض أَبْتَغِي الخَيْرَ من الرزق ؛ قال اللَّه ، عز وجل : وإِذا ضَرَبْتُم في الأَرض ؛ أَي سافرتم

wa-ḍaraba fī al-arḍi yaḍribu ḍarban wa-ḍarbanān wa-maḍraban bi-l-fatḥ, kharaja fīhā tājiran aw ghāziyan, wa-qīla asraʿa, wa-qīla dhahaba fīhā, wa-qīla sāra fī ibtighāʾi ar-rizq, yuqālu inna lī fī alf dirhamin la-maḍraban ayy ḍarban, wa-ṭ-ṭayru ḍ-ḍawāribu allatī taṭlubu ar-rizq, wa-ḍarabtu fī al-arḍi abtaghī al-khayra mina ar-rizq, qāla Allāhu ʿazza wa-jalla, wa-idhā ḍarabtum fī al-arḍ, ayy sāfartum

"He struck through the land, meaning he went out as a trader or fighter. It is also said that he hurried, went away, or traveled seeking a living."

"One says, "I have a way to trade with a thousand dirhams," meaning a means of trading. Birds that strike through the land are birds that seek their food."

"I traveled through the land seeking the good of provision. Allah said, "When you travel through the land," meaning when you journey."

- Lisan al-Arab (Ibn Manzur), vol. 1, p. 544, entry ḍaraba, travel

Al-Jawhari's al-Sihah stacks the senses in one entry, to strike a person, to travel for provision, to set a parable. The same page carries the verb for time's dealings and the wound's throbbing beside them.

[ ضرب ] ضربه يضربه ضربا . وضرب في الأرض ضربا ومضربا بالفتح ، أي سار في ابتغاء الرزق . يقال : إن في ألف درهم لمضربا ، أي ضربا . و ( ضرب الله مثلا ) ، أي وصف وبين

ḍarabahu yaḍribuhu ḍarban, wa-ḍaraba fī al-arḍi ḍarban wa-maḍraban bi-l-fatḥ, ayy sāra fī ibtighāʾi ar-rizq, yuqālu inna fī alf dirhamin la-maḍraban ayy ḍarban, wa-ḍaraba Allāhu mathalan ayy waṣafa wa-bayyan

"The verb daraba means he struck him, and he strikes him, striking. Daraba fi al-ard means that he travelled in search of provision."

"One may say, "A thousand dirhams offer much room for travel," meaning much travel. "Allah sets forth an example" means that He describes and makes it clear."

- al-Sihah (al-Jawhari), vol. 1, p. 192, entry ḍaraba

Then the entry reaches the sense that dominates the Qur'an's usage.

ضَرْبُ الأَمْثالِ ، وهو اعْتبارُ الشيء بغيره وتمثيلُه به

ḍarbu al-amthāl, wa-huwa iʿtibāru ash-shayʾi bi-ghayrihi wa-tamthīluhu bih

"Setting forth parables means comparing one thing with another and representing it through that thing."

- Lisan al-Arab (Ibn Manzur), vol. 1, p. 550, entry ḍaraba, similes

The dictionaries fix the range before the Qur'an is even opened. The verb strikes bodies, coins money, crosses land, seals ears, and sets up similes. That is the pool a translator chooses from at 4:34, and the pool is the first fact against the claim that one sense is forced.

How the Qur'an Itself Uses the Verb

The attack's own appendix counts the verb's occurrences and finds more physical uses than figurative ones. The count is right and the conclusion does not follow, because the verse's own context is what selects a sense. The Qur'an's repeated command uses the verb to set up parables, and no hand and no body appears in any of them. At Qur'an 29:64 the same imperative form as 4:34's commands the Prophet ﷺ to strike the simile of the worldly life, water from the sky that feeds plants and then dries to stubble. At Qur'an 36:13 it strikes the simile of the town whose people mocked the messengers.

وَاضْرِبْ لَهُمْ مَثَلًا أَصْحابَ الْقَرْيَةِ إِذْ جاءَهَا الْمُرْسَلُون

wa-iḍrib lahum mathalan aṣḥāba al-qaryati idh jāʾahā al-mursalūn

"Give them the example of the people of the town, when the messengers came to it."

- Qur'an 36:13, as embedded in Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi (al-Qubaysi al-Amili), vol. 4, p. 151

Two imperative uses, both the same form as 4:34's, both meaning set forth a parable. The lexicon closes the loop with the cave verse, quoting the Qur'an inside the dictionary entry and glossing the verb with the grammarian al-Zajjaj.

وقوله تعالى : فَضَرَبنا على آذانهم في الكَهْفِ سنينَ عَدَداً ؛ قال الزجّاج : مَنَعْناهم السَّمْعَ أَن يَسْمَعُوا

wa-qawluhu taʿālā fa-ḍarabnā ʿalā ādhānihim fī al-kahfi sinīna ʿadadā, qāla az-Zajjāj manaʿnāhumu as-samʿa an yasmaʿū

"Allah said, "We sealed their hearing in the cave for many years." Al-Zajjaj said, “We prevented them from hearing."

- Lisan al-Arab (Ibn Manzur), vol. 1, p. 550, on Qur'an 18:11

A modern commentator states the point in plain terms at the verse where the verb means to turn away.

والضرب هنا : بمعنى التنحي والابتعاد والإهمال ، تقول : ضربت عن فلان صفحا ، إذا أعرضت عنه وتركته ، والصفح : مصدر صفحت عنه ، إذا أعرضت عنه ، وذلك بأن تعطيه صفحة وجهك أي : جانبه .

وهو منصوب لنضرب من غير لفظه ، كما في قولهم : قعدت جلوسا .

أو على الحال من الفاعل : على المصدرية أي : صافحين

"Here, darb means turning away, keeping distant, and leaving alone. You say, "I turned away from someone," when you leave him."

"Safh is the verbal noun of "I turned away from him." This means that you give him the side of your face. It stands in the accusative as a verbal noun outside the wording of the verb, like their saying, "I sat a sitting." It may also describe the subject as a verbal noun, meaning people who turn away."

- al-Tantawi, al-Tafsir al-Wasit, vol. 13, p. 61, on Qur'an 43:5

So the usage survey the attack itself demands, run honestly, widens the verb at the exact verse in dispute. What the survey cannot do is pick the sense, and no survey of frequency ever could. That choice belongs to the verse's own frame, and the frame is the next two exhibits.

The Verse in Question

Here is the verse, whole, from its corpus tafsir text.

الرِّجالُ قَوَّامُونَ عَلَى النِّساءِ بِما فَضَّلَ اللَّهُ بَعْضَهُمْ عَلى بَعْضٍ وَبِما أَنْفَقُوا مِنْ أَمْوالِهِمْ فَالصَّالِحاتُ قانِتاتٌ حافِظاتٌ لِلْغَيْبِ بِما حَفِظَ اللَّهُ وَاللاَّتِي تَخافُونَ نُشُوزَهُنَّ فَعِظُوهُنَّ وَاهْجُرُوهُنَّ فِي الْمَضاجِعِ وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ فَإِنْ أَطَعْنَكُمْ فَلا تَبْغُوا عَلَيْهِنَّ سَبِيلاً إِنَّ اللَّهَ كانَ عَلِيًّا كَبِيراً (34)

ar-rijālu qawwāmūna ʿalā an-nisāʾi bi-mā faḍḍala Allāhu baʿḍahum ʿalā baʿḍin wa-bi-mā anfaqū min amwālihim, fa-aṣ-ṣāliḥātu qānitātun ḥāfiẓātun lil-ghaybi bi-mā ḥafiẓa Allāh, wa-allātī takhāfūna nushūzahunna fa-ʿiẓūhunna wa-hjurūhunna fī al-maḍājiʿi wa-iḍribūhunna, fa-in aṭaʿnakum fa-lā tabghū ʿalayhinna sabīlā, inna Allāha kāna ʿaliyyan kabīrā

"Men are responsible for women because Allah has given some of them advantages over others, and because they spend from their wealth. Righteous women are devoted and guard what is unseen through what Allah has guarded."

"As for women whose defiance you fear, advise them, leave them in their beds, and strike them. If they obey you, seek no way against them."

"Allah is Most High, Most Great."

- Qur'an 4:34, as embedded in Tafsir al-Sufi al-Falsafi (Muhammad Ghazi Arabi), vol. 1, p. 126

Three steps run in order, counsel, then the bed, then the verb in dispute, and a cap closes them, once they obey, no course against them. The verse gives no measure for its verb, and a permission without a measure is exactly what the rest of the sources supply. The next verse is part of the same passage, and the attack never quotes it.

وَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ شِقاقَ بَيْنِهِما فَابْعَثُوا حَكَماً مِنْ أَهْلِهِ وَحَكَماً مِنْ أَهْلِها إِنْ يُرِيدا إِصْلاحاً يُوَفِّقِ اللَّهُ بَيْنَهُما إِنَّ اللَّهَ كانَ عَلِيماً خَبِيراً (35)

wa-in khiftum shiqāqa baynahumā fabʿathū ḥakaman min ahlihi wa-ḥakaman min ahlihā, in yurīdā iṣlāḥan yuwaffiqi Allāhu baynahumā, inna Allāha kāna ʿalīman khabīrā

"If you fear a split between them, send an arbiter from his family and an arbiter from her family. If they both seek reconciliation, Allah will bring harmony between them."

"Allah is All-Knowing, All-Aware."

- Qur'an 4:35, as embedded in Tafsir al-Sufi al-Falsafi (Muhammad Ghazi Arabi), vol. 1, p. 126

The passage ends not in a blow but in a tribunal, two arbiters, one from each family, and Allah's help for their mending. Whatever the disputed verb permits, the frame around it is a family court, and the lexicons say the verb itself never had to mean a blow.

2. The Farewell Sermon Bounds the Verse

The Clause the Attack Drops

Event: the farewell address, Arafat, 10 AH

Sources: al-Milani, Nafahat al-Azhar 2:71, and al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan 4:412

Claim: the Prophet's ﷺ own reading of 4:34 bounds the act to a strike that is not severe

The attack quotes the farewell address from Guillaume's translation and concedes in passing that the beating is not to be severe. What it never shows is the sermon's own text, because the Arabic carries the bound inside the permission itself, in the same sentence, beside the command the attack omits entirely.

أيها الناس ! إن لنسائكم عليكم حقا وإن لكم عليهن حقا ، لكم عليهن ألا يوطئن فرشكم غيركم ، ولا يدخلن أحدا تكرهونه بيوتكم إلا بإذنكم ، ولا يأتين بفاحشة ، فإن فعلن فإن الله قد أذن لكم أن تعضلوهن وتهجروهن في المضاجع وتضربوهن ضربا غير مبرح ، فإن انتهين وأطعنكم فعليكم رزقهن وكسوتهن بالمعروف .

وإن النساء عندكم عوار لا يملكن لأنفسهن شيئا ، أخذتموهن بأمانة الله ، واستحللتم فروجهن بكلمة الله ، فاتقوا الله في النساء واستوصوا بهن خيرا

"People, your wives have a right over you, and you have a right over them. Their duty to you is that they do not let anyone you dislike sit on your beds, and that they do not admit into your homes anyone you dislike without your permission."

"They must not commit open sexual wrongdoing. If they do so, Allah has permitted you to restrain them, leave them in their beds, and strike them without causing injury."

"If they stop and obey you, you must provide their food and clothing in a fair way. Women are entrusted to your care and do not control anything for themselves."

"You took them through Allah's trust, and you made sexual relations lawful through Allah's word. So fear Allah regarding women, and treat them well."

- The Prophet's ﷺ farewell sermon, in al-Milani, Nafahat al-Azhar, vol. 2, p. 71

Read the sequence of the sermon's own sentences. The permission comes conditioned, a strike that is ghayr mubriḥ, not severe, not injurious. The moment she stops, the husband's duty resumes, food and clothing in fairness. Then the sermon names the standing posture toward wives, they are a trust held from Allah, so fear Allah regarding them and treat them well. The permission and the mercy are one passage, spoken to the whole community in the Prophet's ﷺ last public address. A quotation that keeps the permission and drops the condition and the command has not summarized the sermon. It has cut it.

The Same Words in al-Tabari

The Sunni tradition's own oldest running tafsir carries the same bounded permission through a chain that runs through the Imams ﵈.

حدثني يونس بن سليمان البصري ، قال : ثنا حاتم بن إسماعيل ، قال : ثنا جعفر بن محمد ، عن أبيه ، عن جابر أن رسول الله (ص) ، قال : اتقوا الله في النساء ، فإنكم أخذتموهن بأمانة الله ، واستحللتم فروجهن بكلمة الله ، وإن لكم عليهن أن لا يوطئن فرشكم أحدا تكرهونه ، فإن فعلن ذلك فاضربوهن ضربا غير مبرح ولهن عليكم رزقهن وكسوتهن بالمعروف

"Yunus ibn Sulayman al-Basri reported to me. He said, Hatim ibn Ismail reported to us."

"He said, Jafar ibn Muhammad reported to us from his father, from Jabir, that the Messenger of Allah said, “Fear Allah regarding women. You took them through Allah's trust, and you made sexual relations lawful through Allah's word."

"They must not let anyone you dislike sit on your beds. If they do that, strike them without causing injury."

"They have the right to food and clothing from you in a fair way.”"

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, from his father Imam al-Baqir ﵇, from Jabir, in al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan, vol. 4, p. 412

Al-Tabari heard the report through Jafar ibn Muhammad from his father from Jabir, the Imams ﵈ in the middle of a Sunni chain, and its wording bounds the strike before it grants it. Both camps therefore transmit the same boundary from the farewell hajj. The sermon's clause is not a Shia reading laid over a Sunni text. It is the oldest gloss both traditions share, and it was in the attack's own source material all along.

3. The Imams Bind the Act to the Symbolic

A Strike the Siwak Can Carry

Gloss chain: the Prophet's ﷺ ruling, then Ibn Abbas, then Imam al-Baqir ﵇, then the jurists

Sources: al-Tamhid fi Ulum al-Qur'an 7:172-173; Mawsu'at al-Qur'an al-Azim 2:780

Claim: the school reduces the permitted act to a strike with the siwak that leaves no trace

The bound ghayr mubriḥ gets its content from the reports that define it. Al-Ma'rifat collects them in one passage.

أخرج ابن‌جرير عن عكرمة - في الآية - قال : قال رسول اللّه ﵌ : «اضربوهنّ إذا عصينكم في المعروف ، ضربا غير مبرّح» .

ورواه أيضا بإسناده عن حجّاج مضيفا إليه تفسيره «غير مبرّح» بغير مؤثّر .

يعنى : لايؤثّر في تغيير لون البشرة ، حتّى الحمرة .

وعن عطاء قال : قلت لابن عبّاس : ما الضرب غير المبرّح ؟ قال : بالسواك ونحوه .

وعن قتادة : ضربا غير مبرّح أي غير شائن

"Ibn Jarir reports from Ikrimah about this verse. The Messenger of Allah said, "Strike them if they disobey you in what is right, with a strike that does no harm." He also reports it through his chain from Hajjaj, who explained "does no harm" as a blow that leaves no effect."

"It does not change the color of the skin, even causing redness. Ata said, "I asked Ibn Abbas, ‘What is a strike that does no harm?’" Ibn Abbas replied, "With a siwak or something similar." Qatadah said, "A strike that does no harm means one that does not disgrace her.""

- The Prophet's ﷺ ruling, with Ibn Abbas' gloss, in al-Ma'rifat, al-Tamhid fi Ulum al-Qur'an, vol. 7, p. 172

Three definitions stack up. The Prophet's ﷺ own ruling, strike them if they disobey in what is right, with a strike not severe. The relay through al-Hajjaj, not severe means it leaves no effect, it does not change the skin's color, not even to red. And Ibn Abbas, asked directly what the not-severe strike is, answers, with the siwak or the like. The same gloss stands in the encyclopedia the attack could have consulted.

وسئل ابن عباس في تفسير الحديث فقال : اضربوهن بالسواك ونحوه

"Ibn Abbas was asked about the meaning of this report. He said, "Strike them with a siwak or something similar.""

- Ibn Abbas' answer, in al-Hafni, Mawsu'at al-Qur'an al-Azim, vol. 2, p. 780

The school's own Imam gives the same answer, and the jurist writes it into law.

الإمام أبو جعفر الباقر ﵇ : هو بالسواك

"Imam Abu Jafar al-Baqir said, "It is with a siwak.""

- Imam al-Baqir ﵇, in al-Ma'rifat, al-Tamhid fi Ulum al-Qur'an, vol. 7, p. 173

قال القاضي ابن‌البرّاج الطرابلسي ﵋ : وأمّا الضرب فهو ضرب تأديب ، كما يضرب الصبيان على الذنب ، ولا يضربها ضربا مُبرّحا ولا مزمنا ولا مدميا ويفرّقه على بدنها ويتّقي وجهها . وإذا ضربها كذلك فليكن بالمسواك

"Judge Ibn al-Barraj al-Tarabulsi said, "Striking is discipline, like striking children for wrongdoing. He must not strike her in a way that causes lasting harm or draws blood."

"He should spread the blows over her body and avoid her face. If he strikes her in this way, he should use a siwak.""

- Ibn al-Barraj al-Tarabulsi, in al-Ma'rifat, al-Tamhid fi Ulum al-Qur'an, vol. 7, p. 173

Follow the chain of authorities. Ibn Abbas says siwak. Imam al-Baqir ﵇ says siwak. Ibn al-Barraj turns both into the statute, discipline like a child's correction, nothing chronic, nothing bloody, no blow to the face, and the instrument is the toothbrush stick. A strike the siwak can deliver is a gesture, a physical punctuation of a warning, and that is the whole content the school gives the verse's verb. The green bruise of the attack's showcase hadith is outside this permission by every definition in the chain.

"The Messenger of Allah said, "The best of you are the best to their wives, and I am the best of you to my wives.""

- The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, in al-Saduq, Man la Yahduruhu al-Faqih, vol. 3, p. 443, no. 4538

"Al-Sadiq said, "May Allah have mercy on a servant who treats his wife well. Allah has placed her forelock in his hand and made him responsible for her.""

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Saduq, Man la Yahduruhu al-Faqih, vol. 3, p. 443, no. 4537

The Prophet ﷺ names himself the best of his community to his own wives, and makes that excellence the measure of a man. Aisha Names the Man Who Never Struck

The school's rule has a witness the attack's own tradition transmits.

"Aishah reported, "The Messenger of Allah never struck anything with his hand. He never struck a woman or a servant, except while striving in the path of Allah."

- Aisha, Sahih Muslim, relayed in al-Fayruzabadi, Fada'il al-Khamsa min al-Sihah al-Sitta, vol. 1, p. 133

Aisha, the same wife whose case the attack presses into service, swears in Muslim's collection that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ never struck anything with his hand, never a woman, never a servant, nothing but jihad in Allah's path, and that no one who wronged him ever faced his revenge unless Allah's own limits were violated. The man the attack must paint as a beater is the man his own household describes as never striking at all. That report settles what the bounded permission was in his practice, a limit he legislated for others and never once needed himself.

4. The Verse's Own Frame Is Arbitration

The Sequence the School Reads

Passage: Qur'an 4:34-35 and 4:128, one sura, one subject

Sources: Tafsir al-Qummi 1:163; al-Kafi 11:697; Jawahir al-Kalam 31:200; Tafsir Ibn Kathir 3:510; Anwar al-Tanzil 2:101

Claim: nushuz is marital breakdown, and the passage's remedy is arbiters and settlement

The school's tafsir maps the verse's steps as a process with an exit, not a license with no floor.

وقوله (واللاتي تخافون نشوزهن فعظوهن واهجروهن في المضاجع واضربوهن ، فان اطعنكم فلا تبغوا عليهن سبيلا) وذلك أن نشزت المرأة عن فراش زوجها قال زوجها اتقى الله وارجعي إلى فراشك ، فهذه الموعظة ، فان اطاعته فسبيل ذلك وإلا سبها وهو الهجر فان رجعت إلى فراشها فذلك وإلا ضربها ضربا غير مبرح فان اطاعته وضاجعته يقول الله " فان أطعنكم فلا تبغوا عليهن سبيلا " يقول لا تكلفوهن الحب

"Allah says, "As for those women whose defiance you fear, advise them, leave them alone in their beds, and strike them. If they obey you, do not seek a way against them." When a woman turned away from her husband's bed, he would say, "Fear Allah and return to your bed." This was the advice."

"If she obeyed him, that was enough. If she refused, he would insult her."

"This was leaving her alone. If she returned to his bed, that was enough."

"If she refused, he would strike her with a blow that did no harm. If she obeyed him and shared his bed, Allah says, "If they obey you, do not seek a way against them." This means, do not demand love from them."

- The reading of the verse's sequence, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 1, p. 163

Each step answers a refusal with the mildest available move, counsel, then the bed, then the bounded act, and the cap closes the whole sequence, do not demand love from them. The end the verse seeks is restored marriage, and the means stop at the point of return.

Two Arbiters, Not Two Fists

The same tafsir then reads the next verse into working procedure, and the procedure is a hearing.

وقوله (وان خفتم شقاق بينهما فابعثوا حكما من أهله وحكما من أهلها) فبما حكم به الحكمان فهو جائز يقول الله (ان يريدا اصلاحا يوفق الله بينهما بعني الحكمين فإذا كانا عدلين دخل حكم المرأة فيقول أخبريني ما في نفسك ، فاني لا أحب ان اقطع شيئا دونك ، فان كانت هي الناشزة قالت اعطوه من مالي ما شاء وفرق بيني وبينه ، وان لم تكن ناشزة قالت أنشدك الله ان لا تفرق بيني وبينه ، ولكن استزد لي في النفقة فإنه مسيئ ويخلو حكم الرجل يجئ إلى الرجل فيقول حدثني بما في نفسك فاني لا أحب ان اقطع شيئا دونك ، فإن كان هو الناشز قال خذ لي منها ما استطعت وفرق بيني وبينها فلا حاجة لي فيها

"Allah says, "If you fear a split between them, send an arbiter from his family and an arbiter from her family." Whatever the two arbiters decide is binding. Allah says, "If they seek reconciliation, Allah will bring them together." This refers to the two arbiters."

"If they are just, the woman's arbiter goes in and says, "Tell me what is in your heart. I do not want to decide anything without you." If she is the one who has turned away, she says, "Give him whatever he wants from my property and separate me from him." If she has not turned away, she says, "I ask you by Allah, do not separate me from him."

"Ask him to increase my maintenance, because he mistreats me." The man's arbiter then meets the man alone and says, "Tell me what is in your heart. I do not want to decide anything without you." If the man has turned away, he says, "Take from her for me whatever you can, and separate me from her."

"I have no need of her.""

- The arbitration procedure, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 1, p. 163

The woman's arbiter asks her in private what she wants. The man's arbiter asks him the same. Either side can end the marriage, she by giving property, he by releasing her, and the two arbiters seek the repair. The verse's machinery is testimony, deliberation, and settlement. Nothing in it runs through a husband's hand.

The Wife's Nushuz in the Same Sura

The attack's appendix claims nushuz means simple disobedience about household matters. The sura itself uses the word again, and this time it is the wife who fears it from the husband.

فأما قضية سودة ففي الصحيح عن عائشة رضي الله ﵎ عنها وهي سبب نزول قوله تعالى (وإن امرأة خافت من بعلها نشوزا أو إعراضا فلا جناح عليهما أن يصلحا بينهما صلحا)

"As for the case of Sawdah, Aishah reports it in the Sahih collection. It was the reason for the revelation of Allah's words, "If a woman fears defiance or neglect from her husband, there is no blame on them if they make peace between themselves.""

- Ibn Kathir, Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Azim, vol. 3, p. 510, on Qur'an 4:128

( وَإِنِ امْرَأَةٌ خافَتْ مِنْ بَعْلِها ) * توقعت منه لما ظهر لها من المخايل ، وامرأة فاعل فعل يفسره الظاهر .

* ( نُشُوزاً ) * تجافيا عنها وترفعا عن صحبتها كراهة لها ومنعا لحقوقها .

* ( أَوْ إِعْراضاً ) * بأن يقل مجالستها ومحادثتها .

* ( فَلا جُناحَ عَلَيْهِما أَنْ يُصْلِحا بَيْنَهُما صُلْحاً ) * أن يتصالحا بأن تحط له بعض المهر ، أو القسم ، أو تهب له شيئا تستميله به

""If a woman fears defiance from her husband" means she expects it from signs that have become clear to her. "Defiance" means that he draws away from her, rises above sharing life with her because he dislikes her, and withholds her rights. "Or neglect" means he spends less time sitting and speaking with her. "There is no blame on them if they make peace between themselves" means they may settle matters between themselves. She may give up part of her dowry, part of her allotted nights, or give him something to win his affection."

- al-Baydawi, Anwar al-Tanzil, vol. 2, p. 101, on Qur'an 4:128

In Ibn Kathir the verse came down over Sawdah, a wife whose husband was turning from her, and the remedy is settlement. In al-Baydawi the nushuz is the husband's, his drawing away and withholding her rights. The word therefore cannot mean the wife's obedience about housework. It names the breakdown itself, from whichever side it comes.

What Nushuz Names

The school's jurisprudence fixes the term.

( القول في النشوز : ) ( وهو الخروج ) من الزوج أو الزوجة ( عن الطاعة ) الواجبة على كل واحد منهما للآخر ( وأصله ) لغة ( الارتفاع ) يقال : نشز الرجل ينشز إذا كان قاعدا فنهض قائما ، ومنه قوله تعالى (1) : " وإذا قيل انشزوا فانشزوا " أي انهضوا إلى أمر من أمور الله تعالى ، وسمى خروج أحدهما نشوزا لأنه بمعصيته قد ارتفع عما أوجب الله تعالى عليه من ذلك للآخر ، قيل : ولذلك خص النشوز بما إذا كان الخروج من أحدهما ، لأن الخارج ارتفع على الآخر فلم يقم بحقه ، ولو كان الخروج منهما معا خص باسم الشقاق كما يأتي ، لاستوائهما معا في الارتفاع ، فلم يتحقق ارتفاع أحدهما عن الآخر ، وقال بعضهم : يجوز إطلاق النشوز على ذلك أيضا

"The discussion of nushuz, meaning marital defiance, is when a husband or wife leaves the obedience that each owes the other. Its linguistic root means rising."

"People say that a man rose when he had been sitting and then stood up. From this comes Allah's saying, "When you are told to rise, then rise, which means rising for one of Allah's commands."

"A spouse's departure from what Allah requires toward the other spouse is called nushuz because, through disobedience, that person has risen above the duty Allah placed upon them. Some say this term applies when one spouse leaves that duty, because the departing spouse has risen above the other and failed to fulfill that person's right."

"When both leave their duties together, it is called shiqaq, meaning a split between them, because both have risen equally and neither has risen above the other. Others say that nushuz may also be used for this case."

- al-Najafi, Jawahir al-Kalam, vol. 31, p. 200, the definition of nushuz

Nushuz is one spouse's exit from the obedience owed the other, and when both exit, the Qur'an itself calls it shiqaq, the breach that Qur'an 4:35 answers with arbiters. The Imam's ﵇ own reading of the wife's nushuz case shows the remedy working.

عَلِيُّ بْنُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ ، عَنِ ابْنِ أَبِي عُمَيْرٍ ، عَنْ حَمَّادٍ ، عَنِ الْحَلَبِيِّ : عَنْ أَبِي عَبْدِ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ ، قَالَ : سَأَلْتُهُ عَنْ قَوْلِ اللَّهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ : « وَإِنِ امْرَأَةٌ خافَتْ مِنْ بَعْلِها نُشُوزاً أَوْ إِعْراضاً » ؟ فَقَالَ : « هِيَ الْمَرْأَةُ (6) تَكُونُ عِنْدَ الرَّجُلِ ، فَيَكْرَهُهَا ، فَيَقُولُ لَهَا : إِنِّي أُرِيدُ أَنْ أُطَلِّقَكِ ، فَتَقُولُ لَهُ : لَاتَفْعَلْ ؛ إِنِّي أَكْرَهُ أَنْ تُشْمَتَ (7) بِي ، وَلكِنِ انْظُرْ (8) فِي (9) لَيْلَتِي ، فَاصْنَعْ بِهَا (10) مَا شِئْتَ ، وَمَا كَانَ سِوى ذلِكَ مِنْ شَيْءٍ فَهُوَ لَكَ ، وَدَعْنِي عَلى حَالَتِي ، فَهُوَ قَوْلُهُ تَبَارَكَ وَتَعَالى : « فَلا جُناحَ عَلَيْهِما أَنْ يُصْلِحا بَيْنَهُما صُلْحاً » (11) وَهُوَ هذَا (12) الصُّلْح

"Ali ibn Ibrahim reports from his father, from Ibn Abi Umayr, from Hammad, from al-Halabi. Al-Halabi said, "I asked Abu Abd Allah about Allah's words, ‘If a woman fears defiance or neglect from her husband.’" Abu Abd Allah replied, "This is a woman who lives with a man, and he comes to dislike her."

"He says to her, ‘I want to divorce you.’ She says, ‘Do not do it. I hate for people to rejoice at my misfortune."

"Look at my night and do with it whatever you wish. Everything else is yours."

"Leave me as I am.’ This is what Allah says, ‘There is no blame on them if they make peace between themselves.’ This is that settlement.""

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, al-Kafi (Dar al-Hadith ed.), vol. 11, p. 697, no. 11020

The wife the verse protects is the one being discarded. She keeps her standing and her maintenance by settling, and the Imam ﵇ calls that settlement the very peace the verse names. A sura that ends a wife's abandonment with arbitration and maintenance does not license her beating in the same breath. The two readings of the passage belong to one subject, marital breakdown, and the breakdown's cure in both halves is settlement.

5. The Hadith Battery Answers Itself

The Green Bruise Works Against the Husband

Reports answered: Bukhari's green bruise, Muslim's Abu Jahm, the Umar sequence, the no-asking report, the chest push, the fist

Claim: the battery's best reports condemn beating or come from figures acting alone

The attack's centerpiece is Bukhari's report of the woman whose skin was greener than her clothes. Here is the report from the corpus.

"Muhammad ibn Bashshar told us, and Abd al-Wahhab told us. Ayyub reported from Ikrimah that Rifa'ah divorced his wife."

"Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Zubayr al-Qurazi then married her. Aishah said that the woman came wearing a green head covering."

"She complained to Aishah and showed her bruises on her skin. When the Messenger of Allah arrived, the women supported one another."

"Aishah said, "I have never seen believing women suffer as this woman does. Her skin is greener than her garment." Her husband heard that she had gone to the Messenger of Allah."

"He came with two sons from another wife. The woman said, "By Allah, I have no complaint against him except that what he has is no more useful to me than this." She took a fringe from her garment."

"He said, "By Allah, Messenger of Allah, she lies. I satisfy her vigorously, like a tanned hide being shaken."

"She is defiant and wants Rifa'ah." The Messenger of Allah said, "If that is so, you will not become lawful for him until he tastes your sweetness." - The report of Rifa'ah's wife, Bukhari, Sahih, in al-Naqdi, Abqat al-Anwar, vol. 22, p. 175

Read what the report actually contains. A beaten woman reaches the Prophet ﷺ and is heard. Her bruise is displayed and recorded, in a tradition the community preserved for centuries, which is how it became the attack's evidence at all. The Prophet ﷺ rules on the remarriage question the case raised and never utters one word approving the blow. Aisha's cry, that she never saw women suffer as the believing women suffer, is a complaint against the husbands of her community, spoken to the Prophet ﷺ to be answered. The report is a case file of a woman seeking justice, and it entered the canon as a warning against the men in it. The husband's own defense is that she was nashiza, wanting her former husband, which is a dispute over marital intent, and the Prophet's ﷺ ruling addresses that dispute, not the bruise. On the report's own words, the bruise is evidence heard, the woman is believed about her injury, and the man stands accused before the community's judge.

Abu Jahm Disqualified for Beating

The attack reads the Abu Jahm reports as proof that beating was lawful. The reports say the opposite in their first clause.

قالت فلما حللت أخبرته أن معاوية وأبا جهم خطباني فقال " أما معاوية فصعلوك لا مال له وأما أبو جهم فلا يضع عصاه عن عاتقه انكحي أسامة

"When my waiting period ended, I told him that Muawiyah and Abu Jahm had proposed to me. He said, "Muawiyah is a poor man with no wealth."

"Abu Jahm does not put his staff down from his shoulder. Marry Usamah.""

- The Prophet's ﷺ words to Fatima bint Qays, in al-Muzni, Mukhtasar al-Muzni, p. 171

"Sahih Muslim states clearly that he was the man meant. The words, "a man who strikes often," also appear in another report as, "He does not put down his stick from his shoulder." This means that he struck women often."

- al-Nawawi, al-Majmu', vol. 16, p. 180, on Abu Jahm

A woman asks the Prophet ﷺ which of two suitors to marry. He rules out Muawiya as a penniless man and Abu Jahm as a man who does not put his staff down from his shoulder, and al-Nawawi records the tradition's own gloss that the phrase means his repeated beating of women. The tradition treats habitual beating as a disqualifying defect, named to a bride as grounds to refuse the man. The report the attack cites as a permit was recorded by Muslim as a warning label.

The Standing Rule and the Umar Sequence

The Umar cluster is the battery's backbone, and its own text carries its own ending.

لا تضربوا إماء الله " قال فأتاه عمر ﵁ فقال يا رسول الله ذئر النساء على أزواجهن فأذن في ضربهن فأطاف بآل محمد نساء كثير كلهن يشتكين أزواجهن فقال ﵌ " لقد أطاف بآل محمد سبعون امرأة كلهن يشتكين أزواجهن فلا تجدون أولئك خياركم " ويحتمل أن يكون قوله ﵇ قبل نزول الآية بضربهن ثم أذن فجعل لهم الضرب فأخبر أن الاختيار ترك الضرب

"The Prophet said, "Do not strike the female servants of Allah." Umar came to him and said, "Messenger of Allah, the women have become bold toward their husbands." He then permitted them to strike their wives. Many women came around the household of Muhammad, all complaining about their husbands."

"He said, "Seventy women have come around the household of Muhammad, all complaining about their husbands. Those men are not your best people." This may have occurred before the verse permitting their beating was revealed."

"He later gave permission, then explained that the better choice was to refrain from beating."

- The Umar sequence, in al-Muzni, Mukhtasar al-Muzni, p. 186

واختلفت الأخبارُ في ضرب النساء ، فروي عن النبي ﵌ أنه قال : " لا تضربوا إماء الله " (1) فجاء عمر وقال : " يا رسول الله [ذَئِر] (2) النساء أي [نشزن] (3) واجترأن ، فأذِن في ضربهن ، فبسط الرجال أيديهم إلى ضرب النساء ، وجاوزوا الحد ، فأطاف بآل محمد أي : بحُجَرِ نسائه وأهل بيته ﵌ نساءٌ كثيرات كلهن يشتكين أزواجهن ، فخرج ﵌ وقال : لقد أطافَ الليلة بآل محمد سبعون امرأة كلُّهن يشتكين أزواجهن ! ! خيارُكم خيارُكم لنسائكم ، لا يضربن أحدُكم ظعينتَهُ ضربه أَمَته

"Reports differ about beating women. The Prophet said, "Do not strike the female servants of Allah." Umar came and said, "Messenger of Allah, the women have become defiant and bold." He permitted their beating."

"The men began striking women and exceeded the limit. Many women then came to the household of Muhammad, meaning the rooms of his wives and family, all complaining about their husbands."

"He came out and said, "Seventy women came around the household of Muhammad tonight, all complaining about their husbands. The best of you are the best to your wives."

"None of you should beat his wife as he beats his slave woman.""

- al-Juwayni, Nihayat al-Matlab, vol. 13, p. 274

The standing rule is the Prophet's ﷺ own, do not beat Allah's handmaids. Umar then presses him, the women have grown bold toward their husbands, and permission follows. The men who took that permission beat past every bound, and seventy women came to the Prophet's ﷺ household in one night. His answer closes the file. Those men are the worst of you, or in al-Juwayni's wording, the best of you are the best to your wives, and none of you should beat his wife as a master beats a slave girl. Ibn Majah's own footnote, which the attack's 2011 revision concedes, says the closing phrase refers to the men who beat. The sequence therefore shows a community permission abused within hours, then condemned by the Prophet ﷺ in the strongest terms his office allowed. The attack prints the middle of the story and stops before the ending.

The Report Ibn Majah's Own Note Explains

ولمّا ضرب ابن عمر زوجته سألوه عن السبب فقال : سمعت رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم يقول : « لا يسأل الرجل فيم ضرب أهله

"When Ibn Umar struck his wife, people asked him why. He said, "I heard the Messenger of Allah say, 'A man is not asked why he struck his wife.'""

- Ibn Umar, in al-Hafni, Mawsu'at al-Qur'an al-Azim, vol. 2, p. 780

The no-asking report comes from Ibn Umar explaining why he beat his own wife, and the encyclopedia's context around it gives Ibn Abbas' definition of the permitted strike as the siwak. The report never says the beating was righteous. It records what a man said to justify himself, in the same literature that transmits the Prophet's ﷺ rebuke of exactly such men.

The Chest Report and the Fist

The attack's two personal reports about the Prophet's ﷺ household deserve separate statements. Muslim's report has Aisha following him at night to Baqi, being caught, and receiving a single push that hurt, and the corpus text of this relay locates the push in her back.

فأنت السّواد الّذي رأيت أمامي ، قلت : نعم ، فلهزني في ظهري (3) لهزة فأوجعتني وقال : أظننت أن يحيف عليك اللّه ورسوله

"He said, "You were the dark shape that I saw before me." I said, "Yes." He then struck me once on the back, and it hurt me. He said, "Did you think that Allah and His Messenger would treat you unjustly?""

- Aisha's account of the night she followed him, al-Askari, Ahadith Umm al-Mu'minin Aisha, vol. 1, p. 33

The report is Aisha's own telling, in her own voice, of a night she was frightened and found out. It is not a beating, it carries no permission, and the school reads such reports about the Prophet ﷺ against the Book, which says he does not speak from desire, and against Aisha's own sworn report in the same collection that he never struck anything with his hand. A report and its direct contradiction cannot both be licenses.

The fist report has Abu Bakr striking Aisha violently with his fist over the lost necklace episode. The actor is the school's enemy class acting on his own, Aisha's own narration presents herself as wronged by the blow, and the Prophet ﷺ is asleep in the report and does nothing but lie beside her. The corpus search for this report's Arabic returned no verbatim text, so it is answered here on the attack's own English quotation. A father-in-law's fist is not the verse's permission. It is the conduct the verse's own tradition was rebuking in the same century.

6. The Attack's Own Authorities Bind the Act

Tabari's Own Limit

Method: the tafsirs the attack marshals, quoted at the lines the attack omits

Sources: Jami' al-Bayan 5:82; Tafsir Ibn Kathir (Beirut ed.) 2:258; Ma'alim al-Tanzil 1:387; al-Majmu' 16:449

Claim: the uniformity the attack advertises is a uniformity of bounds it never reports

The attack calls Tabari's commentary evidence for the permission. Tabari's text carries the bound in the same line.

عن جويبر ، عن الضحاك في قوله : * (الرجال قوامون على النساء بما فضل الله بعضهم على بعض) * يقول : الرجل قائم على المرأة يأمرها بطاعة الله ، فإن أبت ، فله أن يضربها ضربا غير مبرح ، وله عليها الفضل بنفقته وسعيه

"Juwaybir reported from al-Dahhak concerning the words, "Men are responsible for women because Allah has favored some over others." He said, “A man is responsible for a woman and commands her to obey Allah. If she refuses, he may strike her without causing serious injury."

"He has a degree over her because he provides for her and works for her support.”"

- al-Dahhak's reading, in al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan, vol. 5, p. 82

The verb 4:34 uses, Tabari's own authority says, reaches only a strike that is not severe. The permission and the ceiling are one sentence in the tradition the attack quotes first.

Ibn Kathir Prints the Bound

وفي السنن والمسند عن معاوية بن حيدة القشيري أنه قال : يا رسول اللّه ما حق امرأة أحدنا عليه ؟ قال « أن تطعمها إذا طعمت ، وتكسوها إذا اكتسيت ، ولا تضرب الوجه ، ولا تقبح ، ولا تهجر إلا في البيت » .

وقوله : وَاضْرِبُوهُنَّ ، أي إذا لم يرتد عن بالموعظة ولا بالهجران ، فلكم أن تضربوهن ضربا غير مبرح ، كما ثبت في صحيح مسلم عن جابر عن النبي صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم أنه قال في حجة الوداع « واتقوا اللّه في النساء ، فإنهن عندكم عوان ، ولكم عليهن أن لا يوطئن فرشكم أحدا تكرهونه ، فإن فعلن ذلك فاضربوهن ضربا غير مبرح ، ولهن عليكم رزقهن وكسوتهن بالمعروف

"Muawiyah ibn Haydah al-Qushayri said, "Messenger of Allah, what right does the wife of one of us have over him?" He replied, "Feed her when you eat. Clothe her when you clothe yourself."

"Do not strike her face. Do not insult her."

"Do not leave her except within the home." The words, "Strike them," mean that when advice and separation in bed do not make them return, you may strike them without causing serious injury. Jabir reported in Sahih Muslim that the Prophet said during the Farewell Pilgrimage, "Fear Allah concerning women."

"They are in your care. You have the right that they do not let anyone you dislike enter your beds."

"If they do so, strike them without causing serious injury. They have the right to food and clothing from you according to what is fair.""

- Ibn Kathir, Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Azim (Beirut ed.), vol. 2, p. 258, on Qur'an 4:34

Ibn Kathir prints the Prophet's ﷺ farewell ruling from Muslim with its clause, a strike that is not severe, and beside it the rights of the wife, feed her as you feed yourself, clothe her as you clothe yourself, and never strike her face. The wife's rights passage and the bounded permission stand on the same page of the commentary the attack cites for the permission alone.

Baghawi and al-Shafi'i Close the Case

"This means that if they do not leave off after separation in bed, strike them without causing serious injury or disgrace. Ata said, “Strike with a siwak.” A report from the Prophet says, "A woman's right is that you feed her when you eat, clothe her when you clothe yourself, do not strike her face, do not insult her, and do not leave her except within the home.""

- al-Baghawi, Ma'alim al-Tanzil, vol. 1, p. 387, on Qur'an 4:34

وأما الضرب فقال الشافعي : لا يضربها ضربا مبرحا لا مدميا ولا مدمنا ويتقى الوجه

"As for striking, al-Shafii said, “He must not strike her severely, draw blood, or keep striking her. He must avoid the face."

- al-Shafi'i, in al-Nawawi, al-Majmu', vol. 16, p. 449

فالمبرح الفادح الذي يخشى تلف النفس منه أو تلف عضو أو تشويهه ، والمدمى الذي يجرح فيخرج الدم ، والمدمن أن يوالي الضرب على موضع واحد

"A severe blow is a crushing one that may destroy life, destroy a limb, or deform it. A bloody blow cuts the skin and brings out blood."

"Repeated striking means continuing to strike the same place."

- The definitions of the forbidden blows, in al-Nawawi, al-Majmu', vol. 16, p. 449

Baghawi gives the same bound, not severe and not disfiguring, hands the instrument to Ata's siwak, and carries the no-face rule from the Prophet ﷺ. Al-Shafi'i, the founder of the school Tabari and Ibn Kathir wrote in, rules that the strike must never be severe, bloody, or repeated, and that the face is never to be touched, and his definitions name every blow that threatens life, limb, or shape as forbidden. This is the uniformity the attack's tafsir section actually contains. Every authority it lists binds the act with rules the attack's readers never see. An argument that must hide the center of its own evidence has answered itself.

Final Verdict

The charge that Qur'an 4:34 licenses husbands to scourge disobedient wives fails on five grounds, each carried by the sources the attack itself quotes or could not avoid.

First, the word. The lexicons, Lisan al-Arab and al-Sihah, carry the verb of 4:34 across minting, traveling, sealing, and setting parables, and the Qur'an's own imperative uses of the verb command parables at 29:64 and 36:13. A translator who prints only scourge has chosen the harshest sense and sold it as the only one, against the dictionaries he did not open.

Second, the Prophet's ﷺ own reading. The farewell sermon bounds the verse in the sentence that grants it, a strike that is ghayr mubriḥ, not severe, and closes with the command to fear Allah regarding women and treat them well. Al-Tabari carries the same bounded permission through the Imams ﵈ themselves. The attack quotes the sermon and drops the bound from its own quotation.

Third, the school's chain of definition. The Prophet's ﷺ ruling, Ibn Abbas' gloss, and Imam al-Baqir's ﵇ answer agree that the permitted strike is the siwak, and the jurists wrote it into law, nothing chronic, nothing bloody, no blow to the face. Aisha's own report in Muslim adds that the Prophet ﷺ never struck anything with his hand, no woman, no servant.

Fourth, the verse's own frame. Nushuz names marital breakdown, the wife's as much as the husband's, since 4:128 came down over a wife being abandoned and prescribed settlement, and the school's Jawahir defines the term as either spouse's exit from owed obedience. The passage's remedy is two arbiters, a private hearing for each spouse, and reconciliation. The cap closes it, once she returns, no course against her.

Fifth, the battery. The green bruise is a woman's evidence heard against her husband, with no word of approval from the Prophet ﷺ. Abu Jahm is disqualified for marriage because he beats. The Umar sequence ends with the beaters named the worst of you, per Ibn Majah's own footnote. The chest push is Aisha's own complaint, contradicted by her own sworn report in the same collection, and the fist is Abu Bakr's alone. The tafsirs the attack marshals as uniform carry the same bounds throughout, Tabari's not severe, Ibn Kathir's printed clause, Baghawi's siwak, al-Shafi'i's list of prohibitions.

The verse permits a symbolic act inside a process that ends in arbitration, bounded by the Prophet's ﷺ sermon, defined down to a toothbrush stick by both schools' authorities, and condemned in practice by the reports the attack pressed into service. The scourge is not in the verse. It is in the quotation marks the attack put around the verse's missing clauses.
