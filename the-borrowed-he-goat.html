Qur'an and Hadith Analysis

The Borrowed He-Goat

A man pronounces three divorces in one breath, and the only door back to his wife is a stranger the Prophet ﷺ cursed and named the borrowed he-goat. The Sunni canon itself dates the trap to two years into Umar’s caliphate, and its own law books keep the door open to this day. The primary sources, from the Musnad to the fatwas of Ibn Taymiyya.

The Argument Under Examination

The Charge

The halala marriage, in which a divorced woman marries another man so her next divorce returns her to her first husband, is defended as a divine law the Qur’an itself states. The Sunni canon’s own record tests that defense. The record shows a Prophet’s ﷺ ruling counting three as one, a dated change two years into Umar’s caliphate, a curse on the men who exploit the changed ruling, and fourteen centuries of work to keep the practice the curse condemns.

The Method

The record is built from the Sunni canon and the jurists’ own manuals, and one practice report arrives through an Imami digest, marked as such where it appears. The Imami ruling, that three in one sitting is one divorce, is the Companion-era practice the Sunni canon itself preserves.

Premise 1, the Verse States a Rule for a Real Marriage

Qur’an 2:230 makes a thrice-divorced woman lawful to her first husband only after a real second marriage. The verse describes marriage that is intended as marriage, with its own consummation and its own ending.

Premise 2, the Triple Was One Divorce Under the Prophet ﷺ

Ibn Abbas reports on the chain of Sahih Muslim that three pronounced together counted as one through the era of the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr’s caliphate, and two years of Umar’s . Umar then enforced the triple, and al-Nawawi calls the report problematic for exactly that reason.

Premise 3, the Prophet ﷺ Cursed the Exploiters

Once the triple became instant, 2:230 turned into a door for hired husbands. The Prophet ﷺ named the muhallil the borrowed he-goat and cursed him and his client. The Companions called the practice promiscuity and stoned it.

Premise 4, the Permission Survived the Curse

The Hanafi and Shafi’i schools keep the muhallil’s marriage valid when the tahleel was only intended, early jurists called the muhallil rewarded, and the manuals built five different readings to keep the curse away from the practice it names. The Imami ruling, one divorce in one sitting, keeps the practice of the Prophet ﷺ and empties the trap the curse answers.

Conclusion, the Halala Is a Man-Made Trap

Premises 1 and 2 fix the verse’s world, where three was one and 2:230 guarded a real second marriage. Premise 2 dates the change, and Premise 3 shows the curse meeting the practice the change scaled. Premise 4 shows the schools validating what was cursed, rewarding the man who was cursed, and reading the curse itself five ways to keep the door open. The Imami school keeps the count of the Prophet ﷺ, and the hired-husband trade loses its supply of customers. On this record the halala trade is the fruit of the changed count, and the borrowed he-goat is its child.

1. The Verse and Its World

A Rule for a Real Second Marriage

Claim: 2:230 describes a second marriage that is intended as a marriage, not a service contract

Source: Qur’an 2:230

The verse that governs the thrice-divorced woman is one sentence, and every word of it speaks of marriage as marriage.

فَإِن طَلَّقَهَا فَلَا تَحِلُّ لَهُۥ مِنۢ بَعْدُ حَتَّىٰ تَنكِحَ زَوْجًا غَيْرَهُۥ

fa-in ṭallaqahā fa-lā taḥillu lahu min baʿdu ḥattā tankiḥa zawjan ghayruhū

"But if he divorces her, she is not lawful to him afterward until she marries a husband other than him."

- Qur’an 2:230

She marries a husband. The verse gives her a real marriage with a real ending, divorce or death, and only after that ending may the first husband return. In the verse’s own world this rule guards the woman’s dignity. She is given a genuine second chance, and the first husband learns he cannot recycle her on demand. The verse itself walks through three divorces, one, then one, then one, each with its own waiting period. The trade the verse is now famous for did not exist when the verse was revealed, because the instant triple that fills that trade with customers did not exist either. A woman reached the third divorce by three deliberate steps, and the verse guarded the door she then walked through. That world, and the day it ended, is where the record begins.

The Count Under the Prophet ﷺ

Claim: a man who pronounced three divorces in one sitting had issued one divorce and could take his wife back

Source: Awali al-La’ali, vol.1, p280

The practice report comes from Ibn Abbas , on the case of a Companion who divorced in one breath and grieved over it.

وروى ابن عباس قال : طلق ابن كنانة امرأته ثلاثا في مجلس واحد فحزن عليها حزنا شديدا فسأله رسول الله ﵌ : " كيف طلقتها ؟ " فقال : طلقتها ثلاثا في مجلس واحد فقال ﵇ : " إنما تلك واحدة : فراجعها

"Ibn Abbas reported, Ibn Kinana divorced his wife three times in one sitting, and he grieved over her with intense grief. The Messenger of God ﷺ asked him, how did you divorce her? He said, I divorced her three times in one sitting. He ﷺ said, that is but one. Take her back if you wish. And he took her back."

- Ibn Abi Jumhur al-Ahsa{P}i, Awali al-La{P}ali, vol.1, p280, no.130

One sitting, one divorce. The Prophet ﷺ ruled the count himself, on a live case, and the marriage stayed alive with it. Ibn Kinana walked home with his wife. This is the ruling the Imami school keeps to this day, and it is the ruling the rest of the record is measured against.

Abu al-Sahba’s Testimony

Claim: the Companion-era count was still the public practice years into Umar’s rule

Source: Abd al-Razzaq’s Musannaf, vol.6, p392, no.11337

Author’s Madhhab: Abd al-Razzaq al-San’ani (d.211 AH), the Yemeni traditionist, independent (no single school); transmits Ibn Jurayj’s report

A second witness fixes the era, this time on a Sunni chain. Abu al-Sahba’, the freed slave of Ibn Abbas , puts the question to him directly.

(11337) - عبد الرزاق عن ابن جريج قال : أخبرني ابن طاووس عن أبيه أن أبا الصهباء قال لابن عباس : تعلم (5) أنها كانت الثلاث تجعل واحدة على عهد النبي ﵌ ، وأبي بكر ، وثلاثا من إمارة عمر ؟ فقال ابن عباس : نعم (6)

"Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Jurayj, who said, Ibn Tawus informed me, from his father, that Abu al-Sahba said to Ibn Abbas, do you know that the three used to be counted as one in the time of the Prophet ﷺ, and Abu Bakr, and three of the emirate of Umar? Ibn Abbas said, yes."

- Abd al-Razzaq al-San{P}ani (d.211 AH), al-Musannaf, vol.6, p392, no.11337; the same report runs in Sahih Muslim

Ibn Abbas confirms the count from living memory, and his answer carries no date for the change itself. The versions differ on the month count, the Musnad says two years of Umar’s caliphate, this report says three of his emirate, and a record divided on the number is still united on the fact. The count of one ruled after the Prophet ﷺ died, and it changed inside Umar’s caliphate. The next section shows the change itself, dated and named.

2. The Day the Triple Became Three

The Musnad’s Dated Report

Claim: the triple was enforced as three by Umar’s decision, two years into his caliphate, after the people had rushed into it

Source: Musnad Ahmad, vol.5, p61, no.2875

Author’s Madhhab: Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d.241 AH), Hanbali; the report’s chain is that of Sahih Muslim

The Musnad of Ahmad ibn Hanbal carries the report with its full chain.

2875 - حَدَّثَنَا عَبْدُ الرَّزَّاقِ ، حَدَّثَنَا مَعْمَرٌ ، عَنِ ابْنِ طَاوُسٍ ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ ، عَنِ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ ، قَالَ : " كَانَ الطَّلاقُ عَلَى عَهْدِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ ، وَأَبِي بَكْرٍ وَسَنَتَيْنِ مِنْ خِلافَةِ عُمَرَ بْنِ الْخَطَّابِ ، طَلاقُ الثَّلاثِ : وَاحِدَةً " فَقَالَ عُمَرُ : " إِنَّ النَّاسَ قَدِ اسْتَعْجَلُوا فِي أَمْرٍ كَانَتْ (2) لَهُمْ فِيهِ أَنَاةٌ ، فَلَوْ أَمْضَيْنَاهُ عَلَيْهِمْ . فَأَمْضَاهُ عَلَيْهِمْ

"...Abd al-Razzaq transmitted to us. Ma'mar informed us, from Ibn Tawus, from his father, from Ibn Abbas, who said, divorce in the time of the Messenger of God ﷺ, and of Abu Bakr, and for two years of the caliphate of Umar ibn al-Khattab, the divorce of three counted as one. Then Umar said, the people have rushed into a matter in which they had room for patience, so let us carry it against them. And he carried it against them."

- Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Musnad, vol.5, p61, no.2875, ed. al-Risala; the chain of Sahih Muslim

The words name the change, the reason, and the changer. The people rushed, so Umar let the rushed ruling stand against them. A disciplinary measure became a law of divorce. The same chain runs in Sahih Muslim, and Muslim’s own version carries Umar’s reason with it, the people rushed into divorce, so he allowed it against them. One edition closes with the sentence that names the act, he carried it against them, and one stops at the reason. A book that keeps the reason has already kept the ruling. The Imami school counts this report among its own proofs, because it preserves the count of the Prophet ﷺ the school still rules by. One divorce in one sitting is the practice of the Prophet ﷺ, and the school that kept it never needed the escape door the new ruling forced open.

Why the Harmonizers Call It Problematic

Claim: al-Nawawi ranks the hadith among the problematic ones, and al-Suyuti records the workaround

Source: al-Dibaj ala Muslim, vol.4, p88

Author’s Madhhab: al-Suyuti (d.911 AH), Shafi’i

A report in the Sahih that contradicts the school’s law needs an answer. Al-Suyuti records al-Nawawi’s.

قال النووي هذا الحديث معدود من الأحاديث المشكلة والأصح في تأويله أن معناه أنه كان في أو الأمر إذا قال لها أنت طالق أنت طالق أنت طالق ولم ينو تأكيدا ولا استئنافا يحكم بوقوع طلقة لقلة إرادتهم الاستئناف بذلك فحمل على الغالب الذي هو إرادة التأكيد فلما كثر في زمن عمر وكثر استعمال الناس لهذا الصيغة وغلب إرادة الاستئناف بها حملت عند الإطلاق على الثلاث عملا بالغالب السابق إلى الفهم منها في ذلك العصر وذكر القرطبي أنه ألف في هذا الحديث جزءا أشبع فيه القول

"Al-Nawawi said, this hadith is counted among the problematic hadiths, and the soundest interpretation is that in the early period, when a man said to her, you are divorced, you are divorced, you are divorced, without intending renewal, one divorce was ruled to have fallen, because renewing it was rarely intended. When the usage multiplied in Umar's time and the intention of renewal became dominant, the formula was carried on the prevailing intent. Al-Qurtubi mentions he wrote a treatise on this hadith in which he exhausted the discussion."

- al-Suyuti (d.911 AH), al-Dibaj ala Muslim, vol.4, p88

The harmonizers hold one more door. Perhaps the count of one belonged to the years before the divorce verses were revealed, and the verses themselves brought the count of three, which would make Umar’s enforcement a return to scripture. The door closes on the record. Abu al-Sahba’s question was whether the three counted as one in the time of the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr, long after the verses were revealed, and Ibn Abbas answered yes, with no reservation for dates of revelation. And Umar’s own reason, as the Musnad records it, was never a verse. The people rushed, so he carried the rushed formula against them. A man enforcing scripture says so. A man disciplining haste says what Umar said.

The workaround is humanizing, and it concedes the whole point. If the count of the Prophet ﷺ reflected mere habit of speech, then the ruling never turned on the words’ meaning but on the era’s presumed intent, and the verse’s world was ruled by intent until Umar fixed the words as three. A law of marriage and divorce that turns on which century’s presumptions govern the formula is a law in trouble. The schools live inside that trouble because they inherited the change as fact. There is one loose end. Ahmad ibn Hanbal said every student of Ibn Abbas transmitted against Tawus’s report, and later reports have Ibn Abbas ruling the triple as three. His testimony about the Prophet’s ﷺ practice and his later rulings under the new regime sit in the same books, and the canon keeps both. A witness who confirms the old count from living memory is not erased by his later compliance with the new one. He reported what was, and then he lived under what replaced it. And consensus cannot form around a ruling the Companion with the fullest memory of the old one kept confirming, nor around a change Abu al-Sahba could still put to Ibn Abbas as a question. A practice the canon itself argues about was never agreed on. The curse in the next section is the trouble speaking.

3. The Curse the Canon Cannot Sleep On

The Borrowed He-Goat

Claim: the Prophet ﷺ named and cursed the muhallil, the man who marries to unlock the first husband

Source: Ibn Majah, no.1936; al-Kaba’ir, p185

Author’s Madhhab: al-Dhahabi (d.748 AH), Shafi’i; Ibn Majah’s compilership follows no school

The guard of 2:230 has its own case law, and men probed it early. The Prophet ﷺ named the man who would be hired to walk a woman through the door and hand her back, and he named him with a curse.

(1936) - حَدَّثَنَا يَحْيَى بْنُ عُثْمَانَ بْنِ صَالِحٍ الْمِصْرِيُّ ، قَالَ : حَدَّثَنَا أَبِي ، قَالَ : سَمَعْتُ اللَّيْثَ بْنَ سَعْدٍ يَقُولُ : قَالَ لِي أَبُو مُصْعَبٍ مِشْرَحُ بْنُ هَاعَانَ ، قَالَ عُقْبَةُ بْنُ عَامِرٍ : قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ : « أَلَا أُخْبِرُكُمْ بِالتَّيْسِ الْمُسْتَعَارِ ؟ » قَالُوا : بَلَى ، يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ، قَالَ : « هُوَ الْمُحَلِّلُ . لَعَنَ اللَّهُ الْمُحَلِّلَ وَالْمُحَلَّلَ لَهُ » .

"Yahya ibn Uthman ibn Salih al-Misri transmitted to us. He said, my father transmitted to me. He said, I heard al-Layth ibn Sa'd say, Abu Mus'ab Mishrah ibn Ha'anan said to me, Uqbah ibn Amir said, the Messenger of God ﷺ said, shall I tell you about the borrowed he-goat? They said, yes, Messenger of God. He said, it is the muhallil. May God curse the muhallil and the one for whom halal is made."

- Ibn Majah, al-Sunan, vol.3, p368, no.1936, ed. Dar al-Jil

The chain of this one copy runs through Abu Mus'ab Mishrah ibn Ha'anan, and Sunni critics have words for him. The answer is the canon's own breadth. Ibn Abd al-Barr records the curse as transmitted from four Companions, Ali, Ibn Mas'ud, Abu Hurayra, and Uqbah, and al-Tirmidhi grades the report of Ibn Mas'ud’s chain sound. Four roads do not close because one traveler is weak, and no chain in the record carries the he-goat image except Uqbah’s. The image stands on its own narrator, and the curse stands on four.

A borrowed he-goat, brought for one service and returned to his owner. The image is brutal because the practice is brutal. A man is hired, the woman is passed to him, and the marriage exists to be destroyed on schedule. Ibn Abbas was asked about the muhallil directly, and his answer sets the only lawful door.

وعن ابن عباس ﵄ قال سيل رسول الله ﵌ عن المحلل فقال لا الا نكاح رغبه لا نكاح دلسه ولا استهزاء بكتاب الله ﷿ حتي يذوق العسيله

"And from Ibn Abbas, who said, the Messenger of God ﷺ was asked about the muhallil and said, no, except a marriage of real desire, not a marriage of deception, nor mockery of the Book of God, until he tastes the sweetness of consummation."

- al-Dhahabi, al-Kaba{P}ir, p185, from Abu Ishaq al-Jawzajani

No marriage of deception and no mockery of the Book of God. The door 2:230 opens is a real marriage, and a real marriage has its own life. The Companions enforced that standard with the hardest words they had, and their words are the next card.

The Woman at the Door

Claim: the Prophet ﷺ applied the guard of 2:230 to a real second marriage, not to a hired husband

Source: Musnad Abi Ya’la, vol.7, p397, no.4423; Musnad Ahmad, vol.6, p37

Author’s Madhhab: Abu Ya’la al-Mawsili (d.307 AH) and Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d.241 AH), Hanbali

The strongest Sunni counter sits here and must be met here. Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim carry the case of the wife of Rifa’a al-Qurazi, a woman who married a second husband and then wanted her first husband back, and the Prophet ﷺ held the door of 2:230 shut against her. The case, read whole, is the verse’s guard working, and it agrees with everything above.

جاءت امرأة رفاعة القرظي إلى رسول الله فقالت إن رفاعة طلقني فبت طلاقي فتزوجت بعده عبد الرحمن بن الزبير وإن ما معه مثل هدبة الثوب فتبسم رسول الله ﵌ فقال تريدين أن ترجعي إلى رفاعة لا حتى يذوق من عسيلتك وتذوقي من عسيلته

"The wife of Rifa'a al-Qurazi came to the Messenger of God ﷺ and said, Rifa'a divorced me and made the divorce final. After him I married Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Zubayr, and all he has is like the fringe of a garment. The Messenger of God ﷺ smiled and said, do you want to return to Rifa'a? No, until he tastes of your sweetness and you taste of his."

- Abu Ya’la al-Mawsili (d.307 AH), Musnad Abi Ya’la, vol.7, p397, no.4423; the same case in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim

The verse before 2:230 fixes the shape of the walk. Its own words first.

ٱلطَّلَٰقُ مَرَّتَانِ ۖ فَإِمْسَاكٌۢ بِمَعْرُوفٍ أَوْ تَسْرِيحٌۢ بِإِحْسَٰنٍ

al-ṭalāqu marratāni fa-imsākun bi-maʿrūfin aw tasrīḥun bi-iḥsān

"Divorce is twice. Then keep her in kindness or release her with goodness."

- Qur’an 2:229

Twice, then a decision, then the third divorce of 2:230 for the man who walks past every open door. The verse counts steps, and a step is a thing taken one at a time. Three words in one breath are three steps collapsed into one fall.

Read what the case actually holds. Her second marriage was real, contracted for herself, and the Prophet ﷺ asks no question about any agreement to return her. She simply wants her first husband, and the verse’s price stands, a real consummation and a real ending. The case is the guard of 2:230 applied to a real marriage, exactly the reading Premise 1 drew from the verse’s words. No hired husband appears anywhere in it. A hostile reader who cites her against the argument here cites a woman the record’s own reading protects. What the case never shows is a man contracted to divorce on schedule, and that man is what the rest of this record is about. The Companions met him with the hardest words they had, and their words are the next card.

The Companions Call It Promiscuity

Claim: Ibn Umar and Umar treated the halala as promiscuity, punishable by stoning

Source: al-Kaba’ir, p186; Abd al-Razzaq’s Musannaf, nos.10776-10777

Author’s Madhhab: al-Dhahabi (d.748 AH), Shafi’i; Abd al-Razzaq al-San’ani (d.211 AH), independent

Ibn Umar was asked the question as a live case, a man who married solely to unlock the first husband.

وعن ابن عمر أن رجلا سأله فقال ما تقول في امرأة تزوجتها أحلها لزوجها لم يأمرني ولم يعلم فقال له ابن عمر لا إلا نكاح رغبة إن أعجبتك أمسكتها وإن كرهتها فارقتها وإنا كنا نعد هذا سفاحا على عهد رسول الله

"And from Ibn Umar, a man asked him, what do you say of a woman I married to make her lawful for her husband, without his order and without his knowledge? Ibn Umar said to him, no, a marriage of real desire only. If she pleases you, keep her, and if you dislike her, part from her. And we used to count this as promiscuity in the time of the Messenger of God ﷺ."

- al-Dhahabi, al-Kaba{P}ir, p186

We counted this as promiscuity in the time of the Prophet ﷺ. The same verdict, in the same words, runs through Abd al-Razzaq’s Musannaf.

عبد الرزاق عن معمر عن الزهري عن عبد الملك بن المغيرة قال : سئل ابن عمر عن تحليل المرأة لزوجها ، فقال : ذلك السفاح

"Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Abd al-Malik ibn al-Mughira, Ibn Umar was asked about making a woman lawful for her husband, and he said, that is promiscuity."

- Abd al-Razzaq al-San{P}ani, al-Musannaf, vol.6, p265, no.10776

And Umar’s own ruling, on its own chain.

عبد الرزاق عن الثوري ومعمر عن الأعمش عن المسيب (2) بن رافع عن (3) قبيصة بن جابر الأسدي قال : قال عمر بن الخطاب : لا أوتى بمحلل ولا بمحللة إلا رجمتهما

"Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri and Ma'mar, from al-A'mash, from al-Sayib ibn Rafi', from Qubaysa ibn Jabir, who said, Umar ibn al-Khattab said, bring me a muhallil and the one for whom halal was made, and I will stone them both."

- Abd al-Razzaq al-San{P}ani, al-Musannaf, vol.6, p265, no.10777

Stoning is the penalty of adultery. And note the date Ibn Umar gives. He binds the verdict to the Prophet’s ﷺ own era, which means men were already probing the door before the instant triple existed, and the early verdicts met them there. What the changed count produced was the trade at scale, one sitting in place of three deliberate steps, and the flood of volunteers the caliphs then faced. The caliph who enforced the triple as three also ruled that the escape door the triple created deserves the adultery penalty. The two rulings side by side are the whole problem in miniature. Umar made the trap and cursed the people it caught. The Imami school removed the trap by keeping the count of the Prophet ﷺ, and it never has to choose between stoning a man for a marriage its law calls valid and blessing a marriage the Prophet ﷺ cursed.

Two Caliphs, Two Answers

Claim: Umar stoned the practice while his own case law upheld it, and Uthman turned the volunteer away

Source: al-Sunan al-Kubra, nos.14307-14308; Kanz al-Ummal, no.28052

Author’s Madhhab: al-Bayhaqi (d.458 AH), Shafi’i; al-Muttaqi al-Hindi (d.975 AH), Hanafi

Al-Bayhaqi preserves Umar’s pulpit ruling and the case that tests it.

عن المُسَيَّبِ بنِ رافِعٍ، عن قَبيصَةَ بنِ جابِرٍ قال: قال عُمَرُ -رضى اللَّه عنه-: لا أوتَى بمُحَلِّلٍ (¬٢) ولا مُحَلَّلٍ له إلَّا رَجَمتُهُما

"...from al-Sayib ibn Rafi', from Qubaysa ibn Jabir, who said, Umar said, bring me a muhallil and the one for whom halal was made, and I will stone them both."

- al-Bayhaqi, al-Sunan al-Kubra, vol.15, no.14307

Then a real volunteer reached Uthman , and the caliph’s answer shows how the ruling sat with the men who lived it.

حَدَّثَنى محمدُ بنُ عبدِ الرَّحمَنِ، عن أبى مَرزوقٍ التُّجيبِىِّ، أنَّ رَجُلًا أتَى عثمانَ بنَ عَفَّانَ -رضى اللَّه عنه- فى خِلافَتِه وقَد رَكِبَ، فسألَه فقالَ: إنَّ لى إلَيكَ حاجَةً يا أميرَ المُؤمِنينَ. قال: إنِّى الآن مُسْتَعْجِلٌ فإِن أرَدتَ أن تَركَبَ خَلفِى حَتَّى تَقضِىَ حاجَتَكَ؟ فرَكِبَ خَلفَه فقالَ: إنَّ جارًا لى طَلَّقَ امرأتَه فى غَضَبِه ولَقِىَ شِدَّةً، فأَرَدتُ أن أحتَسِبَ بنَفسِى ومالى فأَتَزَوَّجَها ثُمَّ أبتَنِىَ بها ثُمَّ أُطَلِّقَها فتَرجِعَ إلَى زَوجِها الأوَّلِ؟ فقالَ له عثمانُ: لا تَنكِحْها إلَّا نِكاحَ رَغبَةٍ

"...from Abu Marzuq al-Tujibi, that a man came to Uthman ibn Affan during his caliphate while he was mounted, and asked him, saying, I have a need with you, Commander of the Faithful. He said, I am in haste now, so ride behind me if you wish to settle your need. So he rode behind him and said, my neighbor divorced his wife in anger and met hardship, and I wanted to devote myself and my wealth, so I would marry her, then consummate with her, then divorce her, and she returns to her first husband. Uthman said to him, do not marry her except a marriage of real desire."

- al-Bayhaqi, al-Sunan al-Kubra, vol.15, no.14308

Do not marry her except a marriage of real desire. Uthman’s answer is the verse’s own spirit, a second marriage must mean itself. And yet the case law had already gone the other way, because the trap was producing volunteers faster than the caliphs could shame them. Kanz al-Ummal keeps the strangest case of all, the one where the tables turned on the client.

عن ابن سيرين أن رجلا طلق امرأته وأمر رجلا يقال له : ذو الخرقتين أن يتزوجها ليحلها له ، فمكث ثلاثا لا يخرج ، ثم خرج وعليه ثوب ، فقال له الرجل : أين ما قاولتك عليه ؟ فأبى أن يطلقها فأتى في ذلك عمر بن الخطاب فقال : الله رزق ذا الخرقتين وأمضى نكاحه

"From Ibn Sirin, that a man divorced his wife and ordered a man called Dhu al-Khariqatayn to marry her to make her lawful for him. He stayed three days without leaving, then left wearing a garment, and the man said to him, where is what we agreed upon? He refused to divorce her, so the matter was brought to Umar ibn al-Khattab, who said, God has provided Dhu al-Khariqatayn, and he upheld the marriage."

- al-Muttaqi al-Hindi, Kanz al-Ummal, vol.9, p704, no.28052, from Ibn Jarir

The hired husband liked the woman and kept her, and Umar upheld the marriage. The man who set the trap by the triple ruling watched his own device capture its inventor’s case. The halala’s law was already ungovernable in the first generation, and every school since has had to pick a compromise. Those compromises are the next section.

4. The Schools and the Permission

Void or Valid, No Third Door

Claim: the Maliki and Hanbali schools void the halala contract, and Ibn Taymiyya voids even the hidden intention

Source: al-Mughni, vol.7, p574-575; al-Fatawa al-Kubra, vol.6, p8

Author’s Madhhab: Ibn Qudama (d.620 AH), Hanbali; Ibn Taymiyya (d.728 AH), Hanbali

Ibn Qudama’s manual gathers the voiding position with its proof.

ولنا ما روي عن النبي ﵌ أنه قال (لعن الله المحلل والمحلل له) رواه أبو دود وابن ماجة والترمذي وقال حديث حسن صحيح والعمل عليه عند أهل العم من أصحاب النبي ﵌ منهم عمر بن الخطاب وعثمان وعبد الله بن عمر وهو قول الفقهاء من التابعين

"And our proof is what was transmitted from the Prophet ﷺ, that he said, may God curse the muhallil and the one for whom halal is made. Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah, and al-Tirmidhi transmitted it, and al-Tirmidhi said, a sound hadith, and the practice upon it among the scholars of the Companions, among them Umar ibn al-Khattab, Uthman, and Abd Allah ibn Umar, and it is the position of the jurists among the Successors."

- Ibn Qudama, al-Mughni, vol.7, p574

The majority void the contract when tahleel is stipulated. But stipulation is only words, and the intention can live without them, so Ibn Taymiyya closes that door too, and his paragraph is the fullest statement of the problem.

فَإِذَا تَزَوَّجَهَا رَجُلٌ بِنِيَّةِ أَنْ يُطَلِّقَهَا لِتَحِلَّ لِزَوْجِهَا الْأَوَّلِ كَانَ هَذَا النِّكَاحُ حَرَاماً بَاطِلاً ، سَوَاءٌ عَزَمَ بَعْدَ ذَلِكَ عَلَى إمْسَاكِهَا ، أَوْ فَارَقَهَا ، وَسَوَاءٌ شُرِطَ عَلَيْهِ ذَلِكَ فِي عَقْدِ النِّكَاحِ ، أَوْ شُرِطَ عَلَيْهِ قَبْلَ الْعَقْدِ ، أَوْ لَمْ يُشْرَطْ عَلَيْهِ لَفْظاً بَلْ كَانَ مَا بَيْنَهُمَا مِنْ الْخِطْبَةِ وَحَالِ الرَّجُلِ وَالْمَرْأَةِ وَالْمَهْرِ نَازِلاً بَيْنَهُمْ مَنْزِلَةَ اللَّفْظِ بِالشُّرُوطِ ، أَوْ لَمْ يَكُنْ شَيْءٌ مِنْ ذَلِكَ بَلْ أَرَادَ الرَّجُلُ أَنْ يَتَزَوَّجَهَا ، ثُمَّ يُطَلِّقَهَا لِتَحِلَّ لِلْمُطَلِّقِ ثَلَاثاً مِنْ غَيْرِ أَنْ تَعْلَمَ الْمَرْأَةُ وَلَا وَلِيُّهَا شَيْئاً مِنْ ذَلِكَ ، سَوَاءٌ عَلِمَ الزَّوْجُ الْمُطَلِّقُ ثَلَاثاً ، أَوْ لَمْ يَعْلَمْ ، مِثْلُ أَنْ يَظُنَّ الْمُحَلِّلُ أَنَّ هَذَا فِعْلُ خَيْرٍ وَمَعْرُوفٍ مَعَ الْمُطَلِّقِ وَامْرَأَتِهِ بِإِعَادَتِهَا إلَيْهِ لِمَا أَنَّ الطَّلَاقَ أَضَرَّ بِهِمَا وَبِأَوْلَادِهِمَا وَعَشِيرَتِهِمَا وَنَحْوِ ذَلِكَ . بَلْ لَا يَحِلُّ لِلْمُطَلِّقِ ثَلَاثاً أَنْ يَتَزَوَّجَهَا حَتَّى يَنْكِحَهَا رَجُلٌ مُرْتَغِباً لِنَفْسِهِ نِكَاحَ رَغْبَةٍ لَا نِكَاحَ دُلْسَةٍ ، وَيَدْخُلُ بِهَا بِحَيْثُ تَذُوقُ عُسَيْلَتَهُ وَيَذُوقُ عُسَيْلَتَهَا

"So when a man marries her intending to divorce her so she becomes lawful for her first husband, this marriage is forbidden and void, whether he then resolves to keep her or to part from her, and whether it was stipulated upon him in the contract of marriage, or stipulated before the contract, or nothing was stipulated in words at all... or rather the man wanted to marry her then divorce her so she becomes lawful for the one who gave the triple divorce, without the woman or her guardian knowing anything of it, whether the first husband knew or did not know..."

- Ibn Taymiyya, al-Fatawa al-Kubra, vol.6, p8

Forbidden and void, whether stipulated, agreed in advance, or hidden entirely from the woman and her guardian. Ibn Taymiyya’s ruling protects the woman from the deception the earlier manuals tolerated. It also voids a marriage that meets every formal requirement of the school’s own law, which is the price of keeping Umar’s triple. The hidden-intention marriage is valid in the school’s case law, as Who Forbade Mut’ah? documents, and void here, because the intention behind it embarrasses the law. A law that must void its own valid contracts to avoid a curse has lost its footing. The other two schools refused to pay that price, and their own manuals say so.

The Contract Stands

Claim: the Hanafi and Shafi’i schools keep the muhallil’s marriage valid when the tahleel was intended and never spoken, and some Hanafis called the work devotion

Source: al-Istidhkar, vol.5, p449; al-Dhakhira, vol.4, p321

Author’s Madhhab: Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), Maliki; al-Qarafi (d.684 AH), Maliki

Ibn Abd al-Barr records the validating positions of the two schools side by side with the voiding ones, and attributes them by name.

وَقَالَ أَبُو حَنِيفَةَ وَأَبُو يُوسُفَ النِّكَاحُ جَائِزٌ وَلَهُ أَنْ يُقِيمَ عَلَى نِكَاحِهِ ذَلِكَ

"Abu Hanifa and Abu Yusuf said the marriage is valid, and he may remain in that marriage."

- Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Istidhkar, vol.5, p449

The marriage is valid, and the muhallil may stay in it. The intention that made him a muhallil changes nothing in the law. Al-Shafi’i arrived at the same place in stages.

وَإِنْ تَزَوَّجَهَا تَزْوِيجًا مُطْلَقًا لَمْ يَشْتَرِطْ وَلَا اشْتُرِطَ عَلَيْهِ التَّحْلِيلُ إِلَّا أَنَّهُ نَوَاهُ وَقَصَدَهُ فَلِلشَّافِعِيِّ فِي كِتَابِهِ الْقَدِيمِ الْعِرَاقِيِّ فِي ذَلِكَ قَوْلَانِ أَحَدُهُمَا مِثْلُ قَوْلِ مَالِكٍ وَالْآخَرُ مِثْلُ قَوْلِ أَبِي حَنِيفَةَ وَلَمْ يَخْتَلِفْ قَوْلُهُ فِي كِتَابِهِ ( ( الْجَدِيدِ ) ) الْمِصْرِيِّ أَنَّ النِّكَاحَ صَحِيحٌ إِذَا لَمْ يَشْتَرِطِ التَّحْلِيلَ فِي قَوْلِهِ وَهُوَ قَوْلُ دَاوُدَ

"If he married her with an unqualified contract, not stipulating nor having tahleel stipulated upon him, but only intending and aiming at it, al-Shafi'i held two views in his old Iraqi book. One matched Malik's view. The other matched Abu Hanifa's view. In his new Egyptian book his view was settled, the marriage is valid when the tahleel was not stipulated in his words. And this is Dawud's view."

- Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Istidhkar, vol.5, p449

His old Iraqi book still wavered between Malik’s voiding and Abu Hanifa’s validation. His final Egyptian book stopped wavering. A marriage built on the tahleel intention is valid so long as the intention stayed silent, and Dawud al-Zahiri held the same. An older line of jurists had said as much with the condition spoken aloud.

وَقَدْ رُوِيَ عَنِ الثَّوْرِيِّ فِي نِكَاحِ الْمُحَلِّلِ وَنِكَاحِ الْخِيَارِ أَنَّهُ قَالَ النِّكَاحُ جَائِزٌ وَالشَّرْطُ بَاطِلٌ وَهُوَ قَوْلُ بْنِ أَبِي لَيْلَى فِي نِكَاحِ الْمُحَلِّلِ وَنِكَاحِ الْمُتْعَةِ أَبْطَلَ الشَّرْطَ فِي ذَلِكَ وَأَجَازَ النِّكَاحَ وَهُوَ قَوْلُ الْأَوْزَاعِيِّ أَيْضًا أَنَّهُ قَالَ فِي نِكَاحِ الْمُحَلِّلِ بِئْسَ مَا صَنَعَ وَالنِّكَاحُ جَائِزٌ

"It is reported from al-Thawri, on the muhallil marriage and the option marriage, that he said, the marriage is valid and the condition is void. And it is the view of Ibn Abi Layla on the muhallil marriage and the mut'a marriage. He voided the condition in that and allowed the marriage. And it is the view of al-Awza'i as well, who said of the muhallil marriage, he did a vile thing, and the marriage is valid."

- Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Istidhkar, vol.5, p449

The condition falls, the marriage stays. Al-Awza’i condemns the act and validates the contract in one breath. Al-Qarafi later gathered the whole map into one paragraph, and its last clause is the steepest step in the record.

وَوَافَقْنَا ابْنَ حَنْبَلٍ عَلَى فَسَادِ الْعَقْدِ إِذَا اشْتَرَطَ عَلَيْهِ التَّحْلِيلَ مَعَ الْعَقْدِ أَوْ قَبْلَهُ أَوْ نَوَاهُ وَقَالَ ش وح يَحِلُّ إِذَا نَوَاهُ مِنْ غَيْرِ شَرْطٍ وَجَوَّزَ ح نِكَاحَ الْمُحَلِّلِ وَالْإِقَامَةَ عَلَيْهِ بَلْ قَالَ بَعْضُ الْحَنَفِيَّةِ ذَلِكَ قُرْبَةٌ بِالْإِحْسَانِ لِلْمُطَلِّقِ

"We agreed with Ibn Hanbal that the contract is corrupt when the tahleel was stipulated with the contract, before it, or intended. Al-Shafi'i and Abu Hanifa said she becomes lawful when he intended it without a condition. Abu Hanifa allowed the muhallil's marriage and remaining in it. Some Hanafis even said it is devotion, a kindness to the divorcer."

- al-Qarafi (d.684 AH), al-Dhakhira, vol.4, p321

Read the ladder from the bottom. Ahmad voids on stipulation, prior agreement, or intention. Al-Shafi’i and Abu Hanifa validate on bare intention. Abu Hanifa validates the marriage and the muhallil’s staying in it. Then some Hanafis climb one rung higher and call the muhallil’s work devotion, a kindness to the divorcer. The curse of the Prophet ﷺ stands at the bottom of the ladder the whole time. And validating the contract was only the first concession. The canon also preserves jurists who called the muhallil’s work rewarded.

Al-Shafi’i in His Own Voice

Claim: al-Shafi’i validates the muhallil’s marriage on the tahleel intention alone, because intention is the self’s private talk and the contract was unqualified

Source: al-Shafi’i (d.204 AH), Kitab al-Umm, vol.5, p86

Author’s Madhhab: al-Shafi’i (d.204 AH), founder of the Shafi’i school

Ibn Abd al-Barr reported al-Shafi’i’s ruling secondhand. The ruling itself sits in al-Umm, in al-Shafi’i’s own text, and his reason stands under it.

وإن قدم رجل بلدا وأحب أن ينكح امرأة ونيته ونيتها أن لا يمسكها إلا مقامه بالبلد أو يوما أو اثنين أو ثلاثة كانت على هذا نيته دون نيتها أو نيتها دون نيته أو نيتهما معا ونية الولي غير أنهما إذا عقدا النكاح مطلقا لا شرط فيه فالنكاح ثابت ولا تفسد النية من النكاح شيئا لأن النية حديث نفس وقد وضع عن الناس ما حدثوا به أنفسهم وقد ينوى الشئ ولا يفعله وينويه ويفعله فيكون الفعل حادثا غير النية ،

وكذلك لو نكحها ونيته ونيتها أو نية أحدها دون الآخر أن لا يمسكها إلا قدر ا يصيبها فيحللها لزوجها ثبت النكاح وسواء نوى ذلك الولي معهما أو نوى غيره أو لم ينوه ولا غيره والوالي والولي في هذا لا معنى له أن يفسد شيئا ما لم يقع النكاح بشرط يفسده

"When a man arrives in a town and wants to marry a woman, and his intention and hers is that he will keep her only for his stay in the town, or a day or two or three, whether the intention was his alone, or hers alone, or both together, or the guardian's, then when they contract the marriage unqualified, with no condition in it, the marriage stands, and the intention takes nothing from the marriage."

"Intention is only the self's private talk, and God has lifted from people what they say to themselves. A man may intend a thing and not do it, and he may intend it and do it, and then the deed is a new thing, other than the intention."

"And likewise, if he married her and his intention and hers, or one of theirs alone, was not to keep her beyond the extent of his having her so as to make her lawful for her husband, the marriage stands."

"Whether the guardian intended it with them, or intended otherwise, or intended nothing, the guardian has no hand here that could spoil anything, so long as the marriage did not fall upon a condition that spoils it."

- al-Shafi’i (d.204 AH), Kitab al-Umm, vol.5, p86

This is the source beneath Ibn Abd al-Barr’s summary. The final Egyptian position is here in person. The reason is the private talk of the self, lifted from the ledger. Only a spoken condition could void the contract, and the muhallil who kept his aim silent kept his marriage valid. The ladder’s bottom rung now rests on the master’s own hand, and the Hanafi rung beneath it has its own primary voice next.

Rewarded for the Cursed Work

Claim: named Successor jurists permitted the tahleel marriage and called the muhallil rewarded, and the roster is preserved unretracted

Source: al-Tamhid, vol.13, p227-228

Author’s Madhhab: Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), Maliki

Ibn Abd al-Barr’s encyclopedia preserves the full roster of the permissive position, name by name.

وَقَالَ سَالِمٌ وَالْقَاسِمُ لَا بَأْسَ أَنْ يَتَزَوَّجَهَا لِيُحِلَّهَا إِذَا لَمْ يَعْلَمِ الزَّوْجَانِ قَالَا وَهُوَ مَأْجُورٌ وَقَالَ رَبِيعَةُ وَيَحْيَى بْنُ سَعِيدٍ إِنْ تَزَوَّجَهَا لِيُحِلَّهَا فَهُوَ مَأْجُورٌ وَقَالَ دَاوُدُ بْنُ عَلِيٍّ لَا أُبْعِدُ أَنْ يَكُونَ مُرِيدُ نِكَاحِ الْمُطَلَّقَةِ لِيُحِلَّهَا لِزَوْجِهَا مَأْجُورًا إِذَا لَمْ يُظْهِرْ ذَلِكَ فِي اشْتِرَاطِهِ فِي حِينِ الْعَقْدِ لِأَنَّهُ قَصَدَ إِرْفَاقَ أَخِيهِ الْمُسْلِمِ وَإِدْخَالَ السُّرُورِ عَلَيْهِ إِذَا كَانَ نَادِمًا مَشْغُوفًا فَيَكُونُ فَاعِلُ ذَلِكَ مَأْجُورًا إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ وَقَالَ أَبُو الزِّنَادِ إِنْ لَمْ يَعْلَمْ وَاحِدٌ مِنْهُمَا فَلَا بَأْسَ بِالنِّكَاحِ وَتَرْجِعُ إِلَى زَوْجِهَا الْأَوَّلِ وَقَالَ عَطَاءٌ لَا بَأْسَ أَنْ يُقِيمَ الْمُحَلِّلُ عَلَى نِكَاحِهِ

"Salim and al-Qasim said, there is no harm in marrying her to make her lawful when the two husbands do not know, and he is rewarded. Rabi'a and Yahya ibn Sa'id said, if he marries her to make her lawful, he is rewarded. Dawud ibn Ali said, it is not far-fetched that the one who intends to marry the divorced woman to make her lawful for her husband is rewarded, when he does not voice it as a condition at the time of the contract, because he aimed to show kindness to his Muslim brother and bring him joy when he was regretful and lovesick, so the one who does that is rewarded, if God wills. Abu al-Zinad said, if neither of them knows, there is no harm in the marriage, and she returns to her first husband. And Ata' said, there is no harm in the muhallil remaining in his marriage."

- Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Tamhid, vol.13, p227-228

Salim ibn Abd Allah ibn Umar and al-Qasim ibn Muhammad were among the seven jurists of Medina. They allowed the tahleel marriage when neither of the two husbands knew, and they called the muhallil rewarded. Rabi’a and Yahya ibn Sa’id dropped even the secrecy condition. Dawud al-Zahiri reasoned that the muhallil aims to comfort his Muslim brother. Abu al-Zinad allowed the marriage and the return, and Ata’ allowed the muhallil to stay in it. The Prophet ﷺ cursed the muhallil by name, and these jurists answer the curse with the word rewarded. Ibn Abd al-Barr records the roster and moves on. The permission survived, and the curse still had to be explained. The explanations are the next section.

The Hanafi School’s Own Books

Claim: the Hanafi manuals record Abu Hanifa validating the muhallil marriage even with the tahleel spoken, and call the secret muhallil rewarded

Source: al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), al-Mabsut, vol.30, p228; Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Tamhid, vol.13, p227, from Zufar and al-Hasan ibn Ziyad

Author’s Madhhab: al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), Hanafi, recording Abu Hanifa (d.150 AH), Abu Yusuf (d.182 AH), and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan (d.189 AH), all Hanafi

Abu Hanifa left no book of his own on marriage, so his ruling reaches the record through his students. The chain closest to him runs through Zufar, his direct pupil, reported by al-Hasan ibn Ziyad, and Ibn Abd al-Barr preserves it with the attribution named.

>

وروى الحسن بن زياد عن زفر إذا شرط تحليلها للأول فالنكاح جائز والشرط باطل ويكونا محصنين بهذا التزويج مع الجماع وتحل للأول قال وهو قول أبي حنيفة وقال أبو يوسف النكاح على هذا الشرط فاسد ولها مهر المثل بالدخول ولا يحصنها هذا ولا يحلها لزوجها الأول ولمحمد بن الحسن عن نفسه وعن أصحابه اضطراب كثير في هذا الباب

"Al-Hasan ibn Ziyad reported from Zufar, if he stipulated her making-lawful for the first husband, the marriage is valid and the condition is void, and the two become chaste by this marriage with consummation, and she becomes lawful for the first. He said, and it is the view of Abu Hanifa."

"Abu Yusuf said, the marriage upon this condition is corrupt, and she has a fair dower by consummation, and this does not chaste her nor make her lawful for her first husband. And Muhammad ibn al-Hasan has much wavering from himself and from his companions in this chapter."

- Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), al-Tamhid, vol.13, p227

وذكر عن سالم ابن عبد الله انه سئل عن رجل طلق امرأته ثلاثا فانقضت عدتها فتزوجها رجل ليحللها للزوج الأول لم يأمره الزوج بذلك ولا المرأة قال هذا ما يجوز وهو قول أبي حنيفة ﵀ وبه نأخذ لأنه تزوجها نكاحا مطلقا والنكاح سنة مرغوب فيها وإنما قصد بذلك ارتفاع الحرمة بينهما ليمنعها بذلك على ارتكاب المحرم ويوصلهما إلى مرادهما بطريق حلال فتكون إعانة على البر والتقوي وذلك مندوب إليه فالظاهر أن كل واحد منهما نادم على ما كان منه من سوء الخلق خصوصا إذا كان بينهما ولد فلو امتنع الثاني من أن يتزوجها ليحلها للأول ربما يحملها الندم أو فرط ميل كل واحد منهما إلى صاحبه على أن يتزوجها من غير محلل فهو يسعى إلى تمام مرادهما على وجه يندبان إليه في الشرع فيكون مأجورا فيه

"It is reported from Salim ibn Abd Allah that he was asked about a man who divorced his wife three times, and her waiting period ended, and a man married her intending to make her lawful for the first husband, without the husband commanding it and without the woman, and he said, this is permitted."

"And it is the view of Abu Hanifa, and it is what we take. He married her with an unqualified marriage, and marriage is a sunna that is sought. His aim in it was only the lifting of the prohibition between them, so as to keep her from falling into the forbidden, and to bring the two of them to their goal by a lawful path. That is aid in righteousness and piety, and it is commanded."

"The plain case is that each of the two is regretful over the harshness that came from him, above all when there is a child between them. Were the second man to refuse to marry her to make her lawful for the first, regret, or the pull of each of the two toward the other, might carry them to marry without a muhallil, so he works their purpose to its end in a way the law calls for, and he is rewarded for it."

- al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), al-Mabsut, vol.30, p228

The school’s reasoning is stated in its own terms. The muhallil who worked in secret was aiding two regretful spouses toward a lawful end, and the reward vocabulary of the roster above is here given its cause. Then al-Sarakhsi sets the harder case beside it, the tahleel spoken aloud, and distributes the three companions’ answers.

فإذا تزوجها بهذا الشرط بان قالت المرأة له تزوجني فحللني أو قال له الزوج الأول تزوج هذه المرأة فحللها لي أو قال الثاني للمرأة أتزوجك فأحللك للأول فهذا مكروه وهو معنى قوله ﵇ لعن الله المحلل والمحلل له وقال عليه السلام ألا أنبئكم بالتيس المستعار قالوا بلى قال هو الرجل يتزوج المرأة فيحللها لزوج كان لها قبله ولكن مع هذا يجوز النكاح ويثبت الحل للأول بدخول الثاني بها عند أبي حنيفة ﵀ لان هذا المنهى لمعني في غير النكاح فلا يمنع صحة النكاح والدخول بالنكاح الصحيح يحللها للزوج الأول ثبت ذلك بالسنة وعلى قول أبى يوسف ﵀ هذا النكاح فاسد لأنه في معنى التوقيت للنكاح والتوقيت مفسد للنكاح كما لو تزوج امرأة شهرا وإذا فسد النكاح الثاني فالدخول بالنكاح الفاسد لا يوجب الحل للزوج الأول وقال محمد رحمه الله النكاح جائز ولكن الشرط باطل لان النكاح يهدم الشرط ولا يبطل بالشرط الفاسد

"So if he married her upon this condition, whether the woman said to him, marry me and make me lawful, or the first husband said to him, marry this woman and make her lawful for me, or the second said to the woman, I will marry you and make you lawful for the first, this is disliked, and it is the meaning of his words, God curse the muhallil and the one for whom tahleel is done."

"And he said, peace upon him, shall I tell you of the borrowed he-goat. They said, yes. He said, he is the man who marries the woman to make her lawful for a husband she had before."

"But with all of this, the marriage is valid, and the making-lawful is fixed by the second's entering upon her, in the view of Abu Hanifa, because what is forbidden here is forbidden for a reason outside the marriage, and that does not prevent the marriage's validity. Entry through a valid marriage makes her lawful for the first husband, and that is established by the Sunna."

"In Abu Yusuf's view this marriage is corrupt, because it is in the meaning of a term limit upon the marriage, and a term limit corrupts the marriage, like a man who marries a woman for a month. And when the second marriage is corrupt, entry upon it does not make her lawful for the first husband."

"Muhammad said the marriage is valid and the condition is void, because the marriage tears up the condition and is not voided by a corrupt condition."

- al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), al-Mabsut, vol.30, p228

Abu Hanifa validates even with the tahleel stated in words, because the prohibition falls on a matter outside the contract and the Prophet’s ﷺ Sunna fixes lawful restoration on valid entry. Abu Yusuf voids it as a disguised term marriage. Muhammad ibn al-Hasan, the direct student, keeps the contract and voids the condition, because the marriage tears up the condition and is not voided by it. The school’s own books therefore confirm the ladder from its lowest rung, and the reward reading of the roster above now carries its stated cause.

5. Five Ways to Read a Curse

Five readings of one hadith. The curse falls on a void marriage only, on a valid one, on the men’s belief, on the stipulation alone, or it becomes proof of validity and a mere dislike. The first three readings are Shafi’i. The fourth is the one the Maliki and Hanbali schools act on when they void the stipulated contract, and the one al-Sarakhsi answers inside the Hanafi school. The fifth is Hanafi law itself.

The Curse Means Something Else

Claim: the Shafi’i school reads the curse as falling only on a void marriage, or on the men’s belief rather than the contract

Source: Nihayat al-Matlab, vol.12, p404; al-Thamar al-Dani, p461

Author’s Madhhab: al-Juwayni (d.478 AH), Shafi’i; Ibn al-Mulaqqin (d.804 AH), Shafi’i

A school that keeps the contract valid still owns a hadith that curses the men inside it, and the roster just named the men it must un-curse. Al-Juwayni’s manual hands down the Shafi’i readings.

وَاللَّعْنُ مَحْمُولٌ عَلَى فَسَادِ النِّكَاحِ بِشَرْطِ الطَّلَاقِ ، أَوْ عَلَى تَأْقِيتِهِ إِلَى أَوَّلِ وَطْأَةٍ ؛ فَإِنَّهُ لَوْ أَقْتَ كَذَلِكَ ، فَسَدَ النِّكَاحُ مِنْ أَصْلِهِ وَجْهًا وَاحِدًا ؛ فَإِنَّهُ نِكَاحُ مُتْعَةٍ

"The curse is carried on the marriage being corrupt through a stipulated divorce, or on its being limited to the first intercourse. For if he did that, the marriage would be corrupt from its root by a single view, for it is a mut'a marriage."

- al-Juwayni (d.478 AH), Nihayat al-Matlab, vol.12, p404

The curse falls only on a marriage void for a stipulated divorce or a time limit. A marriage that meets the school’s form escapes it, whatever the man came for. But one Shafi’i reading concedes more than it saves.

وَقَالَ بَعْضُ أَصْحَابِنَا : النِّكَاحُ وَإِنْ صَحَّ ، فَإِذَا جَرَى عَنِ اسْتِدْعَاءٍ مِنَ الْمُحَلَّلِ لَهُ ، فَإِنَّهُمَا يَتَعَرَّضَانِ لِلَّعْنِ

"Some of our companions said the two men still expose themselves to the curse when the marriage itself is valid, whenever it proceeded upon a request from the one for whom halal was made."

- al-Juwayni (d.478 AH), Nihayat al-Matlab, vol.12, p404

If the curse can fall on a marriage the school calls valid, then cursed and valid live in one contract. Sin stops implying voidness, and the hadith stops deciding law. Al-Juwayni’s own teacher went further and emptied the names themselves.

وَكَانَ شَيْخِي أَبُو مُحَمَّدٌ يَحْمِلُ اللَّعْنَ عَلَى صُورَةِ فَسَادِ النِّكَاحِ ، وَيَقُولُ : الْمُحَلِّلُ وَالْمُحَلَّلُ لَهُ مَحْمُولَانَ عَلَى الِاعْتِقَادِ لَا عَلَى التَّحْقِيقِ

"My teacher Abu Muhammad carried the curse on the case of a corrupt marriage, and said the names muhallil and muhallal lahu apply to the men's belief, not to what actually occurs."

- al-Juwayni (d.478 AH), Nihayat al-Matlab, vol.12, p404

On this reading the Prophet ﷺ named the two men for what they believed, and the belief is the curse’s target. The words he spoke become a report about the men’s minds. The Maliki school, which voids the contract, faces the same naming question, because its courts say no lawfulness ever occurred, and a man cannot be a muhallil who makes nothing lawful. Al-Abi’s gloss answers with the same move.

ثم قوله : لعن الله المحلل والمحلل له سماه محللا بحسب زعمهم ، والمحلل بكسر اللام الأولى الذي يتزوج مطلقة ثلاثا بعد العدة والمحلل له هو الزوج الأول

"Then his words, may God curse the muhallil and the one for whom halal is made. He named him muhallil according to their claim. The muhallil, with a first kasra on the lam, is the one who marries a thrice-divorced woman after her waiting period, and the muhallal lahu is the first husband."

- al-Abi al-Azhari, al-Thamar al-Dani, p461

According to their claim. The Prophet’s ﷺ own naming becomes the actors’ word for themselves. Both voiding and validating schools need the naming defused, because the hadith presupposes that the tahleel works, and under Umar’s triple it does. The Hanafi school goes further than defusing the curse. It turns the curse into a proof.

The Curse Becomes Proof of Validity

Claim: al-Sarakhsi uses the Prophet’s ﷺ naming as proof the tahleel works, and Ibn Nujaym reads the curse as proof of mere dislike

Source: al-Mabsut, vol.6, p95; al-Bahr al-Ra’iq, vol.4, p97

Author’s Madhhab: al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), Hanafi; Ibn Nujaym (d.970 AH), Hanafi

Al-Sarakhsi’s Mabsut makes the Hanafi argument in the open.

وَلِأَنَّهُ مُوجِبٌ لِلْحَلِّ فَانَ صَاحِبَ الشَّرْعِ سَمَاهُ مُحَلِّلًا فَقَالَ ﵌ لَعَنَ اللَّهَ الْمُحَلِّلَ وَالْمُحَلَّلَ لَهُ وَإِنَّمَا كَانَ مُحَلِّلًا لِكَوْنِهِ مُوجِبًا لِلْحَلِّ

"And because he causes the lawfulness, the Lawgiver named him muhallil. The Prophet ﷺ said, may God curse the muhallil and the one for whom halal is made, and he was only named muhallil because he causes the lawfulness."

- al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), al-Mabsut, vol.6, p95

Because the Prophet ﷺ named him muhallil, the one who makes lawful, the marriage must actually make her lawful. The hadith that curses the practice becomes evidence of its effect. That is al-Sarakhsi’s proof, and he knows the other side of it. An objector will say the hadith names the man only to curse him, so al-Sarakhsi answers the curse itself, and his answer empties the naming he just used.

وَسَمَاهُ مُحَلِّلًا لِأَنَّهُ شَرْطٌ لِلْحَلِّ لَا لِأَنَّهُ مُوجِبٌ لِلْحَلِّ أَلَا تَرَى أَنَّهُ سَمَاهُ مَلْعُونًا بِاشْتِرَاطِ مَالَا يَحِلُّ لَهُ شَرْعًا

"And He named him muhallil because he is a condition for the lawfulness, not because he causes the lawfulness. Do you not see that He named him cursed for stipulating what is not lawful for him in the law?"

- al-Sarakhsi (d.483 AH), al-Mabsut, vol.6, p95

The naming means condition, and the curse falls on the stipulation. What remains after the stipulation is set aside is a valid marriage that carries a sin. Five centuries later Ibn Nujaym compressed the whole method into two clauses.

لِأَنَّهُ لَوْ كَانَ فَاسِدًا لَمَّا سَمَاهُ مُحَلِّلًا ، وَلَوْ كَانَ غَيْرَ مَكْرُوهٍ لَمَا لَعَنَهُ

"For were it corrupt, he would not have named him muhallil, and were it not disliked, he would not have cursed him."

- Ibn Nujaym (d.970 AH), al-Bahr al-Ra’iq, vol.4, p97

The first clause proves validity from the naming. The second reads the curse as proof of dislike, karahiyya, the school’s mildest category of wrong, an act a Muslim may do while he prays and fasts. God’s curse, expulsion from mercy, is entered in the law books as a mild fault. The Prophet ﷺ cursed the muhallil, and the law hands the cursed man his contract and lets him keep the wife. There remains the enforcement record, the stoning and the verdict of promiscuity, and that record was managed too.

The Stoning Becomes a Warning

Claim: al-Tahawi reads Umar’s stoning as rhetorical severity, and Ibn Taymiyya records scholars saying the client sins in no case

Source: al-Istidhkar, vol.5, p449; Majmu’ al-Fatawa, vol.20, p275

Author’s Madhhab: Ibn Abd al-Barr (d.463 AH), Maliki; Ibn Taymiyya (d.728 AH), Hanbali

The Companions enforced the curse with stoning and the word promiscuity. That record contradicts every validating ruling, so it too had to be read another way. Al-Tahawi performs the operation, and Ibn Abd al-Barr preserves it.

قَالَ الطَّحَاوِيُّ وَيُحْتَمَلُ أَنْ يَكُونَ تَشْدِيدًا وَتَغْلِيظًا وَتَحْذِيرًا لِئَلَّا يُوَاقِعَ ذَلِكَ أَحَدٌ كَنَحْوِ مَا هَمَّ بِهِ النَّبِيُّ - عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ - أَنْ يَحْرِقَ عَلَى مَنْ تَخَلَّفَ عَنْ صَلَاةِ الْجَمَاعَةِ بُيُوتَهُمْ

"Al-Tahawi said it may be severity, harshness, and warning, so that no one falls into it. It resembles what the Prophet ﷺ intended, to burn the houses of those who stayed back from congregational prayer."

- al-Tahawi (d.321 AH), in Ibn Abd al-Barr, al-Istidhkar, vol.5, p449

Umar’s stoning threat becomes stern warning, like the Prophet’s ﷺ threat to burn houses. A fixed legal penalty becomes a sermon device. The same passage treats Ibn Umar’s verdict of promiscuity the same way, since a penalty can be set aside by doubt. What could not be managed away is the state the schools had reached by Ibn Taymiyya’s time, and he records it while refuting it.

فَإِنَّ مِنَ الْعُلَمَاءِ مَنْ يَقُولُ : إنَّ هَذَا لَا يَأْثَمُ بِحَالٍ فَإِنَّهُ لَمْ يَكُنْ رُكْنًا فِي الْعَقْدِ الْأَوَّلِ بِحَالٍ حَتَّى يُقَالَ : لُعِنَ لِاعْتِقَادِهِ وُجُوبَ الْوَفَاءِ بِالتَّحْلِيلِ

"For among the scholars are those who say this man sins in no case at all, since he was no pillar of the first contract in any way, so that one could say he was cursed for believing he must fulfil the tahleel."

- Ibn Taymiyya (d.728 AH), Majmu’ al-Fatawa, vol.20, p275

Ibn Taymiyya lists the ways the curse gets divided. Is the client cursed for the tahleel itself, or only for believing the stipulation binding, or for both together? He calls the whole exercise void. His refutation is also a witness statement. Scholars had reached the point of saying the client sins in no case whatsoever, and the curse had become a question of which belief, in which man’s mind, on which reading of a stipulation. The plain reading, that the curse means what it says about the practice Umar’s ruling produced, is the one reading no school could adopt without giving up the triple. One question is left, and it goes to the man himself.

6. Ask the He-Goat

Subhani’s Question

Claim: the halala husband has no share in anything marriage is, and the Imami count removes the need for him entirely

Source: Silsilat al-Masa’il al-Fiqhiyya, vol.15, p79

Author’s Madhhab: al-Subhani (d.1442 AH), Imami (Twelver Shia)

The modern Imami jurist Ja’far Subhani gathers the whole anatomy of the practice into one question addressed to the man himself.

فسل التيس المستعار : هل له من ذلك نصيب ، أو هو من حكمة هذا العقد ومقصوده ومصلحته أجنبي غريب

"So ask the borrowed he-goat, does he have a share of any of that, or is he a stranger to the wisdom of this contract, its purpose and its benefit?"

- Ja{P}far Subhani, Silsilat al-Masa{P}il al-Fiqhiyya, vol.15, p79

Ask the he-goat. Does he keep her, house her, provide for her, want children with her, stand beside her in hardship? The halala husband does none of it, because the marriage was built to end, and he was hired to end it. Every element the Qur’an gives marriage, rest, affection, mercy, is absent by design. The schools that kept the triple had fourteen centuries to ask the question and answered it with five readings of a curse. The Imami school never needs to ask. One divorce in one sitting is the count of the Prophet ﷺ, so three words in one breath never strand a woman outside her marriage, and no woman is ever handed to a borrowed goat for them. A woman who reaches a third divorce by three real steps still walks through the door of 2:230, a marriage meant as a marriage.

Glossary of Key Terms

halala: the practice of marrying a thrice-divorced woman to make her lawful for her first husband, named from 2:230’s word halla, to become lawful

tahleel: the act of making the thrice-divorced woman lawful again, the word the jurists use for the purpose behind the muhallil’s marriage

muhallil: the man who contracts the halala, the borrowed he-goat of the Prophet’s ﷺ curse

muhallal lahu: the first husband, the one for whom the halala is arranged, named in the curse beside the muhallil

triple divorce: pronouncing three divorces in one sitting, counted as one under the Prophet ﷺ and as three after Umar’s ruling

nikah al-raghba: a marriage of real desire, Ibn Abbas’s and Uthman’s condition for the second marriage of 2:230

karahiyya: dislike, the legal category of an act a Muslim may commit though it is mildly wrong, where Ibn Nujaym files the curse

ta’wil: carrying a text to a meaning away from its apparent sense, the instrument behind every reading in section 5

musnad: a hadith collection organized by Companion, the genre of Ahmad ibn Hanbal’s Musnad

musannaf: a hadith collection organized by legal topic, the genre of Abd al-Razzaq’s work

Final Verdict

The verse never built a trap. The ruling that came fourteen years later built the trap, the Prophet’s ﷺ own words brand the men caught inside it, and the schools’ own manuals keep the door open to this day.

Qur’an 2:230 guards a real second marriage. Under the Prophet ﷺ, three divorces in one sitting counted as one, on his own ruling in the case of Ibn Kinana, and the count held through Abu Bakr’s caliphate and two years of Umar’s . Then Umar enforced the rushed triple as three, on his own statement in the Musnad, and al-Nawawi ranks the report of the old count among the problematic hadiths because the school’s law needs the new one. The instant triple turned 2:230 into a revolving door, and the door produced the muhallil, the borrowed he-goat the Prophet ﷺ cursed, the practice Ibn Umar called promiscuity, the case Umar ruled to stone, and the volunteer Uthman turned away.

The law books then did their work in the open. Abu Hanifa validated the muhallil’s marriage and his staying in it, al-Shafi’i validated it on the intention alone, Salim and al-Qasim and Rabi’a and Yahya ibn Sa’id called the muhallil rewarded, and some Hanafis called his work devotion. The curse itself was read as a stipulation, a naming according to their claim, a belief in the men’s minds, a proof of validity, a mere dislike, and a warning that never became law, everything except the practice it names.

The Imami school kept the count of the Prophet ﷺ. One sitting, one divorce, no trap, no goat, no woman handed to a man hired to return her. On this record the halala is not a divine law the school must live with. It is the fruit of a dated change the canon itself preserves, and the borrowed he-goat is its child.
