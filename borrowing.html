A Christian Charge the Book Already Records

The Borrowing Charge the Qur'an Already Answers

A circulating attack charges that Muhammadﷺ borrowed the Qur'an's stories from Judaism and Christianity. The Qur'an records that charge and answers it with the clarity of its own Arabic, as the early tafsir shows.

← Faith & Reason in Islam Overview Facts 1. The Accusers Named 2. The Charge's Circle 3. Cain and Abel 4. Abraham and Idols 5. Christian Themes 6. Confirm, Not Conceal Final Verdict

The Argument Under Examination

Premise 1. The Qur'an Records the Charge and Names the Accusers

The Qur'an itself records the claim that Muhammadﷺ borrowed its stories and names the pagan Meccans who made it. The early tafsir names them in turn. The exposure the attack claims to make was already answered in the Book.

Premise 2. The Qur'an Answers With Clarity and a Challenge

The Book answers the charge by pointing to the clarity of its Arabic and by issuing a literary challenge no borrower could meet. Bring a chapter like it, and bring your witnesses. A borrowed text could be parodied by the sources it copied.

Premise 3. Shared Stories Are Not Proof of Copying

A shared Near Eastern story world is not transmission. The critics supply no chain of transmission, and they admit the influence was oral. Parallel is not plagiarism.

Premise 4. Where a Source Is Named, the Qur'an Omits Its Details

Where the attack cites the Mishnah, the Midrash, or the apocrypha, the Qur'an's own text leaves out the very details those sources supply. The Qur'an's Abraham names no Nimrod, and its Maryam﵍ names no apocryphal infancy. That shows independence, not copying.

Premise 5. The Qur'an States Its Own Design

The Book says it confirms what came before and that it is preserved. If it shares substance with earlier revelation, that is its stated design, not a secret theft. The manuscript trail the charge requires was never supplied.

Conclusion. The Charge Is the Qur'an's Own Recorded Objection

The Qur'an records the borrowing charge and names the men who made it. The Book answers with the clarity of its Arabic and with a challenge no source could meet. Where the attack points to the Mishnah, the Midrash, or the apocrypha, the Qur'an omits those sources' details. The critics admit the influence was oral and supply no document linking Muhammadﷺ to the texts. Parallel is not plagiarism, and the charge fails on the Qur'an's own terms.

1. The Accusers Are Named and Answered

Sources. al-Qummi, v2, p111; Nur al-Thaqalayn, v4, p6; Tabarsi Mukhtasar, v2, p425; al-Burhan, v4, p112

Charge. pagan Meccans accused Muhammadﷺ of repeating tales he had written down and of being taught by a man

Claim. the Qur'an records the charge and answers it with the clarity of its Arabic, before any modern critic wrote

The Charge in the Qur'an's Own Words

The charge was made by pagan Meccans, not by the Qur'an's friends. The early tafsir names them. al-Qummi (v2, p111) says they learned the tales from Jews and Christians. Nur al-Thaqalayn (v4, p6) names al-Nadr b. al-Harith and his helpers. Tabarsi Mukhtasar (v2, p425) lists Addas, Yasar, and Jud.

وَقَالَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا إِنْ هَذَا إِلَّا إِفْكٌ افْتَرَاهُ وَأَعَانَهُ عَلَيْهِ ق

wa-qāla lladhīna kafarū inna hādhā illā ifkun iftarāhu wa-aʿānahu ʿalayhi qawmun ākharūna fa-qad jāʾū ẓulman wa-zūrā

Those who disbelieve say, "This is only a lie he invented, and other people helped him with it." They have brought injustice and falsehood.

Qur'an 25:4

وَقَالُوا أَسَاطِيرُ الْأَوَّلِينَ اكْتَتَبَهَا فَهِيَ تُمْلَى عَلَيْهِ بُكْرَةً وَأَ

wa-qālū asāṭīru l-awwalīna ktatabahā fa-hiya tumlā ʿalayhi bukratan wa-aṣīlā

They say, "These are tales of earlier peoples that he had written down. They are dictated to him morning and evening."

Qur'an 25:5

The Answer in the Qur'an's Own Words

The Qur'an does not hide the charge. It states the charge and then answers it in the same breath. The sign it gives is the language itself, clear Arabic, not the foreign tongue the accusers named.

وَلَقَدْ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّهُمْ يَقُولُونَ إِنَّمَا يُعَلِّمُهُ بَشَرٌ لِسَانُ الَّذِي

wa-laqad naʿlamu annahum yaqūlūna innamā yuʿallimuhu basharun lisānu lladhī yulḥidūna ilayhi aʿjamiyyun wa-hādhā lisānun ʿarabiyyun mubīn

We know that they say, "A human being teaches him." The language of the man they point to is foreign, while this is clear Arabic speech.

Qur'an 16:103

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِآَيَاتِ اللَّهِ لَا يَهْدِيهِمُ اللَّهُ وَلَهُمْ

inna lladhīna lā yu'minūna bi-āyāti llāhi lā yahdīhimu llāhu wa-lahum ʿadhābun alīm

Allah does not guide those who do not believe in the signs of Allah. A painful punishment awaits them.

Qur'an 16:104

The answer is linguistic and internal. The Qur'an says the tongue the accusers named is foreign, while the revelation is clear Arabic. The charge returns as the Book's own opening move, already answered.

Who the Accusers Were

The attack builds its case on verses the Qur'an already quotes against itself. The exposure it claims was answered in the Book fourteen centuries ago. The pagan accusers are named in the tafsir, and the Qur'an's reply stands in the same breath as the charge.

2. The Charge Assumes Its Own Conclusion

Sources. Margoliouth, Watt, Torrey, Geiger, Rodinson, as marshalled by the attack

Charge. the argument moves from parallels to hearing to plagiarism in a circle

Claim. the Qur'an answers with a literary challenge, and the critics admit the influence was oral

A Circle, Not a Proof

The attack and the scholars it quotes move in a circle. They argue that if Muhammadﷺ heard these stories, the Qur'an is not revelation. They then say he must have heard them because the parallels exist. The parallels are taken as proof of the hearing, and the hearing is taken as proof of the parallels.

A Literary Challenge, Not a Confession

The Qur'an meets the charge with a challenge. Bring a chapter like it, and bring your witnesses. A borrowed book could be parodied by its sources. The Qur'an says no such rival text can be produced.

وَإِنْ كُنْتُمْ فِي رَيْبٍ مِمَّا نَزَّلْنَا عَلَى عَبْدِنَا فَأْتُوا بِسُورَةٍ مِنْ

wa-in kuntum fī raybin mimmā nazzalnā ʿalā ʿabdinā fa-tū bi-sūratin min mithlihi wa-dʿū shuhadā'akum min dūni llāhi in kuntum ṣādiqīn

If you doubt what We sent down to Our servant, then bring a chapter like it. Call your witnesses apart from Allah, if you speak the truth.

Qur'an 2:23

قُلْ لَئِنِ اجْتَمَعَتِ الْإِنْسُ وَالْجِنُّ عَلَى أَنْ يَأْتُوا بِمِثْلِ هَذَا الْق

qul la-ini jtamaʿati l-insu wa-l-jinnu ʿalā an ya'tū bi-mithli hādhā l-qur'āni lā ya'tūna bi-mithlihi wa-law kāna baʿḍuhum li-baʿḍin ẓahīrā

Say, "If all people and jinn gathered to produce the like of this Qur'an, they could never produce its like, even if they helped one another."

Qur'an 17:88

Parallels Are Not Plagiarism

The critics admit the influence was oral. The attack's author writes that his sources all agree Muhammadﷺ learned by word of mouth and that no one asserts Muhammadﷺ read these other writings. Torrey says Muhammadﷺ does not deny the human teacher, but only insists that the teaching came down from heaven. The charge is influence, not transcription, and the Qur'an answers influence by naming its source as revelation.

3. Cain and Abel Is a Law for Israel

Sources. Noldeke, cited in the attack; Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5

Charge. the Qur'an took Cain and Abel from the Mishnah

Claim. the Qur'an presents the life for life rule as its own ordinance to Israel, not a quote from a Rabbi

The Qur'an's Version

The attack says the Qur'an took the Cain and Abel story from Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5. Noldeke makes the claim on the life for life rule in Qur'an 5:32. The Qur'an presents the rule as its own ordinance to the Israelites, not as a quote from a Rabbi.

وَاتْلُ عَلَيْهِمْ نَبَأَ ابْنَيْ آَدَمَ بِالْحَقِّ إِذْ قَرَّبَا قُرْبَانًا فَتُقُبّ

wa-tlu ʿalayhim naba'a bnay Ādama bi-l-ḥaqqi idh qarrabā qurbānan fa-tuqibbala min aḥadihimā wa-lam yutaqabbal mina l-ākhari qāla la-aqtulannaka qāla innamā yataqabbalu llāhu mina l-muttaqīn

Recite to them the true account of Adam's two sons. Each offered a sacrifice. It was accepted from one of them and was not accepted from the other. One said, "I will surely kill you." The other replied, "Allah accepts only from those who are mindful of Him."

Qur'an 5:27

مِنْ أَجْلِ ذَلِكَ كَتَبْنَا عَلَى بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ أَنَّهُ مَنْ قَتَلَ نَفْسًا بِغ

min ajli dhālika katabnā ʿalā Banī Isrā'īla annahu man qatala nafsan bi-ghayri nafsin aw fasādin fī l-arḍi fa-ka-annamā qatala n-nāsa jamīʿā

Because of this, We decreed for the Children of Israel that whoever kills a person, without that person having killed someone or spread corruption in the land, is as though he killed all people. Whoever saves a life is as though he saved all people. Our messengers came to them with clear proofs. Yet many of them still commit excesses in the land.

Qur'an 5:32

What the Mishnah Says

The Mishnah reads Genesis 4:10, where the earth cries out from Abel's blood, as a plural bloods to teach that one who destroys a single life destroys a whole world. The Qur'an reaches the same moral from its own revealed law. A shared ethical conclusion across traditions is not a textual lift, and the Qur'an credits the law to Allah, not to a Rabbi.

Why the Echo Is Not a Lift

The Qur'an frames Cain and Abel as a sign of godwariness, not as a legal citation. It tells the story to teach accountability before God. The thematic echo with a much later Rabbi's reading shows a common moral world, not a copied page. Noldeke himself says the likeness could readily have been picked up in conversation, which is his own admission that no document links the two.

4. Abraham and the Idols

Sources. Tabari history; Genesis Rabbah; al-Burhan, v3, p823

Charge. the Qur'an took Abraham's break with idolatry from the Midrash

Claim. the Qur'an's Abraham is a rational monotheist, and the text names no Nimrod, furnace, or Midrashic genealogy

A Rational Monotheist

The attack says the Qur'an took Abraham's break with idolatry from Genesis Rabbah. The Qur'an's Abraham is a rational monotheist who reasons with his people and is delivered from the fire. It names no Nimrod, no furnace, and no Midrashic genealogy.

وَلَقَدْ آَتَيْنَا إِبْرَاهِيمَ رُشْدَهُ مِنْ قَبْلُ وَكُنَّا بِهِ عَالِمِينَ

wa-laqad ātaynā Ibrāhīma rushdahu min qablu wa-kunnā bihi ʿālimīn

We gave Ibrahim sound judgment before this, and We knew him well.

Qur'an 21:51

The Idols Broken

فَجَعَلَهُمْ جُذَاذًا إِلَّا كَبِيرًا لَهُمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ إِلَيْهِ يَرْجِعُونَ

fa-jaʿalahum juzāzan illā kabīran lahum laʿallahum ilayhi yarjiʿūn

Then he broke them into pieces, leaving their largest idol, so that they might return to it.

Qur'an 21:58

The Fire Made Safe

قُلْنَا يَا نَارُ كُونِي بَرْدًا وَسَلَامًا عَلَى إِبْرَاهِيمَ

qulnā yā nāru kūnī bardan wa-salāman ʿalā Ibrāhīm

We said, "Fire, be cool and peaceful for Ibrahim."

Qur'an 21:69

What the Midrash Adds

The Nimrod and furnace story belongs to Tabari's history and to Genesis Rabbah, not to the Qur'an. al-Burhan (v03 p823) notes that the Nimrod account appears in the tafsir while the Qur'an text leaves Nimrod unnamed. The attack conflates the Qur'an with later Muslim historiography to manufacture a copy the Qur'an does not contain. The Qur'an's polemic is against idolatry itself, not a retelling of the Midrash. On the wider question of how earlier scriptures changed, see the destruction of earlier texts.

5. The Christian Themes Differ from the Apocrypha

Sources. Bell, Origin of Islam; Trimingham, Christianity Among the Arabs; Protevangelium of James; Arabic Gospel of the Infancy

Charge. the Mary﵍ and Jesus﵇ stories came from New Testament apocrypha

Claim. the Qur'an's Maryam﵍ is chaste and purified, its Jesus﵇ a messenger and spirit from Allah, cutting against both apocrypha and the dogma the attack defends

Confirming, Not Copying

The attack says the Mary﵍ and Jesus﵇ stories came from New Testament apocrypha such as the Protevangelium of James and the Arabic Gospel of the Infancy. The Qur'an's Maryam is the one who guarded her chastity and is called purified. Its Jesus is a messenger and a spirit from Allah, not a divine child. These cut against both the apocrypha and the mainstream dogma the attack defends.

نَزَّلَ عَلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ بِالْحَقِّ مُصَدِّقًا لِمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ وَأَنْزَلَ الت

nazzala ʿalayka l-kitāba bi-l-ḥaqqi muṣaddiqan limā bayna yadayhi wa-anzala t-tawrāta wa-l-injīl

He sent down to you the Book with the truth, confirming what came before it. He also sent down the Torah and the Gospel.

Qur'an 3:3

Maryam﵍ Chosen and Purified

وَإِذْ قَالَتِ الْمَلَائِكَةُ يَا مَرْيَمُ إِنَّ اللَّهَ اصْطَفَاكِ وَطَهَّرَكِ وَاصْ

wa-idh qālati l-malā'ikatu yā Maryamu inna llāha iṣṭafāki wa-ṭahharaki wa-iṣṭafāki ʿalā nisā'i l-ʿālamīn

The angels said to Mary, Allah has chosen you, purified you, and chosen you above the women of all worlds.

Qur'an 3:42

قَالَتْ رَبِّ أَنَّى يَكُونُ لِي وَلَدٌ وَلَمْ يَمْسَسْنِي بَشَرٌ قَالَ كَذَلِكِ اللّ

qālat rabbi annā yakūnu lī waladun wa-lam yamsasnī basharun qāla ka-dhāliki llāhu yakhluqu mā yashā'u idhā qaḍā amran fa-innamā yaqūlu lahu kun fa-yakūn

She said, My Lord, how can I have a child when no man has touched me? He said, Allah creates what He wills. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, "Be," and it is.

Qur'an 3:47

The Annunciation

وَاذْكُرْ فِي الْكِتَابِ مَرْيَمَ إِذِ انْتَبَذَتْ مِنْ أَهْلِهَا مَكَانًا شَرْقِيًّ

wa-udhkur fī l-kitābi Maryama idh intabadhat min ahlihā makānan sharqiyyā

Remember Mary in the Book, when she withdrew from her family to an eastern place.

Qur'an 19:16

قَالَ إِنَّمَا أَنَا رَسُولُ رَبِّكِ لِأَهَبَ لَكِ غُلَامًا زَكِيًّا

qāla innamā ana rasūlu rabbiki li-ahaba laki ghulāman zakiyyā

He said, I am only a messenger from your Lord, sent to give you a pure son.

Qur'an 19:19

Who Jesus﵇ Is

ذَلِكَ عيسَى ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ قَوْلَ الْحَقِّ الَّذِي فِيهِ يَمْتَرُونَ

dhālika ʿĪsā bnu Maryama qawlu l-ḥaqqi lladhī fīhi yamtarūn

This is Jesus, son of Mary. It is the true word about which they dispute.

Qur'an 19:34

إِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ اذْكُرْ نِعْمَتِي عَلَيْكَ وَعَلى وَالِ

idh qāla llāhu yā ʿĪsā bnu Maryama udhkur niʿmatī ʿalayka wa-ʿalā wālidatiki ayyadtuka bi-rūḥi l-qudusi tukallimu n-nāsa fī l-mahdi wa-kahlan wa-ʿallamtuka l-kitāba wa-l-ḥikmata wa-t-tawrāta wa-l-injīl

Allah said to Jesus, son of Mary, remember My favor upon you and your mother. I strengthened you with the Holy Spirit. You spoke to people from the cradle and in maturity. I taught you the Book, wisdom, the Torah, and the Gospel. By My permission, you shaped clay like a bird, then breathed into it, and it became a bird by My permission. You healed the blind and the leper by My permission. You brought the dead out by My permission. I held the Children of Israel back from you when you came to them with clear proofs. Those who disbelieved among them said, This is plainly magic.

Qur'an 5:110

The School's Reading

The early tafsir reads these verses on the school's own terms. al-Ayyashi (v01 p180) has Imam al-Baqir ﵇ teach that Maryam﵍ was chosen and purified of illegitimacy. Tabarsi Mukhtasar (v01 p440), al-Burhan (v02 p383), and Nur al-Thaqalayn (v01 p347) read 5:110 as Allah's reminder of His favors to Jesus ﵇, not as a borrowing from a Christian legend.

6. The Qur'an Confirms, It Does Not Conceal

Sources. Qur'an 3:3, 10:37, 26:196; al-Burhan, v3, p397; Tabarsi Mukhtasar, v1, p194

Charge. the shared substance with earlier revelation is a secret theft

Claim. the Qur'an states its own design, it confirms what came before and preserves itself

It Confirms What Came Before

The charge of borrowing fails on the Qur'an's own description of itself. The Book says it confirms what came before, and it says it is preserved. If it shares substance with earlier revelation, that is its stated design, not a secret theft.

وَمَا كَانَ هَذَا الْقُرْآَنُ أَنْ يُفْتَرَى مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ وَلَكِنْ تَصْدِيقَ ا

wa-mā kāna hādhā l-qur'ānu an yuftarā min dūni llāhi wa-lākin taṣdīqa lladhī bayna yadayhi wa-tafṣīla l-kitābi lā rayba fīhi min rabbi l-ʿālamīn

This Qur'an could never be invented apart from Allah. It confirms what came before it and explains the Book in detail. There is no doubt in it. It is from the Lord of all worlds.

Qur'an 10:37

It Is Preserved

إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا الذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ

innā naḥnu nazzalnā dhdh-dhikra wa-innā lahu la-ḥāfiẓūn

We sent down the Reminder, and We will surely preserve it.

Qur'an 26:196

al-Burhan (v03 p397) records Imam Ali ﵇ saying the family of the Prophetﷺ are the people of the Reminder and the mine of its interpretation. Tabarsi Mukhtasar (v01 p194) reads 3:3 as the sura confirming what preceded it and sent down with the Torah and the Gospel. The claim of preservation stands against the charge of theft, and the question of how the earlier scriptures were altered is treated at the preservation of the Qur'an.

Final Verdict

The borrowing charge is the Qur'an's own recorded objection, and the Book answers it on its own terms.

The Qur'an names the pagan accusers, answers them with the clarity of its Arabic, and issues a challenge no source could meet. Where the attack points to the Mishnah, the Midrash, or the apocrypha, the Qur'an's text omits the very details those sources supply, which shows independence rather than copying. The critics themselves admit the influence was oral and that no document links Muhammadﷺ to the texts.

Parallel is not plagiarism, and the manuscript trail the charge requires was never supplied. The Qur'an states its own design when it says it confirms earlier revelation and preserves itself. The accusation fails on the Qur'an's own record.
