The Expedition of Dumat al-Jandal

Word came of a gathering at Dumat al-Jandal, the first town of Syria on the road from Medina, lawless in one line and merely armed in the others, and the Prophet ﷺ marched a thousand men by night to the oasis. The town emptied before him, leaving herds, one prisoner who walked free a Muslim, and a peace made on the road home. Part 16 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Town That Emptied

Dumat al-Jandal was the first town of Syria on the road from Medina, an oasis of brigands five nights from Damascus, the one called al-Jawf today. Word came that a crowd there wronged every traveler and meant to move on Medina. The Prophet ﷺ marched with a thousand men, moving by night and lying hidden by day, guided by a man of Udhra. The town emptied at his approach, and the march came home with herds, one convert, and a peace made on the road.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Ibn Ishaq's line comes through Ibn Kathir, who quotes Ibn Hisham's notes with it, the month and the deputy.

قال ابن إسحاق : ثم غزا رسول الله ﵌ دومة الجندل (1) . قال ابن هشام في ربيع الأول ، - يعني من سنة خمس - واستعمل على المدينة سباع بن عرفطة الغفاري . قال ابن إسحاق : ثم رجع إلى المدينة قبل أن يصل إليها ولم يلق كيدا ، فأقام بالمدينة بقية سنته

"Ibn Ishaq said. Then the Messenger of Allah marched on Dumat al-Jandal. Ibn Hisham said, in Rabi' al-Awwal, of year five, and he put Siba ibn Urfuta al-Ghifari in charge of Medina. Ibn Ishaq said. The Messenger of Allah turned back to Medina before reaching it, having met no hostility, and he stayed in Medina the rest of his year."

- Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Kathir, al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, vol.4, p.105, with Ibn Hisham's notes

The fuller operation is carried in a later compilation, cited here by its own page.

في ربيع الأول على رأس تسعة وأربعين شهرا من مهاجره (صلى الله عليه واله) وهي بلدة بينها وبين دمشق خمس ليال وبينها وبين المدينة خمس أو ست عشرة ليلة بقرب تبوك وهي أقرب بلاد الشام إلى المدينة وهي التي تسمى اليوم الجوف بلغه أن بها جمعا كثيرا يظلمون من مر بهم وأنهم يريدون أن يدنوا من المدينة فخرج إليهم لخمس ليال بقين من ربيع الأول في ألف من المسلمين فكان يسير الليل ويكمن النهار ومعه دليل من بني عذرة اسمه مذكور فلما دنا منهم إذا آثار النعم والشاء فهجم على ماشيتهم ورعاتهم فأصاب من أصاب وهرب من هرب وجاء الخبر أهل دومة فتفرقوا ونزل بساحتهم فلم يجد بها أحدا وبث السرايا فرجعت ولم تصب أحدا وجاءت كل سرية بابل وأخذ منهم رجل فسأله عنهم فقال هربوا حيث سمعوا أنك أخذت نعمهم فعرض فأسلم ورجع (صلى الله عليه واله) إلى المدينة ولم يلق كيدا لعشر ليال بقين من ربيع الآخر . (1)

"In Rabi' al-Awwal, forty nine months after his migration, he marched on Dumat al-Jandal, a town five nights from Damascus and fifteen or sixteen nights from Medina, near Tabuk, the nearest town of Syria to Medina, the one called al-Jawf today. Word reached him that a great crowd there wronged everyone who passed by them, and that they meant to draw near Medina. He went out against them with five nights of Rabi' al-Awwal left, at the head of a thousand Muslims, marching by night and lying hidden by day, with a guide of Banu Udhra called Madhkur. When they drew near, there were the fresh tracks of camels and sheep, and they fell on the herds and their herders. Some were struck and some fled, and the news reached the people of Duma, and they scattered. He camped on their open ground and found no one in it, and he sent out detachments, and they came back having caught no one, each detachment bringing camels, and one man was taken of them. He asked him about the people, and he said, 'They fled when they heard you had taken their herds.' He offered him Islam, and he became Muslim, and the Prophet went back to Medina having met no hostility, with ten nights of Rabi' al-Akhir left."

- Maktabat al-Marja', p.1831

"In Rabi' al-Awwal came the expedition of Dumat al-Jandal. Its cause was word that reached the Prophet that a gathering of idolaters stood there, and he marched against them and met no hostility, and he left Siba ibn Urfuta al-Ghifari over Medina."

- Ibn al-Athir, al-Kamil fi l-Ta'rikh, vol.2, p.177

"The expedition of Dumat al-Jandal, in Rabi' al-Awwal of the fifth year. The Muslims with the Messenger of Allah were a thousand. The Messenger of Allah heard that a huge gathering had collected at Dumat al-Jandal to raid Medina, so he marched against it, and he learned the report was false, and turned back, and on the road he made peace with Uyayna ibn Hisn."

- al-Nadwi, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, p.650

The peace itself, with the man and the terms, in the critical sira's line.

موادعة عُيينة بن حصن الغادر : ويذكر المؤرخون : أنه لما رجع النبي «﵌» من دومة الجندل وادع عيينة بن حصن الذي كانت أرضه قد أجدبت : أن يرعى بتغلمين وما والاه إلى المراض ، وكان ما هناك قد أخصب ، وهو موضع بينه وبين المدينة ستة وثلاثون ميلاً على طريق الربذة

"The pact with Uyayna ibn Hisn. The historians mention that when the Prophet returned from Dumat al-Jandal, he made a pact with Uyayna ibn Hisn, whose land had been struck by drought, to graze at Tughlm and what borders it up to al-Marad, for what was there had grown rich, and it is a place thirty six miles from Medina on the al-Rabadha road."

- al-Amili, al-Sahih min Sira al-Nabi al-A'zam, vol.10, p.117

Commentary

The Sources for Dumat al-Jandal

Source spine: Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Kathir, al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, vol.4, p.105, with Ibn Hisham's notes. The serial note cited Ibn Hisham's Sira at a page the corpus does not hold, so the page quotes the edition the corpus does hold

Other carriers: Ibn al-Athir, al-Kamil fi l-Ta'rikh, vol.2, p.177. al-Nadwi, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, p.650

Fuller line: Maktabat al-Marja', p.1831, with the night marches and the guide

Agreements: the month, the deputy Siba ibn Urfuta, the empty town, the return without battle

Ibn Ishaq keeps the march to two lines, turned back before arriving. The fuller line adds the thousand men, the night movement, the herds, and the one prisoner who accepted Islam, and the modern digest adds that the gathering itself proved false and that a peace was made with Uyayna on the road home.

A Thousand Men Moving by Night

Cause: one line names brigandage, wronging every traveler, a second names a gathering of idolaters, a third says the report was false

Force: a thousand Muslims. Deputy over Medina, Siba ibn Urfuta al-Ghifari

Method: night marches, days hidden, a local guide, Madhkur of the Udhra

Contact: the herds taken, some struck and some fled, the town empty

Prisoner: one man, questioned, offered Islam, and he accepted

Return: no hostility met, ten nights of Rabi' al-Akhir remaining

The operation reads like a police action on a highway. The army moved under concealment, struck the herds that fed the gathering, camped on the empty ground, sent detachments out, questioned the one man it caught, and offered him the faith instead of the sword. The herds' herders bore the one recorded violence, some struck and some fled, and the men struck are not named or counted. The town's fighting population had run before the army arrived.

What the March Left Behind

Brigands: scattered, their herds gone

The road: the Syrian route from Medina had seen a thousand men walk it and return, and no garrison stayed behind

Peace: a grazing pact with Uyayna ibn Hisn, a chief whose drought-struck land needed pastures, made on the return road

Convert: one prisoner of Duma

Dumat al-Jandal sat where every caravan and every raid between Medina and Syria passed, and a thousand men camped on its ground. No carrier reports what the oasis made of the visit, only that the town fled it. The peace on the return road closed the march's account the way this series has come to expect, a grazing pact that tied a needy chief to Medina.

Was the Expedition of Dumat al-Jandal Justified

The cause, in one line, was brigandage, a crowd wronging everyone who passed, and a second line names a gathering of idolaters without the wronging. A third says the report itself was false, and that line is kept beside the others. What every line agrees on is the shape of the march.

The army took the herds, the town emptied itself at the news before the army arrived, and the march came home with one prisoner, who walked free a Muslim. The taking of the herds struck some of the herders, the fullest line says, and the men struck are not named or counted.

The expedition could have been prevented. The prevention was the gathering's to make, by not wronging the travelers, if the two lines that report a gathering are believed. On the false-report line, the march found herds and empty houses and became a demonstration, and that is what it became on either reading, since no battle happened.
