The March into the Ambush

The Prophet ﷺ had just come back from the treaty pilgrimage when the detachment marched. Ibn Abi al-Awja went out at the head of fifty men toward Banu Sulaym, and one of the fifty was a spy for the very tribe they marched against. He raced ahead, the tribe gathered a host, and the fifty walked into an ambush ready made. The invitation to Islam was answered with arrows, the reinforcements hemmed them in, and most of the fifty died where they stood.▼ commentary▼ commentary

لما رجع رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم من عمرة القضاء سنة سبع - رجع في ذي الحجة سنة سبع - بعث ابن أبي العوجاء السلمي في خمسين رجلاً، فخرج إلى بني سليم. وكان عين لبني سليم معه، فلما فصل من المدينة خرج العين إلى قومه فحذرهم وأخبرهم، فجمعوا جمعاً كثيراً. وجاءهم ابن أبي العوجاء والقوم معدون له، فلما رآهم أصحاب رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم ورأوا جمعهم دعوهم إلى الإسلام، فرشقوهم بالنبل ولم يسمعوا قولهم، وقالوا: لا حاجة لنا إلى ما دعوتم إليه. فراموهم ساعة، وجعلت الأمداد تأتي حتى أحدقوا بهم من كل ناحية، فقاتل القوم قتالاً شديداً حتى قتل عامتهم، وأصيب صاحبهم ابن أبي العوجاء جريحاً مع القتلى، ثم تحامل حتى بلغ رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم.

"'Muhammad related to me, from al-Zuhri, and he said. When the Messenger of Allah came back from the pilgrimage of al-Qada in the seventh year, his return in Dhu al-Hijja, he sent Ibn Abi al-Awja al-Sulami in fifty men, and he went out against Banu Sulaym. And a spy of Banu Sulaym was among them. When he left Medina the spy raced ahead to his people and warned them and told them, and they gathered a great host. Ibn Abi al-Awja came upon them with the people ready for him, and when the companions of the Messenger of Allah saw them and saw their host, they called them to Islam, and they shot arrows at them and would not hear them, and said, "We have no need of what you call us to." So the two traded arrows for a while, and the reinforcements kept coming until they hemmed them in from every side, and the men fought a hard fight until most of them were killed, and Ibn Abi al-Awja was struck down wounded among the dead, and then he bore himself up until he reached the Messenger of Allah.'"

- al-Zuhri's report, in Maktabat al-Marja', p.2114, and in al-'Amili, al-Sahih min Sira, vol.19, p.229

Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, carrying Ibn Sa'd, adds the date of the return.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"'Ibn Sa'd said. Then the detachment of Ibn Abi al-Awja against Banu Sulaym, in Dhu al-Hijja of the seventh year. They said the Messenger of Allah sent Ibn Abi al-Awja al-Sulami in fifty men against Banu Sulaym, and he went out to them, and a spy of theirs went ahead of him and warned them, and they gathered. Ibn Abi al-Awja came upon them ready for him, and he called them to Islam, and they said, "We have no need of what you call us to." So they traded arrows for a while, and the reinforcements kept coming until they hemmed them in from every side, and the men fought a hard fight until most of them were killed, and Ibn Abi al-Awja was struck down wounded among the dead, and then he bore himself up until he reached the Messenger of Allah, and they came into Medina on the first day of Safar of the eighth year.'"

- Ibn Sa'd, carried by Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.2, p.160

Commentary

The Sources for the March

Source spine: al-Zuhri's report, carried in Maktabat al-Marja', p.2114, and in al-'Amili, al-Sahih min Sira, vol.19, p.229

Sunni line: Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat as carried by Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.2, p.160, with the return date

A note on the catalog: al-Waqidi's master list counts this among the Prophet's ﷺ expeditions, and the reports describe it as a detachment of fifty under Ibn Abi al-Awja that the Prophet ﷺ sent, and both facts stay in view together

The two lines carry the same story from the same root, al-Zuhri, and the Sunni line adds the homecoming date, the first of Safar of year eight.

The Ambush and the Remnant

Dispatch: fifty men, on the Prophet's ﷺ return from al-Qada in Dhu al-Hijja

Commander: Ibn Abi al-Awja al-Sulami

Betrayal: a spy among the fifty warned Banu Sulaym, and the tribe gathered a host and stood ready

The fight: an invitation to Islam answered with arrows, then a hard fight hemmed in from every side

The cost: most of the fifty killed, the commander left wounded among the dead, who bore himself up until he reached the Prophet ﷺ

Return: the remnant entered Medina on the first of Safar, year eight

This is the series' first disaster in the field since Uhud. The record holds the facts and little else. The dead are counted, and none is named, in these reports. The wounded commander dragged himself from among the dead to the Prophet ﷺ, and the reports add no blame to him. The spy in the ranks is the point of the story.

What the Loss Meant

Tactical: a detachment destroyed, most of fifty men lost

Strategic: light in ground and heavy in the calendar, the defeat stood in the month before the conquest of Mecca

Context: Sulaym had been the frontier enemy since al-Kudr, and the loss changed no alliance on record

The month matters. The remnant came home in Safar of year eight, and within weeks the army that took Mecca marched. The defeat slowed nothing in the state's course, and the conquest that followed is the next part of this series.

Was the Dispatch Justified

The reports give a thin cause. A detachment marched toward Sulaym, the frontier enemy of the whole series, and no further cause for war is recorded. What the record does carry is the conduct of the fifty. They called to Islam before the arrows, and they fought to the end when the reinforcements closed. Nothing in the report touches a settlement, a family, or property. The march stands on the frontier war's own record, and the reports quoted here go no further.

Prevention lay with the spy. The ambush existed because his warning existed, and the story carries its own answer.
