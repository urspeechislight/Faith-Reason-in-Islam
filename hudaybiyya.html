Historical Objection · Debate

The Treaty the Other Side Broke First

The charge that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ signed a humiliating truce, relabeled it a victory by revelation, and then broke it himself collapses on the record, because the sura itself names the truce and the bloodless restraint as the victory and dates the dream to the next year, the Muslim side kept the pact and performed its pilgrimage clause on schedule, and the breach that ended it was Banu Bakr's night raid on Khuza'a aided by Quraysh horses, weapons, and named men, while the two breach charges against the Muslim side, Abu Basir and the migrating women, fail on the sources' own words. The Argument Under Examination

Background

One attack holds that in AD 628 the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ signed a humiliating truce at Hudaybiyya, relabeled the humiliation a victory by revelation, promised his followers the booty of Khaibar and took it, refused to return the believing women who fled to him, let Abu Basir raid Quraysh from Muslim ground, and finally broke the treaty himself by marching on Mecca in 630. Every pillar of that case has sources behind it, and every pillar turns on what those sources actually say, and the answer stands on the treaty's own text, on the sura's own definition of the victory, on the night raid that ended the truce, on the documents of the two breach charges, and on the words of the attack's own chief witness, and it rests on five premises.

Premise 1. The Terms Were Mutual and the Record Carries Them Whole

The treaty's own text was a settlement with mutual obligations, and the sources of both schools carry it. A ten-year truce. An open door for any tribe that wished to ally with either side. A return clause covering men who came without their guardian's leave, with apostates excluded from any return. Islam practiced openly inside Mecca, with no one forced from his religion. And a three-day pilgrimage for the Muslims the following year, proposed by Quraysh's own envoys. The humiliation reading rests on a paraphrase of two clauses. The document itself balanced duties on both sides.

Premise 2. The Victory Was the Qur'an's Own Claim With Its Own Content

The victory was the Qur'an's claim before it was anyone's explanation, and the sura names its own content. Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ said Sura al-Fath came down for the truce. The school's tafsir reads the "near victory" as the truce itself, the first of many openings. Verse 24 names the victory as restraint, Allah holding back both armies' hands in the valley of Mecca. Verse 27 dates the dream to the next year, and the next year the Muslims entered and circumambulated on the agreed days. The spoils verse was good news whose proof, in the school's words, Allah showed them soon after. Two years of open contact grew the community from fourteen hundred to ten thousand.

Premise 3. The Other Side's Camp Broke the Truce

The truce held for seventeen or eighteen months, and then the other side's camp broke it. Banu Bakr, standing inside Quraysh's pact, fell upon Khuza'a, standing inside the Prophet's ﷺ pact, by night at the water of al-Watir. Quraysh supplied horses and weapons, and Quraysh men fought under cover of the dark, among them, by the school's own account, Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl and Suhayl ibn Amr, the treaty's own signatory. Khuza'a's poet rode to Medina and called the pact in verse, and the Prophet ﷺ accepted the appeal at once. The Qur'an had fixed the rule beforehand. The Muslims' duty lasted exactly as long as the other side's conduct did.

Premise 4. The Two Breach Charges Fail on the Sources' Own Words

The two breach charges against the Muslim side fail on the sources' own words. Abu Basir was surrendered while the clause bound him, escaped to ground that belonged to neither city, and when his band closed Quraysh's trade routes, Quraysh dropped the clause in writing and begged the Prophet ﷺ to recall the men. The women who migrated were excluded from return by Qur'an 60:10, with the dowries repaid, and the school's sources record the return clause as a clause about men. The Prophet's ﷺ own words to Abu Basir, treachery does not befit us, governed the whole affair.

Premise 5. Khaibar Was a War Base and the Humiliation Reading Dies on Umar's Record

Khaibar was the remaining war base of the coalition that had besieged Medina, and its chiefs were the coalition's recruiters. Sallam ibn Abi al-Huqayq, Huyayy ibn Akhtab, and Kinana ibn al-Rabi, exiles of Banu Nadir, went to Mecca and Ghatafan and gathered the confederate army of the Trench. The closing march on that base stands on the Qur'an's own ground, the command to fight those who broke their oaths and began first, glossed by the early tafsir as the people of Mecca who helped Banu Bakr. And the humiliation reading dies on Umar's own record. His doubt that day, his question about which year, and the expiation he paid for the rest of his life are all carried by the same tradition that carries his objection.

Conclusion. The Other Side Broke It First

A truce the Qur'an itself called a victory, kept by the Muslim side to the letter and beyond it, honored by the pilgrimage of the next year, and ended by a night raid that the other side's allies committed with the other side's horses, weapons, and named men, cannot sustain a charge of treachery against the side that kept it. The attack's case needs a treaty the record does not show and a breach the record assigns to the accusers.

Two words were traded away, a formula and a title, and the Muslim side kept the substance. A treaty that needed Suhayl's signature could not open with a confession of Suhayl's faith. The concession bought what the next clause shows, a ten-year peace that bound the stronger party's hand as well. What the writing produced is the document the attack never quotes in full.

A Truce With the Door Left Open

The school's own record carries the treaty text through a report it classes as sahih, sound by the school's standard. The document opens with the truce and closes with the pilgrimage.

"Then he wrote these words. "This is the agreement between Muhammad ibn Abdullah, the leaders of Quraysh, and Suhayl ibn Amr.

Read the clauses as a set. War laid down for ten years, with each side holding back from the other. No theft, no treachery, and rancor kept covered. An open door for any tribe that wished to enter either side's alliance, which is the clause that decided the war's end two years later. A return clause about men who come without their guardian's leave. Islam practiced openly inside Mecca, with coercion and harassment barred, a clause the attack never mentions. And the three-day entry the next year, with only a traveler's sheathed swords. A document with six mutual clauses is a settlement. The humiliation reading picks the one clause it likes and drops the other five.

The alliance clause did its work the day the ink dried, and the two tribes who used it decided who would be standing where when the truce broke.

Two Tribes Stood Up and Chose

The treaty's open door was used at once, and the users named themselves before witnesses.

قال : فلمّا كتبوا الكتاب قامت خزاعة فقالت : نحن في عهد محمّد رسول الله (﵌) وعقده ، وقامت بنو بكر فقالت : نحن في عهد قريش وعقدها

"After they wrote the agreement, Khuzaah stood and said, "We enter the covenant and alliance of Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah." Then Banu Bakr stood and said, "We enter the covenant and alliance of Quraysh.""

Al-Wilaya al-Ilahiyya, vol. 1, p. 241, the day the pact was signed

Khuza'a, an old ally of the Prophet's ﷺ house, entered his pact. Banu Bakr, an old client of Quraysh, entered theirs. From that day, an attack by either tribe on the other drew its patron into the treaty's own terms, because the pact of the protégé is the pact of the patron. The stage for the breach was set by the treaty itself, in the open, on the first day.

The treaty's most attacked clause still needs reading against its own words, and the school's sources carry those words too.

What the Return Clause Actually Said

The attack paraphrases the return clause as a surrender of every Muslim who flees to Medina. The school's own tafsir carries the negotiation that produced the clause, in the words of the envoys who demanded it.

قالوا له وترد الينا كل من جاءك من رجالنا ونرد إليك كل من جاءنا من رجالك فقال رسول الله ﵌ : من جاءكم من رجالنا فلا حاجة لنا فيه ولكن على أن المسلمين بمكة لا يؤذون في إظهارهم الاسلام ولا يكرهون ولا ينكر عليهم شئ يفعلونه من شرائع الاسلام ، فقبلوا ذلك

"They said, "Return to us every man from our people who comes to you, and we will return to you every man from your people who comes to us." The Messenger of Allah replied, "We have no need for any of our men who come to you. The Muslims in Mecca must be safe when they practice Islam openly.

No one may force them, harm them, or object to anything they do from Islam's rites." They accepted this."

Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 2, p. 311, the envoys' demand and the Prophet's ﷺ counter-clause

Two things stand in the recorded exchange. The clause is about men, rijal, in both halves of the envoys' own sentence. And the Prophet's ﷺ answer bought a counter-clause the attack never quotes, that the Muslims of Mecca would worship openly, unforced, and unharmed. The other recension of the clause narrows it the same way.

The Muslims said, "Glory be to Allah. How can we return to the idolaters someone who came to us as a Muslim?" This condition weighed heavily on them.

They asked, "Messenger of Allah, will you accept this condition against yourself?" He replied, "Yes. Whoever of us leaves Islam, may Allah banish him.

Return applied to a Qurayshi convert whose guardian had not released him. Whoever left Islam and went back to Quraysh was refused return by Quraysh's own text. The clause cut one way in form, and the Muslims' objection to it is preserved in the same passage. The Prophet ﷺ accepted it with a promise attached, that Allah would make relief and a way out for whoever was sent back. The way out kept that promise, and its name was Abu Basir.

Suhayl demanded his son back under a treaty not yet finished, and the Prophet ﷺ asked him for protection for the boy, from the man beating him in front of the assembly. Suhayl refused, and two other Quraysh nobles granted the protection the Prophet ﷺ had asked for. The scene the attack calls betrayal is a scene of a father reclaiming a prisoner under a clause that was being written at that table, with the Prophet ﷺ publicly seeking the son's protection. The treaty bound the Muslim side, and the Muslim side kept saying so in its own sources. What the clause did to the men it caught, and how the promise of a way out kept its word, is the story section 5 carries.

The Night Raid at al-Watir

The truce held through its first year and more. Then the older blood feud between Banu Bakr and Khuza'a, a feud that predated Islam, broke out again, and Quraysh saw the darkness as cover.

قالا كان في صلح رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يوم الحديبية بينه وبين قريش ان ما شاء ان يدخل في عقد النبي ﵌ وعهده دخل فيه ومن شاء ان يدخل في عهد قريش وعقدهم دخل فيه فتواثبت خزاعة فقالوا ندخل في عقد محمد وعهده وتواثبت بنو بكر فقالوا ندخل في عقد قريش وعهدهم فمكثوا في تلك الهدنة نحو السبعة عشر أو الثمانية عشر شهرا ثم إن بنى بكر الذين كانوا دخلوا في عقد قريش وعهدهم وثبوا على خزاعة الذين دخلوا في عقد رسول الله ﵌ وعهده ليلا بماء لهم يقال له الوتير قريب من مكة فقالت قريش ما يعلم بنا محمد ﵌ وهذا الليل وما يرانا أحد فأعانوهم عليهم بالكراع والسلاح فقاتلوهم معهم للضغن على رسول الله ﵌

"They said that the Treaty of al-Hudaybiyyah between the Messenger of Allah and Quraysh allowed anyone who wished to enter the Prophet's covenant and alliance to do so. Anyone who wished to enter Quraysh's covenant and alliance could also do so.

Khuzaah quickly entered Muhammad's covenant and alliance. Banu Bakr quickly entered Quraysh's covenant and alliance.

They remained in that truce for about seventeen or eighteen months. Then Banu Bakr, who had entered Quraysh's covenant and alliance, attacked Khuzaah, who had entered the Messenger of Allah's covenant and alliance.

They attacked them at night at a water source called al-Watir near Mecca. Quraysh said, "Muhammad will not know about us in this darkness.

No one will see us." They helped Banu Bakr with horses and weapons. They fought beside them because of their hatred for the Messenger of Allah."

Al-Durr al-Manthur, vol. 3, p. 215, relaying Ibn Ishaq and al-Bayhaqi

Every element of the breach is in the passage. The raiders stood inside Quraysh's pact. The victims stood inside the Prophet's ﷺ pact. The attack came by night, at the water of al-Watir near Mecca. Quraysh said aloud that the night would hide them, then sent horses and weapons and fought beside the raiders, out of malice against the Messenger of Allah, in the passage's own words. A treaty clause about men fleeing to Medina had now been answered by a massacre of allied tribesmen in their sleep. Whatever else a breach is, this was one, and the school's own fiqh book names the men.

Quraysh Men Fought That Night

The school's jurisprudence of the affair does not leave the participation vague.

Al-Wilaya al-Ilahiyya, writing the school's legal case for the Meccan march, fixes the detail the Sunni narratives soften. Quraysh armed Banu Bakr, and Quraysh men fought hidden by night, and among them in person were Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl and Suhayl ibn Amr, the same Suhayl who signed the treaty and stood beating his son at its signing. The appeal followed at once, and its form mattered.

The Appeal Made in Verse

Khuza'a's answer to the raid was not a raid in return. Their poet, Amr ibn Salim, rode to Medina and stood before the Prophet ﷺ in the mosque, and invoked the pact in the form Arabia understood best.

"O Allah, I call upon Muhammad by the alliance of our father and his father. We were father and child, then we became Muslims and never withdrew our hand.

The poem names the alliance, names the conversion, names the broken promise and the confirmed covenant, and names the dead who were killed bowing and prostrating. The Prophet's ﷺ answer was immediate, you have been helped, Amr ibn Salim. No delay for counsel, no weighing of options. A pact invoked by its injured party, with the blood of prayer-men on the other side of it, was already decided in the hearing. What remained was the legal ground of the response, and the Qur'an had set it down before the event.

The Rule the Qur'an Set for Treaties

The treaty rule that governs Hudaybiyya was revealed in Sura al-Tawba, and the early tafsir applies it to this exact affair.

كَيْفَ يَكُونُ لِلْمُشْرِكِينَ عَهْدٌ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ وَعِنْدَ رَسُولِهِ إِلاَّ الَّذِينَ عاهَدْتُمْ عِنْدَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرامِ فَمَا اسْتَقامُوا لَكُمْ فَاسْتَقِيمُوا لَهُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُتَّقِينَ (7)

kayfa yakūnu li-l-mushrikīna ʿahdun ʿinda Allāhi wa-ʿinda rasūlihi illā alladhīna ʿāhadtum ʿinda al-masjidi al-ḥarām, fa-mā istaqāmū lakum fa-staqīmū lahum, inna Allāha yuḥibbu al-muttaqīn

"How can the idolaters have a covenant with Allah and with His Messenger, except those with whom you made a treaty at the Sacred Mosque? As long as they remain true to you, remain true to them. Allah loves those who are mindful of Him."

Qur'an 9:7

The rule is conditional on its face. The Muslims keep the treaty while the other side keeps straight. Qatada's gloss, carried by Suyuti's documentation, applies the verse to Hudaybiyya and states the application plainly.

وأخرج ابن أبي حاتم وأبو الشيخ عن قتادة ﵁ في قوله الا الذين عاهدتم عند المسجد الحرام قال هو يوم الحديبية فما استقاموا لكم فاستقيموا لهم قال فلم يستقيموا ونقضوا عهدكم أعانوا بنى بكر حلفاء قريش على خزاعة حلفاء النبي ﵌

"Ibn Abi Hatim and Abu al-Shaykh reported from Qatadah concerning the words, "except those with whom you made a treaty at the Sacred Mosque." He said, "This was on the day of al-Hudaybiyyah. As long as they remain true to you, remain true to them." They did not remain true.

They broke your covenant and helped Banu Bakr, the allies of Quraysh, against Khuzaah, the allies of the Prophet."

Al-Durr al-Manthur, vol. 3, p. 214, Qatada's gloss on Qur'an 9:7

They did not keep straight. They broke the covenant, and the gloss names the exact act, helping Banu Bakr, the allies of Quraysh, against Khuza'a, the allies of the Prophet ﷺ. The school's fiqh draws the legal conclusion as a standing rule.

فهذا الاستثناء وهذا التأكيد على وجوب الوفاء بعهد هؤلاء عبارة اُخرى عمّا نحن بصدده من وجوب الوفاء على المسلمين بالعهد الّذي عاهدوه مع هؤلاء المشركين ، ففيه دلالة واضحة على مشروعية عهد الصلح وعلى وجوب الوفاء به وهو المطلوب

"This exception, and this emphasis on keeping their covenant, expresses the duty of Muslims to keep the covenant they made with these polytheists. It clearly proves that peace treaties are lawful and must be honored. This is the point in question."

Al-Wilaya al-Ilahiyya, vol. 1, p. 527, the fiqh of the truce verses

The truce was lawful and binding, and it bound until the night at al-Watir. A charge of treachery therefore has a direction problem. The record points the breach at the side whose allies were raided upon, in the sources of both schools, before any march was dreamed of. What the victory sura actually claimed, and when, comes next.

When the truce was concluded, the sura came down. The victory label was pinned to the event at the event, by revelation, in the school's own transmitted words. The sura's opening runs as follows.

"Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory, so that Allah may forgive your past and future faults, complete His blessing upon you, guide you along a straight path, and grant you a mighty victory."

Qur'an 48:1-3

The school's mukhtasar of Majma' al-Bayan carries both readings of the word victory in the exegetical tradition, and both point forward.

The conquest reading and the truce reading agree that the sura promised something ahead, and the text itself names what the near term held. The first named victory involved no battle at all.

The Bloodless Victory the Verse Names

Inside the same sura, the Qur'an names a victory by its content, and the content is restraint.

وَهُوَ الَّذِي كَفَّ أَيْدِيَهُمْ عَنْكُمْ وَأَيْدِيَكُمْ عَنْهُمْ بِبَطْنِ مَكَّةَ مِنْ بَعْدِ أَنْ أَظْفَرَكُمْ عَلَيْهِمْ وَكانَ اللَّهُ بِما تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيراً (24)

wa-huwa alladhī kaffa aydīyahum ʿankum wa-aydīyakum ʿanhum bi-baṭni Makkata min baʿdi an aẓfarakum ʿalayhim, wa-kāna Allāhu bimā taʿmalūna baṣīrā

"He is the One who held their hands back from you, and your hands back from them, in the valley of Mecca after He gave you victory over them. Allah sees all that you do."

Qur'an 48:24

He held their hands back from you and your hands back from them. That is the verse's own definition of an act of God at Hudaybiyya, two armies restrained in the valley of Mecca, and the school's tafsir on the same page reads the near victory the same way.

""And He rewarded them with a near victory." This was the treaty, with the circumstances that made it a victory and the beginning of many victories. The conquest of Khaybar may have been one of them."

Al-Qubaysi, Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi, vol. 4, p. 354, on Qur'an 48:18

The truce, with its circumstances, was itself the victory, and the beginning of many openings, with Khaibar named as one of them, not as the prize of a fraud but as one item in a series. The spoils promise of verse 20 gets the same reading.

Al-Qubaysi, Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi, vol. 4, p. 354, on Qur'an 48:20

Good news, then proof shown soon after. The attack calls the Khaibar promise a bribe to soothe a humiliated camp. The school's tafsir calls it a prophecy fulfilled on a short clock, in a sura that had already dated its other promise to a calendar.

The dream of entering the Mosque is the attack's strongest card, because the Muslims came home without entering. The school's tafsir takes the card and shows its date.

Mukhtasar Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 3, p. 303, on the dream verse, Qur'an 48:27

The dream was seen in Medina before the march. The companions hoped for that same year, and the hypocrites mocked the miss, and the verse answered by moving the date, not the promise. The mukhtasar's next lines name the fulfillment and the calendar.

Before you entered Mecca, He granted you a near victory, the conquest of Khaybar. Mukhtasar Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 3, p. 303, the fulfillment and the calendar

The near victory was Khaibar, and the dream was fulfilled in Dhul-Qa'da of year 7, the very month of the barring, with the three days in Mecca that the treaty had written. A promise with a year attached, kept in the month attached. The dream objection was answered before it was made.

What the Truce Opened in Two Years

The fruit of the truce was measurable, and the early tradition measured it. Sharaf al-Din al-Musawi relays the Prophet's ﷺ own answer to the companion who asked how a turned-back pilgrimage could be a victory.

لقد أنزلت علي سورة هي أحب إلي مما طلعت عليه الشمس " ثم قرأ : (إنا فتحنا لك فتحا مبينا) .

"A surah has been revealed to me that I love more than all that the sun rises upon." Then he recited, "Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory." One of his companions said, "How is this a victory? They kept us from the House, stopped our sacrificial animals, and sent back two believing men who had come out to us."

Al-Nass wa-l-Ijtihad, p. 201, relaying Sahih al-Bukhari

That is the victory of a side that gained its aims without a battle. Al-Sha'bi, a generation later, put the fruit in numbers.

During those two years, every sensible person whom Muslims spoke with entered Islam. The number who entered Islam in those two years equaled, or exceeded, all who had entered before.

Fourteen hundred at Hudaybiyya, ten thousand at the Conquest, with the entry into Islam in those two years matching or exceeding all the years before. The truce did what the sword had not, it opened Arabia to the message, and the tradition the attack itself quotes, that Islam doubled in two years, is the same tradition. A victory defined by its fruits, its restraint, and its dated promise needs no relabeling. What Khaibar itself was, and why it was the next march, is the dossier the Trench left behind.

Khaibar was the address the coalition came from, the paymaster outside the gate, and the refuge the expulsions had built. For Medina, the war of the Trench did not end when the trenches emptied. It paused, under a truce, with the enemy's base intact.

The March the Qur'an Itself Grounded

When the truce broke at al-Watir, the march that followed had a revealed legal ground, and the early tafsir names the parties.

أَ لا تُقاتِلُونَ قَوْماً ما لكم لا تقاتلون قوما يعنى أهل مكة نَكَثُوا أَيْمانَهُمْ نقضوا عهودهم التي بينكم وبينهم وَهَمُّوا بِإِخْراجِ الرَّسُولِ أرادوا قتل الرسول حيث دخلوا دار الندوة وَهُمْ بَدَؤُكُمْ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍ بنقض العهد منهم حيث أعانوا بنى بكر حلفاءهم على بنى خزاعة حلفاء النبي صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم

""Will you not fight a people?" Why do you not fight the people of Mecca? "They broke their oaths." They broke the treaties between you and them.

"They planned to drive out the Messenger." They wanted to kill the Messenger when they entered the House of Assembly. "They began against you first." They first broke the treaty when they helped Banu Bakr, their allies, against Banu Khuzaah, the allies of the Prophet."

Ibn Wahb, al-Wadih fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, vol. 1, p. 308, the gloss on Qur'an 9:12-13

Ibn Wahb's gloss assigns the oath-breaking to the people of Mecca, reads the scheming against the Messenger as the old council of Dar al-Nadwa, and defines who began first, they did, when they helped Banu Bakr against the Prophet's ﷺ allies. The Conquest of Mecca was thus not the tearing of a treaty. It was the execution of a treaty's own default clause, after the other side's breach, on a rule the Qur'an had published in advance.

The Pilgrimage the Muslim Side Performed

The treaty's pilgrimage clause was honored on its own calendar by the side that signed it. The mukhtasar of Majma' al-Bayan, on the dream verse, records the fulfillment as history.

( فَعَلِمَ ) سبحانه من الصلاح في صلح الحديبية ( ما لَمْ تَعْلَمُوا ) أو من صلاح تأخركم عن دخول مكة ( فَجَعَلَ مِنْ دُونِ ذلِكَ ) أي من قبل دخول مكة ( فَتْحاً قَرِيباً ) فتح خيبر ، ثم عاد ( ص ) في السنة الثانية لصلح الحديبية وهي سنة سبع للهجرة في ذي القعدة الشهر الذي صده فيه المشركون على المسجد الحرام ودخل مكة مع أصحابه معتمرين وأقاموا بمكة ثلاثة أيام ثم رجعوا إلى المدينة

"Allah knew the good in the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah that you did not know. He also knew the good in your delay before entering Mecca.

Before you entered Mecca, He granted you a near victory, the conquest of Khaybar. Then, in the second year after the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, during Dhu al-Qidah in the seventh year after the Hijrah, the month when the idolaters had barred him from the Sacred Mosque, the Prophet returned with his companions for the lesser pilgrimage. They stayed in Mecca for three days, then returned to Medina."

Mukhtasar Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 3, p. 303, the fulfillment and the calendar

The same passage names Khaibar as the near victory granted before the entry, and then the entry itself, in Dhul-Qa'da of year 7, the month of the barring, three days in Mecca, then home. The Muslim side's record through the truce's life is performance, not breach. A pilgrimage performed on schedule, a base closed after the other side's raid, and a march on revealed ground leave the treachery charge with nothing to stand on. The two incidents the attack does call Muslim breaches still need their own hearing.

Treachery does not befit us. That is the treaty law of the Muslim side in four words, spoken while handing over a Muslim to his persecutors, with the promise of a way out repeated. The clause was honored to the letter, and the letter's cost was carried.

The handover itself, and the way out, ran as the sources give them.

The Prophet ﷺ surrendered Abu Basir under the clause, and Abu Basir settled the matter with the sword of the escort and fled to the seacoast, ground that belonged to neither city. The Prophet's ﷺ own remark, the mother of a war-kindler, said in front of the fleeing escort, told Quraysh what they were dealing with, and told Abu Basir he would be returned if taken. So he stayed outside the wall. The question a breach charge must answer is who broke what, and where the duty ran.

The Clause Quraysh Itself Cancelled

The coast band grew, and its arithmetic was economic.

وانفلت أيضاً من قريش «أبو جندل بن سهيل بن عمرو» فلحق بأبي بصير ، وجعل لا يخرج من قريش رجل قد أسلم إلّاالحق بأبي بصير ، حتّى اجتمعت منهم عصابة ، فما كانوا يسمعون بعِير خرجت لقريش إلّااعترضوا لهم فقتلوهم وأخذوا أموالهم ، فأرسلت قريش إلى النبيّ ﵌ تناشده اللَّه والرحم ، فكتب النبيّ ﵌ إلى أبي بصير وأبي جندل ليقدما عليه ومَن معهما من المسلمين ، فقدم كتابه ﵌ وأبو بصير يموت ، فمات وكتاب النبيّ ﵌ بيده يقرأه ، فدفنه أبو جندل مكانه وصلّى عليه وبنى على قبره مسجداً (1)

"Abu Jandal ibn Suhayl ibn Amr also escaped from Quraysh and joined Abu Basir. Whenever a man from Quraysh became Muslim, he joined Abu Basir.

A group gathered around them. Whenever they heard of a Quraysh caravan leaving, they intercepted it, killed its men, and took its wealth.

Quraysh sent word to the Prophet, calling on Allah and their family ties. The Prophet wrote to Abu Basir and Abu Jandal, ordering them and the Muslims with them to come to him.

His letter arrived while Abu Basir was dying. He died holding the Prophet's letter and reading it. Abu Jandal buried him there, prayed over him, and built a mosque over his grave."

Ajwibat al-Masa'il, vol. 2, p. 126, the continuation

Abu Jandal and the converts of Mecca gathered to Abu Basir until no Quraysh caravan moved safely. A clause that sent Muslim fugitives back to torture was, within a year, strangling the trade of the state that demanded it. Quraysh's response is the passage the attack never quotes.

فاضطرت قريش أن تكتب لرسول الله تسأله بالأرحام التي بينه وبينها ، إلا آواهم ، وأرسلت أبا سفيان بن حرب في ذلك ، فأبلغه أبو سفيان : " إنا أسقطنا هذا الشرط من شروط الهدنة ، فمن جاءك منهم فأمسكه من غير حرج "

"Quraysh had to write to the Messenger of Allah and appeal to the family ties between them, asking him to give them refuge. They sent Abu Sufyan ibn Harb for this purpose.

Al-Nass wa-l-Ijtihad, p. 205, Quraysh's cancellation of the clause

We have dropped this condition from the terms of the truce. Spoken by Abu Sufyan, in writing, in exchange for the recall of the band. The return clause died by the hand of its own authors, and the Prophet's ﷺ letter recalled Abu Basir and Abu Jandal, arriving as Abu Basir died with the letter in his hand. A clause dropped by its demandant cannot later be counted as the Muslim side's breach. The women's case rests on words just as specific.

The Women the Verse Excluded

The attack's second breach charge is the refusal to return the believing women who migrated, with Umm Kulthum named. The verse that governed the refusal is Qur'an 60:10.

يا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذا جاءَكُمُ الْمُؤْمِناتُ مُهاجِراتٍ فَامْتَحِنُوهُنَّ اللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِإِيمانِهِنَّ فَإِنْ عَلِمْتُمُوهُنَّ مُؤْمِناتٍ فَلا تَرْجِعُوهُنَّ إِلَى الْكُفَّارِ لا هُنَّ حِلٌّ لَهُمْ وَلا هُمْ يَحِلُّونَ لَهُنَّ وَآتُوهُمْ ما أَنْفَقُوا

"Believers, when believing women come to you as emigrants, test them. Allah knows their faith best.

Qur'an 60:10

The verse tests the women's faith, forbids their return, severs the marriage bond, and orders the dowries repaid. The school's tafsir carries the occasion with the treaty in view.

وجاءت قريش تطلب ردهن تنفيذا للمعاهدة .

ويظهر أن النص لم يكن قاطعا وشاملا للرجال والنساء .

فنزلت هاتان الآيتان تمنعان ردّ المهاجرات المؤمنات إلى الكفار .

While the Messenger of Allah and the Muslims were at the bottom of the valley, believing women came to him seeking to emigrate and join the abode of peace in Medina.

Quraysh came demanding that the women be returned under the treaty. The wording appears not to have been decisive and inclusive of both men and women.

Al-Qubaysi, Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi, vol. 4, p. 454, on the occasion of Qur'an 60:10

Al-Qubaysi's reading is careful where the attack is loose. Quraysh demanded the women under the treaty, and the wording, in his judgment, was not decisive and inclusive of men and women, and the revelation then settled the women's case by excluding them. The older report the school relays says the same exclusion in plainer words.

The dowry return is not a fine print excuse. It is the verse's own order, and the school reads it as the rule of justice between the two camps. A treaty clause about men, a revealed exclusion for women, and compensation paid on the verse's terms leave the broken-word charge without a word that was broken. Both breach charges close the same way, on documents the attack had in its footnotes all along. One pillar of the case remains, the humiliation itself.

Why Forgiveness Opens the Sura

The attack finds it strange that forgiveness of sins should be attached to a victory. In the school's reading the attachment is the point, and the Imam ﵇ was asked about it directly.

قال : قلت لأبي عبد الله ﵇ قول الله في كتابه " ليغفر لك الله ما تقدم من ذنبك وما تأخر " قال : ما كان له من ذنب ولا هم بذنب ولكن الله حمله ذنوب شيعته ثم غفرها له

"I asked Abu Abd Allah about Allah's words in His Book, "So that Allah may forgive your earlier and later sins." He replied, "He had committed no sin and had never intended any. Allah placed the sins of his followers upon him, then forgave them for him.""

Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 2, p. 314, on Qur'an 48:2

He committed no sin, and the sins carried in the verse are the followers' burdens borne and forgiven, in the Imam's ﵇ words. The opening forgives before it conquers, which is the sura's own order of goods, and the school's exegesis of the whole affair returns one answer at every level. The truce was the victory, the restraint was the act of God, the dream kept its date, the other side broke the pact, and the two breach charges dissolve on the documents. What remains is the verdict.

Final Verdict

The treaty of Hudaybiyya fails as a case against the Prophet ﷺ because every pillar of the case lands on the other side.

The terms were mutual, and the record carries them whole. A ten-year truce, an open alliance door, a return clause about men, public worship for the Muslims of Mecca, no theft and no treachery, and a three-day pilgrimage the next year, proposed by Quraysh's own envoys. The school carries the document as a sahih text. The humiliation reading quotes one clause and drops five.

The victory was the Qur'an's, defined in the Qur'an's own words. The sura came down for the truce, by the Imam's ﵇ word. The named act of God is held-back hands in the valley of Mecca. The near victory is the truce itself, the first of many openings. The dream was dated by the tafsir to the next year and landed in the named month. And the spoils promise was good news with its proof shown soon after. Fourteen hundred became ten thousand in two years, and al-Sha'bi's arithmetic matches the attack's own source.

The breach was the other side's, in the sources of both schools. Banu Bakr, inside Quraysh's pact, night-raided Khuza'a, inside the Prophet's ﷺ pact, at al-Watir, with Quraysh's horses and weapons and hidden Quraysh fighters, among them Ikrima and Suhayl the signatory. Khuza'a invoked the pact in verse, and the Prophet ﷺ answered at once. The Qur'an had already published the rule, keep the treaty while they keep straight, and Qatada's gloss names who did not.

The Muslim-side breach charges close on their own documents. Abu Basir was surrendered under the clause with the words treachery does not befit us, escaped to no man's land, and when his band closed Quraysh's trade, Quraysh dropped the clause in writing and begged for the band's recall. The women were excluded from return by Qur'an 60:10, with dowries repaid by the verse's order, and the school's sources record the clause as a clause about men from the negotiation onward.

Khaibar and the Conquest stood on war and revelation, not on treaty-breaking. Khaibar housed the Nadir exiles whose recruiters, Sallam, Huyayy, and Kinana, had built the confederate army of the Trench, and its march closed that war. The march of 630 executed the treaty's default clause on the Qur'an's published rule, fight those who broke their oaths and began first, glossed by the early tafsir as the Meccans who aided Banu Bakr.

And the humiliation died in the mouth of its own witness. Umar's doubt, Umar's year question, and Umar's lifelong expiation, alms, fasting, prayer, and freeing slaves over words spoken at Hudaybiyya, are all in the tradition the attack cites. The companions who objected came back and apologized, and the sura of good pleasure met them.

The treaty the attack calls a broken humiliation was a truce the Qur'an named a victory, the Muslim side performed to its calendar, and the other side's camp broke with horses, weapons, and named men under cover of night. The verdict of the record is the title of this file. The other side broke it first.
