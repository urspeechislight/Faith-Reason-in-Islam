Qur'anic Objection · Debate

The Sword Verse That Executes a Broken Treaty

The claim that Qur'an 9:5 is a standing order to kill polytheists near and far collapses on the passage's own brackets, because 9:4 exempts treaty-keepers, 9:2 grants four months of safe passage, and 9:6 orders asylum and an escort home for any enemy who asks, while the record fixes the occasion in Quraysh's breach of Hudaybiyya and the school scopes every sword verse to a defined body under the Imams' ﵈ authority. From Tafsir al-Qummi, al-Kafi, Tahdhib al-Ahkam, Bihar al-Anwar, al-Khara'ij wa-l-Jara'ih, Ahkam al-Qur'an, Lubab al-Ta'wil, and Zahrat al-Tafasir.

The Argument Under Examination

Background

The attack states its case in one sentence. The verse of the sword commands killing the polytheists wherever they are found, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ acted on it with armed force, the tafsirs and the tomes read it as the foundation of an offensive jihad, and the caliphs who knew him best carried it to Iraq and Persia. The sentence has real sources behind it. Sura 9 does open with a disavowal and a kill clause, the classical commentaries discuss a war reading, and Khalid's armies did march. Everything in the charge hangs on reading one verse free of the verses around it, and the case against that reading stands on the passage's own brackets, on the record of the year it belongs to, and on the school's war doctrine, and it rests on five premises.

Premise 1: The Passage Brackets Its Own Kill Clause

Qur'an 9:4 exempts every polytheist who honoured the treaty and aided no enemy, 9:2 grants four months of unmolested travel, 9:6 orders asylum and a safe escort home for any combatant who asks, and 9:7 binds the Muslim side to reciprocity as long as the other side stands by the pact. A command ringed by an exemption, an amnesty clock, and an asylum duty is treaty enforcement against violators. The attack prints the kill clause and drops the brackets around it.

Premise 2: The Occasion Is Fixed in the Record

Quraysh broke the treaty of Hudaybiyya by aiding Banu Bakr's night attack on Khuza'a, the Prophet's ﷺ allies. Abu Sufyan then came to Medina to renew the pact and was refused, and Ali ﵇ proclaimed the disavowal at the Hajj with a four-month clock set to named dates. The treaty partners themselves feared they had breached. The claim that the pagans did nothing is the attack's own assertion against its own sources.

Premise 3: The School's Sword Doctrine Is Bounded

The five-swords report in al-Tusi's Tahdhib assigns each fighting verse its own defined body, and reserves one sword's ruling to the Imams ﵈ themselves. The Kafi report, relayed in Wasa'il, gates the permission to fight on being wronged while keeping the conditions of faith, and Ali ﵇ ordered his men, do not begin fighting until they begin. The standing-conquest doctrine the attack builds from 9:5 has no home in this corpus.

Premise 4: The Peace Verses Stay in Force

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ concluded Hudaybiyya with polytheists while they still held their polytheism, and the transmitted rule forbids loosening a treaty knot before its term or without open notice on equal terms. The abrogation claim was contested from the first century, and the jurists' own rule reads a restricted verse as specifying an unrestricted one, never as repealing it.

Premise 5: The Corroborating Verses Carry Their Own Bounds

At 9:29 the school reads the fight as ending at the covenant, and at 9:123 al-Qummi reads each people as bound to the enemy near their own lands, and no farther. The attack's corroboration works only by reading three scoped verses as one standing edict.

Conclusion: The Standing War Is the Attack's Own Construction

Qur'an 9:5 executes a named broken treaty after public notice, with amnesty running and asylum open. The permanent war the attack reads out of the verse comes from his own tafsir selections, not from the verse's words, not from the school's hadith, and not from the record of the year the passage belongs to.

1. The Passage Brackets Its Own Kill Clause

The Exception Before the Command

The attack renders 9:5 as a free-standing edict. The passage refuses that reading one verse earlier, in the exemption the attack never prints.

إلا الذين عاهدتم من المشركين ثم لم ينقصوكم شيئا ولم يظاهروا عليكم أحدا فاتموا إليهم عهدهم إلى مدتهم

illā alladhīna ʿāhadtum mina al-mushrikīna thumma lam yanquṣūkum shayʾan wa-lam yuẓāhirū ʿalaykum aḥadan fa-atimmū ilayhim ʿahdahum ilā maddatihim

"Except those of the polytheists with whom you made a covenant. They did not fail you in anything and did not support anyone against you."

"Fulfill their covenant with them until their term."

- Qur'an 9:4, as embedded in Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 1, p. 309

The exception sorts the polytheists by conduct. Parties who kept the terms stay protected until the term runs out. Parties who broke them stand alone at 9:5. The category the kill clause reaches is defined one verse before it, and creed is not the line. Conduct is.

The Kill Clause and Its Exit

Here is the contested clause with its own terminus, from the corpus text of al-Kafi.

فَإِذَا انْسَلَخَ الْأَشْهُرُ الْحُرُمُ فَاقْتُلُوا الْمُشْرِكِينَ حَيْثُ وَجَدْتُمُوهُمْ

fa-idhā insalakha al-ashhur al-ḥurum fa-uqtulū al-mushrikīna ḥaythu wajadtumūhum

"When the sacred months have passed, kill the polytheists wherever you find them."

- Qur'an 9:5, opening clause, as embedded in al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol. 2, p. 209

The command opens on a clock, the passing of the sacred months. A command wired to a calendar is not open-ended. The same verse then builds its own exit, which the attack quotes as proof of forced conversion.

فَإِن تَابُوا وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ فَخَلُّوا سَبِيلَهُمْ

fa-in tābū wa-aqāmū aṣ-ṣalāta wa-ātaw az-zakāta fa-khallū sabīlahum

"If they repent, establish the prayer, and give the zakah, then let them go their way."

- Qur'an 9:5, exit clause

The exit is release, ordered in the same breath as the threat. Repentance joined to prayer and zakah is the public door into the polity, and the verse commands the Muslim side to clear the way, then stand down. A convert-or-die edict does not order its own stand-down.

Asylum Inside the Same Passage

The passage then does what no kill-on-sight law can do. It orders asylum for the enemy himself.

وَإِنْ أَحَدٌ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ اسْتَجارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ حَتَّى يَسْمَعَ كَلامَ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُ

wa-in aḥadun mina al-mushrikīna istajāraka fa-ajirhu ḥattā yasmaʿa kalāma Allāhi thumma abligghu maʾmanah

"If any one of the polytheists seeks your protection, grant him protection until he hears the word of God. Then convey him to his place of safety."

- Qur'an 9:6, as embedded in al-Bahrani, al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, vol. 2, p. 740

The school's earliest running tafsir reads the clause as a working procedure.

ثم قال (وان أحد من المشركين استجارك فاجره حتى يسمع كلام الله ثم أبلغه مأمنه) قال إقرأ عليه وعرفه لا تتعرض له حتى يرجع إلى مأمنه

"Then He said, 'If one of the polytheists seeks your protection, grant it to him until he hears the word of God, then convey him to his place of safety.' He said, recite to him, inform him. Do not confront him until he returns to his place of safety."

- The reading of 9:6, Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 1, p. 309

The combatant is read to, informed, and escorted home alive. The asylum duty sits inside the same passage as the kill clause, and it addresses the very group the kill clause names. The passage wants the breach ended, and it keeps a door open even while the notice runs.

Reciprocity One Verse Later

The passage closes its opening with the rule that governs everything after it.

كَيْفَ يَكُونُ لِلْمُشْرِكِينَ عَهْدٌ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ وَعِنْدَ رَسُولِهِ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ عاهَدْتُمْ عِنْدَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ فَمَا اسْتَقامُوا لَكُمْ فَاسْتَقِيمُوا لَهُمْ

kayfa yakūnu li-l-mushrikīna ʿahdun ʿinda Allāhi wa-ʿinda rasūlihi illā alladhīna ʿāhadtum ʿinda al-masjidi al-ḥarāmi fa-mā istaqāmū lakum fa-staqīmū lahum

"How can the polytheists have a pact with Allah and His Messenger, except those you made a pact with at the Sacred Mosque? As long as they stand straight with you, stand straight with them."

- Qur'an 9:7, as embedded in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 97, p. 47

The Muslim side's duty tracks the other side's conduct. That is the grammar of treaty law, and the passage states it in its own words. With the exemption at 9:4, the clock at 9:2, the asylum at 9:6, and the reciprocity at 9:7, the kill clause at 9:5 sits inside four walls the attack never mentions. What survives inside those walls is enforcement against violators. Whether the year's events match that frame is the next question, and the record answers it.

2. The Occasion Is Fixed in the Record

Who Broke the Treaty

Event. the disavowal of the Hudaybiyya treaty regime, year 9 AH

Sources. Ahkam al-Qur'an of al-Jassas, Lubab al-Ta'wil of al-Khazin, al-Khara'ij wa-l-Jara'ih of al-Rawandi, Bihar al-Anwar of al-Majlisi

Claim. the treaty partners breached first, sought renewal, failed, and received a dated public notice

The attack asserts that the pagans did nothing and that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ broke the truces himself. His own jurists state the opposite, in the works the attack's argument depends on. Al-Khazin fixes the exception of 9:4 to the event.

وإنما هم الذين قال اللّه ﷿ فيهم إلا الذين عاهدتم من المشركين ثم لم ينقصوكم شيئا كما نقصكم قريش ولم يظاهروا عليكم أحدا كما ظاهرت قريش بني بكر على خزاعة وهم حلفاء رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم

"These are the ones Allah meant in His words, 'Except those of the polytheists with whom you made a treaty.' They never failed you in anything, as Quraysh failed you. They never aided anyone against you, as Quraysh aided Banu Bakr against Khuzaa, the allies of the Messenger of Allah."

- Al-Khazin, Lubab al-Ta'wil fi Ma'ani al-Tanzil, vol. 2, p. 338, on the exception in Qur'an 9:4

The exception names the breach. Quraysh aided a night raid on Khuza'a, and Khuza'a stood inside the Prophet's ﷺ pact. Al-Jassas draws the legal consequence.

قد كانوا نقضوا العهد بمعاونتهم بني كنانة على قتل خزاعة وكانت حلفاء للنبي ﵌ ، ولذلك جاء أبو سفيان إلى المدينة يسأل النبي ﵌ تجديد العهد بينه وبين قريش فلم يجبه النبي ﵌ إلى ذلك ، فمن أجل ذلك لم يحتج إلى النبذ إليهم إذ كانوا قد أظهروا نقض العهد بنصب الحرب لحلفاء النبي ﵌

"They had broken the treaty by helping Banu Kinanah kill Khuza'ah, who were allies of the Prophet. Abu Sufyan therefore came to Medina and asked the Prophet to renew the treaty between him and Quraysh."

"The Prophet did not answer his request. He did not need to announce the treaty's end to them, since they had openly broken it by waging war against the Prophet's allies."

- Al-Jassas, Ahkam al-Qur'an, vol. 3, p. 88, on Qur'an 8:58

The jurist's analysis is decisive on the attack's own ground. The breach was Quraysh's, it was open, and it was military. The two tribes are named differently in the two works, Kinana in al-Jassas and Banu Bakr in al-Khazin, and both name the same event, the raid on Khuza'a with Quraysh's aid. A reader who checks the attack's own tafsir shelf finds this in it.

The Renewal Abu Sufyan Sought

The school's own sira collection carries the embassy the attack calls unnecessary.

إن أبا سفيان جاء إلى المدينة لاخذه تجديد العهد من رسول الله ﵌ فلم يقبل : فجاء إلى علي ﵇ ، قال : هل لابن عمك أن يكتب لنا أمانا ؟ فقال : إن النبي ﵌ عزم على أمر لا يرجع فيه أبدا

"Abu Sufyan came to Medina to seek a renewal of the treaty from the Messenger of Allah. He refused."

"Abu Sufyan then came to Ali and asked, 'Can your cousin write us a guarantee of safety?' Ali replied, 'The Prophet has resolved on a matter from which he will never turn back.'."

- Abu Sufyan's failed embassy, in al-Rawandi, al-Khara'ij wa-l-Jara'ih, vol. 1, p. 240, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq

The men who broke the pact knew what they had done. They sent their own chief to patch it, and he went home without an answer. The claim that the pagans were weak, demoralized, and oblivious collapses on this embassy, which exists only because the breach existed.

Four Months on a Named Calendar

The disavowal itself was served in public, at the Hajj gathering, with dates.

فقال : لا إلا أنه أنزل عليه أنه لا يبلغ إلا رجل منك ، فلما قدم علي ﵇ مكة وكان يوم النحر بعد الظهر وهو يوم الحج الأكبر قام ثم قال : إني رسول رسول الله إليكم فقرأ عليهم : " براءة من الله ورسوله إلى الذين عاهدتم من المشركين فسيحوا في الأرض أربعة أشهر " عشرين من ذي الحجة والمحرم وصفر وشهر ربيع الأول وعشر من شهر ربيع (2) الآخر ، وقال : لا يطوف بالبيت عريان ولا عريانة ولا مشرك ، ألا (3) ومن كان له عهد عند رسول الله فمدته إلى هذه الأربعة الأشهر

"He said, no, except that it was revealed to him that none conveys it but a man from you. When Ali arrived at Mecca on the Day of Sacrifice after midday, the Greatest Day of Pilgrimage, he stood."

"He said, 'I am the messenger of the Messenger of Allah to you.' He read out to them, 'An acquittal from Allah and His Messenger to those of the polytheists with whom you had made a treaty. Move freely in the land for four months,' twenty days of Dhu l-Hijja, then Muharram, Safar, and Rabi al-Awwal, and ten of Rabi al-Akhar."

"And he said, 'No naked man or woman may circumambulate the House, and no polytheist. Hear, whoever has a treaty with the Messenger of Allah, its term is these four months.'."

- The bara'a proclamation, in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 21, p. 275

The proclamation names the addressees, gives them four months of free movement, and sets the clock to exact dates. Every treaty-holder inside the pact keeps his term. This is a fixed-notice termination served on named parties at the largest gathering of the year. The attack calls it a surprise war on the peaceful. The text calls it a dated public notice.

The Rule Against Secret Termination

The charge of treachery meets the tradition's own standing rule, the report al-Jassas relays from Abu Dawud on the same page as his analysis of the breach.

فَأَرْسَلَ إِلَيْهِ مُعَاوِيَةُ فَسَأَلَهُ ، فَقَالَ : سَمِعْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ يَقُولُ : « مَنْ كَانَ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَ قَوْمٍ عَهْدٌ فَلَا يَشُدُّ عُقْدَةً وَلَا يَحُلُّهَا حَتَّى يَنْقَضِيَ أَمَدُهَا أَوْ يَنْبِذَ إِلَيْهِمْ عَلَى سَوَاءٍ » فَرَجَعَ مُعَاوِيَةُ

"Mu'awiyah sent for him and asked him. He said, 'I heard the Messenger of Allah say, whoever has a treaty with a people must neither tighten its knot nor loosen it until its term ends, or until he throws it back to them on equal terms.' Mu'awiyah returned."

- The Prophet's ﷺ rule on treaties, Sunan Abi Dawud (Dar al-Hadith ed.), vol. 3, p. 135, relayed by al-Jassas in Ahkam al-Qur'an, vol. 3, p. 88

The rule binds every treaty-holder, and the report's own ending shows its force. Muawiya, marching on a treaty that had simply expired, turned his army back when the rule was read to him. The school's law of war forbids exactly what the attack accuses the Prophet ﷺ of, secret termination by convenience. The event followed the rule, open breach, failed renewal, public notice, then a clock. The doctrine built on the verse is the next thing to check, and it refuses the attack's reading at every level.

3. The School's Sword Doctrine Is Bounded

Five Swords and Five Addresses

Report. the five swords, from Imam al-Baqir ﵇ through his son Imam al-Sadiq ﵇

Source. al-Tusi, Tahdhib al-Ahkam, vol. 6, p. 136

Claim. each fighting verse has its own defined addressee, and one sword's ruling belongs to the Imams ﵈

The attack treats 9:5 as the foundation of one standing war. The school's own war doctrine starts from a report that cuts the war verses apart.

وسيف منها مكفوف ، وسيف منها مغمود سله إلى غيرنا وحكمه إلينا ، فاما السيوف الثلاثة الشاهرة فسيف على مشركي العرب قال الله تعالى (فاقتلوا المشركين حيث وجدتموهم) (2) فهؤلاء لا يقبل منهم إلا القتل أو الدخول في الاسلام والسيف الثاني على أهل الذمة قال الله تعالى : (قاتلوا الذين لا يؤمنون بالله ولا باليوم الآخر) (3) الآية فهؤلاء لا يقبل منهم إلا الجزية أو القتل ، والسيف الثالث سيف على مشركي العجم يعني الترك والخزر والديلم

"One of these swords is restrained, and one is sheathed. Its blade is drawn against others than us, and its ruling belongs to us."

"As for the three drawn swords, the first is against the polytheists of the Arabs. Allah said, 'Kill the polytheists wherever you find them.' From these nothing is accepted except death or entry into Islam."

"The second sword is against the People of the Covenant. Allah said, 'Fight those who do not believe in Allah nor in the Last Day,' to the end of the verse."

"From these nothing is accepted except the jizya or death. The third sword is against the polytheists of the non-Arabs, meaning the Turks, the Khazars, and the Daylam."

- Imam al-Baqir ﵇, from Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Tusi, Tahdhib al-Ahkam, vol. 6, p. 136

The report the attack needs as one pillar is a taxonomy. Three drawn swords, three distinct bodies, three distinct verses, three distinct exits. The sword against the Arab polytheists, the body 9:5 addresses, carries its own end, death or entry into Islam, and that body is gone from history. And one sword's ruling, the report says, belongs to the Imams ﵈ alone. Whoever wants to wield that sword needs their commission, which is the point the conquest caliphate cannot pass.

The Conditions That Gate the Permission

The report al-Kafi carries, relayed by al-Hurr al-Amili, states the gate in the Imam's ﵇ own words, and it is not creed.

وبحجة هذه الآية يقاتل مؤمنو كل زمان ، وإنما أذن الله ﷿ للمؤمنين الذين قاموا بما وصف الله ﷿ من الشرائط التي شرطها الله ﷿ على المؤمنين في الايمان والجهاد ومن كان قائما بتلك الشرائط فهو مؤمن وهو مظلوم ومأذون له في الجهاد بذلك المعنى ، ومن كان على خلاف ذلك فهو ظالم وليس من المظلومين ، وليس بمأذون له في القتال

"By the proof of this verse, believers in every age fight. Allah granted permission only to believers who fulfill the conditions that He set for faith and jihad."

"Whoever fulfills those conditions is a believer who has been wronged and has permission for jihad in this sense. Whoever acts against those conditions is an oppressor, is not among those who have been wronged, and has no permission to fight."

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Hurr al-Amili, Wasa'il al-Shi'a, vol. 15, p. 39, relaying al-Kafi

The permission tracks a wrong received and conditions kept. Unbelief is nowhere in the formula. The fighter who leaves the conditions loses the licence in the same sentence that granted it. This is the school's standing doctrine of war, and it reads the sword verses exactly as the passage in section 1 reads, bounded by conduct.

The Rule Ali Enforced

The first Imam to wield these verses after the Prophet ﷺ left the rule in writing.

وما قاتل علي أصحاب الجمل وأهل صفين ليسلموا وإنما قاتلهم لبغيهم عليه ، لذلك قال لأصحابه : لا تبدؤهم بقتال حتى يبدؤكم ، ونهى عن اتباع من أدبر منهم وعن أن يذفف على جريح منهم

"Ali did not fight the people of the Camel and the people of Siffin to make them submit to Islam. He fought them because they had rebelled against him."

"He therefore told his companions, 'Do not begin fighting them until they begin fighting you.' He forbade them from pursuing those who fled and from finishing off their wounded."

- Abd al-Qahir al-Baghdadi's finding, in al-Mar'ashi, Sharh Ihaqaq al-Haqq, vol. 8, p. 557

The attack's summary reads 9:5 as license to make people Muslim by force. Ali ﵇, the man the passage's own proclamation sent out, fought two armies of Muslims who never left Islam, and his stated cause was their rebellion, never their creed. His standing order was to wait for the enemy's own first strike. The verse's first inheritor read it as a conditioned instrument, and enforced the condition against his own side.

The Conduct Limit the Record Keeps

The charge of commanded terrorism meets the conduct rule the record preserves from the same years.

فقال لرجل أدرك خالدا وقل له يقول لك رسول الله ﵌ لا تقتلوا امرأة ولا ولدا ولا عسيفا

"He said to a man, 'Catch up with Khalid and tell him. The Messenger of Allah says to you, do not kill a woman, or a child, or a hired laborer.'."

- The Prophet's ﷺ instruction to Khalid, in Ibn Hibban, al-Thiqat, vol. 2, p. 76

The category the Prophet ﷺ protects is combat status, and he enforced it by messenger against his own commander mid-campaign. A terror edict open season on a population does not send riders after its own general to stop him killing non-combatants. The school's doctrine is bounded, gated, led, and policed. What remains is the attack's abrogation argument, and it fares no better.

4. The Peace Verses Stay in Force

A Treaty With Polytheists as Such

The abrogation story requires the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to have dropped treaty-making once the sword verse arrived. His own record shows him making the treaty the whole passage is about, with polytheists, on polytheist terms.

وقد عقد رسول اللّه ص مع مشركي قريش صلح الحديبية - وهم على شركهم - بشروط لم يسترح إليها بعض المسلمين الجاهلين للأهداف البعيدة التي يراها أهل العلم لا غير وكان من ابرز الثائرين لهذا الصلح ابن الخطاب الذي كل الناس أفقه منه حتى ربات الحجال ) على حد تعبيره هو ولم يمض الا الوقت القصير حتى خانت قريش العهد وفتح رسول اللّه مكة عنوة ودخلها على قريش أذلاء ورسول اللّه ص وأصحابه هم الأعزاء الفاتحون

"The Messenger of Allah concluded the treaty of Hudaybiyya with the polytheists of Quraysh while they still held their polytheism. Its terms troubled some Muslims."

"They could not see the far-reaching aims that only people of knowledge can see. Ibn al-Khattab objected loudest to this treaty."

"In his own words, every person on earth was more learned than he was, even the women behind the curtains. Only a short time passed before Quraysh betrayed the treaty."

"The Messenger of Allah then conquered Mecca by force. He entered it while Quraysh stood humbled, and the Messenger of Allah and his companions were the honored conquerors."

- Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi (al-Qubaysi al-Amili), vol. 3, p. 80, on Qur'an 8:61

The verse the attack says was repealed is the verse the Prophet ﷺ acted on at Hudaybiyya, with partners the passage itself still calls polytheists. The peace clause worked, produced a treaty, and held until Quraysh broke it. A repealed verse does not govern the flagship treaty of the era.

If They Incline to Peace

The verse itself, from its corpus text.

وَإِنْ جَنَحُوا لِلسَّلْمِ فَاجْنَحْ لَها وَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى اللَّهِ

wa-in janahū li-s-salmi fa-jnah lahā wa-tawakkal ʿalā Allāh

"If they incline toward peace, incline toward it, and place your trust in Allah."

- Qur'an 8:61, as embedded in Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi (al-Qubaysi al-Amili), vol. 1, p. 367

The attack's abrogation story has 9:5 cancelling this verse. The page this verse is quoted from indeed relays an abrogation claim of that kind, which is the attack's strongest card. The claim itself has a history, and the history is a dispute.

The Abrogation Claim Was Contested From the Start

وهكذا قال ابن مسعود ابن زيد : إنها منسوخة بآية السيف ، وقال الباقون : هي محكمة (2)

"So said Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Zayd, 'It is abrogated by the verse of the sword.' The rest said, 'It remains operative.'."

- The disputed status of the pardon verses, in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 22, p. 19

Two named readers asserted the abrogation. The rest of the early readers denied it. The attack presents as consensus what the record preserves as a minority reading inside a split. And the jurists have a rule that dissolves the whole dispute, which al-Mashhadi states at 9:7 itself.

« فَمَا اسْتَقامُوا لَكُمْ فَاسْتَقِيمُوا لَهُمْ » ، أي : فتربّصوا أمرهم ، فإن استقاموا على العهد فاستقيموا على الوفاء .

وهو كقوله : « فَأَتِمُّوا إِلَيْهِمْ عَهْدَهُمْ » ، غير أنّه مطلق وهذا مقيّد

"'As long as they stand straight with you, stand straight with them,' meaning, wait out their affair. If they stand by the treaty, stand by fulfilling it."

"It is like His saying, 'complete their pact to them,' except that that one is unrestricted and this one is restricted."

- Al-Mashhadi, Kanz al-Daqa'iq wa Bahr al-Ghara'ib, vol. 5, p. 405, on Qur'an 9:7

A restricted verse specifies an unrestricted one. The unrestricted command to complete treaties meets the restricted command at 9:7, and the two coexist, one general, one conditioned by the partner's conduct. Specification needs no repeal, and no rule of the school's jurisprudence turns a conditioned command into an abrogating one. The pardon verses stand, and with them stands the verse the tradition counts as the Book's own conscience.

لا إِكْراهَ فِي الدِّينِ قَدْ تَبَيَّنَ الرُّشْدُ مِنَ الْغَيّ

lā ikrāha fī ad-dīni qad tabayyana ar-rushdu mina al-ghayy

"There is no compulsion in religion. The right way has become distinct from error."

- Qur'an 2:256, as embedded in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 97, p. 3

The compiler of Bihar places this verse inside the same thematic passage as the war verses. The door out at 9:5 and the door open at 2:256 are the same door, entry by consent, and the record of year 9 shows it swinging. One pillar of the attack's case remains, the claim that 9:29 and 9:123 corroborate a universal war.

5. The Corroborating Verses Carry Their Own Bounds

The Fight That Ends at a Covenant

The attack pairs 9:29 with 9:5 as twin edicts. The school's Imam report on 9:29 rejects the flattening reading to its narrator's face.

قال : نعم ، قال : اقرأ (قاتلوا الذين لا يؤمنون بالله ولا باليوم الآخر ولا يحرمون ما حرم الله ورسوله ولا يدينون دين الحق من الذين أوتوا الكتاب حتى يعطوا الجزية عن يد وهم صاغرون (2)) فاستثناء الله ﷿ واشتراطه من الذين أوتوا الكتاب فهم والذين لم يؤتوا الكتاب سواء ؟ (3) قال : نعم ، قال عمن أخذت ذا ؟ قال : سمعت الناس يقولون ، قال : فدع ذا ، فإن هم أبوا

"He said, yes. He said, recite."

"'Fight those who do not believe in Allah nor in the Last Day, and do not forbid what Allah and His Messenger have forbidden, and do not adopt the religion of truth, from those who were given the Book, until they pay the jizya by hand while they are subdued.' Did Allah thereby make an exception and a condition from those who were given the Book? Are they and those who were not given the Book the same?."

"He said, yes. He said, from whom did you take this?."

"He said, I heard people say it. He said, drop this."

"If they refuse, then fight them."

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol. 5, p. 25

The reading that melts 9:29 into 9:5, one target class, one standing war, is dismissed in the report as something people say. The verse's exception and condition belong to the People of the Book specifically, and the verse ends where the covenant begins. A modern jurist of al-Azhar states the end in plain terms.

وإن هذا القتال له نهاية وهو العهد ، ولذا قال تعالى : حَتَّى يُعْطُوا الْجِزْيَةَ عَنْ يَدٍ وَهُمْ صاغِرُونَ

"This fighting has an endpoint, and it is the covenant. Therefore God said, until they pay the jizya willingly while they are subdued."

- Muhammad Abu Zahra, Zahrat al-Tafasir, vol. 6, p. 481, on Qur'an 9:29

A fight whose stated end is a covenant is a fight to a settlement, and the settlement is protection with a levy. The report the attack's own summary quotes, fight the people until they testify, is scoped inside this same literature, and the scoping gloss survives in Bihar.

حتى يقولوا لا إله إلا الله ، أراد به عبدة الأوثان دون أهل الكتاب ، لأنهم يقولون لا إله إلا الله ثم لا يرفع عنهم السيف حتى يقروا بنبوة محمد ﵌ أو يعطوا الجزية

"By 'until they say there is no god but Allah' he meant the worshippers of idols, not the People of the Book. For the People of the Book do say there is no god but Allah."

"Yet the sword is not lifted from them until they affirm the prophethood of Muhammad, or pay the jizya."

- The scoping gloss on the fight-the-people report, in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 65, p. 244, citing al-Baghawi, Sharh al-Sunna

The terminal clause of the attack's favorite report names idolaters of the Arabs, the body already gone from history, and gives the People of the Book their own separate door. The report corroborates the taxonomy of the five swords, and it corroborates nothing else.

The Enemy Near You and No Farther

The last corroborating verse is 9:123, and the school's earliest tafsir fixes its scope in one line.

وقوله (يا أيها الذين آمنوا قاتلوا الذين يلونكم من الكفار وليجدوا فيكم غلظة) قال يجب على كل قوم ان يقاتلوا الذين من يليهم ممن يقرب من بلادهم من الكفار ولا يجوزوا ذلك الموضع والغلظة اي غلظوا لهم القول والقتل

"And His words, 'Believers, fight the disbelievers who are near you, and let them find harshness in you.' He said, every people must fight those next to them, the disbelievers whose lands lie close to their own, and they may not go beyond that place. The harshness means harshness of word and of killing toward them."

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 1, p. 333, on Qur'an 9:123

Each people face the enemy at their own border, and the verse's own geography is the limit. The attack's use of 9:123, that the Prophet ﷺ prepared against the Romans and so the verse means worldwide war, reads a border rule as a globe rule. The tafsir the school transmits forbids exactly that reading in the verse's own words, they may not go beyond that place. Three scoped verses, three scopes, one pattern. The corroboration the attack stacks is a stack of boundaries.

Final Verdict

The charge that Qur'an 9:5 founded a permanent war of conversion fails on five grounds, each carried by the sources the attack itself leaned on.

First, the passage. 9:4 exempts treaty-keepers, 9:2 sets a four-month clock, 9:6 orders asylum and an escort home for any enemy who asks, and 9:7 binds the Muslim side to reciprocity. A kill clause inside four walls of exemption, amnesty, and asylum is treaty enforcement. The attack prints the clause and omits the walls.

Second, the occasion. Quraysh broke Hudaybiyya by aiding the raid on Khuza'a, Abu Sufyan's embassy to renew the pact failed, and Ali ﵇ served a dated public notice at the Hajj. The jurists of the attack's own tradition state the breach was Quraysh's and open, and the treaty rule they transmit forbids secret termination. The pagans did something, and the sources the attack cites say what.

Third, the doctrine. The five swords report assigns each fighting verse its own body and reserves one sword's ruling to the Imams ﵈. The Kafi report, relayed in Wasa'il, gates the permission to fight on being wronged under kept conditions. Ali ﵇ fought two Muslim armies over rebellion, never creed, under a standing order to wait for the enemy's first strike, and the Prophet ﷺ sent riders to stop his own general from killing non-combatants. The school's war doctrine is bounded, gated, led, and policed, and the standing conquest the attack reads into 9:5 has no place in it.

Fourth, the abrogation story. The Prophet ﷺ concluded Hudaybiyya with polytheists under the very peace clause said to be repealed. Two early readers asserted the abrogation and the rest denied it, and the jurists' rule reads 9:7's conditioned command as specifying the general command to complete treaties, which is coexistence, never repeal. The no-compulsion verse stands where Bihar places it, beside the war verses.

Fifth, the corroborating verses. 9:29's exception and condition attach to the People of the Book, its fight ends at the covenant, and the flattening reading is dismissed in the Imam's ﵇ own report as something people say. 9:123 binds each people to the enemy near their own lands, and no farther. The attack's corroboration is a stack of boundaries presented as one open license.

Qur'an 9:5 executed a named broken treaty after public notice, with a clock running and asylum open, under a doctrine that conditioned every sword and reserved its ruling to the House ﵈ the Prophet ﷺ left in charge of the Book. The permanent war the attack builds from the verse is his own construction, assembled from tafsir selections read without their bounds, and it falls when any one of those bounds is read back in.
