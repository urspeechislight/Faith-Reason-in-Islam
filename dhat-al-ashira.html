The Expedition of Dhat al-Ashira

Mecca pooled its whole wealth into one caravan under Abu Sufyan, and the Prophet ﷺ marched to catch it and missed it by days, coming home with a second pact, this time with Banu Mudlij. The missed caravan became the cause of Badr. Part 4 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Pooled Caravan

The fourth march aimed at the richest target yet. Quraysh had pooled its wealth into a single caravan bound for Syria, a thousand camels carrying fifty thousand dinars, commanded by Abu Sufyan. The Prophet ﷺ went out to intercept it with one hundred and fifty men, or two hundred by another count, all Emigrants, the Meccan Muslims who had followed him to Medina, riding thirty camels in turns. He reached Dhat al-Ashira, a watering place of Banu Mudlij near Yanbu', the al-Ushayra of the sources, and found the caravan had passed by days. He made a pact with Banu Mudlij and their allies and went home.▼ commentary▼ commentary▼ commentary

Al-Tahtawi's digest carries the fullest account, including the caravan's worth and its guard.

وأما غزوة العشيرة - بالعين المهملة والشين المعجمة وبالمهملة أيضا ، على صيغة التصغير ، موطن ببطن الينبع (وهو منزل الحاجّ المصري) - لبنى مدلج ، فكانت علي الصحيح في جمادى الأولى سنة اثنين ، لقصد إدراك عير قريش المتوجّهة إلي الشام ، وذلك أن قريشا جمعت أموالها في تلك العير ، ولم يبق بمكة قرشي ولا قرشية له مثقال فصاعدا إلا بعث به في تلك العير ، إلا حويطب بن عبد العزي . ويقال : إن في تلك العير خمسين ألف دينار ، وألف بعير ، وكان قائدها أبا سفيان ، وكان معه سبعة وعشرون رجلا ، وقيل : تسعة وثلاثون رجلا منهم مخرمة بن نوفل ، وعمرو بن العاص ، وهي العير التي خرج إليها رسول الله صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم حين رجعت من الشام ، وكانت سببا لوقعة بدر الكبرى ، خرج صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم في مائتين من المهاجرين خاصة ، حتّى بلغ العشيرة - بالتصغير - (وأما التي بغير تصغير العشيرة فهي غزوة تبوك) واستخلف على المدينة أبا سلمة بن عبد الأسد ، وحمل اللواء عمّه حمزة بن عبد المطلب ؛ وكان اللواء أبيض ، خرجوا على ثلاثين بعيرا يعتقبونها (1) ، فوجدوا العير قد مضت قبل ذلك بأيام ، ورجع ولم يلق حربا ، ووادع صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم فيها بنى مدلج ،

"As for the expedition of al-Ushayra, spelled with an unmarked ayn and a marked shin, in the diminutive form, it is a place in the valley of Yanbu', the stopping place of the Egyptian pilgrims, and it belonged to Banu Mudlij. On the sound view it fell in Jumada al-Ula of the second year, and its aim was to catch the caravan of Quraysh on its way to Syria. Quraysh had gathered its wealth into that caravan. No man or woman of Quraysh who owned a mithqal or more stayed out of it, and only Huwaytab ibn Abd al-'Uzza held back. It is said that the caravan carried fifty thousand dinars and one thousand camels. Its leader was Abu Sufyan, with twenty-seven men, and some say thirty-nine, among them Makhrama ibn Nufayl and Amr ibn al-As. This is the caravan the Messenger of Allah pursued when it came back from Syria, and it became the cause of the great day of Badr. He went out with two hundred men of the Emigrants only, until he reached al-Ushayra, and the non-diminutive form names the ground of Tabuk. He put Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad in charge of Medina, and his uncle Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib carried the banner, which was white. They went out on thirty camels, taking turns riding them. They found the caravan had passed by some days earlier. He went back without meeting war, and there the Messenger of Allah made peace with Banu Mudlij."

- al-Tahtawi, Nihayat al-Ijaz, p.287, the caravan and the march

Al-Waqidi's entry gives the month and the size of the force.

غزوة ذي العشيرة

ثم غزا في جمادى الآخرة على رأس ستة عشر شهراً يعترض لعيرات قريش حين أبدأت إلى الشام فندب أصحابه فخرج في خمسين ومائة - ويقال في مائتين - وكان قد جاءه الخبر بفصول العير من مكة

"The expedition of Dhat al-Ushayra. Then he marched in Jumada al-Akhira, sixteen months after the Hijra, to intercept the caravans of Quraysh as they set out for Syria. He called his companions, and they went out one hundred and fifty strong, and some say two hundred. News had reached him of the caravan's departure from Mecca."

- al-Waqidi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.16

أو ذي العشيرة بضم العين المهملة مصغرا موضع لبني مدلج بينبع وكانت في جمادي الآخرة على رأس ستة عشر شهرا من الهجرة خرج في مائة وخمسين رجلا من المهاجرين أو مائتين معهم ثلاثون بعيرا يعتقبونها يريد عير قريش التي صدرت من مكة وكانت ألف بعير فيها خمسون ألف دينار وهي التي كانت بسببها وقعة بدر حين رجعت من الشام ولواؤه مع حمزة بن عبد المطلب وهو أبيض ففاتته العير ولم يلق كيدا ووادع فيها بني مدلج وحلفاءهم ورجع . "Or Dhat al-Ushayra, with the ayn unvowelled, in the diminutive form, a place of Banu Mudlij near Yanbu'. It fell in Jumada al-Akhira, sixteen months after the Hijra. He went out with one hundred and fifty men of the Emigrants, or two hundred, with thirty camels they rode in turns. He had in view the caravan of Quraysh that had left Mecca, one thousand camels carrying fifty thousand dinars, the caravan that caused the day of Badr when it returned from Syria. The banner was with Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib, and it was white. The caravan escaped him and he met no hostility. He made peace there with Banu Mudlij and their allies, then returned."

- Maktabat al-Marja', p.1816

Al-Mar'ashi's Ihqaq al-Haqq quotes Ibn al-Jawzi's al-Muntazam, and the quotation adds two details found nowhere else among these sources. No one was compelled to march, and Ali ﵇ received there the kunya Abu Turab, a kunya being an honorific byname of the form father of so-and-so.▼ commentary

قال : وفي هذه السنة كانت غزاة ذي العشيرة في جمادى الآخرة على رأس ستة عشر شهرا من الهجرة ، وخرج رسول الله ﵌ في خمسين ومائة راكب - وقيل : في مائتين - من المهاجرين ، ولم يكره أحدا على الخروج ، واستخلف على المدينة أبا سلمة بن عبد الأسد ، ومضى يعترض لعير قريش ، وكانوا قد بعثوا فيها أموالهم ، فبلغ ذا العشيرة - وهي لبني مدلج بناحية ينبع ، وبينها وبين المدينة تسعة برد ، ففاتته العير ، وهي العير التي رجعت من الشام ، فخرج لطلبها ، وخرجت قريش تمنعها ، فكانت وقعة بدر ، وبذي العشيرة كنى عليا أبا تراب لأنه رآه نائما على التراب فقال : اجلس أبا تراب . وقد روي أن ذلك كان بالمدينة ، رآه نائما في المسجد على التراب

"He said. In this year came the expedition of Dhat al-Ushayra, in Jumada al-Akhira, sixteen months after the Hijra. The Messenger of Allah went out with one hundred and fifty riders, and some say two hundred, from the Emigrants, and he compelled no one to go out. He put Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad in charge of Medina. He went to intercept the caravan of Quraysh, for they had sent their wealth in it. He reached Dhat al-Ushayra, which belongs to Banu Mudlij near Yanbu', nine barid from Medina. The caravan had passed. It was the caravan that returned from Syria. He went out seeking it, and Quraysh came out to protect it, and that became the day of Badr. At Dhat al-Ushayra he gave Ali the kunya Abu Turab, because he saw him sleeping on the dust, and he said, 'Rise, Abu Turab.' It is also related that this happened in Medina, where he saw him sleeping in the mosque on the dust."

- Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Muntazam, quoted in al-Mar'ashi, Sharh Ihqaq al-Haqq, vol.30, p.140

Commentary

The Sources for Dhat al-Ashira

Source line: al-Waqidi, al-Maghazi, vol.1, p.16, followed by al-Tahtawi, Nihayat al-Ijaz, pp.287-288

Shia lines: Maktabat al-Marja', p.1816. al-Mar'ashi, Sharh Ihqaq al-Haqq, vol.30, p.140, quoting Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Muntazam. Ibn Jarir al-Tabari, al-Mustarshad, p.130

Dating disagreement: Jumada al-Ula per Nihayat al-Ijaz, Jumada al-Akhira per al-Waqidi and Ibn al-Jawzi, who both put the march at sixteen months after the Hijra

The sources agree on the shape and divide at the edges. The month carries two readings, the force one hundred fifty or two hundred, and the guard twenty-seven or thirty-nine. Every line agrees the caravan passed, the march met no war, and the pact was made. The deputy over Medina was Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad in every line, Sunni and Shia alike. Al-Waqidi's standing as a transmitter is weighed on the series index, and the story keeps to what his entry and the others actually say.

The Caravan That Became Badr

Caravan: one thousand camels, fifty thousand dinars, the pooled wealth of Mecca

Guard: Abu Sufyan, twenty-seven men or thirty-nine, among them Makhrama ibn Nufayl and Amr ibn al-As

Force: one hundred and fifty to two hundred Emigrants, thirty camels ridden in turns

Commander: the Prophet ﷺ. Deputy over Medina, Abu Salama. Banner, Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib

Mecca had emptied its treasury into that caravan. Every Qurashi who owned a mithqal or more, the mithqal being the standard gold weight, sent it along, and only Huwaytab ibn Abd al-'Uzza held his back. The march missed the caravan going out, and that failure wrote the opening of the next chapter. When the caravan turned home from Syria, the Prophet ﷺ went out to meet it, and Quraysh marched an army to protect it, and the two met at Badr. The Ushayra pact and the Badr day grow from one root.

Two details give the march its human face. No one was compelled to go out, in the words Ibn al-Jawzi preserved. And Ali ﵇, found asleep on the dust, was given there the kunya Abu Turab, father of dust, with a variant that places the scene in the mosque of Medina.

What the March Left Behind

Pact: peace with Banu Mudlij and their allies, on the Yanbu' road

Ground: nine barid from Medina, a barid being a day's stage on the post road

Cost: no battle, no dead, no plunder, thirty camels worn by sharing

The pact with Banu Mudlij extended the security the Damra pact had begun. The road from Medina toward the coast now ran through friendly or neutral tribes for a full day's march. No companion is reported to have fled, and no man died on either side.

Was the March to Dhat al-Ashira Justified

The march carried the same defensive aim as Waddan and Buwat, pressed against a richer target. Mecca had put its whole liquid wealth into one caravan and sent it through the country of tribes the Muslims were courting. The enemy that had driven the Emigrants from their homes now moved that wealth through the open country of the alliance contest. That expulsion is the wrong the Qur'an names in the verses of permission to fight, quoted in part 1.

The outcome stayed within the pattern of these marches. The caravan was missed, no blood was shed, and the march closed with a pact, with no raid behind it. The tactical outcome was a third failed interception. The strategic outcome was a web of treaties on the western road and a reach of nine barid, nine days' stages, from Medina.

Could the encounter have been prevented? No encounter happened. The caravan passed days ahead of the Muslims, and the timing did the preventing. As for the march itself, its target was an enemy asset in open country, and no offer of compensation or settlement from Mecca stands in the record to make staying home the just course.
