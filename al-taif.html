The Siege of al-Ta'if

The Hunayn fugitives under Malik ibn Awf locked themselves into the fortress of al-Ta'if, the Prophet ﷺ besieged it twenty nights with the catapult and could not take it, and his word on withdrawing was a prayer for Thaqif's guidance. Part 27 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Walls That Held

The fugitives of Hunayn ran to the one fortress that could hold, Thaqif's walled city, with Malik ibn Awf over them. The Prophet ﷺ came in pursuit, destroyed Malik's own fortress on the way, and camped before the walls of al-Ta'if for some twenty nights, catapult against stone. The walls held. The siege lifted without the city, and the word the Prophet ﷺ spoke on leaving was a prayer for the besieged.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"'He marched on it to fight those who had scattered to it from Hunayn, and on his way he passed the fortress of Malik ibn Awf al-Nasri, the commander of Hawazin, and destroyed it, then marched on and besieged the people of al-Ta'if some twenty nights of Shawwal, and fought them a hard fight and did not prevail over them, after he had hurled the catapult at them and burned their vineyards. And when he turned away it was said to him, pray against them, and he said, "Allah, guide Thaqif and bring them to me." So Allah guided them at his prayer, and they came to Medina as Muslims, after Malik ibn Awf had gone ahead of them and entered Islam, then gone back to them and called them to Allah, and brought them to the Prophet as Muslims. And of the poetry of Malik ibn Awf when he entered Islam, "I have never seen nor heard of the like among all the people, like Muhammad. Most faithful, giving generously when asked, and when you will he tells you what is in tomorrow. And when the squadron bares its teeth, with the Samhari spears and the striking of every sword, he is like a lion over his cubs in the midst of the dust, crouched in ambush."'"

- al-Bahraq al-Hadrami, Hada'iq al-Anwar, p.358

"'Rabi'a ibn Rafi' ibn Ahan al-Sulami, known as Ibn al-Daghna after his mother, came up with Durayd ibn al-Simma and took the halter of his camel, thinking him a woman, for he was in a howdah, and behold, a man, and he made him kneel, and behold, an old man, and it was Durayd ibn al-Simma, and the youth did not know him. Durayd said to him, "What do you want of me?" He said, "To kill you." He said, "And who are you?" He said, "Rabi'a ibn Rafi' al-Sulami." And he struck him with his sword and it availed nothing, and Durayd said, "How poorly your mother armed you. Take this sword of mine from the back of my saddle in the howdah, then strike with it, and raise it from the bones and lower it from the brain, for so I used to strike the men. And when you come to your mother, tell her you killed Durayd ibn al-Simma, for many a day, by Allah, I defended your women."'"

- Ibn Kathir, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, vol.3, p.640, the death of Durayd

"'Malik ibn Awf passed by and entered the fortress of al-Ta'if, and there fell of them about a hundred men, and Allah gave the Muslims their wealth and their women as spoils, and the Messenger of Allah ordered the children and the wealth carried down to al-Ji'rana, and he put Budayl ibn Warqa al-Khuzaa over the spoils, and marched in their track until he came to al-Ta'if in pursuit of Malik ibn Awf, and he besieged the people of al-Ta'if the rest of the month. Commentary

The Sources for al-Ta'if

Source spine: al-Bahraq al-Hadrami, Hada'iq al-Anwar, p.358

Other reports: Ibn Kathir, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, vol.3, p.640, on the pursuit and Durayd's death. Al-Tabarsi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol.5, p.35, on the siege and the spoils at al-Ji'rana

Timing: the rest of Shawwal of year eight, lifting with the entry of Dhu al-Qa'da

Honest limits: the reports gathered here give the pursuit, the siege, the prayer, and the division of the spoils, and do not narrate the city's inner councils

The reports agree on the frame. They share the pursuit of the Hunayn fugitives, Malik's flight into the fortress, the siege of about twenty nights, and the withdrawal without the city.

The Walls That Held

Enemy: the Thaqif of al-Ta'if with the Hunayn fugitives, behind their walls

The siege: some twenty nights of Shawwal, a hard fight, the catapult, the burned vineyards

The result: the walls held, the city kept, the siege lifted with the entry of Dhu al-Qa'da

The word on leaving: a prayer for Thaqif's guidance, and not against them

The after-answer: Thaqif came to Medina as Muslims within the year, with Malik ibn Awf, once the enemy commander, going ahead of them

The ending is the event. The siege failed of its walls and closed with the conqueror praying for the besieged. A tribe won within the year by the prayer, where the catapult had failed, carries the whole series in one evening. Malik's own poetry on entering Islam closes the report, the enemy commander of the dawn ambush reciting the praise of the man he had ambushed.

What the Siege Left

Tactical: the city untaken, the withdrawal by decision

Strategic: the armed holdout of the Hawazin and Thaqif coalition besieged into quiet, and converted by the year's end

Spoils: the captives and herds of Hunayn and Awtas, divided at al-Ji'rana after the lift

The strategic ledger counts the conversion as the siege's fruit. Thaqif entered the faith within the year, and Malik ibn Awf, the commander who had brought the families to the battlefield, brought his people to the Prophet ﷺ as Muslims.

Was the Siege of al-Ta'if Justified

The siege was the pursuit of a war still hot. The fugitives of a battle fought that same week shut themselves in a fortress, under the commander who had assembled the coalition. They were followed to it, as every commander of that age followed a routed enemy to his wall.

The conduct of the siege, and its ending, are the record that stands. The walls were assaulted with the catapult, and the vineyards burned, the siegecraft of that world. When the walls held, the army went home. No sack followed, because there was no city taken. The prayer on leaving, and the conversion it preceded, are the reports' own verdict on the campaign.

Prevention lay with Thaqif. The road to al-Ta'if was opened at Hunayn, by the coalition that assembled there, and the city could have opened its gates to the faith it accepted within the year.
