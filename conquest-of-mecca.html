The Conquest of Mecca

Quraysh broke the Hudaybiyya truce by arming Bakr's raid on Khuzaa, the Prophet's Muslim allies, and in Ramadan of year eight the Prophet ﷺ marched on Mecca, and the city opened under a general amnesty. Part 25 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Breach and the March

The truce of Hudaybiyya died in a raid on Khuzaa. A clan of Bakr fell on Khuzaa, the Prophet's ﷺ allies, with Quraysh's arms and help behind them. The treaty's clause against betrayal and raiding lay broken with the dead of that raid. The Prophet ﷺ marched in Ramadan of year eight with the army of the state. The story the reports carry is one of the strangest in the series, a conquest carried by an amnesty.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Al-Khattabi's line on the breach.▼ commentary

كان سبب نقض العهد إن خزاعة كانت حلفاء رسول الله ﵌ فقاتلهم بنو بكر فأعانت قريش بني بكر على خزاعة فنقضوا بذلك العهد

"'The cause of the breach of the treaty was that Khuzaa were the allies of the Messenger of Allah, and Bakr fought them, and Quraysh aided Bakr against Khuzaa, and by that they broke the treaty.'"

- al-Khattabi, Ma'alim al-Sunan, vol.2, p.335

Urwa's letter to the caliph, in al-Tabari, carries the frame and the clause.▼ commentary▼ commentary

فاقتتلت طائفه من بنى كعب وطائفه من بنى بكر ، وكان بين رسول الله ص وبين قريش في ذلك الصلح الذي اصطلحوا عليه : لا اغلال ولا إسلال ، فأعانت قريش بنى بكر بالسلاح ، فاتهمت بنو كعب قريشا ، فمنها غزا رسول الله ص أهل مكة

"'A clan of Ka'b [of Khuzaa] and a clan of Bakr fought, and between the Messenger of Allah and Quraysh stood the truce they had made, its terms no betrayal and no raiding, and Quraysh aided Bakr with weapons, and Ka'b suspected Quraysh of it, and from that the Messenger of Allah marched against the people of Mecca.'"

- Urwa's letter, in al-Tabari, Tarikh al-Umam wa-l-Muluk, vol.3, p.55

Commentary

The Sources for the Conquest

Source spine: al-Tabari, Tarikh al-Umam wa-l-Muluk, vol.3, p.55, carrying Urwa ibn al-Zubayr's letter to Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan

Fiqh line: al-Khattabi, Ma'alim al-Sunan, vol.2, p.335, on the breach and the law around the broken truce

Scripture: Qur'an 17:81, Uthmani text

Honest limits: the reports gathered here give the breach, the truce clause, the march, and the amnesty proclamation, and do not carry the skirmish in Khalid's column of the army, the Ka'ba scene in detail, or the entry itself, so those are left out here

The frame rests on Urwa's letter to the caliph Abd al-Malik, carried in al-Tabari.

The Amnesty Proclaimed for the Entry

The breach: Quraysh armed the clan that raided the Prophet's allies, and al-Khattabi's line joins the aid to the breach

The march: Ramadan of year eight, the army of the state

The proclamation: safety for whoever enters Abu Sufyan's house or Hakim's house, shuts his own door, or withholds his hand

The entry: not carried by these reports, only the terms of safety

The proclamation was the law for the day of the entry. A general amnesty, set out for that day, named the enemy chief's own house as the first sanctuary. The reports gathered here contain no order to sack the city and no list of reprisals. The fighting the wider record carries for the conquest is thin and localized, and the reports gathered here do not carry even that, only the amnesty.

What the Conquest Settled

Tactical: the amnesty's terms proclaimed for the entry, on the reports gathered here

Strategic: the center of the peninsula, the sanctuary, and the tribe that had warred on the faith from the beginning passed into the faith's own keeping without a battle in the streets

Scripture: Qur'an 17:81, the verse of falsehood's end

Ledger: the treaty ended by the enemy's own act, and the war with Quraysh ended by the conqueror's own pardon

The strategic fact is simple. The city that had driven the faith out now hosted it, and every alliance in Arabia had to reckon with the new day. The two great battles that followed, Hunayn and al-Ta'if, were fought by coalitions raised against this day, and they belong to the next parts of the series.

The First-Named House

The naming: the amnesty's first sanctuary was the house of Abu Sufyan, the enemy chief

The man: the head of the enemy city's war effort, the man the Shia verdict names

The later word: his own word to Uthman in the years after, kingship was what he had warred for, and of paradise and fire he knew nothing

The amnesty named Abu Sufyan's house first. The verdict speaks to the man himself, his war for kingship, and his later word to Uthman. The naming of his house first reads as statecraft against that record, and Abu Sufyan's own later word is the verdict's evidence on what the amnesty left unchanged. The Prophet's ﷺ amnesty stands as the day's act.

Was the Conquest Justified

The legal cause is direct. A truce with named terms was broken in blood, by the armed aid of Quraysh to the clan that raided the Prophet's allies, and the reports preserve both the terms and the breach. On the ordinary law of treaties, the wronged side may treat the treaty as ended and act.

The moral ledger is the amnesty. The conqueror entered the city that had driven him out, the city that had killed his uncle at Uhud and mutilated his dead. He proclaimed safety for whoever shuts his door, and the enemy chief's house was the first named sanctuary. The conquest became a settlement. The reports gathered here read the day this way, and the account follows its sources.

The treaty was Quraysh's to keep or break, and Quraysh broke it. After the breach, the amnesty was the prevention of bloodshed.
