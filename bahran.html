The Third Sulaym Alarm

The third alarm of one spring. Word came that a great gathering of Banu Sulaym stood at Bahran, a mine of the al-Farc country in the Hijaz, the western highlands. The Prophet ﷺ made ready quietly, left Ibn Umm Maktum over Medina, and marched with three hundred men.

A night short of the ground, the army met a man of Sulaym and asked him about the gathering, and he told them it had already broken up and gone back to its waters. The Prophet ﷺ came to Bahran, found no one, stayed some days, and went home after ten nights away.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Al-Waqidi's report, carried by the Shia encyclopedist al-Yusufi.

روى الواقدي عن الزهري قال : بلغ رسول اللّه أن جمعا كثيرا ( قد اجتمع عليه ) من بني سليم في بحران . فتهيّأ رسول اللّه لذلك ، ولم يبد وجها خاصا ، واستخلف على المدينة ابن أمّ مكتوم .

ثم خرج في ثلاثمائة رجل من أصحابه ، فأسرعوا السير حتى إذا كانوا دون بحران بليلة لقوا رجلا من بني سليم فاستخبروه عن اجتماع القوم فأخبرهم : أنهم قد افترقوا ورجعوا إلى مائهم .

فسار النبيّ حتى ورد بحران فإذا ليس به أحد ، فأقام أياما ولم يلق كيدا فرجع . وكانت غيبته عشر ليال .

قال الواقدي : كانت الغزوة لليال خلون من جمادى الأولى (1) .

"Al-Waqidi reports from al-Zuhri, and he said. The Messenger of Allah learned that a great gathering of Banu Sulaym had come together against him at Bahran. The Messenger of Allah made ready for it and showed no special concern, and he put Ibn Umm Maktum in charge of Medina. Then he went out with three hundred men of his companions, and they hurried the march until, a night short of Bahran, they met a man of Banu Sulaym and asked him about the gathering. He told them it had broken up and gone back to its waters. The Prophet marched on until he reached Bahran, and there was no one there. He stayed some days, met no hostility, and went back. His absence was ten nights. Al-Waqidi said the expedition fell in the first nights of Jumada al-Ula."

- al-Waqidi from al-Zuhri, in Mawsu'at al-Tarikh al-Islami, vol.2, p.241

Ibn Sa'd's dating, carried by Maktabat al-Marja'.

وسماها ابن سعد في الطبقات غزوة بني سليم فقال وغزوة بني سليم ببحران لست خلون من جمادي الأولى على رأس سبعة وعشرين شهرا من مهاجره وذلك إنه بلغه إن بها جمعا من بني سليم كثيرا فخرج في ثلاثمائة رجل من أصحابه وأغذ السير حتى ورد بحران فوجدهم قد تفرقوا في مياههم فرجع ولم يلق كيدا وكانت غيبته عشر ليال . (1)

"Ibn Sa'd named it in the Tabaqat the expedition of Banu Sulaym. He said. The expedition of Banu Sulaym at Bahran fell six nights into Jumada al-Ula, twenty seven months after his migration, for he had learned that a great gathering of Banu Sulaym was there. He went out with three hundred men of his companions and pushed the march until he reached Bahran, and he found they had scattered to their waters, so he went back without meeting any hostility. His absence was ten nights."

- Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat via Maktabat al-Marja', p.1818

Commentary

The Sources for Bahran

Source spine: al-Waqidi from al-Zuhri, carried in Mawsu'at al-Tarikh al-Islami, vol.2, p.241

Other carriers: Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat via Maktabat al-Marja', p.1818. Ibn Abd al-Barr, al-Durar, p.116, carrying Ibn Ishaq with Ibn Hisham's note. al-Mas'udi, Muruj al-Dhahab, vol.2, p.281

Citation note: the vault note cites al-Dhahabi's Tarikh al-Islam for the Ibn Ishaq line. The corpus holds that line in Ibn Abd al-Barr's al-Durar, and the citation follows the corpus's carrier

The carriers divide on the aim of the march, and on its length. Al-Waqidi and Ibn Sa'd make it an answer to a Sulaym gathering, dissolved before contact, and count ten nights away. The Ibn Ishaq line, on Ibn Abd al-Barr's page, makes it a march with Quraysh in view that stayed two months in the country. No carrier bridges the two, and both readings stay in view.

The series follows the Waqidi line, as its fixed list does, and al-Waqidi's standing as a transmitter is weighed on the series index.

A Quiet March

Cause: a great reported gathering of Banu Sulaym at Bahran

Force: three hundred men. Deputy over Medina, Ibn Umm Maktum

Intelligence: a Sulaymi met on the road reported the gathering broken up

Ground: Bahran, a mine of the Hijaz in the al-Farc country

Result: no contact, some days on the ground, ten nights away

The march is a study in quiet. The Prophet ﷺ made ready for the gathering and, in al-Waqidi's words, showed no special concern and no alarm. The army pushed the march, learned from a man of the very tribe concerned that the danger had dissolved, verified the ground with its own eyes, and came home. No one was killed, nothing was taken, and no battle happened.

The tactical outcome was an empty ground. The strategic outcome was the third demonstration in one spring that Medina answered every alarm within days. Al-Mas'udi's sequence shows what the spring bought, and the next event in his list is Uhud.

Was the Expedition of Bahran Justified

On the Waqidi line the series follows, the march answered a report of a war gathering, the same class of cause as al-Kudr and Dhat Amr, and it is the mildest example of the three. The response was a fast march with three hundred men, no show of alarm, no punitive action on arrival, and a return once the ground proved empty.

The Ibn Ishaq line would read it as a two-month showing at the mine with Quraysh in view, and that reading stays in view with the rest.

Nothing in the Waqidi record invites a charge of aggression. The army entered no settlement, took nothing, and harmed no one, and the intelligence that emptied the cause came from a tribesman met casually on the road. The two-month reading is the one a hostile reader would press, and it is held in view. The prevention of any clash was built into the march itself, speed and presence, and the tribe's own scattering did the rest.

The march could not have been prevented. The only act to prevent was the march itself, and its cause was a report the Prophet ﷺ could not ignore after the raid at al-Arid and the two Ghatafan gatherings. Ignoring a reported gathering of Sulaym in the spring before Uhud would have been the harder charge to answer.
