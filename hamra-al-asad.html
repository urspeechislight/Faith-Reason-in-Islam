The Wounded March Out

The morning after Uhud, the Prophet ﷺ sent the herald through Medina calling the army out again. The men who answered were the wounded of the day before, and the march ran out to Hamra al-Asad. Abu Sufyan's army heard the pursuit was coming, weighed returning to finish the Muslims, took the counsel of Safwan against it, and went home instead. No second battle happened, and the Qur'an gave the march its own pair of verses.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Al-Kharkushi carries the call, the march, and the Prophet's ﷺ word on the counsel that turned Quraysh away.

ثم إن رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم استنفر أصحابه فقال : إن هذه وقعة تسامع بها العرب ، فاطلبوهم حتى يسمعوا بأنكم قد طلبتموهم ، فخرجوا وبهم ألم الجراح ، حتى بلغوا حمراء الأسد فلم يدركوهم ، فقال رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم : أرشدهم صفوان وما كان برشيد ، والذي نفسي بيده لقد سومت لهم حجارة من سجيل ، ولو أضحوا لكانوا كالأمس الذاهب ، فرجع رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم ولم يلق كيدا

"Then the Messenger of Allah called his companions out to arms and said, 'This is a battle the Arabs have heard of. Pursue them, until they hear that you have pursued them.' They went out with the pain of their wounds on them, until they reached Hamra al-Asad and did not catch the enemy. The Messenger of Allah said, 'Safwan counseled them, and his counsel was not sound. By Him in whose hand is my soul, stones of sijjil have been made ready for them, and had they stayed until morning they would have been like yesterday, gone.' The Messenger of Allah went back and met no hostility."

- al-Kharkushi, Sharaf al-Mustafa, vol.3, p.34

Al-Maqrizi adds the deputy and the fasting order.▼ commentary

خروجه وأمره بالإفطار من الصوم واستعمل ﵌ على المدينة أبا لبابة بن عبد المنذر ورده من الروحاء ، وقدم خبيب بن يساف (1) بالروحاء مسلما . وخرج ﵌ فصام يوما أو يومين ثم نادى مناديه : يا معشر العصاة إني مفطر فأفطروا ، وذلك أنه قد قال لهم قبل ذلك : أفطروا فلم يفعلوا .

"He went out and ordered the breaking of the fast. He put Abu Lubaba ibn Abd al-Mundhir over Medina and sent him back from al-Rawha. Khubayb ibn Yasaf came to al-Rawha as a Muslim. The Messenger of Allah went out and fasted a day or two, then his herald called, 'Company of the disobedient, I am breaking my fast, so break yours.' That was because he had told them before, break your fast, and they had not done it."

- al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.1, p.139

The Qur'an set the march into two verses.

ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَجَابُوا۟ لِلَّهِ وَٱلرَّسُولِ مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَآ أَصَابَهُمُ ٱلْقَرْحُ ۚ لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا۟ مِنْهُمْ وَٱتَّقَوْا۟ أَجْرٌ عَظِيمٌ

alladhīna stajābū lillāhi wa-l-rasūli min baʿdi mā aṣābahumu l-qarḥu lilladhīna aḥsanū minhum wa-ttaqaw ajrun ʿaẓīm

"Those who answered Allah and the Messenger after the wound had touched them, for those of them who did good and were mindful of Allah there is a great reward."

- Qur'an 3:172, Uthmani text

ٱلَّذِينَ قَالَ لَهُمُ ٱلنَّاسُ إِنَّ ٱلنَّاسَ قَدْ جَمَعُوا۟ لَكُمْ فَٱخْشَوْهُمْ فَزَادَهُمْ إِيمَٰنًۭا وَقَالُوا۟ حَسْبُنَا ٱللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ ٱلْوَكِيلُ

alladhīna qāla lahumu l-nāsu inna l-nāsa qad jamaʿū lakum fa-khshawhum fa-zādahum īmānan wa-qālū ḥasbunā llāhu wa-niʿma l-wakīl

"Those to whom the people said, the people have gathered against you, so fear them, and it only increased them in faith, and they said, Allah is enough for us, and He is the best guardian."

- Qur'an 3:173, Uthmani text

Commentary

The Sources for Hamra al-Asad

Source spine: al-Kharkushi, Sharaf al-Mustafa, vol.3, p.34

Other carriers: al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.1, p.139

Scripture: Qur'an 3:172-173, Uthmani text

The record is thin and decisive. The wounded answered the call, the enemy chose the road home over a second engagement, and the Qur'an named the men who marched after their wound, and the alarm that met them on the road.

The Wounded Answer the Call

Commander: the Prophet ﷺ, one day after being wounded at Uhud

Force: the wounded of Uhud. Deputy over Medina, Abu Lubaba ibn Abd al-Mundhir

Ground: Hamra al-Asad, with the turning back from al-Rawha

Contact: none. Quraysh, warned by the pursuit, declined the second battle

The march is a study in what deterrence costs. The men who limped out the morning after a rout were the same men the Qur'an praised, those who answered after the wound. The alarm that met the Muslims on the road, the people have gathered against you, was answered with the words the Qur'an kept, Allah is enough for us. Safwan's counsel turned Quraysh home, and the Prophet's ﷺ word on it was that the counsel saved them from stones already made ready. No one was killed, and no battle happened.

The Prophet's ﷺ fasting order belongs to the march. The men who fasted on the road after he told them to break it were called, through his herald, the company of the disobedient, a word so sharp the carriers kept it for a fast.

What the March Achieved

Tactical: no engagement, the enemy declined battle and went home

Strategic: the rout of Uhud was buried within a week, and the tribes read the pursuit, and not the rout, as the measure of Medina

Verse: a great reward for those who answered after the wound

The strategic ledger is the whole point of the march. Uhud left the tribes of the plateau waiting to see whether Medina was finished, and the answer reached them as an army in the field days after a defeat, wounded and fasting and unafraid. The alarm verse shows the other side of it, Quraysh's name still moved fear, and the Muslims' answer to that fear became the Qur'an's own idiom of trust.

Was the Expedition of Hamra al-Asad Justified

The march was pursuit of an army that had just mauled the state on its own ground and might return. No blood was shed, no prisoner taken, and the one act of force on the road was the fasting order against the Prophet's ﷺ own command. The cause was survival, and the method was presence.

Could it have been prevented? The march itself was the prevention, of the second battle Quraysh debated on the road home. Had the wounded stayed in Medina and the tribes read the rout as the last word, the war that followed would have found the city alone. The choice to march was the choice to be counted, and the Qur'an's reward verse is the verdict on it.
