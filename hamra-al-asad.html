The Wounded March Out

The morning after Uhud, in Shawwal of the third year, the Prophet ﷺ sent the herald through Medina calling the army out again. The men who answered were the wounded of the day before, two hundred of them in the fuller count, and the march ran out to Hamra al-Asad, eight miles from the city.

Abu Sufyan's army heard the pursuit was coming, weighed returning to finish the Muslims, took the counsel of Safwan, a chief of Quraysh, against it, and went home instead. No second battle happened, and the Qur'an gave the march its own pair of verses.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Al-Kharkushi carries the call, the march, and the Prophet's ﷺ word on the counsel that turned Quraysh away.

ثم إن رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم استنفر أصحابه فقال : إن هذه وقعة تسامع بها العرب ، فاطلبوهم حتى يسمعوا بأنكم قد طلبتموهم ، فخرجوا وبهم ألم الجراح ، حتى بلغوا حمراء الأسد فلم يدركوهم ، فقال رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم : أرشدهم صفوان وما كان برشيد ، والذي نفسي بيده لقد سومت لهم حجارة من سجيل ، ولو أضحوا لكانوا كالأمس الذاهب ، فرجع رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم ولم يلق كيدا

"Then the Messenger of Allah called his companions out to arms and said, 'This is a battle the Arabs have heard of. Pursue them, until they hear that you have pursued them.' They went out with the pain of their wounds on them, until they reached Hamra al-Asad and did not catch the enemy. The Messenger of Allah said, 'Safwan counseled them, and his counsel was not sound. By Him in whose hand is my soul, stones of sijjil have been made ready for them, and had they stayed until morning they would have been like yesterday, gone.' The Messenger of Allah went back and met no hostility."

- al-Kharkushi, Sharaf al-Mustafa, vol.3, p.34

Al-Maqrizi adds the deputy and the fasting order.▼ commentary

خروجه وأمره بالإفطار من الصوم واستعمل ﵌ على المدينة أبا لبابة بن عبد المنذر ورده من الروحاء ، وقدم خبيب بن يساف بالروحاء مسلما . وخرج ﵌ فصام يوما أو يومين ثم نادى مناديه : يا معشر العصاة إني مفطر فأفطروا ، وذلك أنه قد قال لهم قبل ذلك : أفطروا فلم يفعلوا .

"He went out and ordered the breaking of the fast. He put Abu Lubaba ibn Abd al-Mundhir over Medina and sent him back from al-Rawha. Khubayb ibn Yasaf came to al-Rawha, a staging post on the road, as a Muslim, the record's one conversion of the march. The Messenger of Allah went out and fasted a day or two, then his herald called, 'Company of the disobedient, I am breaking my fast, so break yours.' That was because he had told them before, break your fast, and they had not done it."

- al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.1, p.139

The tafsir of al-Bahr al-Muhit fixes the size of the force and the length of the stay.

نهض معه مائتا رجل من المؤمنين حتى بلغ حمراء الأسد وهي : على ثمانية أميال من المدينة وأقام بها ثلاثة أيام

"Two hundred men of the believers rose with him, until they reached Hamra al-Asad, which is eight miles from Medina, and he stayed there three days."

- Abu Hayyan, al-Bahr al-Muhit, vol.3, p.435

How the news reached Quraysh, the record tells through Ma'bad ibn Abi Ma'bad al-Khuza'i, sent after Abu Sufyan to discourage him.

وأقبل معبد بن أبي معبد الخزاعي إلى رسول اللّه - صلى اللّه عليه وسلم - فأمره أن يلحق بأبي سفيان فيخذّله ، فلحقه بالروحاء ، ولم يعلم بإسلامه ، فقال : وما وراءك يا معبد ؟ فقال : محمد وأصحابه قد تحرقوا عليكم ، وخرجوا في جمع لم يخرجوا في مثله ، وقد ندم من كان تخلف عنهم من أصحابهم . فقال : ما تقول ؟ فقال : ما أرى أن ترتحل حتى يطلع الجيش وراء هذه الأكمة ! فقال أبو سفيان : واللّه لقد أجمعنا الكرة عليهم لنستأصلهم . قال : فلا تفعل فإني لك ناصح ! فرجعوا على أعقاب

"Ma'bad ibn Abi Ma'bad al-Khuza'i came to the Messenger of Allah, and he ordered him to catch up with Abu Sufyan and discourage him. He caught him at al-Rawha, and he did not know he had become Muslim. He said, 'What is behind you, Ma'bad?' He said, 'Muhammad and his companions are burning for you. They went out in a force unlike any they have gone out in, and those of their companions who stayed behind regret it.' He said, 'What do you say?' He said, 'I do not think you should move until the army appears behind this hill.' Abu Sufyan said, 'By Allah, we had resolved to turn back on them and finish them.' He said, 'Then do not do it, for I am a sincere adviser to you.' So they went back on their heels."

- Sayyid Qutb, Fi Zilal al-Qur'an, vol.1, p.463, carrying the sira line

The Qur'an set the march into its verses.▼ commentary▼ commentary

ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَجَابُوا۟ لِلَّهِ وَٱلرَّسُولِ مِنۢ بَعْدِ مَآ أَصَابَهُمُ ٱلْقَرْحُ ۚ لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا۟ مِنْهُمْ وَٱتَّقَوْا۟ أَجْرٌ عَظِيمٌ

alladhīna stajābū lillāhi wa-l-rasūli min baʿdi mā aṣābahumu l-qarḥu lilladhīna aḥsanū minhum wa-ttaqaw ajrun ʿaẓīm

"Those who answered Allah and the Messenger after the wound had touched them, for those of them who did good and were mindful of Allah there is a great reward."

- Qur'an 3:172, Uthmani text

ٱلَّذِينَ قَالَ لَهُمُ ٱلنَّاسُ إِنَّ ٱلنَّاسَ قَدْ جَمَعُوا۟ لَكُمْ فَٱخْشَوْهُمْ فَزَادَهُمْ إِيمَٰنًۭا وَقَالُوا۟ حَسْبُنَا ٱللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ ٱلْوَكِيلُ

alladhīna qāla lahumu l-nāsu inna l-nāsa qad jamaʿū lakum fa-khshawhum fa-zādahum īmānan wa-qālū ḥasbunā llāhu wa-niʿma l-wakīl

"Those to whom the people said, the people have gathered against you, so fear them, and it only increased them in faith, and they said, Allah is enough for us, and He is the best guardian."

- Qur'an 3:173, Uthmani text

فَٱنقَلَبُوا۟ بِنِعْمَةٍۢ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَفَضْلٍۢ لَّمْ يَمْسَسْهُمْ سُوٓءٌۭ وَٱتَّبَعُوا۟ رِضْوَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ ذُو فَضْلٍ عَظِيمٍ

fa-nqalabū bi-niʿmatin mina llāhi wa-faḍlin lam yamsashum sū'un wa-ttabaʿū riḍwāna llāhi wa-llāhu dhū faḍlin ʿaẓīm

"So they returned with favor from Allah and bounty, no harm having touched them, and they pursued the pleasure of Allah. Allah is the possessor of great bounty."

- Qur'an 3:174, Uthmani text

Commentary

The Sources for Hamra al-Asad

Source spine: al-Kharkushi, Sharaf al-Mustafa, vol.3, p.34

Other carriers: al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.1, p.139. Abu Hayyan, al-Bahr al-Muhit, vol.3, p.435, on the force and the stay. Sayyid Qutb, Fi Zilal al-Qur'an, vol.1, p.463, carrying the Ma'bad episode

Scripture: Qur'an 3:172-174, Uthmani text

The record is thin and decisive. The wounded answered the call, the enemy chose the road home over a second engagement, and the Qur'an named the men who marched after their wound, and the alarm that met them on the road.

The Wounded Answer the Call

Commander: the Prophet ﷺ, one day after being wounded at Uhud

Force: the wounded of Uhud. Deputy over Medina, Abu Lubaba ibn Abd al-Mundhir

Ground: Hamra al-Asad, with the turning back from al-Rawha

Contact: none. Quraysh, warned by the pursuit, declined the second battle

The march is a study in what deterrence costs. The men who limped out the morning after a rout were the same men the Qur'an praised, those who answered after the wound. The alarm that met the Muslims on the road, the people have gathered against you, was answered with the words the Qur'an kept, Allah is enough for us.

Safwan's counsel turned Quraysh home, and the Prophet's ﷺ word on it was that the counsel saved them from the stones of sijjil, the baked-clay stones of punishment the Qur'an names elsewhere, already made ready for them. No one was killed, and no battle happened.

The Prophet's ﷺ fasting order belongs to the march. The men who fasted on the road after he told them to break it were called, through his herald, the company of the disobedient, a word so sharp the carriers kept it for a fast.

What the March Achieved

Tactical: no engagement, the enemy declined battle and went home

Strategic: the stated aim, that the Arabs hear of the pursuit, was served by Ma'bad's report, which turned the victors' resolve to return

Verse: a great reward for those who answered after the wound

The Prophet's stated aim, in al-Kharkushi's line, was the ears of the Arabs. Pursue them until they hear that you have pursued them, he said, of a battle the Arabs had heard of.

The carriers record no report of how the tribes read the answer, only the aim that they should hear it, and the mechanism, Ma'bad's word at al-Rawha, that turned the victors' resolve around. The alarm verse shows the other side of it. Quraysh's name still moved fear, and the Muslims' answer to that fear became the Qur'an's own idiom of trust.

Was the Expedition of Hamra al-Asad Justified

The march was pursuit of an army that had just defeated the state on its own ground and might return. No blood was shed and no prisoner taken, and the one breach of order on the road was the men's fasting after the Prophet ﷺ had told them to break it. The cause was survival, and the method was presence.

The pursuit could not have been prevented. The march itself was the prevention, of the second battle Quraysh had resolved on at al-Rawha, until Ma'bad's word turned them. Had the wounded stayed in Medina, the word reaching the Arabs would have been the rout alone, and the Prophet's ﷺ stated aim was that the word be the pursuit. The Qur'an's reward verse is the verdict on the choice.
