Battle Narration

The Battle of Badr

The caravan the Muslims had missed twice was coming home from Syria, and this time Quraysh sent an army to guard it. Three hundred and thirteen Muslims met it at Badr, and the day ended with seventy of Quraysh dead, seventy captive, and Medina secured as a state. Part 5 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Promise and the Two Parties

The caravan the Muslims had missed twice was coming home from Syria, and this time Quraysh, the ruling tribe of Mecca, sent an army out of the city to guard it. The two forces met at Badr on 17 Ramadan of the second year after the Hijra, the migration from Mecca to Medina. The Muslims numbered three hundred and thirteen. What followed was the first battle of Islam, and it turned on counsel, a wrecked peace attempt, three duels, and a promise of God.▼ commentary▼ commentary

The Shia exegete al-Qummi fixes the cause and the promise.

قال القمي في تفسيره : كانت بدر على رأس ستة عشر شهرا من مقدم رسول اللّه المدينة (2) وكان سبب ذلك أن عيرا لقريش خرجت إلى الشام فيها خزائنهم (3) ( ورجعت ) (4) فأمر رسول اللّه أصحابه بالخروج إليها ليأخذوها وأخبرهم : أن اللّه قد وعده إحدى الطائفتين : إما العير وإما قريش إن ظفر بهم .

فخرج في ثلاثمائة وثلاثة عشر رجلا (5) .

"Al-Qummi said in his tafsir, 'Badr occurred sixteen months after the Messenger of Allah arrived in Medina. The cause was a Quraysh caravan that went to Syria carrying their wealth. It returned. The Messenger of Allah commanded his companions to go out and seize it. He told them Allah had promised him one of two parties, either the caravan or Quraysh, if he overcame them. He went out with three hundred and thirteen men.'"

- al-Qummi's Tafsir, quoted in Mawsu'at al-Tarikh al-Islami, vol.2, p.110

The Qur'an itself records that a party of the believers marched unwillingly.

كَمَآ أَخْرَجَكَ رَبُّكَ مِنۢ بَيْتِكَ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَإِنَّ فَرِيقًۭا مِّنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ لَكَٰرِهُونَ

kamā akhrajaka rabbuka min baytika bi-l-ḥaqqi wa-inna farīqan mina l-mu'minīna la-kārihūn

"Just as your Lord brought you out of your home with the truth, while a party of the believers were unwilling."

- Qur'an 8:5, Uthmani text

Inside the camp, the Prophet ﷺ took counsel. Al-Miqdad answered for the Emigrants, the Meccan Muslims who had followed him to Medina.

وقال الإمام أحمد : حدثنا وكيع ، حدثنا سفيان ، عن مخارق بن عبد الله الأحمسي ، عن طارق - هو ابن شهاب - أن المقداد قال لرسول الله ﵌ يوم بدر : يا رسول الله إنا لا نقول لك كما قالت بنو إسرائيل لموسى : " اذهب أنت وربك فقاتلا إنا هاهنا قاعدون " ولكن اذهب أنت وربك فقاتلا إنا معكم مقاتلون . وهذا إسناد جيد من هذا الوجه ، وله طرق أخرى . قال أحمد : حدثنا أسود بن عامر ، حدثنا إسرائيل ، عن مخارق ، عن طارق بن شهاب ، قال : قال عبد الله بن مسعود ﵁ : لقد شهدت من المقداد مشهدا ، لان أكون أنا صاحبه ، أحب إلى مما عدل به . أتى رسول الله ﵌ وهو يدعو على المشركين فقال (1) : والله يا رسول الله لا نقول لك كما قالت بنو إسرائيل لموسى : " اذهب أنت وربك فقاتلا إنا هاهنا قاعدون " ، ولكننا نقاتل عن يمينك ، وعن يسارك ومن بين يديك ومن خلفك . فرأيت وجه رسول الله ﵌ يشرق لذلك وسر بذلك

"Imam Ahmad said. Waki' told us, Sufyan told us, from Mukhariq ibn Abd Allah al-Ahmasi, from Tariq ibn Shihab, that al-Miqdad said to the Messenger of Allah on the day of Badr, 'Messenger of Allah, we do not say to you what the Children of Israel said to Moses, go you and your Lord and fight while we sit here. Go you and your Lord and fight, and we are with you fighting.' This chain is sound from this route, and it has others. Ahmad said. Aswad ibn Amir told us, Isra'il told us, from Mukhariq, from Tariq ibn Shihab, and he said, Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud said, 'I witnessed a scene from al-Miqdad for which I would rather have been the man myself than have anything equal to it. He came to the Messenger of Allah while he was calling down Allah's anger on the idolaters and said, by Allah, Messenger of Allah, we do not say to you what the Children of Israel said to Moses, go you and your Lord and fight while we sit here. We fight on your right and your left, in front of you and behind you.' The face of the Messenger of Allah brightened at that, and he was glad."

- Imam Ahmad's Musnad, quoted by Ibn Kathir, Qasas al-Anbiya, vol.2, p.101

Sa'd ibn Mu'adh answered for the Ansar, the native Muslims of Medina.

والذي بعثك بالحق نبيا ، لو استعرضت بنا هذا البحر فخضته لخضناه معك ، ما تخلف منا رجل واحد ، ونكره أن تلقى بنا عدونا غدا ، إنا لصبر في الحرب ، صدق في اللقاء . ولعل الله يريك منا ما تقربه عينك ، فسر بنا على بركة الله

فرح رسول الله ﵌ وتهلل وجهه الشريف وقال : سيروا على بركة الله ، فإنه وعدني إحدى الطائفتين (إما النصر ، وإما الشهادة) ولن يخلف الله وعده ، والله . . . لكأني أنظر إلى مصارع القوم .

"'By the One who sent you with the truth as a Prophet, if you made us wade through this sea, we would wade through it with you. Not one man of us would stay behind. We hate that you meet our enemy tomorrow without us. We are patient in war and true in the encounter. Perhaps Allah will show you from us what delights your eye, so march with us on the blessing of Allah.' The Messenger of Allah rejoiced, and his noble face lit up. He said, 'March on the blessing of Allah, for He promised me one of the two parties, either victory or martyrdom, and Allah does not break His promise. By Allah, it is as if I see the places where these people will fall.'"

- al-Shakiri, al-A'lam min al-Sahaba, vol.8, p.86

On the Meccan side, an attempt was made to turn the army back. Hakim ibn Hizam appealed to Utba ibn Rabia, and Utba rose and called for retreat. Abu Jahl wrecked it.▼ commentary

إنك كبير قريش وسيدها والمطاع فيها ، فهل لك إلى أن تفعل شيئا تذكر به بخير إلى آخر الدهر ؟ فقال عتبة : وما ذاك يا حكيم

قال : ترجع بالناس وتحمل أمر حليفك عمرو بن الحضرمي

قال عتبة : قد فعلت . . ثم قام عتبة خطيبا في الناس فقال :

يا معشر قريش إنكم واللَّه ما تصنعون بأن تلقوا محمدا وأصحابه شيئا ، واللَّه لئن أصبتموه لا يزال الرجل ينظر في وجه رجل يكره النظر إليه . قتل ابن عمه أو ابن خاله . . فارجعوا وخلوا بين محمد وبين سائر العرب فإن أصابوه فذاك الذي أردتم ، وإن كان غير ذلك ألفاكم ولم تعرضوا منه ما تريدون

وبلغ كلام عتبة أبا جهل فسبه . . ثم بعث أبو جهل إلى ابن الحضرمي فقال له : هذا حليفك عتبة يريد أن يرجع بالناس ، وقد رأيت ثارك بعينك ، فقم فأنشد خفرتك ومقتل أخيك - أي : فقم فاطلب من الناس الوفاء بالعهد والأخذ بثأر أخيك . .

"'You are the elder of Quraysh and its master, the one obeyed in it. Have you it in you to do a thing remembered well to the end of time?' Utba said, 'And what is that, Hakim?' He said, 'Turn the people back, and take on the cause of your ally Amr ibn al-Hadrami.' Utba said, 'I have done it.' Then Utba rose and spoke to the people. 'Assembly of Quraysh, by Allah, you gain nothing by meeting Muhammad and his companions. By Allah, if you overcome him, each man of you will keep looking into the face of a man he hates to look at, the killer of his cousin or his kinsman. Turn back, and leave Muhammad to the rest of the Arabs. If they overcome him, that is what you wanted, and if it turns out otherwise, you will have met him without exposing yourselves to what you seek.' The speech of Utba reached Abu Jahl, and he insulted him. Then Abu Jahl sent to Ibn al-Hadrami and said to him, 'Here is your ally Utba trying to turn the people back, and you have seen the price of your blood with your own eye. Rise and call on your covenant and the blood of your brother.' Ibn al-Hadrami rose, tore his garment, and screamed, 'Amr, Amr.' The war grew hot, the affair of the people hardened, they settled firmer on the evil they were on, and Abu Jahl wrecked the counsel to which Utba had called the people."

- Ibn Ishaq via al-Tantawi, al-Tafsir al-Wasit, vol.6, p.20, the retreat wrecked

The battle opened with duels. Hamza killed al-Aswad at the basin, then the champions of Quraysh came out, and the Prophet ﷺ sent out Hamza, Ali ﵇, and Ubayda ibn al-Harith to meet them.▼ commentary

قال ابن إسحاق : ثم خرج الأسود بن عبد الأسد المخزومي - وكان شرسا سيئ الخلق - فقال : أعاهد اللَّه لأشربن من حوضهم أو لأهدمنه ، أو لأموتن دونه فلما دنا منه خرج إليه حمزة بن عبد المطلب . فلما التقيا ضربه حمزة فأطن قدمه بنصف ساقه - أي . أطارها - وهو دون الحوض ، فوقع على ظهره تشخب رجله دما نحو أصحابه . ثم حبا إلى الحوض حتى اقتحم فيه ، فضربه حمزة حتى قتله في الحوض

ثم خرج عتبة بين أخيه شيبة وابنه الوليد بن عتبة . . فنادى يا محمد : أخرج إلينا أكفاءنا من قومنا . فقال رسول اللَّه صلى اللَّه عليه وسلم قم يا عبيدة وقم يا حمزة وقم يا علي . . أما حمزة فلم يمهل شيبة أن قتله ، وأما على فلم يمهل الوليد أن قتله ، واختلف عبيدة وعتبة بينهما ضربتين كلاهما أثبت صاحبه - أي : جرحه جرحا شديدا لا يملك معه الحركة - وكر حمزة وعلى بأسيافهما على عتبة فأجهزا عليه ، واحتملا عبيدة فحازاه إلى أصحابه .

"Ibn Ishaq said. Then al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Asad al-Makhzumi came out, a fierce and ill-tempered man, and said, 'I covenant with Allah to drink from their basin or to tear it down, or to die before it.' When he came near, Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib came out to him. When the two met, Hamza struck him and cut his leg off at half the shin, that is, he severed it, while he was still short of the basin. He fell on his back, his leg pouring blood toward his companions. Then he crawled to the basin and threw himself into it, and Hamza struck him until he killed him in it. Then Utba came out between his brother Shayba and his son al-Walid ibn Utba and called out, 'Muhammad, bring out to us our equals from our people.' The Messenger of Allah said, 'Rise, Ubayda. Rise, Hamza. Rise, Ali.' Hamza did not give Shayba time before he killed him. Ali did not give al-Walid time before he killed him. Ubayda and Utba exchanged two blows, and each gravely wounded the other. Hamza and Ali turned on Utba with their swords and finished him, and they lifted Ubayda and carried him back to their companions."

- Ibn Ishaq via al-Tantawi, al-Tafsir al-Wasit, vol.6, p.20, the duels

The ranks closed under orders, and the Prophet ﷺ set the lines and withdrew to the shelter to pray.

قال ابن إسحاق : ثم تزاحف الناس ، ودنا بعضهم من بعض ، وقد أمر رسول اللَّه الناس أن لا يحملوا حتى يأمرهم ، وقال : « إن اكتنفكم القوم فانضحوهم عنكم بالنبل » . . . ثم عدل رسول اللَّه صلى اللَّه عليه وسلم الصفوف ، ورجع إلى العريش فدخله - ومعه أبو بكر الصديق

"Ibn Ishaq said. Then the two sides closed, each drawing near the other. The Messenger of Allah ordered the people not to charge until he ordered them, and he said, 'If they press you from both sides, drive them off with arrows.' Then the Messenger of Allah set the ranks straight, went back to the shelter, and entered it, and Abu Bakr al-Siddiq was with him."

- Ibn Ishaq via al-Tantawi, al-Tafsir al-Wasit, vol.6, p.20, the ranks and the shelter

From the shelter the Prophet ﷺ implored his Lord, and the lines met. The Qur'an carries the answer of that hour.

إِذْ تَسْتَغِيثُونَ رَبَّكُمْ فَٱسْتَجَابَ لَكُمْ أَنِّى مُمِدُّكُم بِأَلْفٍۢ مِّنَ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ مُرْدِفِينَ

idh tastaghīthūna rabbakum fa-stajāba lakum annī mumiddukum bi-alfin mina l-malā'ikati murdifīn

"When you implored your Lord, and He answered you, I will reinforce you with a thousand angels in succession."

- Qur'an 8:9, Uthmani text

The charge was released on command, and the day turned into the rout the death roll records.

The Qur'an closes the day in one verse.

وَلَقَدْ نَصَرَكُمُ ٱللَّهُ بِبَدْرٍۢ وَأَنتُمْ أَذِلَّةٌۭ ۖ فَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

wa-laqad naṣarakumu llāhu bi-badrin wa-antum adhillatun fa-ttaqū llāha laʿallakum tashkurūn

"Allah had already given you victory at Badr while you were weak and humbled. So be mindful of Allah, so that you may be grateful."

- Qur'an 3:123, Uthmani text

Badr is the best documented day in this series. Every layer has later works carrying them, Sunni and Shia, hadith and sira and tafsir, and they agree on the frame. The kill report of Abu Jahl is the one recorded split, carried openly below. Three hundred and thirteen marched, the promise named one of two parties, the battle was joined after a wrecked retreat, and seventy dead and seventy captives closed the day.

The Promise and the Reluctant Party

Force: three hundred and thirteen men, per al-Qummi's line

Promise: one of the two parties, the caravan or the army, per al-Qummi and Sa'd's speech

Reluctance: a party of the believers were unwilling, per Qur'an 8:5

Flight on the day: no source names a Muslim who fled at Badr

The reluctance came before the battle, and the Qur'an states it without naming anyone. The unwilling party marched anyway and stood in the lines. On the day itself, the sources carry no report of any Muslim turning his back. The two answers in the council, al-Miqdad's and Sa'd's, show the spirit of the men who actually fought.

The Peace That Abu Jahl Wrecked

The attempt: Hakim ibn Hizam asked Utba to turn the army back

The speech: Utba told Quraysh they gain nothing by meeting Muhammad and his companions

The wreck: Abu Jahl shamed Utba, roused Ibn al-Hadrami to a blood cry, and the army hardened

The prevention of Badr stood one speech away from success. Utba's argument was plain self-interest, kinship against civil war, and it was working before Abu Jahl spent the blood of Amr ibn al-Hadrami to heat the war up. The sources put the blame for the fought battle on that sabotage, and on Quraysh's choice to march an army out of Mecca to protect the caravan wealth after driving the Muslims from their homes.

The Duels That Opened the Battle

First blood: Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib killed al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Asad at the basin

The champion duel: Utba, Shayba, and al-Walid against Hamza, Ali ﵇, and Ubayda

The cost: Ubayda ibn al-Harith mortally wounded, the three Meccan champions dead

The death of the three champions broke the top command of Quraysh in the first minutes. Utba ibn Rabia, the same man who had tried to turn the army back, lay dead with his brother and his son. The Muslims paid with Ubayda, whose severed leg killed him on the road home. Each of these deaths moved the day. The champions fell, the line of Quraysh lost its head, and the battle turned into a rout that left seventy dead and seventy in bonds.

The Death Roll and the Spoils

Dead of Quraysh: the two sons of Rabi'a, the two sons of al-Hajjaj, Abu Jahl, Zam'a ibn al-Aswad, Umayya ibn Khalaf, among seventy

Captive: Suhayl ibn Amr, among seventy

Dead of the Muslims: Ubayda ibn al-Harith, on the road home

Spoils: what the army carried, taken as lawful spoils

Abu Jahl was struck down by Mu'adh ibn Amr ibn al-Jamuh and Mu'adh ibn Afra in the report the two Sahihs carry, with one wording giving the two sons of Afra, and Ibn Mas'ud found him cold. His last recorded words pit class against faith, that a farmer had killed him. Umayya ibn Khalaf, whose caravan had been the target at Buwat, also fell. The killing of Abu Jahl decided more than a battle. The voice that had sabotaged the retreat at Badr and driven Quraysh's hostility for years was gone, and the politics of Mecca changed shape at once.

Was the Battle of Badr Justified

The road to Badr runs through five bloodless marches and two broken chances. Quraysh had driven the Muslims from their homes and stripped them of their property, and now marched an army to protect the wealth of the caravan. The Muslims marched on a promise that named one of two parties, the caravan or the army. The Qur'an names the dispossession itself, in the verse that fixes the Emigrants' right to the spoils.

لِلْفُقَرَآءِ ٱلْمُهَٰجِرِينَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ مِن دِيَٰرِهِمْ وَأَمْوَٰلِهِمْ يَبْتَغُونَ فَضْلًۭا مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرِضْوَٰنًۭا وَيَنصُرُونَ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥٓ ۚ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلصَّٰدِقُونَ

"A share of the spoils belongs to the poor Emigrants, who were driven out of their homes and their property, seeking bounty from Allah and His pleasure, and aiding Allah and His Messenger. These are the truthful."

- Qur'an 59:8, Uthmani text

The battle itself was forced by the clash of the two marches, and the last chance to avoid it was Utba's, wrecked by Abu Jahl's cry of blood. Once joined, the Muslims fought a set battle under orders, held from charging until commanded, and took captives. When the companions urged a chase after the caravan, the Prophet ﷺ accepted al-Abbas's correction against it. One captive of the hard core, Nadr ibn al-Harith, was executed, and the record carries it.

The tactical outcome was a complete victory, seventy dead and seventy captives of Quraysh against a Muslim dead roll that names Muhaja the first killed and Ubayda dead of his wound. The strategic outcome was the survival of Medina as a state. The Qur'an's own verdict binds the two, victory given while the believers were weak, and gratitude owed for it. For the political picture, Badr made Medina a power the tribes had to reckon with, and Mecca answered within two years with the revenge army of Uhud.

Could the battle have been prevented? Twice over, yes. Abu Sufyan could have passed with his caravan without summoning an army, and Utba's retreat could have carried the day if Abu Jahl had let it. Both chances belonged to Quraysh, and both were spent on the field.
