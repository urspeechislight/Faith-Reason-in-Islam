Why Maryam Is Called the Sister of Aaron

The Quran calls Maryam ﵍ the sister of Aaron because Arabic names a woman by her house, exactly as it calls Hud the brother of Ad. The Imams ﵈ called strangers the brother of the people of Egypt, and a woman of the Ansar the sister of the Ansar. From Qur'an 19:28, the Ahl al-Bayt tafsir, Lisan al-Arab, and the Gospel of Luke.

The Argument Under Examination

When her people confront Maryam ﵍ with her infant, the Qur'an gives their address, the sister of Aaron, joined to a defense of her parents' honor (Qur'an 19:27-28). Aaron the prophet, brother of Moses ﵇, lived about a thousand years before her. Christian critics say the Qur'an has confused two women, Maryam ﵍ the mother of Isa ﵇ and Miriam the sister of Moses ﵇, and rolled them into one. If she was not Aaron's ﵇ sister, the charge concludes, the verse should never have said sister.

The verse said sister because that is how the language names a woman's house. The words the crowd speaks defend a lineage, her father never an evil man, her mother never a wanton woman, and the address opens the same defense. It names her by the greatest saint of her line, and the grammar it uses is the standing grammar of Arabic, Hebrew, and the Qur'an itself.

The Imams ﵈ read the verse this way from the beginning. Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329) narrates the whole scene and explains the address inside it. Al-Sharif al-Murtada (d.436) gave a session of his Amali to this one verse alone and wrote the school's classic statement of the idiom. The idiom is that the address names a woman of Aaron's ﵇ line the way an Arab is named by his tribe. The objections of the Christians of Najran reached the Prophet ﷺ in his own lifetime, and his answer has chains in the Sunni and the Shia books alike. The lexicons and the poets, and the Gospel of Luke, keep the same kinship grammar.

Premise 1: Arabic kinship words name houses and lines, and they do this in the Quran itself.

The Qur'an calls Hud their brother when it sends him to Ad, and Salih their brother when it sends him to Thamud. The meaning is one of their descendants and their kind, as Muqatil ibn Sulayman explained in a report al-Murtada preserved. It calls the whole nation the sons of Israel a thousand years after Israel died, and it calls Maryam ﵍ herself the daughter of Imran (Qur'an 66:12) a thousand years after the ancient Amram. A kinship word joined to an ancestor's name states membership in his house, and sister of Aaron is exactly that.

Premise 2: The Imams ﵈ and the Prophet ﷺ used exactly this address on men and women who were no siblings of theirs.

Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ repeatedly addressed an Egyptian stranger as the brother of the people of Egypt in the courtyard of the Kaaba, in al-Kafi. Ali ﵇ at Nahrawan said the brother of al-Azd. The Prophet ﷺ said the brother of the Ansar and the brother of Thaqif, and he answered a woman of the Ansar with the sister of the Ansar. The lexicons record the brother of Tamim, as stated by Ibn Manzur in Lisan al-Arab, and pre-Islamic poetry carries the brother of the sons of Khalaf.

Premise 3: The charge was pressed at the source and answered at the source.

The Christians of Najran put the question to al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba, the Prophet's ﷺ envoy, within his lifetime. They asked why the Qur'an calls Maryam the sister of Aaron when Moses lived so long before Jesus. He carried it back. The Prophet ﷺ answered that the Children of Israel used to name themselves after their prophets and their righteous men. The school transmits this answer through Ibn Tawus as well as through the Sunni chains; the objection is as old as the verse, and the answer is as old as the objection.

Premise 4: Her Aaronic lineage is supported, though the argument does not depend on it.

The Qur'an fixes her in the House of Imran, the chosen seed (Qur'an 3:33-34), raised in the temple under Zakariyya ﵇. The Gospel of Luke calls Elizabeth, Maryam's ﵍ kinswoman, a woman of the daughters of Aaron (Luke 1:5, 1:36). Even the historians who prefer a Davidic line for her, like Ibn al-Athir (d.630), still concede that her people were called after the righteous, which is the very custom the verse rides. On every branch, the charge fails.

Conclusion: The address is a correct kinship address in the language it was said in.

From Premise 1 and Premise 2, sister of Aaron states a woman's house in the standing grammar of Arabic. From Premise 3, the objection is as old as the verse, and the answer is as old as the objection, given by the Prophet ﷺ in his lifetime and preserved in the books of both communities. From Premise 4, whichever genealogy a reader prefers, Aaronide or Davidic, the address stands. The charge of confusion requires a rule the language never held, that sister can only name one womb. Every layer of evidence, the Qur'an's own usage, the Imams' ﵈ practice, the lexicons, the poets, and the Bible, refuses that rule.

1. The Charge Rests on One Word

Maryam ﵍ withdrew from her people to an eastern place. The spirit was sent to her, and she conceived. She returned carrying the child, and her people confronted her.

The crowd opens with the lineage defense, her father was never evil, her mother was never wanton, and it opens that defense with the lineage's name, sister of Aaron. The address is part of the accusation, a house invoked against one of its daughters.

Verse: Qur'an 19:27-28, Surat Maryam

Locus: Maryam ﵍ returns to her people carrying the infant Isa ﵇

Speaker: the assembly of the Children of Israel, with Zakariyya ﵇ present in Tafsir al-Qummi's narration

Claim: the crowd invokes her lineage's honor while charging her with a thing unheard of

فَأَتَتْ بِهِۦ قَوْمَهَا تَحْمِلُهُۥ ۖ قَالُوا۟ يَٰمَرْيَمُ لَقَدْ جِئْتِ شَيْـًۭٔا فَرِيًّۭا

يَٰٓأُخْتَ هَٰرُونَ مَا كَانَ أَبُوكِ ٱمْرَأَ سَوْءٍۢ وَمَا كَانَتْ أُمُّكِ بَغِيًّۭا

fa-atat bihi qawmahā taḥmiluh, qālū yā Maryamu laqad jiʾti shayʾan fariyyā. Yā ukhta Hārūna mā kāna abūki imraʾa sawʾin wa-mā kānat ummuki baghiyyā.

"Then she came to her people, carrying him. They said, 'Maryam, you have come with a thing unheard of.'"

"O sister of Aaron, your father was not an evil man, and your mother was not wanton."

- Qur'an 19:27-28

The address and the parentage defense are one speech, and both invoke her lineage.

The polemic builds from the Torah itself, which closes the list of Amram's children with and Miriam their sister (Numbers 26:59) and titles Miriam the prophetess the sister of Aaron (Exodus 15:20). The polemicist argues that the Qur'an's author lifted the name Maryam and kept the family register it came with. The result, on his argument, is a verse that calls the mother of Isa ﵇ the sister of a man dead a thousand years. The polemic cites the Torah's own title for Miriam as proof of confusion, and that title names a woman of the house by its priest.

The whole charge rests on what sister can mean in Arabic. If kinship words carry lineage and house membership, sister of Aaron states a truth about Maryam ﵍ precisely, in the register the crowd itself would have used. If sister means only one born of the same parents, the charge lands. The question turns on the evidence of the language.

2. Sister Meant the House

Contested words. The charge reads ukht, sister, as one born of the same parents only. The Imams' ﵈ own definitions, the lexicons, and the poets fix the word as kinship within a house, covering descendants and tribesmen.

The word under dispute is ukht, sister, the feminine of akh, and the dispute concerns its range.

In al-Kafi, a woman named Juwayra is waiting in the Sacred Mosque for a mawla of her family, a freed slave attached to the household. Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ asks her what she is waiting for, and when he learns the man was never freed himself, only his grandfather was, he corrects her word for the relationship.

Source: al-Kafi, vol.6, p.199, narration 4

Speaker: Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇

Locus: Juwayra waits for a mawla of the family in the Sacred Mosque

Claim: the grandson of the freed dependent is called your brother

مر بي أبو عبد الله ﵇ وأنا في المسجد الحرام أنتظر مولى لنا فقال يا أم عثمان ما يقيمك ههنا ؟

قلت أنتظر مولى لنا فقال أعتقتموه ؟ قلت لا فقال أعتقتم أباه ؟ قلت لا أعتقنا جده فقال ليس هذا مولاكم بل هذا أخوكم

"Abu Abd Allah passed by me while I was in the Sacred Mosque waiting for a dependent of ours. He said, 'Umm Uthman, what keeps you here?'"

"I said, 'I am waiting for a dependent of ours.' He said, 'Did you free him?' I said, 'No.' He said, 'Did you free his father?' I said, 'No, we freed his grandfather.' He said, 'He is not your dependent. He is your brother.'"

- Al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol.6, p.199

The Imam ﵇ names the grandson of the freed dependent a brother of the house, inside a ruling on clientage (wala'), and no transmitter in the report questions the word. Akh in the mouth of the school covers kinship across generations.

A man of Tamim came to Umar asking for a share of water in a land grant, and al-Zubayr addressed him by his tribe. Ibn Manzur (d.711) preserves the exchange in Lisan al-Arab, the largest classical dictionary of the language, and al-Azhari (d.370) had carried the same scene three centuries earlier in Tahdhib al-Lugha. The two lexicons quote it as their evidence for a word for wells, and they preserve the address as ordinary speech.

Source: Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol.10, p.447

Speaker: al-Zubayr, before Umar

Dating: the scene from the first century AH, carried by Ibn Manzur (d.711 AH)

Claim: a man of Tamim is addressed as the brother of Tamim

فقال الزبير : إنك يا أخا تميم تسأل خيراً قليلًا ، فقال عمر ، ﵁ : لا بل خير كثير قِرْبَتانِ قِرْبةٌ من ماء وقربة من لبن

"Al-Zubayr said, 'You, O brother of Tamim, ask for little good.' Umar said, 'No, for much good, two skins, a skin of water and a skin of milk.'"

- Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol.10, p.447

Source: al-Azhari, Tahdhib al-Lugha, vol.10, p.20

Claim: the same scene and vocative a century earlier

وقال الزبير : إنك يا أخا تميم تسأل خيراً قليلًا فقال عمر : لا بل خير كثيرٌ ، قِرْبَتانِ ، قِرْبةٌ من ماء وقربة من لبَن

"Al-Zubayr said, 'You, O brother of Tamim, ask for little good.' Umar said, 'No, for much good, two skins, a skin of water and a skin of milk.'"

- Al-Azhari, Tahdhib al-Lugha, vol.10, p.20

Two dictionaries, three centuries apart, cite the same vocative because it was the living idiom of the address. Tamim is a tribe, an ancestor's name carried by a whole people, and the man of that stock is greeted by it. The Imam's ﵇ correction and the lexicons' scene agree from two directions.

The dictionary carries a poet's line in its own pages, a pre-Islamic taunt addressed to a man of Banu Khalaf by his clan's name. The taunt's grammar is the grammar of the 19:28 scene, an address by the house's name in the middle of a rebuke.

Source: a pre-Islamic poet, in Lisan al-Arab, vol.11, p.740

Claim: a man of Banu Khalaf is addressed as their brother

يا زِبْرِقان ، أَخا بني خَلَفٍ ، ما أَنت ، وَيْبَ أَبيك والفَخْر

"O Zibriqan, brother of the sons of Khalaf, what has come over you, fie upon your father, and this boasting?"

- In Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol.11, p.740

Al-Sharif al-Murtada (d.436), the school's theologian of record in fifth-century Baghdad, gave a full session of his Amali to this one verse alone. Among the readings he weighs is the descendant reading, that the address names a woman of Aaron's line the way an Arab is named by his tribe.

Source: al-Sharif al-Murtada (d.436 AH), Amali (Ghurar al-Fawa'id), session 68, vol.2, p.197

Open question: what sister of Aaron means

Claim: the address names a woman of Aaron's ﵇ line the way an Arab is named by his tribe

ومنها أن يكون معنى قوله : يا أُخْتَ هارُونَ يا من هي من نسل هارون أخي موسى ؛ كما يقال للرجل : يا أخا بنى تميم ، ويا أخا بنى فلان

"It may also be that 'O sister of Aaron' means, O you who are of the line of Aaron, the brother of Moses. This is as one says to a man, 'O brother of the Banu Tamim,' or 'O brother of the Banu so-and-so.'"

- Al-Murtada, Amali, vol.2, p.197

This is the sentence the charge has to overturn, and nothing in the ledger overturns it. The address takes a woman of Aaron's ﵇ line and names her by the line's ancestor, exactly as an Arab is addressed by his tribe. A first-rank scholar of the school stated it almost a millennium before the charge's modern carriers.

3. Daughter of Imran, Brother of Ad

The Qur'an itself uses the same kinship words. The clearest case is the verse that calls her the daughter of Imran, and no one has ever filed a charge against it.

Verse: Qur'an 66:12, Surat al-Tahrim

Claim: kinship word plus an ancestor's name states house membership

Claim: Maryam ﵍ is called the daughter of Imran a millennium after the ancient Amram

وَمَرْيَمَ ٱبْنَتَ عِمْرَٰنَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَحْصَنَتْ فَرْجَهَا فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِنَا وَصَدَّقَتْ بِكَلِمَٰتِ رَبِّهَا وَكُتُبِهِۦ وَكَانَتْ مِنَ ٱلْقَٰنِتِينَ

wa-Maryama bnata ʿImrāna allatī aḥṣanat farjahā, fa-nafakhnā fīhi min rūḥinā, wa-ṣaddaqat bi-kalimāti rabbihā wa-kutubihī, wa-kānat mina l-qānitīn

"And Maryam, daughter of Imran, who guarded her chastity, so We breathed into her of Our spirit, and she affirmed the words of her Lord and His books, and she was of the devoutly obedient."

- Qur'an 66:12

No one has ever filed a charge that the Qur'an holds the ancient Amram to have personally fathered Maryam ﵍ a thousand years after his death. The verse says bint, daughter, and every reader, believing and hostile, has understood descent from the house of Imran. The surah of her birth opens with the vow of the wife of Imran (Qur'an 3:35), her own mother, and the tradition reads that Imran as her father. He was a man of the same house carrying its ancient name, the naming custom working inside the family again. The third chapter is itself named for the House of Imran, Aal Imran, the aal that means the line of a father, as the Qur'an says Aal Ibrahim and Aal Yaqub. And the whole corpus calls the nation banu Isra'il, the sons of Israel, century after century after Israel's death.

The charge treats sister of Aaron as a literal sibling claim while letting daughter of Imran pass as descent without complaint. But the two words sit in one system. Bint names descent from the house's father, ukht names kinship inside the house, and aal names the house entire. The charge's rule, that kinship words mean only the womb, would empty half the Qur'an's naming. Its own silence on daughter of Imran shows that no one actually holds that rule.

The Qur'an also names prophets by the nations they were sent to, Hud the brother of Ad and Salih the brother of Thamud, and Muqatil's gloss reaches for this pair.

Verse: Qur'an 7:65

Claim: a prophet of the stock of Ad is called the nation's brother

وَإِلَىٰ عَادٍ أَخَاهُمْ هُودًۭا

wa-ilā ʿĀdin akhāhum Hūdā

"And to Ad, their brother Hud."

- Qur'an 7:65

Verse: Qur'an 7:73

Claim: a prophet of the stock of Thamud likewise

وَإِلَىٰ ثَمُودَ أَخَاهُمْ صَٰلِحًۭا

wa-ilā Thamūda akhāhum Ṣāliḥā

"And to Thamud, their brother Salih."

- Qur'an 7:73

Hud shares no womb with the whole nation Ad, and Salih shares none with every man of Thamud. The Qur'an sends Shu'ayb to Madyan in the same register (Qur'an 7:85). It calls each prophet the brother of the nation he was sent to because he is of their stock and their kind.

Daughter of Imran shows the kinship word joined to a named individual, for descent. Brother of Ad and brother of Thamud show the kinship word joined to a collective, for belonging. What 19:28 writes, a feminine kinship word joined to the house's named saint, composes those two registers. A composition of two daily registers needs no separate attestation, and Muqatil's gloss and al-Murtada's statement give the meaning of the composition at the verse itself. The Qur'an spends the same words more loosely still. It calls the believers brothers wherever they live (Qur'an 49:10), and the reckless spendthrifts brothers of the devils (Qur'an 17:27), where no blood is in question.

4. Four Readings, No Confusion

The school's narration-based tafsir hands the scene down unpolished and weighs several live readings of the address against each other. On every reading, the confusion charge fails.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329 AH), Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.2, p.50

Reports: the school's inherited account of the birth scene of Isa ﵇

Claim: the crowd likened her to a wicked man named Harun of their own time

وأقبلن مؤمنات بني إسرائيل يبزقن في وجهها ، فلم تكلمهن حتى دخلت في محرابها ، فجاء إليها بنو إسرائيل وزكريا (فقالوا لها يا مريم لقد جئت شيئا فريا أي عظيما من المناهي يا أخت هارون ما كان أبوك امرأ سوء وما كانت أمك بغيا)

ومعنى قولهم : يا أخت هارون ان هارون كان رجلا فاسقا زانيا فشبهوها به ، من أين هذا البلاء الذي جئت به والعار الذي ألزمته لبني إسرائيل

"The believing women of the Children of Israel came forward and spat in Maryam's face. She did not speak to them until she entered her prayer-niche. Then the Children of Israel and Zakariyya came to her. They said, 'Maryam, you have come with a thing unheard of, a grave forbidden thing. O sister of Aaron, your father was not an evil man, and your mother was not wanton.'"

"By 'sister of Aaron' they meant that Aaron was an immoral man and an adulterer, and they likened her to him. Where did this affliction you have brought come from, and the shame you have fastened on the Children of Israel?"

- Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.2, p.50

On this reading the crowd named no genealogy of hers. They cut her with a comparison to a notorious man of their own generation, a Harun known for sin, the way a crowd hisses a corrupt family name at a public scandal. The address is an insult, and an insult needs a reference the audience shares. A Harun of their own day gives them one, and the verse reports their words without endorsing their sneer. Even this one reading ends the confusion charge, because a contemporary Harun is no anachronism. Al-Qummi's Harun is a wicked man and the exegetes' Harun is a saint, and the clash is the naming custom seen from two sides. A name forty thousand men carried held both kinds, and a crowd reaching for the house's name could reach for a notorious namesake or a holy one. The two readings answer different questions, al-Qummi's namesake telling which man the crowd reached for, and Muqatil's report telling whose house the name carries.

Source: al-Sharif al-Murtada, Amali, vol.4, p.106, from the early exegetes

Claim: a righteous Harun of her people

Claim: forty thousand men named Harun followed his funeral

لم يكن أخاها على الحقيقة بل كان رجلا صالحا من قومها وإنه لما مات شيع جنازته أربعون ألف رجل كلهم يسمون هارون من بني إسرائيل فلما أنكروا ما ظهر من أمرها قالوا لها يا أخت هارون أي بالشبهة بالصلاح ما كان هذا معروفا منك ولا كان والدك ممن يفعل القبيح ولا يتطرق عليه الريب

"He was not her brother in the literal sense. He was a righteous man of her people. When he died, forty thousand men of the Children of Israel followed his funeral, every one of them named Aaron."

"When they condemned what had appeared of her affair, they said to her, 'O sister of Aaron,' meaning by likeness in righteousness, 'such a thing was never known of you, and your father was never a man of shameful deeds, nor was he open to suspicion.'"

- Al-Murtada, Amali, vol.4, p.106

Forty thousand men named Harun at one funeral is a report's way of saying the name filled the city. Behind the number stands a people who named their sons after the saints of the line so consistently that a righteous Harun could die with a crowd of his namesakes behind his bier. A woman of that people, addressed by the same name, is being measured against the house's standard of piety.

Source: Muqatil ibn Sulayman (d.150 AH), in al-Murtada, Amali, vol.2, p.197

Source: from the Prophet ﷺ

Claim: this Aaron is Aaron the brother of Moses ﵇, and the address means one of his line

وذكر مقاتل بن سليمان في قوله تعالى : يا أُخْتَ هارُونَ قال : روى عن النبيّ صلّى الله عليه وآله أنّه قال : «هارون الّذي ذكروه هو هارون أخو موسى ﵉»

قال مقاتل : تأويل يا أُخْتَ هارُونَ يا من هي من نسل هارون ، كما قال تعالى : وإِلى عادٍ أَخاهُمْ هُوداً ، وإِلى ثَمُودَ أَخاهُمْ صالِحاً يعنى بأخيهم أنّه من نسلهم وجنسهم

"Muqatil ibn Sulayman, on Allah's word 'O sister of Aaron,' said, it is reported from the Prophet that he said, 'The Aaron they mentioned is Aaron, the brother of Moses.'"

"Muqatil explained, 'O sister of Aaron' means O you who are of the line of Aaron, as Allah says, 'And to Ad, their brother Hud,' and 'And to Thamud, their brother Salih.' By 'their brother' He means one of their descendants and their kind."

- In al-Murtada, Amali, vol.2, p.197

The report grants the charge its identification. The Aaron of the address is Aaron the prophet ﵇, brother of Moses ﵇. And the Prophet's ﷺ own explanation, as Muqatil transmits it, is that brother-of in the Qur'an's mouth means one of their descendants and their kind, exactly the Hud and Salih construction shown above.

Muqatil's standing is the obvious objection. He is an early transmitter whom the later critics graded harshly, and a hostile reader will say so; the school's case does not lean on him. Al-Murtada, the hardest critic of reports his generation produced, cites him as one reading among several. Every other reading reaches the same ground by another road, through the Qur'an's own usage, the naming custom from Najran, the lexicons, and the living speech. If Muqatil falls, the idiom stands on the rest; if he holds, he carries the Prophet's ﷺ own sentence for it.

The school's compilation gathers the collected readings a century later, and the Sunni exegetical tradition enters the same ledger. Al-Farra (d.207), the founder of the Kufan school of Qur'anic grammar, keeps a brother in the picture, one who shares neither of her parents.

Source: al-Huwayzi (d.1112 AH), Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.333

Claim: the collected views on sister of Aaron, from Ibn Abbas to al-Mughira

يا أخت هارون قيل فيه أقوال : أحدها أن هارون هذا كان رجلا صالحا في بني إسرائيل ينسب إليه كل من عرف بالصلاح عن ابن عباس وقتادة وكعب وابن زيد والمغيرة بن شعبة يرفعه إلى النبي ﷌

"On 'sister of Aaron' several views have been given. One is that this Aaron was a righteous man among the Children of Israel, and everyone known for righteousness was ascribed to him. This is from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Kab, and Ibn Zayd, and al-Mughira ibn Shubah raises it to the Prophet."

- Al-Huwayzi, Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.333

Source: al-Farra (d.207 AH), Ma'ani al-Qur'an, vol.2, p.167

Claim: a brother who does not share her parents

"She had a brother named Aaron, one of the best of the Children of Israel, though he was not from her two parents. So it was said, 'sister of Aaron,' for his righteousness. Your brother is righteous, and your parents are your parents."

- Al-Farra, Ma'ani al-Qur'an, vol.2, p.167

Al-Farra's Harun is a brother who does not share her parents, and the kinship word is already doing lineage work before anyone thought to doubt it. The wicked Harun of al-Qummi, the righteous Harun of forty thousand namesakes, the brother who shares neither of her parents, and the line of Aaron the prophet ﵇ all stand on one ground. The address claims no shared womb with the brother of Moses ﵇, and its first hearers did not hear one.

5. No One Needed to Explain the Word

The strongest witness to a language's grammar is the speech of the people who grew up inside it. The Four Books of the school carry the Imams ﵈ addressing strangers and tribesmen with the brother-of construction throughout. No one in the reports pauses to explain it, because no one hearing it needed a gloss.

In the longest case, an Egyptian skeptic, a zindiq, a denier of prophethood, traveled to Makkah to argue with Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ and found him in the tawaf, the ritual walk around the Kaaba. The Imam ﵇ answered him question by question, and at every turn he addressed the man by his city.

Source: al-Kafi, vol.1, p.121

Speaker: Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇

Locus: the Egyptian zindiq debates the Imam ﵇ during the tawaf

Claim: a total stranger is addressed as the brother of the people of Egypt

فقال أبو عبد الله ﵇ : أيها الرجل ! ليس لمن لا يعلم حجة على من يعلم ولا حجة للجاهل يا أخا أهل مصر ! تفهم عني فإنا لا نشك في الله أبدا

أما ترى الشمس والقمر والليل والنهار يلجان فلا يشتبهان ويرجعان قد اضطرا ليس لهما مكان إلا مكانهما فإن كانا يقدران على أن يذهبا فلم يرجعان ؟

"Imam al-Sadiq said, 'Man, one who does not know has no argument against one who knows, and the ignorant have no argument. O brother of the people of Egypt, understand me, we never doubt Allah.'"

"'Do you not see the sun and the moon, the night and the day? They enter and never get confused, and they return. They are compelled; they have no place except their own place. If they were able to leave, why would they return?'"

- Al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol.1, p.121

The Imam ﵇ had never seen this man before the man laid a hand on his shoulder mid-tawaf. The address names the stranger by his people, brother of the people of Egypt, and the debate ran on with the address repeated down the page. The vocative, the grammar of calling, carries a kinship word and a people's name together as easily as an English speaker says a name alone. The envoy's trouble at Najran was the thousand years between Moses and Jesus, never the Arabic of the address. The answer the Prophet ﷺ gave him to carry was a fact of custom.

The Commander of the Faithful ﵇ uses the address on campaign at Nahrawan, and Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ on a visitor from Yemen.

"I was sitting when the Commander of the Faithful came to me and said, 'O brother of al-Azd, do you have water for purification?' I said, 'Yes,' and handed him the waterskin."

"Imam al-Sadiq said to him, 'Shall I not add to you, of His bounty, an increase, O brother of Yemen?' He said, 'Increase it for me, son of the Messenger of Allah.'"

- Al-Amili, Wasa'il al-Shia, vol.10, p.351

Al-Azd is a tribe, Yemen is a people, and neither man is a sibling of the speaker. A soldier of the Azd behind the lines at Nahrawan is greeted by his kinship to his tribe's ancestor, and a pilgrim from the south by his country.

Locus: two men ask about prayer and ablution

Claim: each questioner is addressed by his people

"The Prophet said, 'As for you, O brother of the Ansar, you are of a people who give others preference over themselves. You are a townsman, and this man of Thaqif is a Bedouin. One speaker, two addresses, two peoples, neither man a sibling of the Prophet ﷺ. The same address runs across the corpus, man after man.

In al-Kafi, a woman of the Ansar came to the Prophet ﷺ and offered herself in marriage, and he answered her with kindness, calling her the sister of the Ansar.

Locus: a woman of the Ansar offers herself

"O sister of the Ansar, may Allah reward you well on behalf of the Messenger of Allah. Your men have supported me, and your women have inclined to me."

She is a woman of the nation addressed by its name, no sibling in sight, and the feminine vocative works exactly as the masculine does. The records now run brother of Egypt, brother of al-Azd, brother of Thaqif, brother of Yemen, and sister of the Ansar. The address of 19:28 is the same construction in the feminine, and the reports of the school carry it throughout.

6. The Objection Is as Old as the Verse

The Christians of Najran put the question to the Prophet's ﷺ own envoy in his lifetime. They read their scripture and kept their genealogies, and they asked why the Qur'an calls Maryam the sister of Aaron when Moses lived so long before Jesus. The exchange is itself a transmitted report in the books of both communities.

Source: Musnad Ahmad (al-Risala ed.), vol.30, p.141; also Ibn Asakir, Tarikh Dimashq, vol.70, p.98, no.13795; Sahih Muslim, Kitab al-Adab

Speaker: al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba, the envoy to Najran

Claim: the objection was raised in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime and answered by him

بَعَثَنِي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ إِلَى نَجْرَانَ ، قَالَ : فَقَالُوا : أَرَأَيْتَ مَا تَقْرَءُونَ يَا أُخْتَ هَارُونَ وَمُوسَى قَبْلَ عِيسَى بِكَذَا وَكَذَا ؟

قَالَ : فَرَجَعْتُ ، فَذَكَرْتُ ذَلِكَ لِرَسُولِ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ ، فَقَالَ : أَلَا أَخْبَرْتَهُمْ أَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا يُسَمَّوْنَ بِالْأَنْبِيَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ قَبْلَهُمْ

"The Messenger of Allah sent me to Najran. They said, 'Have you considered what you recite, "sister of Aaron," when Moses lived so long before Jesus?' I returned and told the Messenger of Allah, and he said, 'Why did you not tell them, they used to name themselves after the prophets and the righteous before them?'"

- Musnad Ahmad, vol.30, p.141

The report proves the verse was heard and tested within the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime by the people best prepared to break it. It also preserves the answer that came back from the source of the recitation in his own words. The Children of Israel used to attach their children to their prophets and righteous men, and an address by a saint's name rides that custom. The same report stands in Sahih Muslim's collection of authentic reports, Kitab al-Adab, the strongest grading a Sunni chain carries. The school did not borrow this answer from the Sunni books.

Source: Ibn Tawus (d.664 AH), Sa'd al-Su'ud, from al-Azdi, from Simak ibn Harb, in Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.333

Chain: a Shia transmission of the same event

Claim: the school keeps the Najran exchange in its own books

فذكر ذلك للنبي ﷌ فقال : ألا قلت لهم : انهم كانوا يسمون بأنبيائهم والصالحين منهم

"In Sa'd al-Su'ud by Ibn Tawus, from the book of Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad al-Azdi, Simak ibn Harb told me, from al-Mughira ibn Shubah, that the Prophet sent him to Najran. They said, 'Do you recite "sister of Aaron," with all the years between the two?'"

"He told the Prophet, and he said, 'Why did you not say to them, they used to name themselves after their prophets and their righteous men?'"

- Al-Huwayzi, Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.333

Al-Murtada's Amali keeps the exchange in the school's own sitting, where the Prophet's ﷺ rebuke is softened into a lesson. When the Messenger of Allah sent al-Mughira to the people of Najran, they said his Prophet claimed Aaron was the brother of Moses ﵇, when Allah knows the years between Moses and Jesus. Al-Mughira did not know what to answer until he returned. The Prophet ﷺ asked him why he had not said that they used to name themselves after their prophets and the righteous before them (al-Murtada, Amali, vol.2, p.197).

Three records of one exchange survive, two in the Sunni books and one in the school's, and all of them preserve the same narrator, Simak ibn Harb, whom the graders of both communities accepted. Even the question of her blood, whether it ran from the sons of Aaron ﵇ himself, ends by helping the verse.

7. Luke Himself Puts Her in Aaron's Line

The blood question has a Qur'anic frame before it has a Gospel one, and the third surah opens by setting the House of Imran inside the chain of chosen houses.

إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ ٱصْطَفَىٰٓ ءَادَمَ وَنُوحًۭا وَءَالَ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَءَالَ عِمْرَٰنَ عَلَى ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

ذُرِّيَّةًۢ بَعْضُهَا مِنۢ بَعْضٍۢ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ

inna llāha iṣṭafā Ādama wa-Nūḥan wa-āla Ibrāhīma wa-āla ʿImrāna ʿalā l-ʿālamīn. Dhurriyyatan baʿḍuhā min baʿḍin, wa-llāhu samīʿun ʿalīm

"Allah chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of Imran above all worlds, an offspring, one of the other. Allah hears and knows all things."

- Qur'an 3:33-34

The verse binds the houses into one seed, dhurriyya, some of it from some, so that Maryam's ﵍ house is a link in a chosen line. The exegetes of both communities identify the Qur'an's Imran with the Amram of the Torah, and the identification is the natural one, the house's own name recurring, for the tradition also knows a nearer Imran. The school reads her election, which the same surah states, as election into that lineage.

The Imam's ﵇ gloss of Allah has chosen you above the women of the worlds (Qur'an 3:42) reads the choosing as a choosing for the dhurriyya of the prophets. That is the same word the chosen-houses verse used. In the school's reading her sanctity is lineage-bearing before it is anything else. The Qur'an also raises her inside the sanctuary of that line.

8. Sister Names the Saint at the Head of the Line

The Davidic branch closed with the naming custom. If the meaning is descent, why did the crowd not say daughter of Aaron ﵇.

Aal names the house entire, Aal Imran. Bint names the father's descent, daughter of Imran, which the Qur'an already spends on her. Ukht names kinship inside the house, and it is the register that reaches the house's saint himself without dragging the intermediate generations into the address.

The crowd's rhetoric wanted the house's honor at its purest point, a father of clean record, a mother beyond slander, the saint at the door, and the address gives them exactly that.

The woman's name is Maryam, Miriam, the name of Aaron's ﵇ own sister, and her parents reached into the house's memory and took the sister's name for their daughter. 9. The Canon Does Not Pass Its Own Test

Source: the Gospels of Matthew, Luke, and Mark; Helvidius (4th c.); Jerome, Against Helvidius (383); John of Damascus, Homily on the Nativity (8th c.)

Claim: Mary's Davidic line; her Aaronic line through her kinswoman; the perpetual virginity and the brothers of the Lord

Open question: does the Christian canon survive the test it presses on Qur'an 19:28

The Islamic reading of 19:28 now stands on the Qur'an's usage, the Imams' ﵈ speech, the lexicons, the poets, and the naming custom answered at Najran.

Christian tradition traces the Lord's family to David, and Matthew opens his Gospel with the claim. Luke builds the Aaronic link into the infancy narrative through Elizabeth, as shown above. Helvidius, writing at Rome before 383, taught that the brothers named in Mark 6:3 were the Virgin's own sons by Joseph, and Jerome reports him so. Jerome answered that they were kin, the sons of Mary the wife of Alphaeus, called Mary of Clopas. He takes her for the sister or kinswoman of the Lord's mother, so that sons of the mother's sister can be called the Lord's brethren (Jerome, Against Helvidius, 383, chs. 14-16). The older tradition holds the Virgin ever virgin, a title the Second Council of Constantinople confessed in 553. John of Damascus (d.749) gives Mary a double lineage in his Homily on the Nativity, Joachim of Judah and David for her father's side, an Aaronic, Levitical house for her mother's. The canon's own family register thus runs through the same wide kinship words, and the strongest Christian readings of them are readings of kinship. The kinship words carry the range the charge denies to Arabic.

The comparison on the four criteria.

On textual fidelity, the Islamic reading keeps every word of 19:28 in its attested range. Ukht stays house-kinship, Aaron stays the house's saint, and the parentage defense stays the address's own frame. The charge must read one kinship word of one book literally and its sibling idiom, daughter of Imran, idiomatically, with no rule to separate them. On rational coherence, the charge's rule collapses inside the Christian canon itself. Brethren names a nephew in Genesis, a brother nation in Numbers, and the Lord's kin in Mark. A Christian who accepts his own canon's usage has no coherent ground to deny the same range to Arabic. On the ethical criterion, the charge asks a reader to believe that the book of God blundered in a genealogy a child could catch. The reading asks only that God spoke the kinship grammar the language's speakers, lexicons, and poets actually used, and that the first objectors were answered from the source in the verse's own lifetime. On economy, the reading needs one settled idiom to carry every exhibit above, while the charge needs a confusion no early reader reports, an idiom no dictionary supports, and a rule the canon itself breaks.

The Christian who presses the charge has been pressed by the same test, and his canon does not pass it. The canon that reads son of David across a millennium and brethren across a family network cannot rule that sister across a house is a blunder. The Christian who declines the test, reading his own kinship words by context, has granted the whole method. Final Verdict

The charge rests on a single premise, that sister can only name one womb. Every layer of evidence tells against that premise. The Qur'an itself runs the idiom throughout its pages, the sons of Israel, the daughter of Imran, the House of Imran, the brother of Ad, the brother of Thamud. No reader, including the hostile one, reads those literally. The Imams ﵈ used the same grammar their whole lives. A stranger was greeted as the brother of the people of Egypt, a soldier as the brother of al-Azd, and questioners as the brother of the Ansar and the brother of Thaqif. A woman of the Ansar was answered as the sister of the Ansar. The lexicons cite the address as ordinary speech, and the poets carried it before Islam. The Prophet ﷺ, asked by the Christians of Najran in his own lifetime, gave the answer in one line, that they used to name themselves after their prophets and the righteous before them. The school transmits his answer through its own chains.

The genealogy, which looked like the crux, ends by helping the verse. Luke's Gospel makes Maryam's ﵍ kinswoman a daughter of Aaron's ﵇ line. The Qur'an raises her in the temple under Zakariyya ﵇ and fixes her in the House of Imran, and the Torah names Amram as the father of Aaron ﵇ himself. If her blood is Davidic, as Ibn al-Athir held, the naming custom he himself recites still carries the address, and his report joins the Prophet's ﷺ Najran answer word for word. Both branches of the tradition, and both scriptures, land on the same ground. The address names the house, and it names it accurately.

The Gospel's word adelphos covers siblings, kinsmen, and co-religionists inside the New Testament itself, and at Nazareth it names the Lord's kin, James and Joses and Juda and Simon and his sisters (Mark 6:3). The older Christian tradition reads those brothers as kinsmen, and the readers who take them as literal brothers argue it from context, because the word alone cannot carry that meaning. A scripture whose brother-word needs context to mean a womb cannot rule that another scripture's sister-word does. The Greek and the Arabic keep the same wide kinship field, and the charge needs both fields narrow. The verse said sister because sister was true, in the language it was said in, to the woman of Aaron's ﵇ house standing before the assembly with her son in her arms.
