Why Maryam Is Called the Sister of Aaron

The Quran calls Maryam ﵍ the sister of Aaron because Arabic names a woman by her house, exactly as it calls Hud the brother of Ad and as the Imams ﵈ called strangers the brother of the people of Egypt and the brother of al-Azd. From Qur'an 19:28, the Ahl al-Bayt tafsir, Lisan al-Arab, and the Gospel of Luke.

The Argument Under Examination

Christian polemic against the Quran has long fixed on one line. Her people confront Maryam ﵍ with her infant, and the Quran gives their words as an address by the name of Aaron that opens with the honor of her parents (Qur'an 19:27-28). Aaron the prophet, brother of Moses ﵇, lived about a thousand years before Maryam ﵍. The charge says the Quran has confused two women, Maryam ﵍ the mother of Isa ﵇ and Miriam the sister of Moses ﵇, and rolled them into one. If she was not Aaron's ﵇ sister, the charge concludes, the verse should never have said sister.

The verse said sister because that is how the language names a woman's house. The words the crowd speaks defend a lineage, her father never an evil man, her mother never a wanton woman, and the address opens the same defense. It names her by the greatest saint of her line, and the grammar it uses is the standing grammar of Arabic, Hebrew, and the Quran itself.

The Imams ﵈ read the verse this way from the beginning. Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329 AH) narrates the whole scene and explains the address inside it. Al-Sharif al-Murtada (d.436 AH) gave a session of his Amali to this one verse. He wrote the school's classic statement of the idiom, that the address names a woman of Aaron's ﵇ line the way an Arab is named by his tribe. The objections of the Christians of Najran reached the Prophet ﷺ in his own lifetime, and his answer has chains in the Sunni and the Shia books alike. The lexicons and the poets, and the Gospel of Luke, keep the same kinship grammar.

Premise 1: Arabic kinship words name houses and lines, and they do this in the Quran itself.

The Quran calls Hud their brother when it sends him to Ad, and Salih their brother when it sends him to Thamud. The meaning is one of their descendants and their kind, as Muqatil ibn Sulayman explained in a report al-Murtada preserved. It calls the whole nation the sons of Israel a thousand years after Israel died, and it calls Maryam ﵍ herself the daughter of Imran (Qur'an 66:12) a thousand years after the ancient Amram. A kinship word plus an ancestor's name states membership in his house. That is the semantic field of the words the crowd used.

Premise 2: The Imams ﵈ and the Prophet ﷺ used exactly this address on men and women who were no siblings of theirs.

Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ repeatedly addressed an Egyptian stranger as the brother of the people of Egypt in the courtyard of the Kaaba, in al-Kafi. Ali ﵇ at Nahrawan said the brother of al-Azd. The Prophet ﷺ said the brother of the Ansar and the brother of Thaqif, and he answered a woman of the Ansar with the sister of the Ansar. The lexicons record the brother of Tamim, and pre-Islamic poetry carries the brother of the sons of Khalaf. The living speech of Arabic confirms the reading the tafsir gives.

Premise 3: The charge was pressed at the source and answered at the source.

The Christians of Najran put the question to al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba, the Prophet's ﷺ envoy, in his lifetime, and he carried it back. The Prophet ﷺ answered that the Children of Israel used to name themselves after their prophets and their righteous men. The school transmits this answer through Ibn Tawus as well as through the Sunni chains. The objection is as old as the verse, and the answer is as old as the objection.

Premise 4: Her Aaronic lineage is supported, and the support is not even needed.

The Quran fixes her in the House of Imran, the chosen seed (Qur'an 3:33-34), raised in the temple under Zakariyya ﵇. The Gospel of Luke calls Elizabeth, Maryam's ﵍ kinswoman, a woman of the daughters of Aaron (Luke 1:5, 1:36). Even the historians who prefer a Davidic line for her, like Ibn al-Athir, still concede that her people were called after the righteous, which is the very custom the verse rides. Every branch keeps the charge dead.

Conclusion: The address is a correct kinship address in the language it was said in.

From Premise 1 and Premise 2, sister of Aaron states a woman's house in the standing grammar of Arabic. From Premise 3, the objection is as old as the verse and the answer is as old as the objection, given by the Prophet ﷺ in his lifetime and preserved in the books of both communities. From Premise 4, whichever genealogy a reader prefers, Aaronide or Davidic, the address stands. The charge of confusion requires a rule the language never had, that sister can only mean one womb. Every layer of evidence, the Quran's own usage, the Imams' ﵈ practice, the lexicons, the poets, and the Bible, refuses that rule.

1. The Charge at Its Strongest

The surah builds the scene in three beats. She withdraws from her people to an eastern place. The spirit is sent to her, and she conceives. Then she comes back carrying the child, and the surah gives the crowd's words in their own grammar.

The two verses say one thing together. The crowd opens with the lineage defense, her father was never evil, her mother was never unchaste, and it opens that defense with the lineage's name, sister of Aaron. The address belongs to the accusation. It is the sound of a house being invoked against one of its daughters.

Verse: Qur'an 19:27-28, Surat Maryam

Locus: Maryam ﵍ returns to her people carrying the infant Isa ﵇

Speaker: the assembly of the Children of Israel, with Zakariyya ﵇ present in Tafsir al-Qummi's narration

Claim: the crowd invokes her lineage's honor while charging her with a thing unheard of

فَأَتَتْ بِهِۦ قَوْمَهَا تَحْمِلُهُۥ ۖ قَالُوا۟ يَٰمَرْيَمُ لَقَدْ جِئْتِ شَيْـًۭٔا فَرِيًّۭا

يَٰٓأُخْتَ هَٰرُونَ مَا كَانَ أَبُوكِ ٱمْرَأَ سَوْءٍۢ وَمَا كَانَتْ أُمُّكِ بَغِيًّۭا

fa-atat bihi qawmahā taḥmiluh, qālū yā Maryamu laqad jiʾti shayʾan fariyyā. Yā ukhta Hārūna mā kāna abūki imraʾa sawʾin wa-mā kānat ummuki baghiyyā.

"Then she came to her people, carrying him. They said, 'Maryam, you have come with a thing unheard of.'"

"O sister of Aaron, your father was not an evil man, and your mother was not wanton."

- Qur'an 19:27-28

What the words on the page show is the frame. The address and the parentage defense are one speech, and the speech is about lineage. What the charge builds on them comes next.

The polemic is old enough to have a pedigree. The Torah closes the list of Amram's children with and Miriam their sister (Numbers 26:59), and Miriam the prophetess is herself titled the sister of Aaron (Exodus 15:20). The polemicist argues that the Quran's author lifted the name Maryam and kept the family register it came with. The result is a verse that calls the mother of Isa ﵇ the sister of a man dead a thousand years.

The premise is the whole charge, and the premise is a claim about Arabic. If the language's kinship words carry lineage and house membership, then sister of Aaron states a truth about Maryam ﵍ precisely, in the register the crowd itself would have used. If they carry only the womb, the charge lands. So the question turns on the evidence of the language, and the dictionaries come first.

2. What Sister Means in Arabic

Contested words. The charge reads ukht, sister, as one born of the same parents only. The Imams' ﵈ own definitions, the lexicons, and the poets fix the word as kinship within a house, covering descendants and tribesmen.

The word under dispute is ukht, sister, the feminine of akh. The dispute is about its range. The school's own books settle the range with a definition, a scene from the Companions' age preserved inside the dictionaries, and a poet's line.

First the definition, and it comes from the Imam ﵇ of the school himself, in al-Kafi. A woman named Juwayra is waiting in the Sacred Mosque for a dependent of her family, a mawla, a freed slave attached to the household. The Imam ﵇ asks her what she is waiting for, and when he learns the man was never freed himself, only his grandfather was, he corrects her word for the relationship.

Source: al-Kafi, vol.6, p.199, narration 4

Speaker: Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇

Locus: Juwayra waits for a mawla of the family in the Sacred Mosque

Claim: the grandson of the freed dependent is called your brother

مر بي أبو عبد الله ﵇ وأنا في المسجد الحرام أنتظر مولى لنا فقال يا أم عثمان ما يقيمك ههنا ؟

قلت أنتظر مولى لنا فقال أعتقتموه ؟ قلت لا فقال أعتقتم أباه ؟ قلت لا أعتقنا جده فقال ليس هذا مولاكم بل هذا أخوكم

"Abu Abd Allah passed by me while I was in the Sacred Mosque waiting for a dependent of ours. He said, 'Umm Uthman, what keeps you here?'"

"I said, 'I am waiting for a dependent of ours.' He said, 'Did you free him?' I said, 'No.' He said, 'Did you free his father?' I said, 'No, we freed his grandfather.' He said, 'He is not your dependent. He is your brother.'"

- Al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol.6, p.199

A man two generations removed from the household's attachment is the family's brother. This matters because it is the Imam's ﵇ own use of the word, fixing akh as a word for kinship across generations, inside a ruling, with no one in the report surprised by it. The dictionaries now show the same range in the speech of the generation before the school.

A man of Tamim has come to Umar asking for a share of water in a land grant, and al-Zubayr addresses him by his tribe. Ibn Manzur preserves the exchange in Lisan al-Arab, the largest classical dictionary of the language, and al-Azhari had carried the same scene a century earlier in Tahdhib al-Lugha. The two lexicons quote it as their evidence for a word for wells, and they preserve the address as ordinary speech.

Two dictionaries, three centuries apart, cite the same vocative because it was the living idiom of the address. Tamim is a tribe, an ancestor's name carried by a whole people, and the man of that stock is greeted by it. The Imam's ﵇ definition and the lexicons' scene agree from two directions. The poets now show the address at home in verse, before Islam and outside any religious context.

The dictionary carries a poet's line in its own pages, a taunt addressed to a man of Banu Khalaf by his clan's name. The taunt's grammar is the grammar of the 19:28 scene, an address by the house's name in the middle of a rebuke.

- In Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol.11, p.740

So the dictionaries define the range, the Companions' age used it in speech, and the poets used it in verse. One statement of the idiom remains, the school's own, and it is also the bridge to the Quran's usage, because it states the meaning of this very verse.

Al-Sharif al-Murtada, the school's theologian of record in fifth-century Baghdad, gave a full session of his Amali to this one verse alone. Among the readings he weighs is the descendant reading, and with it the school's classic sentence of the idiom.

This is the sentence the whole charge has to survive, and it cannot. A first-rank scholar of the school, a century before the charge's modern carriers were born, says plainly what the address does. It takes a woman of Aaron's ﵇ line and names her by the line's ancestor, exactly as an Arab is addressed by his tribe. Whether the language's own book of revelation speaks this grammar is the next question.

3. One Word System for the Whole Quran

The Quran's own pages carry the grammar the crowd used. The clearest case is the verse that names her father's house, and no one has ever filed a charge against it.

Verse: Qur'an 66:12, Surat al-Tahrim

Claim: kinship word plus an ancestor's name states house membership

Claim: Maryam ﵍ is called the daughter of Imran a millennium after the ancient Amram

وَمَرْيَمَ ٱبْنَتَ عِمْرَٰنَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَحْصَنَتْ فَرْجَهَا فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِنَا وَصَدَّقَتْ بِكَلِمَٰتِ رَبِّهَا وَكُتُبِهِۦ وَكَانَتْ مِنَ ٱلْقَٰنِتِينَ

wa-Maryama bnata ʿImrāna allatī aḥṣanat farjahā, fa-nafakhnā fīhi min rūḥinā, wa-ṣaddaqat bi-kalimāti rabbihā wa-kutubihī, wa-kānat mina l-qānitīn

"And Maryam, daughter of Imran, who guarded her chastity, so We breathed into her of Our spirit, and she affirmed the words of her Lord and His books, and she was of the devoutly obedient."

- Qur'an 66:12

No one has ever filed a charge that the Quran thinks the ancient Amram personally fathered Maryam ﵍ a thousand years after his death. The verse says bint, daughter, and every reader, believing and hostile, has understood descent from the house of Imran without a flicker of difficulty. The surah of her birth opens with the vow of the wife of Imran (Qur'an 3:35), her own mother, and the tradition reads that Imran as her father. He was a man of the same house carrying its ancient name, and the naming custom works inside the family again. The third chapter is itself named for the House of Imran, Aal Imran, the aal that means the line of a father, as the Quran says Aal Ibrahim and Aal Yaqub. And the whole corpus calls the nation banu Isra'il, the sons of Israel, century after century after Israel's death.

This is the quietest and strongest point against the charge. The charge treats sister of Aaron as a literal sibling claim while, in the same book, letting daughter of Imran pass as descent without complaint. The two words sit in one system. Bint names descent from the house's father. Ukht names kinship inside the house. Aal names the house entire. The charge's rule, that kinship words mean only the womb, would empty half the Quran's naming, and the charge's own silence on daughter of Imran shows that no one actually believes that rule.

The system has a second register, and the Quran spends it on prophets sent to nations. Muqatil's gloss, shown in the next section, reaches for this pair, and the verses deserve their own light.

Verse: Qur'an 7:65

Claim: a prophet of the stock of Ad is called the nation's brother

وَإِلَىٰ عَادٍ أَخَاهُمْ هُودًۭا

wa-ilā ʿĀdin akhāhum Hūdā

"And to Ad, their brother Hud."

- Qur'an 7:65

Verse: Qur'an 7:73

Claim: a prophet of the stock of Thamud likewise

وَإِلَىٰ ثَمُودَ أَخَاهُمْ صَٰلِحًۭا

wa-ilā Thamūda akhāhum Ṣāliḥā

"And to Thamud, their brother Salih."

- Qur'an 7:73

Hud is not the uterine brother of the nation Ad, and Salih is not one womb with every man of Thamud, and the Quran sends Shu'ayb to Madyan in the same register (Qur'an 7:85). The Quran calls each prophet the brother of the nation he was sent to because he is of their stock, their kind, their house. Put the pieces side by side and the pieces of the construction of 19:28 stand in the record, part by part. The Quran itself attests kinship word plus a named individual for descent in daughter of Imran. It attests kinship word plus a collective for belonging in brother of Ad and brother of Thamud, and in the living speech shown below, where it also takes the feminine. What 19:28 writes, feminine kinship word plus the house's named saint, is the composition of two attested registers, and Muqatil's gloss and al-Murtada's statement both say the composition outright. A composition needs no separate attestation when both its parts are the language's furniture and an early explainer and the school's classic statement both say the meaning at the verse itself. The Quran spends the same words more loosely still when it pleases. It calls the believers brothers wherever they live (Qur'an 49:10), and the reckless spendthrifts brothers of the devils (Qur'an 17:27), where no blood is in question at all.

4. The Tafsir Weighs the Sister of Aaron

The school's narration-based tafsir does not smooth the verse. It keeps the scene raw, records several live readings, and weighs them against each other, and every reading kills the confusion charge on its own.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329 AH), Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.2, p.50

Reports: the school's inherited account of the birth scene of Isa ﵇

Claim: the crowd likened her to a wicked man named Harun of their own time

وأقبلن مؤمنات بني إسرائيل يبزقن في وجهها ، فلم تكلمهن حتى دخلت في محرابها ، فجاء إليها بنو إسرائيل وزكريا (فقالوا لها يا مريم لقد جئت شيئا فريا أي عظيما من المناهي يا أخت هارون ما كان أبوك امرأ سوء وما كانت أمك بغيا)

ومعنى قولهم : يا أخت هارون ان هارون كان رجلا فاسقا زانيا فشبهوها به ، من أين هذا البلاء الذي جئت به والعار الذي ألزمته لبني إسرائيل

"The believing women of the Children of Israel came forward and spat in Maryam's face. She did not speak to them until she entered her prayer-niche. Then the Children of Israel and Zakariyya came to her. They said, 'Maryam, you have come with a thing unheard of, a grave forbidden thing. O sister of Aaron, your father was not an evil man, and your mother was not wanton.'"

"By 'sister of Aaron' they meant that Aaron was an immoral man and an adulterer, and they likened her to him. Where did this affliction you have brought come from, and the shame you have fastened on the Children of Israel?"

- Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.2, p.50

On this reading the crowd was not naming her genealogy at all. They were cutting her with a comparison to a notorious man of their own generation, a Harun known for sin, the way a crowd hisses a corrupt family name at a public scandal. The address is an insult, and an insult needs a reference the audience shares. A Harun of their own day gives them one, and the verse reports their words without endorsing their sneer. This reading alone ends the confusion charge, because a contemporary Harun is no anachronism. Notice that al-Qummi's Harun is a wicked man and the exegetes' Harun, shown next, is a saint, and the clash is the naming custom seen from two sides. A name forty thousand men carried held both kinds, and a crowd reaching for the house's name could reach for a notorious namesake or a holy one. The two readings answer different questions, al-Qummi's namesake tells which man the crowd reached for, and Muqatil's report, below, tells whose house the name carries.

Source: al-Sharif al-Murtada, Amali, vol.4, p.106, from the early exegetes

Claim: a righteous Harun of her people

Claim: forty thousand men named Harun followed his funeral

لم يكن أخاها على الحقيقة بل كان رجلا صالحا من قومها وإنه لما مات شيع جنازته أربعون ألف رجل كلهم يسمون هارون من بني إسرائيل فلما أنكروا ما ظهر من أمرها قالوا لها يا أخت هارون أي بالشبهة بالصلاح ما كان هذا معروفا منك ولا كان والدك ممن يفعل القبيح ولا يتطرق عليه الريب

"He was not her brother in the literal sense. He was a righteous man of her people. When he died, forty thousand men of the Children of Israel followed his funeral, every one of them named Aaron."

"When they condemned what had appeared of her affair, they said to her, 'O sister of Aaron,' meaning by likeness in righteousness, 'such a thing was never known of you, and your father was never a man of shameful deeds, nor was he open to suspicion.'"

- Al-Murtada, Amali, vol.4, p.106

Forty thousand men named Harun at one funeral is a report's way of saying the name filled the city. Behind the number stands a people who named their sons after the saints of the line so consistently that a righteous Harun could die with a crowd of his namesakes behind his bier. A woman of that people, addressed by the same name, is being measured against the house's standard of piety. And the school's ledger has a stronger entry still, the report that names the Aaron of the address outright.

قال مقاتل : تأويل يا أُخْتَ هارُونَ يا من هي من نسل هارون ، كما قال تعالى : وإِلى عادٍ أَخاهُمْ هُوداً ، وإِلى ثَمُودَ أَخاهُمْ صالِحاً يعنى بأخيهم أنّه من نسلهم وجنسهم

"Muqatil ibn Sulayman, on Allah's word 'O sister of Aaron,' said, it is reported from the Prophet that he said, 'The Aaron they mentioned is Aaron, the brother of Moses.'"

"Muqatil explained, 'O sister of Aaron' means O you who are of the line of Aaron, as Allah says, 'And to Ad, their brother Hud,' and 'And to Thamud, their brother Salih.' By 'their brother' He means one of their descendants and their kind."

- In al-Murtada, Amali, vol.2, p.197

The report meets the charge on its own ground and concedes the identification. Yes, the Aaron of the address is Aaron the prophet ﵇, brother of Moses ﵇. And the Prophet's ﷺ own explanation, as Muqatil transmits it, is that brother-of in the Quran's mouth means one of their descendants and their kind, exactly the Hud and Salih construction shown above.

One honesty belongs here. Muqatil is an early transmitter whom the later critics graded harshly, and a hostile reader will say so. The school's case does not lean on him. Al-Murtada, the hardest critic of reports his generation produced, cites him as one reading among several. Every other reading reaches the same ground by another road, the Quran's own usage, the naming custom from Najran shown below, the lexicons, and the living speech. If Muqatil falls, the idiom stands on the rest. If he holds, he carries the Prophet's ﷺ own sentence for it.

The school's compilation gathers the strand a century later, and the Sunni exegetical tradition enters the same ledger. Al-Farra, the founder of the Kufan school of Quranic grammar, keeps a brother in the picture, and his wording shows how loosely the kinship sits inside the family itself.

Source: al-Huwayzi (d.1112 AH), Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.333

Claim: the collected views on sister of Aaron, from Ibn Abbas to al-Mughira

يا أخت هارون قيل فيه أقوال : أحدها أن هارون هذا كان رجلا صالحا في بني إسرائيل ينسب إليه كل من عرف بالصلاح عن ابن عباس وقتادة وكعب وابن زيد والمغيرة بن شعبة يرفعه إلى النبي ﷌

"On 'sister of Aaron' several views have been given. One is that this Aaron was a righteous man among the Children of Israel, and everyone known for righteousness was ascribed to him. This is from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Kab, and Ibn Zayd, and al-Mughira ibn Shubah raises it to the Prophet."

- Al-Huwayzi, Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.333

Source: al-Farra (d.207 AH), Ma'ani al-Qur'an, vol.2, p.167

Claim: a brother who does not share her parents

كان لها أخ يقال له هارون من خيار بني إسرائيل ولم يكن من أبويها فقيل : يا أخت هارون في صلاحه . أي إن أخاك صالح وأبواك أبواك

"She had a brother named Aaron, one of the best of the Children of Israel, though he was not from her two parents. So it was said, 'sister of Aaron,' for his righteousness. Your brother is righteous, and your parents are your parents."

- Al-Farra, Ma'ani al-Qur'an, vol.2, p.167

Al-Farra's Harun is a brother who does not share her parents, which is to say the kinship word is already doing lineage work before anyone thought to doubt it. Every reading in the ledger agrees on the one point the charge needs to break. The wicked Harun of al-Qummi, the righteous Harun of forty thousand namesakes, and the line of Aaron the prophet ﵇, agrees on the one point the charge needs to break. The address does not claim a shared womb with the brother of Moses ﵇, and its first hearers did not hear one. And beyond the ledger, the living speech of the school keeps the same grammar. It comes next, and the exchange with Najran follows it.

5. Brothers of Egypt, Azd, and Yemen

The strongest witness to a language's grammar is the speech of the people who grew up inside it. The Four Books of the school are full of the Imams ﵈ addressing strangers and tribesmen with the brother-of construction. No one in the reports ever pauses to explain it, because no one hearing it needed a gloss.

The longest case is a debate. An Egyptian skeptic, a zindiq, a man who denied prophethood, traveled to Makkah to argue with Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, and found him in the tawaf, the ritual walk around the Kaaba. The Imam ﵇ dismantled him question by question, and at every turn he addressed the man by his city.

Source: al-Kafi, vol.1, p.121

Speaker: Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇

Locus: the Egyptian zindiq debates the Imam ﵇ during the tawaf

Claim: a total stranger is addressed as the brother of the people of Egypt

فقال أبو عبد الله ﵇ : أيها الرجل ! ليس لمن لا يعلم حجة على من يعلم ولا حجة للجاهل يا أخا أهل مصر ! تفهم عني فإنا لا نشك في الله أبدا

أما ترى الشمس والقمر والليل والنهار يلجان فلا يشتبهان ويرجعان قد اضطرا ليس لهما مكان إلا مكانهما فإن كانا يقدران على أن يذهبا فلم يرجعان ؟

"Imam al-Sadiq said, 'Man, one who does not know has no argument against one who knows, and the ignorant have no argument. O brother of the people of Egypt, understand me, we never doubt Allah.'"

"'Do you not see the sun and the moon, the night and the day? They enter and never get confused, and they return. They are compelled; they have no place except their own place. If they were able to leave, why would they return?'"

- Al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, vol.1, p.121

The Imam ﵇ had never seen this man before the man laid a hand on his shoulder mid-tawaf. The address names the stranger by his people, brother of the people of Egypt, and the debate ran on with the address repeated again and again down the page. The vocative, the grammar of calling, carries a kinship word and a people's name together as easily as an English speaker says a name alone. And the envoy's trouble at Najran, told in the next section, was never about this grammar. Al-Mughira was stumped by the thousand years, not by the word sister, and the answer the Prophet ﷺ gave him to carry was a fact of custom, not a lesson in Arabic.

Two shorter cases show the address in the field and on the road. The Commander of the Faithful uses it on campaign, and the Imam ﵇ uses it on a visitor from the south.

Source: al-Amili, Wasa'il al-Shia, vol.1, p.418

Speaker: Ali at al-Nahrawan

Claim: a soldier of al-Azd is greeted by his tribe

فإني لجالس إذ ورد علي أمير المؤمنين ﵇ فقال : يا أخا الأزد ، معك طهور ؟ قلت : نعم فناولته الإداوة

"I was sitting when the Commander of the Faithful came to me and said, 'O brother of al-Azd, do you have water for purification?' I said, 'Yes,' and handed him the waterskin."

- Al-Amili, Wasa'il al-Shia, vol.1, p.418

Source: al-Amili, Wasa'il al-Shia, vol.10, p.351

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq

Claim: a visitor from Yemen is greeted by his country

فقال له أبو عبد الله ﵇ : أفلا أزيدك من فضله فضلا يا أخا اليمن ؟ قال : زدني يا ابن رسول الله

"Imam al-Sadiq said to him, 'Shall I not add to you, of His bounty, an increase, O brother of Yemen?' He said, 'Increase it for me, son of the Messenger of Allah.'"

- Al-Amili, Wasa'il al-Shia, vol.10, p.351

Al-Azd is a tribe, and Yemen is a people, and neither man is a sibling of the speaker. A soldier of the Azd behind the lines at Nahrawan is greeted by his kinship to his tribe's ancestor, and a pilgrim from the south is greeted by his country. The Prophet ﷺ himself sets two questioners side by side in one report, each named the same way.

Source: al-Saduq, Man la Yahduruh al-Faqih, vol.2, p.202

Speaker: the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Locus: two men ask about prayer and ablution

Claim: each questioner is addressed by his people

فقال النبي ﷌ : أما أنت يا أخا الأنصار فإنك من قوم يؤثرون على أنفسهم وأنت قروي وهذا الثقفي بدوي أفتؤثره بالمسألة ؟

قال : نعم ، قال : أما أنت يا أخا ثقيف فإنك جئت تسألني عن وضوئك وصلاتك

"The Prophet said, 'As for you, O brother of the Ansar, you are of a people who give others preference over themselves. You are a townsman, and this man of Thaqif is a Bedouin. Will you give him preference in the asking?' He said, 'Yes.' He said, 'As for you, O brother of Thaqif, you have come to ask me about your ablution and your prayer.'"

- Al-Saduq, Man la Yahduruh al-Faqih, vol.2, p.202

One speaker, two addresses, two peoples, neither man a sibling of the Prophet ﷺ. The pattern runs so thick through the reports that the same address runs across the corpus, man after man. And the feminine of the address has its own report, from the Prophet ﷺ himself, in al-Kafi.

A woman of the Ansar came to the Prophet ﷺ and offered herself in marriage, and he answered her with kindness and this address.

Source: al-Kafi (Dar al-Hadith ed.), vol.11, p.312

Speaker: the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Locus: a woman of the Ansar offers herself

Claim: the feminine vocative works exactly as the masculine does

يَا أُخْتَ الْأَنْصَارِ ، جَزَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ خَيْراً ، فَقَدْ نَصَرَنِي رِجَالُكُمْ ، وَرَغَبَتْ فِيَّ نِسَاؤُكُمْ

"O sister of the Ansar, may Allah reward you well on behalf of the Messenger of Allah. Your men have supported me, and your women have inclined to me."

- Al-Kulayni, al-Kafi (Dar al-Hadith ed.), vol.11, p.312

She is a woman of the nation addressed by its name, no sibling in sight, and the feminine vocative works exactly as the masculine does. Brother of Egypt, brother of al-Azd, brother of Thaqif, brother of Yemen, and now sister of the Ansar. The address of 19:28 is this grammar in the feminine, and the reports of the school are full of it. What remains is the exchange in which the verse's own first hearers among the Christians pressed the charge, and the answer they received from the source of the recitation.

6. The Christians of Najran Asked First

The objection is not a product of modern archives. The Christians of Najran, who read their scripture and kept their genealogies, put the very question to the Prophet's ﷺ envoy within his lifetime. The exchange is itself a transmitted report in the books of both communities.

Source: Musnad Ahmad (al-Risala ed.), vol.30, p.141; also Ibn Asakir, Tarikh Dimashq, vol.70, p.98, no.13795; Sahih Muslim, Kitab al-Adab

Speaker: al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba, the envoy to Najran

Claim: the objection was raised in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime and answered by him

بَعَثَنِي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ إِلَى نَجْرَانَ ، قَالَ : فَقَالُوا : أَرَأَيْتَ مَا تَقْرَءُونَ يَا أُخْتَ هَارُونَ وَمُوسَى قَبْلَ عِيسَى بِكَذَا وَكَذَا ؟

قَالَ : فَرَجَعْتُ ، فَذَكَرْتُ ذَلِكَ لِرَسُولِ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ ، فَقَالَ : أَلَا أَخْبَرْتَهُمْ أَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا يُسَمَّوْنَ بِالْأَنْبِيَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ قَبْلَهُمْ

"The Messenger of Allah sent me to Najran. They said, 'Have you considered what you recite, "sister of Aaron," when Moses lived so long before Jesus?' I returned and told the Messenger of Allah, and he said, 'Why did you not tell them, they used to name themselves after the prophets and the righteous before them?'"

- Musnad Ahmad, vol.30, p.141

The report proves the verse was heard and tested within the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime by the people best prepared to break it. It preserves the answer that came back from the source of the recitation in his own words. The answer is the naming custom. The Children of Israel attached their children to their prophets and righteous men, and an address by a saint's name rides that custom. The same report stands in Sahih Muslim's collection of authentic reports, Kitab al-Adab, the strongest grading a Sunni chain carries. The school did not borrow this answer from the Sunni books. It has its own chain.

Source: Ibn Tawus (d.664 AH), Sa'd al-Su'ud, from al-Azdi, from Simak ibn Harb, in Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.333

Chain: a Shia transmission of the same event

Claim: the school keeps the Najran exchange in its own books

في كتاب سعد السعود لابن طاوس من كتاب عبد الرحمن بن محمد الأزدي وحدثني سماك بن حرب عن المغيرة بن شعبة ان النبي ﷌ بعثه إلى نجران فقالوا : ألستم تقرؤن يا أخت هارون وبينهما كذا وكذا ؟

فذكر ذلك للنبي ﷌ فقال : ألا قلت لهم : انهم كانوا يسمون بأنبيائهم والصالحين منهم

"In Sa'd al-Su'ud by Ibn Tawus, from the book of Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad al-Azdi, Simak ibn Harb told me, from al-Mughira ibn Shubah, that the Prophet sent him to Najran. They said, 'Do you recite "sister of Aaron," with all the years between the two?'"

"He told the Prophet, and he said, 'Why did you not say to them, they used to name themselves after their prophets and their righteous men?'"

- Al-Huwayzi, Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.333

Al-Murtada's Amali keeps the exchange in the school's own sitting, with the Prophet's ﷺ rebuke softened into a lesson. When the Messenger of Allah sent al-Mughira to the people of Najran, they said his Prophet claimed Aaron was the brother of Moses ﵇, when Allah knows the years between Moses and Jesus. Al-Mughira did not know what to answer until he returned. The Prophet ﷺ asked him why he had not said that they used to name themselves after their prophets and the righteous before them (al-Murtada, Amali, vol.2, p.197).

Three records of one exchange survive, two in the Sunni books and one in the school's, and all of them preserve the same narrator, Simak ibn Harb, whom the graders of both communities accepted. One event, one chain, kept on both sides. The genealogy question follows, and it ends by helping the verse.

7. Is Maryam of Aaron's Blood

The language case closed on its three legs, the Quran's usage, the Imams' ﵈ recorded speech, and the lexicons with the poets. The blood question has a Quranic frame before it has a Gospel one, and the third surah opens by setting the House of Imran inside the chain of chosen houses.

Verse: Qur'an 3:33-34, Surat Aal Imran

َ

ذُرِّيَّةًۢ بَعْضُهَا مِنۢ بَعْضٍۢ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ

inna llāha iṣṭafā Ādama wa-Nūḥan wa-āla Ibrāhīma wa-āla ʿImrāna ʿalā l-ʿālamīn. Dhurriyyatan baʿḍuhā min baʿḍin, wa-llāhu samīʿun ʿalīm

"Allah chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of Imran above all worlds, an offspring, one of the other. Allah hears and knows all things."

- Qur'an 3:33-34

The exegetes of both communities identify the Quran's Imran with the Amram of the Torah, and the identification is the natural one, the house's own name recurring, for the tradition also knows a nearer Imran. The school reads her election, which the same surah states, as election into that lineage.

8. Why the Crowd Said Sister

The fairest question comes last. The Davidic branch closed with the naming custom, and the naming custom raises the question. If the meaning is descent, why did the crowd not say daughter of Aaron ﵇. The system has three registers, and the choice between them is meaningful.

Aal names the house entire, Aal Imran. Bint names the father's descent, daughter of Imran, which the Quran already spends on her. Ukht names kinship inside the house, and it is the register that reaches the house's saint himself without dragging the intermediate generations into the address. A modern exegete states the logic.

Final Verdict

The charge rests on a single premise, that sister can only name one womb. Every layer of evidence breaks that premise. The Quran itself runs the idiom throughout its pages, the sons of Israel, the daughter of Imran, the House of Imran, the brother of Ad, the brother of Thamud. No reader, including the hostile one, reads those literally. The Imams ﵈ used the same grammar their whole lives. A stranger is greeted as the brother of the people of Egypt, a soldier as the brother of al-Azd, questioners as the brother of the Ansar and the brother of Thaqif. And a woman of the Ansar answered as the sister of the Ansar. The lexicons cite the address as ordinary speech, and the poets carried it before Islam. The Prophet ﷺ, asked by the Christians of Najran in his own lifetime, gave the answer in one line, that they used to name themselves after their prophets and the righteous before them. The school transmits his answer through its own chains.

The genealogy, which looked like the crux, ends by helping. Luke's Gospel makes Maryam's ﵍ kinswoman a daughter of Aaron's ﵇ line. The Quran raises her in the temple under Zakariyya ﵇ and fixes her in the House of Imran, and the Torah names Amram as the father of Aaron ﵇ himself. If her blood is Davidic, as Ibn al-Athir held, the naming custom he himself recites still carries the address, and his report joins the Prophet's ﷺ Najran answer word for word. Both branches of the tradition, and both scriptures, land on the same ground. The address names the house, and it names it accurately.

The reflexive point closes the file. The Gospel's word adelphos covers siblings, kinsmen, and co-religionists inside the New Testament itself, and at Nazareth it names the Lord's kin, James and Joses and Juda and Simon and his sisters (Mark 6:3). The older Christian tradition reads those brothers as kinsmen, and the readers who take them as literal brothers argue it from context, because the word alone cannot carry the weight, which is the whole point. A scripture whose brother-word needs context to mean a womb cannot rule that another scripture's sister-word does. The Greek and the Arabic keep the same wide kinship field, and the charge needs both fields narrow. The verse said sister because sister was true, in the language it was said in, to the woman of Aaron's ﵇ house standing before the assembly with her son in her arms.
