Historical Objection · Debate

The Treason Verdict at Qurayza and the Death of Kinana

The charge that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ massacred a neutral Jewish tribe and tortured a prisoner for gold collapses on the record, because Banu Qurayza joined a siege meant to end the Muslim community and were then judged by an arbiter they themselves chose, under the siege law of their own Torah, while the Kinana report is one contested strand whose own words name a war chest, a lawful retaliation, and an agent other than the Prophet ﷺ. From Majma' al-Bayan, Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, al-Majmu', Ma'alim al-Tanzil, Tafsir Ibn Kathir, al-Taysir, Tafsir Abi al-Su'ud, al-Durar, Bihar al-Anwar, al-Diraya, and Sharh Nahj al-Balagha.

The Argument Under Examination

Background

Two attacks stand together. The longer one holds that in AD 627 the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ committed an atrocity against Banu Qurayza, beheading the men and pubescent boys and enslaving the women and children after a siege in which the Jews never fought, with a number fixed between six hundred and nine hundred dead. The shorter one holds that at Khaibar he had a prisoner named Kinana tortured with fire until he was nearly dead, then beheaded, for the treasure of Banu Nadir. Both charges have real sources behind them, and both hang on what the sources actually say, and the case against them stands on the consent the record carries, on the breach the Qur'an itself names, on the siege law of the tribe's own Torah, and on the Kinana strand's own words, and it rests on five premises.

Premise 1. The Verdict Came From the Judge They Chose

When they asked the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to appoint a man to judge them, he told them to choose whomever they wished from his companions, and they chose Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, an ally of their own Aws tribe. The Aws had asked for one of their own as judge, and the tribe had already agreed to be bound by the ruling before Sa'd gave it. Sa'd drew the line at combatants. Every boy whom the inspection found to have reached puberty died, and every boy below that line lived, and the women and children were taken captive. This is a verdict with a court, a consent, and a line, and the attack erases all three.

Premise 2. The Treason Is Fixed in the Record

Huyayy ibn Akhtab, the exiled chief of Banu Nadir, entered the Qurayza fortress during the siege and pressed their chief Ka'b ibn Asad until he broke the treaty, offering him Quraysh, Ghatafan, and their followers, and promising to share their fate inside the fortress if the confederates withdrew. The Qurayza chiefs told Ka'b that where he went they would go. Qur'an 33:26 names the act. Allah brought down those of the People of the Book who backed the confederates from their fortresses. Backing a besieging army is the treason, and no sally from the walls was ever needed to complete it.

Premise 3. Their Own Law Prescribes the Same Ruling

Deuteronomy 20:12-14 commands Israel, for a city that refuses peace and makes war, to kill every male by the sword and take the women, the children, and the spoil. Sa'd ruled what their own book rules for a treacherous city, and the Prophet ﷺ named the ruling God's judgment. The alternative the attack proposes, expulsion, had already been tried twice. The Qaynuqa were pardoned and released, and the Nadir were expelled, and Huyayy the Nadir exile returned with the confederate coalition and sold the breach inside the fortress. Leniency had produced the siege.

Premise 4. The Kinana Report on Its Own Words

The strand makes Kinana the custodian of the war chest of Banu Nadir, the exiled tribe that had raised the coalitions against Medina, and it makes his death a retaliation for Mahmud ibn Maslama, killed at Khaibar when the defenders threw a millstone on him from the wall. The agent of the death is Muhammad ibn Maslama, the victim's brother. And the fire detail the attack leans on collides with the Prophet's ﷺ own documented ruling, carried by Bukhari, that none punishes with fire but Allah, a ruling he issued by revoking his own earlier order.

Premise 5. The Count Is a Range

The sources carry six hundred to seven hundred, and those who say more say eight hundred to nine hundred, and one school tafsir records the claim of six hundred combatants alongside a count of four hundred and fifty killed and seven hundred and fifty taken. A polemic that needs the number fixed cannot get it fixed from this record.

Conclusion. The Atrocity Charge Holds Neither Event

The Qurayza verdict was a treason verdict by an accepted arbiter under the tribe's own law, after a breach that the Qur'an itself names, and the Kinana death is a single-strand report whose own text names a war chest, a lawful retaliation, and an agent other than the Prophet ﷺ. The prophet of the attacks, a massacrer and a torturer for gold, appears in neither record.

1. The Verdict Came From the Judge They Chose

Sources. Majma' al-Bayan 8:149; al-Majmu' 13:364; Ma'alim al-Tanzil 3:519; Tafsir of al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam 2:96

Court. the arbiter was proposed by the accused, accepted by the Prophet ﷺ, and agreed in advance by both camps

Claim. the executions followed a consented verdict, and the line the verdict drew spared every child the inspection placed below puberty

The Choice the Tribe Itself Made

The attack frames the verdict as one man's decree imposed on a surrendered tribe. The school's own tafsir record opens at a different place. Al-Tabrisi, in Majma' al-Bayan, relays the report of al-Zuhri, the early Medinan scholar, and the report begins with the accused asking for a court.

وقال رسول الله ﵌ حين سألوه أن يحكم فيهم رجلا : اختاروا من شئتم من أصحابي . فاختاروا سعد بن معاذ ، فرضي بذلك رسول الله ﵌ ، فنزلوا على حكم سعد بن معاذ . فأمر رسول الله ﵌ بسلاحهم ، فجعل في قبته ، وأمر بهم فكتفوا وأوثقوا وجعلوا في دار أسامة ، وبعث رسول الله ﵌ إلى سعد بن معاذ فجئ به ، فحكم فيهم بأن يقتل مقاتليهم ، وتسبى ذراريهم ونساؤهم ، وتغنم أموالهم ، وإن عقارهم للمهاجرين دون الأنصار . وقال للأنصار : إنكم ذوو عقار ، وليس للمهاجرين عقار . فكبر رسول الله وقال لسعد : (لقد حكمت فيهم بحكم الله ﷿) . وفي بعض الروايات : (لقد حكمت فيهم بحكم الله من فوق سبعة أرقعة) .

"When they asked the Messenger to appoint one man to judge them, he said, \"Choose anyone you wish from among my companions.\" They chose Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, and the Messenger accepted this. They submitted to Sa'd ibn Mu'adh's judgment. The Messenger ordered their weapons brought and placed in his tent. He ordered them bound securely and kept in Usamah's house. The Messenger sent for Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, and he was brought. Sa'd ruled that their fighters should be killed, their children and women taken captive, and their property taken as spoils. Their land would belong to the Emigrants, apart from the Helpers. He told the Helpers, \"You own land, while the Emigrants own no land.\" The Messenger declared God's greatness and told Sa'd, \"You have judged them by God's judgment.\" Some reports say, \"You have judged them by God's judgment from above the seven heavens.\""

Al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 8, p. 149, relaying al-Zuhri

Three moves stand in the text before any sentence is spoken. The tribe asks for a judge. The Prophet ﷺ gives them the whole body of his companions to choose from. They choose Sa'd, and only then, with the court fixed and consented, does Sa'd rule. The Prophet ﷺ does not author the ruling. He receives it, and he names it God's, from above the seven heavens.

The school's hadith collections carry the same sequence as a report of the school itself.

وروينا أن بني قريظة نزلوا من حصونهم على حكم سعد بن معاذ ، فأمر رسول الله (﵌) بأن يحكم سعد فيهم ، فحكم بأن يقتل مقاتليهم ويسبي ذراريهم ، فقال رسول الله (صلى الله عليه وآله) لسعد : (لقد حكمت بحكم الله من فوق سبعة أرقعة) .

"We have reported that Banu Qurayzah came down from their forts and accepted Sa'd ibn Mu'adh's judgment. The Messenger ordered Sa'd to judge them. Sa'd ruled that their fighters should be killed and their children taken captive. The Messenger told Sa'd, \"You have judged by God's judgment from above the seven heavens.\""

Al-Nuri al-Tabarsi, Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol. 11, p. 128, hadith 12621

So the consented arbitration is not a Sunni story the school tolerates. The school's own Mustadrak transmits it. A verdict the accused asked for, from a judge the accused picked, stands in law whatever a modern reader feels about its severity.

What Sa'd Ruled and Where the Line Fell

The attack calls the verdict the slaughter of men and boys, as though no line existed. The line is the oldest layer of the record, and the jurists preserved it precisely because it did the legal work.

حكم سعد بن معاذ ﵁ في بني قريظة فحكم بسبي ذراريهم ونسائهم ، وقسم أموالهم . وقتل من جرت عليه الموسى . فأمر أن يكشف عن مؤتزريهم فمن أنبت منهم فهو من المقاتلة ، ومن لم ينبت فهو من الذراري : فبلغ ذلك النبي ﵌ فقال : لقد حكمت فيهم بحكم الله من فوق سبعه أرقعة .

"Sa'd ibn Mu'adh judged Banu Qurayzah. He ruled that their children and women should be taken captive and that their property should be divided. He ordered the killing of those who had reached maturity. He ordered their waistcloths removed for inspection. Those with pubic hair were counted among the fighters. Those without it were counted among the children. When this reached the Prophet, he said, \"You have judged them by God's judgment from above the seven heavens.\""

Al-Nawawi, al-Majmu', vol. 13, p. 364

The verdict sorts the tribe by combat status, not by existence. The inspection is a military tribunal's age line, and the criterion is the one the jurists of every school used for majority. Atiyyah al-Qurazi, the boy the attack's own sources remember surviving that inspection, walked out of the trench alive, and so did every boy the line placed below puberty.

The verdict also draws a second line the attack never mentions. The land went to the Emigrants and not to the Helpers, because the Helpers, the Aws and Khazraj, already held land. The spoil was distributed, not kept. A greed reading has to explain why the verdict hands the property to the whole body of the poor emigrants and cuts the arbiter's own tribe out of the largest share.

The Word the Aws Trusted

Where did the idea of Sa'd as judge come from? The Sunni tafsir record answers with a scene between the Prophet ﷺ and the Aws, the Arab tribe allied to Qurayza from before Islam.

فلما كلمه الأوس قال رسول الله ﵌ إلا ترضون يا معشر الأوس أن يحكم فيكم رجل منكم قالوا بلى قال فذاك إلى سعد بن معاذ

"When the Aws spoke to him, the Messenger asked, \"People of Aws, will you accept the judgment of one of your own men?\" They answered, \"Yes.\" The Messenger said, \"Then Sa'd ibn Mu'adh will decide.\""

Al-Baghawi, Ma'alim al-Tanzil, vol. 3, p. 519

The Aws came to their ally's side of the case, and the Prophet ﷺ answered them with a judge from their own house. The arbiter was the accused party's own camp's proposal, and the jurist al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam, reading the verse the event belongs to, states the consent in legal terms.

بنو قريظة وكان بينهم وبين الرسول صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم عهد فنقضوه ، والمظاهرة : المعاونة ، فغزاهم الرسول صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم بعد ستة عشر يوما من الخندق فحصرهم إحدى وعشرين ليلة فنزلوا على التحكيم في أنفسهم وأموالهم فحكموا سعدا فحكم بقتل مقاتلتهم وبسبي ذراريهم وأن عقارهم للمهاجرين دون الأنصار فكبر الرسول صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم وقال : « قضى فيهم بحكم الله »

"Banu Qurayzah had a treaty with the Messenger, and they broke it. Supporting means giving help. Sixteen days after the Battle of the Trench, the Messenger marched against them. He besieged them for twenty-one nights. They accepted arbitration over themselves and their property. They appointed Sa'd as judge. Sa'd ruled that their fighters should be killed, their children taken captive, and their land given to the Emigrants apart from the Helpers. The Messenger declared God's greatness and said, \"He has decided among them by God's judgment.\""

Al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam, Tafsir, vol. 2, p. 96, on Qur'an 33:26

The jurist fixes the sequence the attack inverts. The treaty existed. The tribe broke it. The tribe then came down to arbitration over themselves and their property, and appointed Sa'd themselves. The verdict stands on that stack, consent first, ruling last. Whether the number killed was large is the next question, and the record answers it with a range the attack flattens.

How Many Men Died

The attack works with a fixed count, six hundred to nine hundred men and boys, and reads the highest figure as the fact. The strands do not agree, and they say so on their own pages.

وأخرجوا إليهم أرسالا فضربت أعناقهم ، وكانوا ما بين الستمائة إلى السبعمائة ، والمكثر يقول : من الثمانمائة إلى التسعمائة

"Groups were brought out to them, and their necks were struck. Their number was between six hundred and seven hundred. Those who gave the higher estimate said it was between eight hundred and nine hundred."

Al-Ras'ani, Rumuz al-Kunuz, vol. 6, p. 138

فقتل رسول الله ﵌ مقاتليهم ، وكانوا فيما زعموا ست مائة مقاتل . وقيل : قتل منهم أربع مائة وخمسين رجلا ، وسبى سبعمائة وخمسين .

"The Messenger killed their fighters. As was claimed, they were six hundred fighters. Another report says four hundred and fifty men of them were killed, and seven hundred and fifty people were taken captive."

Al-Tabrisi, Majma' al-Bayan, vol. 8, p. 149, the count

Six hundred to seven hundred, or eight hundred to nine hundred for those who say more. A school tafsir carries the six hundred as a claim it distances itself from, beside a count of four hundred and fifty killed and seven hundred and fifty captive. Al-Tabrisi's own hedge, as was claimed, is the record's verdict on its own arithmetic. The count is a range inside one narrative family, and a polemic that needs one number from this record is asking the sources for what they refuse to give.

2. The Treason Is Fixed in the Record

Sources. Tafsir Ibn Kathir 3:486; al-Taysir fi al-Tafsir 6:76; Qur'an 33:26; Qur'an 33:25

Act. joining the confederate siege of Medina, a siege the sources describe as aimed at ending the community

Claim. the breach is named by the parley report, the council report, and the Qur'an, and the absence of a pitched battle never touched it

The Envoy Who Came to the Fortress

The attack's central doubt is that the Jews never fought, so no treason occurred. The record answers before any courtroom is opened. The treaty existed, and the breach had a broker with a name.

نقضوا ما كان بينهم وبين رسول الله ﵌ من العهد وكان ذلك بسفارة حيي بن أخطب النضري لعنه الله دخل حصنهم ولم يزل بسيدهم كعب بن أسد حتى نقض العهد وقال له فيما قال ويحك قد جئتك بعز الدهر أتيتك بقريش وأحابيشها وغطفان وأتباعها ولا يزالون ههنا حتى يستأصلوا محمدا وأصحابه فقال له كعب بل والله أتيتني بذل الدهر ويحك يا حيي إنك مشئوم فدعنا منك فلم يزل يفتل في الذروة والغارب حتى أجابه واشترط له حيي إن ذهب الأحزاب ولم يكن من أمرهم شئ أن يدخل معهم في الحصن فيكون له أسوتهم فلما نقضت قريظة وبلغ ذلك رسول الله ﵌ ساءه وشق عليه وعلى المسلمين جدا

"They broke the treaty between them and the Messenger. Huyayy ibn Akhtab al-Nadiri served as the envoy who led to this. May God curse him. He entered their fort and kept pressing their leader, Ka'b ibn Asad, until he broke the treaty. Huyayy told him, \"Woe to you. I have brought you the strength of this age. I have brought you Quraysh, their allied groups, Ghatafan, and their followers. They will remain here until they wipe out Muhammad and his companions.\" Ka'b answered, \"By God, you have brought me the humiliation of this age. Woe to you, Huyayy. You bring misfortune. Leave us alone.\" Huyayy kept urging him until Ka'b agreed. Huyayy made one condition. If the allied forces left without achieving anything, he would enter the fort with them and share their fate. When Qurayzah broke the treaty, the news reached the Messenger. It deeply distressed him and the Muslims."

Tafsir Ibn Kathir, vol. 3, p. 486, on the Trench verses

The parley names the offer, the aim, and the resistance. Ka'b called Huyayy a misfortune and told him to leave, and Huyayy worked on him until he agreed. The aim is stated in the envoy's own words, to wipe out Muhammad and his companions. A tribe that opens its fortress to the annihilationist coalition from inside the city's rear has committed the treason, whether or not its warriors ever marched out. High treason is joining the enemy, and in a city under siege, joining is an act.

The Council Inside the Fortress

The school's own tafsir corpus carries the same scene from the inside, in a Shia work that relays the sira of the Prophet ﷺ through the school's chains.

فقال لهم كعب : ما ترون ؟ قالوا : أنت سيدنا ، والمطاع فينا ، وصاحب عهدنا وعقدنا ، فإن نقضت نقضنا ، وإن أقمت أقمنا معك ، وإن خرجت خرجنا معك .

"Ka'b asked them, \"What do you think?\" They answered, \"You are our leader, the one we obey among us, and the keeper of our treaty and pact. If you break it, we will break it. If you keep it, we will keep it with you. If you go out, we will go out with you.\""

Al-Zubaydi, al-Taysir fi al-Tafsir li-ruwayat Ahl al-Bayt, vol. 6, p. 76

The chiefs of the fortress put the decision in Ka'b's hands with their own voices, and the same page carries the moment the decision met its refuser. The blind elder Zubayr ibn Bata read the Torah's description of the coming prophet and warned the fortress not to fight him, and Huyayy answered for the breach.

فقال حيي : ليس هذا ذاك ، ذاك النبي من بني إسرائيل ، وهذا من العرب ، من ولد إسماعيل ، ولا يكون بنو إسرائيل أتباعا لولد إسماعيل أبدا

"Huyayy said, \"This is not that one. That prophet was from the Children of Israel. This one is an Arab, descended from Ishmael. The Children of Israel will never follow a descendant of Ishmael.\""

Al-Zubaydi, al-Taysir fi al-Tafsir, vol. 6, p. 76, the answer of Huyayy

Huyayy's answer is the refusal of a revealed recognition, and it is ethnic pride spoken aloud. The fortress had a man who read his own scripture and identified the hour, and the exile who came from the besiegers' camp overruled him. The treason was not drift or panic. In the school's own relay it is a decision, taken with open eyes against the warning.

What the Qur'an Says They Did

The Qur'an enters the event one verse after the Trench's deliverance, and the school's exegetes gloss the verb in legal terms.

وَأَنْزَلَ الَّذِينَ ظاهَرُوهُمْ مِنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتابِ مِنْ صَياصِيهِمْ وَقَذَفَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الرُّعْبَ فَرِيقاً تَقْتُلُونَ وَتَأْسِرُونَ فَرِيقاً

wa-anzala alladhīna ẓāharūhum min ahl al-kitābi min ṣayāṣīhim wa-qadhafa fī qulūbihimu ar-ruʿba farīqan taqtulūna wa-ta'sirūna farīqā

"He brought down those People of the Book who supported them from their strongholds. He cast terror into their hearts. You killed one group and took another captive."

Qur'an 33:26

«وأنزل الذين ظاهروهم» أي عاونوا الأحزاب المردودة «من أهل الكتاب» وهم بنو قريظة «من صياصيهم» من حصونهم

"His words \"and He brought down those who backed them\" mean those who aided the confederates after they were turned back. \"Of the People of the Book\" means Banu Qurayzah. \"From their strongholds\" means from their fortresses."

Abu al-Su'ud, Tafsir, vol. 7, p. 100, on Qur'an 33:26

The verb is backing, and the gloss fixes it as aid to the confederate army. The verse does not say they fought a battle, and it does not need to. It convicts the act the record names, the inner tribe joining the outer siege. The two-part outcome the verse records, a group killed and a group captured, is the shape of Sa'd's ruling on combatants and dependents, and the verse attributes the whole event to God's act, from the fortresses down.

The Battle Allah Spared the Believers

The attack reads Qur'an 33:25 as a confession that nothing happened at all, so the Qurayza could not have been dangerous. The verse says what it says, and the school's oldest reading of it names the instrument of the deliverance.

ورد الله الذين كفروا بغيظهم لم ينالوا خيرا وكفى الله المؤمنين القتال

wa-radda Allāhu alladhīna kafarū bighayżihim lam yanālū khayran wa-kafā Allāhu al-mu'minīna al-qitāl

"God sent back those who disbelieved in their rage. They gained no good. God spared the believers from fighting."

Qur'an 33:25

وأخرج ابن أبي حاتم وابن مردويه وابن عساكر عن ابن مسعود (﵁) أنه كان يقرأ هذا الحرف (وكفى الله المؤمنين القتال) بعلي بن أبي طالب

"Ibn Mas'ud used to read this passage as \"Allah spared the believers the fighting through Ali ibn Abi Talib.\""

Ibn Mas'ud's reading, in Fi Rihab Ahl al-Bayt, vol. 36, p. 68, relaying al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur

Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, the companion reader of Kufa, read the sparing of battle as God sparing the believers through Ali ﵇, whose duel at the trench turned back Amr ibn Abd Wudd and broke the coalition's will to cross. Deliverance by a champion and a storm is still deliverance, and the siege it ended was ten thousand men encamped around a city of three thousand with an armed tribe at its rear. The verse celebrates a rescue from a real danger, and the danger's rear guard was Qurayza. A spared battle convicts the besiegers of failure, and the fortress that joined them of joining.

3. Their Own Law Prescribes the Same Ruling

Sources. Deuteronomy 20:12-14; Ma'alim al-Tanzil 3:519; Tafsir Ibn Kathir 3:486

Comparison. the verdict's three parts against the Torah's siege law for a city that refuses peace

Claim. Sa'd ruled what the tribe's own scripture rules, and the leniency the attack demands had already been tried and had already produced the siege

What Deuteronomy Commands for a Faithless City

The attack calls the Deuteronomy parallel a misapplied defense, a command for one time and place that no later ruler may wield. The parallel is closer than that. Sa'd was not a Muslim legislator borrowing Israel's law. He was the arbiter of a Jewish tribe, ruling on a Jewish tribe's treason, and the law he applied was theirs.

וְאִם־לֹ֤א תַשְׁלִים֙ עִמָּ֔ךְ וְעָשְׂתָ֥ה עִמְּךָ֖ מִלְחָמָ֑ה וְצַרְתָּ֖ עָלֶֽיהָ׃

וּנְתָנָ֛הּ יְהוָ֥ה אֱלֹהֶ֖יךָ בְּיָדֶ֑ךָ וְהִכִּיתָ֥ אֶת־כָּל־זְכוּרָ֖הּ לְפִי־חָֽרֶב׃

רַ֣ק הַ֠נָּשִׁים וְהַטַּ֨ף וְהַבְּהֵמָ֜ה וְכֹל֩ אֲשֶׁ֨ר יִהְיֶ֥ה בָעִ֛יר כָּל־שְׁלָלָ֖הּ תָּבֹ֣ז לָ֑ךְ וְאָֽכַלְתָּ֙ אֶת־שְׁלַ֣ל אֹיְבֶ֔יךָ אֲשֶׁ֥ר נָתַ֛ן יְהוָ֥ה אֱלֹהֶ֖יךָ לָֽךְ׃

وَإِنْ لَمْ تُسَالِمْكَ، بَلْ عَمِلَتْ مَعَكَ حَرْبًا، فَحَاصِرْهَا.

وَإِذَا دَفَعَهَا الرَّبُّ إِلهُكَ إِلَى يَدِكَ فَاضْرِبْ جَمِيعَ ذُكُورِهَا بِحَدِّ السَّيْفِ.

وَأَمَّا النِّسَاءُ وَالأَطْفَالُ وَالْبَهَائِمُ وَكُلُّ مَا فِي الْمَدِينَةِ، كُلُّ غَنِيمَتِهَا، فَتَغْتَنِمُهَا لِنَفْسِكَ، وَتَأْكُلُ غَنِيمَةَ أَعْدَائِكَ الَّتِي أَعْطَاكَ الرَّبُّ إِلهُكَ.

"And if it will make no peace with thee, but will make war against thee, then thou shalt besiege it."

"And when the LORD thy God hath delivered it into thine hands, thou shalt smite every male thereof with the edge of the sword."

"But the women, and the little ones, and the cattle, and all that is in the city, even all the spoil thereof, shalt thou take unto thyself; and thou shalt eat the spoil of thine enemies, which the LORD thy God hath given thee."

Deuteronomy 20:12-14, Westminster Leningrad Codex, Van Dyck Arabic Bible (1865), King James Version

Set the three parts beside Sa'd's ruling. A city that refuses peace and makes war is besieged. Qurayza refused the covenant's peace and joined the war made on Medina. Every male is killed by the sword. Sa'd's line killed the combatants and spared the boys below the age line, which is milder than the command on the page. The women, the children, and the spoil are taken. Sa'd's ruling took the dependents captive and divided the property among the community, with the land going to the landless Emigrants. The verdict tracks the tribe's own scripture for the case the tribe itself created, and the Prophet ﷺ called it God's judgment because it matched the rule God had already legislated for the category.

The attack's reply, that the command belonged to one era and cannot travel, argues against the wrong party. The school does not need Deuteronomy to travel. The arbitrating tribe carried it, and its arbiter, their own choice from their own allies, applied their own book to their own case.

Why Expulsion Had Already Failed Twice

The attack asks why the Prophet ﷺ did not expel Qurayza, as he had expelled the two tribes before. The record answers with the results of those two leniencies.

وقد كان رسول الله ﵌ قبل بني قريظة حاصر بني قنيقاع وكانوا حلفاء الخزرج فنزلوا على حكمه فسألهم إياه عبد الله بن أبي بن سلون فوهبهم إياه

"Before the affair of Banu Qurayzah, the Messenger had besieged Banu Qaynuqa. They were allies of the Khazraj. They submitted to his judgment. Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul asked him to spare them, and he granted them to him."

Al-Baghawi, Ma'alim al-Tanzil, vol. 3, p. 519, the earlier tribe

The Qaynuqa had been besieged, surrendered to the Prophet's ﷺ judgment, and walked away pardoned at a chieftain's request. The Nadir were expelled the next year. Then the Trench came, ten thousand strong, and the man who moved inside the Qurayza fortress to break the treaty was Huyayy ibn Akhtab, the exiled Nadir chief the expulsion had released. The expelled tribes did not disappear into the desert. They went to Khaibar and to Quraysh and returned at the head of the coalition, and Khaibar, the fortress city of the exiles, became the next war. Expelling a fourth time meant arming a fifth coalition with another fortress tribe inside Medina's rear. The verdict the Aws accepted and the tribe consented to was the answer to a leniency that had already been tested twice and had returned both times as a siege. The mercy the attack demands as the obvious option was the policy that produced the battle it calls the Trench.

4. The Kinana Report on Its Own Words

Sources. al-Kashf wa-l-Bayan 9:48; al-Durar, p. 172; Bihar al-Anwar 21:36; al-Diraya 2:120; Sharh Nahj al-Balagha 14:194; Wasa'il al-Shi'a 27:112

Strand. one narrative family, relayed by the tafsir corpus and by Bihar, resting on the sira of Ibn Ishaq

Claim. the strand's own text names a war chest, a retaliation, and an agent other than the Prophet ﷺ, and its fire detail collides with a ruling Bukhari carries

What the Single Strand Says

The Kinana charge rests on one page of the sira, and the tafsir corpus relays the page in full. The honesty this case needs is to quote the whole strand, because the attack quotes only its middle.

وأتى رسول الله بزوجها كنانة بن الربيع بن أبي الحقيق ، وكان عنده كنز بني النضير ، فسأله ، فجحده أن يكون يعلم مكانه ، فأتى رسول الله برجل من اليهود ، فقال لرسول الله (﵇) : إنّي قد رأيت كنانة يطيف هذه الخزنة كلّ غداة ، فقال رسول الله لكنانة : (أرأيت إن وجدناه عندك أقتلك) . قال : نعم . فأمر رسول الله ﵌ بالخزنة ، فحفرت ، فأخرج منها بعض كنزهم ، ثمّ سأله ما بقي ، فأبى أن يؤدّيه ، فأمر به رسول الله الزبير بن العوّام . فقال : (عذّبه حتّى تستأصل ما عنده) . فكان الزبير يقدح بزنده في صدره حتّى أشرف على نفسه ، ثمّ دفعه رسول الله إلى محمّد ابن مسلمة ، فضرب عنقه بأخيه محمود بن مسلمة ، وكانت اليهود ألقت عليه حجراً عند حصن ناعم ، فقتله ، كان أوّل حصن افتتح من حصون خيبر .

"The Messenger brought her husband, Kinanah ibn al-Rabi ibn Abi al-Huqayq, before him. The treasure of Banu al-Nadir was in his keeping. The Messenger asked him about it, and Kinanah denied knowing where it was. Then a Jewish man came before the Messenger and said, \"I saw Kinanah circling this storehouse every morning.\" The Messenger asked Kinanah, \"If we find it with you, shall I kill you?\" Kinanah answered, \"Yes.\" The Messenger ordered the storehouse to be dug up. Part of their treasure was taken out. Then he asked Kinanah about what remained, but Kinanah refused to hand it over. The Messenger handed him to al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam and said, \"Torture him until you draw out what he has.\" Al-Zubayr struck a firesteel against his chest until Kinanah was close to death. Then the Messenger handed him to Muhammad ibn Maslamah, who struck off his head in revenge for his brother Mahmud ibn Maslamah. The Jews had thrown a stone at Mahmud near the fortress of Naim, and it killed him. Naim was the first fortress captured from the fortresses of Khaybar."

Al-Tha'labi, al-Kashf wa-l-Bayan, vol. 9, p. 48, the Khaibar strand

Read the strand against the charge built on it. The gold the attack calls a private hoard is, on the strand's own words, the treasure of Banu Nadir, the war chest of the exiled tribe that had raised and armed the coalitions against Medina. Kinana is not a farmer with savings. He is the Nadir chief's family's custodian, married into the house, holding the military funds of the polity at war. Seizing an enemy's war chest is an act of war, and concealing it in a beaten enemy's keeping is what the interrogation addresses. The strand's own ending removes the murder charge. The agent of the death is Muhammad ibn Maslama, and the stated ground is his brother. The school's Bihar relays the same ending.

صدره حتى أشرف على نفسه ، ثم دفعه رسول الله ﵌ إلى محمد بن مسلمة فضرب عنقه بأخيه محمود بن مسلمة .

"He struck his chest until he was close to death. Then the Messenger handed him to Muhammad ibn Maslamah, who struck off his head in revenge for his brother Mahmud ibn Maslamah."

Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 21, p. 36, the same strand's ending

The school's corpus carries the strand, and the honest reading of it is the one the strand gives itself. A retaliation, carried by the blood brother, for a killing the strand names.

The Death That Made It Retaliation

The strand's last sentence is the foundation the attack never lays. Mahmud ibn Maslama died at Khaibar before his brother's revenge, and the record of how he died is fixed across the corpus.

فكان أول حصن افتتحوه حصنا يسمى ناعما وعنده قتل محمود بن مسلمة أخو محمد بن مسلمة ألقيت عليه رحى فشدخته ﵀ ثم حصنا يدعى القموص وهو حصن بني أبي الحقيق ومن سبايا ذلك الحصن كانت صفية بنت حيي بن أخطب وكانت تحت كنانة بن الربيع بن أبي الحقيق

"The first fortress they captured was called Naim. There Mahmud ibn Maslamah, the brother of Muhammad ibn Maslamah, was killed when a millstone was thrown onto him and crushed his head. Then they captured a fortress called al-Qamus, the fortress of the Banu Abi al-Huqayq. Among the captives from that fortress was Safiyyah bint Huyayy ibn Akhtab. She had been married to Kinanah ibn al-Rabi ibn Abi al-Huqayq."

Ibn Abd al-Barr, al-Durar, p. 172, the first fortress

The defenders of the fortress threw a millstone from the wall and crushed a Muslim warrior's head, and the retaliation the law of every nation of that age recognized fell on the enemy leader taken at that fortress. The same page ties the whole affair to the Nadir house. The fortress al-Qamus belonged to the Banu Abi al-Huqayq, the Nadir family, and Kinana of that house was the husband of Huyayy's daughter. The treasure custodian, the fortress, and the broker of the Qurayza breach are one extended house, the house the expulsion had released and the Trench had brought back. The qisas reading is not an apologist's construction laid over the text. It is the reason the strand itself states, in its final sentence, before any medieval or modern reader touched it.

The Fire the Prophet Himself Banned

The torture detail stands on the same single strand, and it meets a documented ruling of the Prophet ﷺ that the attackers' own most trusted collection carries.

منها حديث أبي هريرة إن وجدتم فلانا وفلانا فاقتلوهما ولا تحرقوهما فإنه لا يعذب بها إلا الله تعالى أخرجه البخاري والبزار وسماهما هبار بن الأسود ونافع بن عبد قيس وكانا قد نخسا بزينب بنت رسول الله ﵌

"Abu Hurayrah said, \"If you find so-and-so and so-and-so, kill them. Do not burn them, for none punishes with fire except Allah Most High.\" Al-Bukhari and al-Bazzar carried it, and they named the two. They were Habar ibn al-Aswad and Nafi ibn Abd Qays, who had goaded Zaynab, the daughter of the Messenger."

Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Diraya fi takhrij ahadith al-Hidaya, vol. 2, p. 120

The two men had attacked the Prophet's ﷺ own daughter and caused the death of her unborn child. He first ordered them burned, and then he revoked his own order, because punishment by fire belongs to Allah alone. The full wording stands in Ibn Abi al-Hadid's relay.

بعث رسول الله ﵌ سرية أنا فيها إلى عير لقريش ، فيها متاع لهم وناس منهم ، فقال : إن ظفرتم بهبار بن الأسود ونافع بن عبد قيس ، فحرقوهما بالنار ، حتى إذا كان الغد بعث فقال لنا : (إني كنت قد أمرتكم بتحريق الرجلين إن أخذتموهما ، ثم رأيت أنه لا ينبغي لأحد أن يعذب بالنار إلا الله تعالى ، فإن ظفرتم بهما فاقتلوهما ولا تحرقوهما)

"The Messenger sent a detachment to a Quraysh caravan carrying their goods and some of their people. I was among them. He said, \"If you capture Habar ibn al-Aswad and Nafi ibn Abd Qays, burn them with fire.\" The next day, he sent for us and said, \"I had ordered you to burn the two men if you captured them. Then I saw that no one should punish with fire except Allah Most High. If you capture them, kill them and do not burn them.\""

Ibn Abi al-Hadid, Sharh Nahj al-Balagha, vol. 14, p. 194

That is the Prophet's ﷺ documented sunna on punishment by fire, issued in the strongest case a father could face, and carried by Bukhari. The Kinana fire detail belongs to one strand of the maghazi genre, the campaign narratives, a genre the hadith scholars never classed with the sifted collections. A report of that class cannot carry the man's sunna against the collection every attacker cites as authority. The strand keeps its kernel, a war chest seized and a retaliation executed by the victim's brother. The fire detail falls at the bar of the Prophet's ﷺ own ruling, and no strand overrides a Book and a sunna the whole community transmits.

The Rule That Judges Every Report

The Imams ﵈ fixed the procedure for exactly this class of case, a report that attributes a wrong to a prophet.

وعنه عن أحمد ، عن علي بن الحكم ، عن عبد الله بن بكير ، عن رجل ، عن أبي جعفر (﵇) - في حديث - قال : إذا جاءكم عنا حديث فوجدتم عليه شاهدا ، أو شاهدين من كتاب الله فخذوا به ، وإلا فقفوا عنده ، ثم ردوه إلينا ، حتى يستبين لكم .

"Abu Jafar said, \"When a report from us reaches you, and you find one or two witnesses for it in the Book of Allah, accept it. Otherwise, hold back from it and return it to us until its meaning becomes clear to you.\""

Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇, reported through Abdullah ibn Bukayr, al-Hurr al-Amili, Wasa'il al-Shi'a (Ahl al-Bayt ed.), vol. 27, p. 112, hadith 33351

A report needs a witness in the Book of Allah before it binds. The Book fixes the Prophet's ﷺ speech as revelation and not desire (Qur'an 53:3-4), and it forbids the believer to transgress the limits Allah set (Qur'an 4:14). A maghazi strand showing the Prophet ﷺ ordering fire on a prisoner's chest for gold finds no witness in that Book. The strand's salvageable kernel, the war chest and the retaliation, stands. The charge the attack builds, a prophet torturing for money, is the gloss of a polemic laid over a text whose own words say otherwise.

Final Verdict

The atrocity charge against Qurayza and the torture charge against Khaibar fail on the sources the attacks themselves lean on.

First, the Qurayza verdict was a verdict. The tribe asked for a judge, the Prophet ﷺ opened his whole body of companions to their choice, and they chose Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, the arbiter their own Aws allies had proposed. The tribe had agreed in advance to be bound, and Sa'd's ruling drew the line at combat status, and every child the inspection placed below puberty lived. The consent is in the Sunni tafsir record, in the jurists, and in the school's own Mustadrak.

Second, the treason is fixed. Huyayy ibn Akhtab, the Nadir exile, entered the fortress and pressed Ka'b into breaking the treaty, with the annihilation of the Muslims stated as the aim, and the chiefs put the decision in Ka'b's hands with their own voices. Qur'an 33:26 names the act, the People of the Book who backed the confederates, and the school's gloss fixes the verb as aid to the besieging army. Joining the siege is the crime, and no pitched battle was ever required to complete it, while the battle Qur'an 33:25 says Allah spared was the deliverance the school reads through Ali's ﵇ duel.

Third, the ruling was their own law. Deuteronomy 20:12-14 commands a besieged faithless city's males killed and its women, children, and spoil taken, and Sa'd ruled inside those bounds and milder than them, sparing the prepubescent and dividing the spoil among the community. The leniency the attack demands had been tried twice. The pardoned Qaynuqa and the expelled Nadir returned as the confederate coalition's brokers and strongholds, and Huyayy the exile was inside the fortress selling the breach. Expelling a third treacherous tribe meant gifting the next coalition another rear guard inside Medina.

Fourth, the count is a range. Six hundred to seven hundred, or eight hundred to nine hundred for the higher sayings, with a school tafsir recording six hundred as a claim it hedges and four hundred and fifty killed beside seven hundred and fifty taken. The record refuses the fixed number the atrocity arithmetic needs.

Fifth, the Kinana report convicts the charge, not the Prophet ﷺ. The strand's own words name the gold as the war chest of Banu Nadir in the custody of the exiled house, name the death as Muhammad ibn Maslama's retaliation for his brother Mahmud, crushed under a millstone at the fortress wall, and the strand is one narrative of the maghazi class. Its fire detail meets the Prophet's ﷺ own revocation, carried by Bukhari, that none punishes with fire but Allah, and the Imams' ﵈ rule sends every such report to the Book for a witness it does not find.

The men of Qurayza died under a consented treason verdict that tracked their own scripture, and Kinana died, on the report's own words, by a brother's lawful retaliation at war. The prophet of the attacks, a massacrer and a torturer for gold, appears in neither record. He was assembled from a flattened count, a dropped consent, and a gloss laid over one strand of the campaign literature.
