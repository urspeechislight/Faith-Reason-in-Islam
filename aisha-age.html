Historical Objection · Debate

How Old Was Aisha When She Married the Prophet

The claim that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ consummated his marriage to Aisha at age nine rests on reports that the standard chronology the same tradition preserves contradicts at every point, on a single narrator standing at the root of every number, and on a dolls argument the sources themselves date years after the claimed wedding. From Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Sa'd, Ibn Hajar, al-Dhahabi, al-Muzaffar's Dala'il al-Sidq, and the Mishnah and Talmud the attack itself cites.

The Argument Under Examination

Background

The attack states its case in one sentence. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ married Aisha when she was six and consummated the marriage when she was nine, before her first menses. And a true prophet could not have done this. The sentence has real sources behind it. Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, and Ibn Majah all carry Aisha's own account, and the biographical literature repeats it. Everything else in the charge, the claims about Islamic law, the medical appendices, the psychological reading of Aisha's later life, hangs from that one sentence. The case against the charge stands on the same tradition's own books, and it rests on four premises.

Premise 1: The Sister's Arithmetic Fixes the Birth Year

The reports giving Aisha's age as six at betrothal and nine at consummation contradict the birth and death dates of her elder sister Asma. The Sunni tradition itself transmits those dates through two independent lines and confirms them arithmetically. Asma was born twenty-seven years before the Hijra and died at one hundred in 73 AH. Asma was ten years older than Aisha. Those numbers fix Aisha's birth about seventeen years before the Hijra and put her near eighteen at consummation, seven months into 1 AH.

Premise 2: One Narrator Behind Every Number

Every version of the six-and-nine report, in Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, and Ibn Majah alike, runs through one narrator, Hisham ibn Urwa. He reports from his father Urwa, who reports from Aisha. Hisham transmitted these reports in Iraq late in his life. The collections do not agree among themselves on the number, six or seven, or on where the consummation happened. Several collections in one voice are one voice counted several times.

Premise 3: The Dolls Date the Marriage Later

The claim that Aisha was prepubescent rests on her playing with dolls after marriage. The doll reports the attack cites are themselves dated by their own words to the return from Tabuk or Hunayn. Those campaigns belong to 7-9 AH, years after the claimed wedding. On the attack's own arithmetic the dolls make her a married woman of her mid-teens still playing, or the reports contradict each other. The school's own commentator al-Muzaffar draws exactly this conclusion from the same texts.

Premise 4: The Jewish Law Comparison Collapses

The moral charge leans on a comparison with Jewish law, which the attack says set a higher minimum age. The Mishnah and Talmud the attack's own appendix points to legislate for betrothal by intercourse from three years and one day. The Talmud records a Roman empress married at six and a mother at seven. A standard that convicts the Prophet ﷺ convicts the sources the attack appeals to.

Conclusion: Nine Is Not Established

The six-and-nine age fails on chronology, fails on transmission, and fails on its own supporting evidence. The certain result is that nine is not established. The best-supported reading of the tradition's own numbers puts the consummation near eighteen.

1. The Charge the Sources Are Asked to Carry

What the Charge Claims

The attack states its case in one sentence. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ married Aisha when she was six and consummated the marriage when she was nine, before her first menses. And a true prophet could not have done this. Everything else in the charge, the claims about Islamic law, the medical appendices, the psychological reading of Aisha's later life, hangs from that one sentence.

The sentence has real sources behind it. No Muslim writer should pretend otherwise. Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, and Ibn Majah all carry Aisha's own account, and the biographical literature repeats it.

Aisha's Own Account

The fullest version of Aisha's account, in Sunan Abi Dawud, carries the whole scene, the contract, the swing, and the consummation, in her own words.

(4933) - حَدَّثَنَا مُوسَى بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ ، حَدَّثَنَا حَمَّادٌ ح وَحَدَّثَنَا بِشْرُ بْنُ خَالِدٍ ، حَدَّثَنَا أَبُو أُسَامَةَ ، قَالَا : حَدَّثَنَا هِشَامُ بْنُ عُرْوَةَ ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ ، عَنْ عَائِشَةَ ، قَالَتْ : إِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ تَزَوَّجَنِي وَأَنَا بِنْتُ سَبْعٍ أَوْ سِتٍّ ، فَلَمَّا قَدِمْنَا الْمَدِينَةَ أَتَيْنَ نِسْوَةٌ ، وَقَالَ بِشْرٌ : فَأَتَتْنِي أُمُّ رُومَانَ وَأَنَا عَلَى أُرْجُوحَةٍ ، فَذَهَبْنَ بِي وَهَيَّأْنَنِي وَصَنَعْنَنِي ، فَأُتِيَ بِي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ فَبَنَى بِي ، وَأَنَا ابْنَةُ تِسْعٍ

"Musa ibn Ismail narrated to us from Hammad, and Bishr ibn Khalid narrated to us from Abu Usamah."

"Both reported from Hisham ibn Urwah, from his father, from Aisha, who said, the Messenger of Allah married me when I was seven or six years old."

"When we arrived in Medina, some women came to us. Bishr said, Umm Ruman came to me while I was on a swing."

"They took me, prepared me, and dressed me. Then the Messenger of Allah was brought to me, and he consummated the marriage with me, and I was nine years old."

- Aisha's account, the fullest version, Sunan Abi Dawud (Dar al-Hadith ed.), vol. 4, p. 446, no. 4933
The Baseline That Contradicts Itself

The baseline is set by Ibn Qutayba, who gives the whole traditional chronology in one paragraph and then undoes it in the same paragraph. He fixes the marriage at six, the consummation at nine, and the Prophet's ﷺ death when she was eighteen. He sets her own death in 58 AH, when she had come close to seventy. The answer to the entire charge begins inside the very text that states it.

Run Ibn Qutayba's own numbers. Nine years old seven months into 1 AH puts her birth about eight years before the Hijra. The same birth year makes her nineteen, close enough to his eighteen, at the Prophet's ﷺ death in 11 AH. It makes her sixty-six at her death in 58 AH. Ibn Qutayba does not say sixty-six. He says she had come close to seventy. A single paragraph cannot give one woman two birth years. The unrounded death notice, not the round schematic numbers, is the one with nothing to gain. This internal crack matters because it shows the six-and-nine figures were never fixed history even in the tradition's own books. The sister's dates break them open completely.

2. The Arithmetic of Her Sister Asma

Asma, Aisha's elder sister, has the best-anchored dates of any woman in this family, and the anchoring comes from the Sunni tradition's own biographers.

The Birth Date That Checks Itself

Source: Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Isaba fi Tamyiz al-Sahaba, vol. 8, entry 10804, p. 14; Abu Nu'aym al-Isbahani, Ma'rifat al-Sahaba, vol. 5, p. 182

Claim: Asma was born twenty-seven years before the Hijra and died at one hundred

Ibn Hajar's entry for Asma holds the two anchors, her birth before the Hijra and her age at death, in one place.

وقال هشام بن عروة عن أبيه بلغت أسماء مائة سنة لم يسقط لها سن ولم ينكر لها عقل وقال أبو نعيم الأصبهاني ولدت قبل الهجرة بسبع وعشرين سنة وعاشت إلى أوائل سنة أربع وعشرين

"Hisham ibn Urwa said, from his father, Asma reached a hundred years old, without losing a tooth or her mind failing."

"Abu Nu'aym al-Isbahani said, she was born twenty-seven years before the Hijra, and she lived to the beginning of the year twenty-four."

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Isaba fi Tamyiz al-Sahaba, vol. 8, entry 10804, p. 14

Abu Nu'aym carries the death notice in his own collection, dated to the year and the event that fixed it.

توفيت أسماء سنة ثلاث وسبعين بمكة بعد قتل ابنها عبد الله ابن الزبير بأيام ، ولها مائة سنة وقد ذهب بصرها

"Asma died in the year seventy-three, in Mecca, a few days after her son Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr was killed, at a hundred years of age, her eyesight having failed."

- Abu Nu'aym al-Isbahani, Ma'rifat al-Sahaba, vol. 5, p. 182

Twenty-seven years before the Hijra plus seventy-three years after it equals exactly one hundred. Two independent transmission lines agree on the birth year and the death year, and the two numbers confirm each other arithmetically. This is the strongest form of confirmation chronological literature can offer.

The Ten-Year Gap

Source: al-Dhahabi, Siyar A'lam al-Nubala, vol. 3, p. 380; Ibn Asakir, Tarikh Madinat Dimashq, vol. 69, pp. 8 and 10

Speaker: Ibn Abi al-Zinad, in al-Dhahabi's entry for Asma

Claim: Asma was ten years older than Aisha

The age distance between the sisters is fixed in a report al-Dhahabi preserves inside Asma's own entry.

قال ابن أبي الزناد : كانت أكبر من عائشة بعشر سنين . قلت : فعلى هذا يكون عمرها إحدى وتسعين سنة . وأما هشام بن عروة ، فقال : عاشت مئة سنة ، ولم يسقط لها سن .

"Ibn Abi al-Zinad said, she was ten years older than Aisha. I say, on this basis her age would be ninety-one."

"As for Hisham ibn Urwa, he said, she lived a hundred years, without losing a tooth."

- al-Dhahabi, Siyar A'lam al-Nubala, vol. 3, p. 380

Ibn Asakir carries the same ten-year report through two separately worded paths back to the same original source. Both paths end at Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi al-Zinad. Al-Dhahabi is visibly doing the arithmetic in his own critical voice and flagging that it does not close. A leading Sunni historian registers the instability from inside the tradition, generations before any modern polemic found it useful.

Apply the doubly attested gap to the doubly confirmed birth year. Asma born twenty-seven years before the Hijra, Aisha ten years younger, puts Aisha's birth seventeen years before the Hijra. Seven months into 1 AH, the date Ibn Qutayba's own paragraph assigns to the consummation, Aisha was about eighteen. The tradition's own numbers, competently added, delete the nine.

A Young Girl When Mecca First Heard Revelation

Source: al-Nasa'i, al-Sunan al-Kubra, vol. 5, p. 6

Speaker: Aisha

Claim: Surat al-Qamar came down in Mecca while Aisha was a young girl playing

Aisha's own memory of the early revelation dates her birth by another route.

وإنه أنزلت والساعة أدهى وأمر بمكة وانى جارية ألعب على محمد وما نزلت سورة البقرة والنساء إلا وأنا عنده

"The Hour is more terrible and more bitter was revealed in Mecca, and I was a young girl playing, in the days of Muhammad. Surah al-Baqarah and Surah al-Nisa came down only when I was already with him."

- Aisha on the revelation of Surat al-Qamar, al-Nasa'i, al-Sunan al-Kubra, vol. 5, p. 6

This is Aisha's own memory, transmitted in the same collections that carry the age reports. The report itself sets Qamar beside the first revelations, a sura of the shorter sections naming Paradise and Hell, before the lawful and unlawful came down. So a Meccan sura of the earliest period found Aisha already born and old enough to play. The long Medinan suras found her married. For the six-and-nine arithmetic to survive, her birth must fall around 614, four years into the revelation. Qamar must then come down years after that, against the report's own placement of it among the first. Her memory fits a birth in the opening years of the 610s, the same birth the sister-arithmetic fixed at about 605.

A Marriage Before the Marriage

Source: Ibn Sa'd, al-Tabaqat al-Kubra (Dar Sadir ed.), vol. 8, p. 59

Speaker: Abdullah ibn Abi Mulaykah

Claim: Aisha was promised to Mut'im's son Jubayr and extracted by divorce before the Prophet's ﷺ proposal

Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat records a prior arrangement that needed a formal divorce to dissolve.

أخبرنا عبد الله بن نمير عن الأجلح عن عبد الله بن أبي ملكية قال خطب رسول الله ﵌ عائشة بنت أبي بكر الصديق فقال إني كنت أعطيتها مطعما لابنه جبير فدعني حتى أسلها منهم فاستسلها منهم فطلقها فتزوجها رسول الله ﵌

"Abdullah ibn Numayr informed us, from al-Ajlah, from Abdullah ibn Abi Mulaykah, who said, the Messenger of Allah asked for Aisha bint Abi Bakr's hand."

"Abu Bakr said, I had already given her to Mut'im, for his son Jubayr, so let me extract her from them. He extracted her from them, they divorced her, and the Messenger of Allah then married her."

- Ibn Sa'd, al-Tabaqat al-Kubra (Dar Sadir ed.), vol. 8, p. 59

Two words carry the weight. Asalaha means to extract her from a bond already in place. Tallaqaha is divorce, the word for dissolving an actual marriage. Mut'im ibn Adi never accepted Islam and died before Badr, so the arrangement belongs to the Meccan years. A family does not contract a real marriage, needing formal release, for a child of five or six. On this report Aisha was already marriageable in Mecca before the Prophet's ﷺ proposal, which the six-year betrothal date cannot survive.

Five independent lines now converge. The death-notice arithmetic, the sister's birth year, the ten-year gap, the Meccan-revelation memory, and the prior marriage all push the same direction. None of them depends on the others. What they jointly rule out, on the tradition's own terms, is nine.

3. One Narrator Behind Every Number

What the Chains Actually Share

Source: Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah

Chain: Aisha, from Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, from his son Hisham ibn Urwah, transmitting in Iraq late in his life

Claim: every version of the six-and-nine report runs through Hisham ibn Urwah

Look again at the Abu Dawud isnad in Aisha's account. Hisham ibn Urwah, from his father Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, from Aisha. Bukhari's versions of the age report run through the same Hisham, from the same father, from the same Aisha. So do Muslim's. So does Ibn Majah's. The collections differ after Hisham, in the Iraqi transmitters who carry the report from him, and they differ in the number, six or seven. The head of the chain is one man every time.

Hisham lived long and transmitted in two phases, in Medina in his prime and in Iraq at the end of his life. The age reports belong to the Iraq phase, the phase the rijal critics themselves treat with reserve for other narrators' material. Several books repeating one late, single-source report are one report counted several times. Formal soundness certifies that named men link back to Hisham. It cannot certify that the number Hisham's Iraq students heard is the number Aisha said. Those students heard it decades after anyone could check it against a living witness.

The Numbers Do Not Even Agree

Source: Ibn Sa'd, al-Tabaqat al-Kubra; al-Kurani, al-Ma' al-Jari fi Ghusl al-Bukhari, p. 197

Claim: the collections disagree on the number and on the place of the marriage scenes

The tradition offers six at betrothal, or seven, or a betrothal three years before the Hijra at seven with consummation at nine. Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat even preserves Aisha's own list of her privileges with the numbers seven and nine side by side inside one self-praising sentence. Everyone treats that genre with care when the speaker is praising herself. And the reports place the intimacy in Mecca in one version and the contract in Medina in another, a contradiction al-Kurani presses.

وقد وصفت عائشة هذا الزواج المأساوي المزعوم ، فقال البخاري (4 / 251) : (باب تزويج النبي ﵌ عائشة وقدومها المدينة وبنائه بها : عن عائشة ﵂ قالت : تزوجني النبي ﵌ وأنا بنت ست سنين فقدمنا المدينة ، فنزلنا في بني الحرث بن خزرج ، فوعكت فتمرق شعري ، فوفى جميمة (تمرط ثم نبت) فأتتني أمي أم رومان وإني لفي أرجوحة ومعي صواحب لي فصرخت بي فأتيتها لا أدري ما تريد بي ، فأخذت بيدي حتى أوقفتني على باب الدار ، وإني لأنهج (أبكيبشدة) حتى سكن بعض نفسي

"Aisha described this supposed tragic marriage herself, as Bukhari said (4 / 251), the chapter on the Prophet's marriage to Aisha, her arrival in Medina, and his building with her."

"Aisha said, the Prophet married me when I was six years old. We arrived in Medina and stayed with Banu al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj. I fell ill and my hair fell out, then it grew back full."

"My mother Umm Ruman came to me while I was on a swing with my friends. She called me, and I came to her not knowing what she wanted with me."

"She took me by the hand and stood me at the door of the house, and I was panting until my breathing settled."

- al-Kurani, al-Ma' al-Jari fi Ghusl al-Bukhari, p. 197

The school's standard for reports about the Prophet's ﷺ household was set by the Imams ﵈ themselves. Every report is measured against the Book of Allah first, and what conflicts is set aside. The two weighty things the Prophet ﷺ left are the Book and his House, whose memory is the standing witness against a lone narrator's figure. A report missing from the family's own transmission, arriving late, through one channel, in a number its own sisters contradict, does not clear that bar. It cannot carry a moral verdict on a prophet.

4. The Dolls That Date the Marriage Later

The Attack's Own Strongest Witness

Source: al-Baghawi's Masabih (hasan), quoted in al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq, vol. 4, pp. 68-69

Speaker: Aisha

Dating: the return from Tabuk (9 AH) or Hunayn (8 AH)

Claim: the doll play happened after one of the two expeditions

The prepubescence claim rests on dolls. Aisha played with dolls, the argument runs, and girls who play with dolls have not reached puberty. She played with them after her wedding, therefore the wedding preceded her puberty. The whole inference hangs on when the playing happened. The report the attack itself quotes fixes the date.

ففي مصابيح البغوي من الحسان ، في باب عشرة النساء ، من كتاب النكاح ، عن عائشة قالت : « قدم رسول اللَّه صلَّى اللَّه عليه واله وسلَّم من غزوة تبوك أو حنين ، وفي بهوتها [ 2 ] ستر ، فهبّت ريح فكشفت ناحية الستر عن بنات لعائشة تلعب بها . فقال : ما هذه يا عائشة ؟ ! قالت : بناتي .

ورأى بينهنّ فرسا له جناحان من رقاع ، فقال : وما هذا الذي أرى وسطهنّ ؟ ! قالت : فرس . قال : وما هذا الذي عليه ؟ ! قالت : جناحان . قال : الفرس يكون له جناحان ؟ ! قالت : أما سمعت أنّ لسليمان خيلا لها أجنحة ؟ ! قالت : فضحك حتّى رأيت نواجذه » [ 1 ] .

"In al-Baghawi's Masabih, among the sound reports, in the chapter on living with women, from Aisha, who said, the Messenger of Allah returned from the expedition to Tabuk or Hunayn."

"A curtain covered a recess in her room. The wind lifted a corner of the curtain and uncovered some dolls of Aisha's she played with. He asked, what are these, Aisha? She answered, my daughters."

"He saw among them a horse with two wings of cloth. He asked, what is this I see among them? She answered, a horse. He asked, and what is this upon it? She answered, two wings."

"He asked, a horse with two wings? She answered, have you not heard that Solomon had horses with wings? She said, he laughed until I could see his molar teeth."

- al-Baghawi's Masabih (hasan), quoted in al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq, vol. 4, pp. 68-69

Tabuk is 9 AH. Hunayn is 8 AH. On the attack's own arithmetic, which makes her nine in 1 AH, the doll scene happens when she is sixteen or seventeen. The dolls stop proving prepubescence at the wedding and start proving something else. A young woman raised in the Prophet's ﷺ house kept her toys, and he laughed with her about them.

The School's Own Commentary on This Very Text

Source: al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq li-Nahj al-Haqq, vol. 4, pp. 69-70

Dating: 14th century AH

Claim: the report dates her playing after the conquest of Mecca, when she was about seventeen

Al-Muzaffar, writing the school's systematic answer, draws the conclusion from the same report.

فإنّها صريحة في لعبها بعد إحدى الغزاتين ، وهما كانتا بعد فتح مكَّة ، ومنه يعلم ما في قوله أخيرا : « ولعب عائشة كان في أوائل الهجرة » .

"The report is explicit that her playing came after one of the two expeditions, and both of them came after the conquest of Mecca."

"From this the flaw in his final statement is known, that Aisha's playing was in the first years of the Hijra."

- al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq li-Nahj al-Haqq, vol. 4, p. 69

ففيه : إنّ الصورة هي : الشكل ، كما في القاموس [ 1 ] ، فتكون الهيئة منها ، وتعليله لا وجه له ؛ لأنّ عائشة لم تكن صغيرة حين اللعب بالأفراس ، بل كانت بنت سبع عشرة تقريبا على رأيهم ، لما سبق من تصريح رواية البغوي بلعبها بها بعد إحدى الغزاتين .

"In it is this, the image is the shape, as in al-Qamus, so the form is one."

"His explanation has no basis, because Aisha was not a young child when she played with the horses. She was about seventeen by their own reckoning, because al-Baghawi's report explicitly dates her playing with them after one of the two expeditions."

- al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq li-Nahj al-Haqq, vol. 4, p. 70

Al-Muzaffar even catches a Sunni commentator quietly trimming the report, dropping the word dolls from the winged-horse scene so the dating problem disappears. The school's literature had already worked this ground centuries ago.

The Other Doll Reports Do Not Escape

Source: Bukhari and Muslim, the earlier doll report; al-Khattabi's gloss; al-Nasa'i, al-Sunan al-Kubra, the racing report

Claim: the other doll reports carry no date, and the puberty gloss is a jurist's inference

The earlier doll report, the one in Bukhari and Muslim about her playmates hiding when the Prophet ﷺ entered, carries no date at all. The gloss the attack quotes from al-Khattabi, that dolls were permitted because she had not reached puberty, is a jurist's inference, not a witness's statement. The racing report in al-Nasa'i's larger collection shows the word the argument leans on, jariya, a young girl, applied to Aisha on a journey in her married life. She raced the Prophet ﷺ on foot and beat him, then lost a rematch years later after she had gained weight. The word names a young wife, not a child, and that is the word the age reports themselves use. The dolls argument, examined on its own evidence, dissolves.

5. The Jewish Law the Attack Quotes

The Higher Floor the Attack Appeals To

The attack's appendix on Jewish culture argues that Judaism set a higher standard, girls at twelve and a half, and that Muhammad ﷺ fell below it. The sources it cites are real, but they say far more than the appendix repeats, and what they say removes the floor the argument stands on.

The Mishnah the appendix's own bibliography belongs to legislates in plain words, in Mishnah Niddah 5:4. A girl of three years and one day whose father has arranged her betrothal is betrothed through intercourse, with the full legal consequences attached. The Babylonian Talmud, Niddah 45a, discusses the same threshold and records the empress Yusteni's words, that she married at six and gave birth at seven. The Gemara debates whether a girl that young can conceive, without disputing that the marriage law stands. Mishnah Ketubot 4:1, the very text the appendix cites for the twelve-and-a-half framework, is about the father's property rights over his daughter's compensation awards. A father administering his minor daughter's affairs is the world the age of twelve and a half actually regulated.

What the Cited Texts Legislate
Source Provision it states
Mishnah Niddah 5:4 Betrothal by intercourse valid from three years and one day
Bavli Niddah 45a Marriage at six and birth at seven recorded in discussion of that law
Mishnah Ketubot 4:1 Father holds the young woman's awards until maturity at twelve and a half

This matters for one reason. A writer who measures the Prophet ﷺ by a moral floor must first show the floor existed. The tradition the attack's own appendix appeals to legislated for marriage with girls far younger than nine, in its founding legal texts. Those texts were codified centuries before Islam and stayed authoritative centuries after. The argument from a higher Jewish standard collapses, and with it the claim that the Prophet ﷺ broke a norm the surrounding scriptures had established. The Qur'an's own standard is a different matter, and a stricter one, and it is the school's.

6. The Qur'an Tests Readiness, Not Birthdays

The Verse the Attack Reads as a License

The attack reads Qur'an 65:4 as a license to marry prepubescent girls, a reading the site has already answered in the Imami reading of Qur'an 65:4. There the wa- construction of the verse carries the doubt of the previous category forward, and the third category names women past the age whose menses are uncertain. The school does not read the verse as the attack needs it read.

The Readiness Test the Book States

The Qur'an's own marriage test runs the other way.

وَابْتَلُوا الْيَتَامَى حَتَّىٰ إِذَا بَلَغُوا النِّكَاحَ فَإِنْ آنَسْتُمْ مِنْهُمْ رُشْدًا فَادْفَعُوا إِلَيْهِمْ أَمْوَالَهُمْ

wa-ibtalū al-yatāmā ḥattā idhā balaghū al-nikāḥa fa-in ānastum minhum rushdan fa-dfaʿū ilayhim amwālahum

"Test the orphans until, when they reach the capacity for marriage, if you perceive in them sound judgment, hand over their property to them."

- Qur'an 4:6

The verse pairs two conditions. Reaching the capacity for marriage, and perceiving sound judgment. The Imamite reading, transmitted from Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ through Ali ibn Ibrahim's tafsir, holds the two together as one joint test of observed readiness. The capacity arrives with judgment and is certified by it, never by a birthday count alone. Whatever any report claims about a marriage, the Book the school submits every report to legislates a readiness test here. The Imams ﵈ set the procedure that follows from it. Present the report to the Book, and what conflicts with the Book is set aside.

So the Qur'an licenses nothing the attack describes, the six-and-nine report fails the joint test the Book states, and the chronological record sets the marriage near eighteen. That is the whole case, assembled.

Final Verdict

The charge that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ consummated a marriage with a nine-year-old Aisha fails on three independent grounds, any one of which is sufficient.

First, the chronology. The Sunni tradition's own biographical numbers, Asma's doubly confirmed birth and death years and the doubly attested ten-year gap between the sisters, fix Aisha's birth seventeen years before the Hijra. On those numbers she was near eighteen at the consummation. Ibn Qutayba's own death notice already contradicts his marriage notice before any external evidence is consulted.

Second, the transmission. One narrator, Hisham ibn Urwa, stands at the head of every version of the six-and-nine report in every collection, transmitting late in Iraq. The collections do not agree on the numbers, and the reports place the scenes in different cities. The school's standard, set by the Imams ﵈, measures every report against the Book, and this one does not survive the measuring.

Third, the supporting evidence. The dolls argument, the only route to prepubescence, dates itself to 8 or 9 AH in its own words. That dating makes Aisha sixteen or seventeen in the doll scene on the attack's arithmetic and dissolves the inference entirely. And the Jewish law invoked as the higher floor legislates for betrothal by intercourse from three years and one day, in the Mishnah and Talmud the attack's own appendix cites.

The certain conclusion is that age nine is not established history. The best-supported figure from the tradition's own arithmetic is eighteen. A charge that must stand on a single late narrator, against the arithmetic of two independent biographical lines, on the back of a doll dated seven years past the wedding, is a charge that has lost its case.
