Historical Objection · Debate

How Old Was Aisha When She Married the Prophet

The claim that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ consummated his marriage to Aisha at age nine rests on reports that the standard chronology the same tradition preserves contradicts at every point, on chains that all surface in second century Kufa, on a single witness whose reliability the Qur'an itself put in question, and on a dolls argument the sources themselves date years after the claimed wedding. From Muslim, al-Mizzi's Tahdhib al-Kamal, Sahih al-Bukhari, Tafsir al-Qummi, the Sira, Ibn Sa'd, Ibn Hajar, al-Dhahabi, al-Muzaffar's Dala'il al-Sidq, and the Mishnah and Talmud the attack itself cites.

The Argument Under Examination

Background

The attack states its case in one sentence. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ married Aisha when she was six and consummated the marriage when she was nine, before her first menses. And a true prophet could not have done this. The sentence has real sources behind it. Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, and Ibn Majah all carry Aisha's own account, and the biographical literature repeats it. Everything else in the charge, the claims about Islamic law, the medical appendices, the psychological reading of Aisha's later life, hangs from that one sentence. The case against the charge stands on the same tradition's own books, and it rests on five premises.

Premise 1: The Sister's Arithmetic Fixes the Birth Year

The reports giving Aisha's age as six at betrothal and nine at consummation contradict the birth and death dates of her elder sister Asma. The Sunni tradition itself transmits those dates through two independent lines and confirms them arithmetically. Asma was born twenty-seven years before the Hijra and died at one hundred in 73 AH. Asma was ten years older than Aisha. Those numbers fix Aisha's birth about seventeen years before the Hijra and put her near eighteen at consummation, seven months into 1 AH.

Premise 2: Every Chain Leads to Kufa

Every number in every collection surfaces in Iraq in the second century. Muslim's own page shows three route families, and the one through al-Zuhri says seven where the others say six, so the chains contradict the figure they are said to confirm. The chains that bypass Hisham run through Kufa end to end. Below the one elder who knew her, one Kufan name was a child of eight at her death. Every other was born after it. The rijal critics themselves flagged Hisham's Iraqi years, and Malik of Medina accepted only what Hisham transmitted before leaving. The number never appears in Medina's own books.

Premise 3: The Witness Herself Fails the Qur'an's Test

Every chain ends at one mouth, Aisha's. The Qur'an itself put her reliability in question. Revelation commanded her and Hafsa to repent and told them their hearts had deviated. She herself records plotting a false statement against the Prophet ﷺ in the honey affair, and her own father said of her report that he did not trust her word. She later broke the Qur'an's command to the Prophet's wives, left her house, and raised an army against the Imam of her time. A witness under these documented rulings cannot carry so heavy a load alone.

Premise 4: The Dolls Date the Marriage Later

The claim that Aisha was prepubescent rests on her playing with dolls after marriage. The doll reports the attack cites are themselves dated by their own words to the return from Tabuk or Hunayn. Those campaigns belong to 7-9 AH, years after the claimed wedding. On the attack's own arithmetic the dolls make her a married woman of sixteen or seventeen still playing, or the reports contradict each other. The Imami school's own commentator, al-Muzaffar, draws exactly this conclusion from the same texts.

Premise 5: The Jewish Law Comparison Collapses

The moral charge leans on a comparison with Jewish law, which the attack says set a higher minimum age. The Mishnah and Talmud the attack's own appendix points to legislate for betrothal by intercourse from three years and one day. The Talmud records a Roman empress married at six and a mother at seven. A standard that convicts the Prophet ﷺ convicts the sources the attack appeals to.

Conclusion: Nine Is Not Established

The six-and-nine age fails on chronology, fails on transmission, and fails on the witness herself. The certain result is that nine is not established. The best-supported reading of the tradition's own numbers puts the consummation near eighteen.

1. The Charge the Sources Are Asked to Carry

What the Charge Claims

The attack states its case in one sentence. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ married Aisha when she was six and consummated the marriage when she was nine, before her first menses. And a true prophet could not have done this. Everything else in the charge, the claims about Islamic law, the medical appendices, the psychological reading of Aisha's later life, hangs from that one sentence.

The sentence has real sources behind it, and honesty requires saying so. Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, and Ibn Majah all carry Aisha's own account, and the biographical literature repeats it.

Aisha's Own Account

The fullest version of Aisha's account, in Sunan Abi Dawud, carries the whole scene, the contract, the swing, and the consummation, in her own words.

(4933) - حَدَّثَنَا مُوسَى بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ ، حَدَّثَنَا حَمَّادٌ ح وَحَدَّثَنَا بِشْرُ بْنُ خَالِدٍ ، حَدَّثَنَا أَبُو أُسَامَةَ ، قَالَا : حَدَّثَنَا هِشَامُ بْنُ عُرْوَةَ ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ ، عَنْ عَائِشَةَ ، قَالَتْ : إِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ تَزَوَّجَنِي وَأَنَا بِنْتُ سَبْعٍ أَوْ سِتٍّ ، فَلَمَّا قَدِمْنَا الْمَدِينَةَ أَتَيْنَ نِسْوَةٌ ، وَقَالَ بِشْرٌ : فَأَتَتْنِي أُمُّ رُومَانَ وَأَنَا عَلَى أُرْجُوحَةٍ ، فَذَهَبْنَ بِي وَهَيَّأْنَنِي وَصَنَعْنَنِي ، فَأُتِيَ بِي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ فَبَنَى بِي ، وَأَنَا ابْنَةُ تِسْعٍ

"Musa ibn Ismail narrated to us from Hammad, and Bishr ibn Khalid narrated to us from Abu Usamah."

"Both reported from Hisham ibn Urwah, from his father, from Aisha, who said, the Messenger of Allah married me when I was seven or six years old."

"When we arrived in Medina, some women came to us. Bishr said, Umm Ruman came to me while I was on a swing."

"They took me, prepared me, and dressed me. Then the Messenger of Allah was brought to me, and he consummated the marriage with me, and I was nine years old."

- Aisha's account, the fullest version, Sunan Abi Dawud (Dar al-Hadith ed.), vol. 4, p. 446, no. 4933

The fullest account carries the number in Aisha's own voice, and its chain runs through Hisham ibn Urwa in Iraq. The third section takes up the weakness of that chain. Her own chronology answers the number first.

The Baseline That Contradicts Itself

That chronology begins inside the tradition's own baseline. The baseline is set by Ibn Qutayba, who gives the whole traditional chronology in one paragraph and then undoes it in the same paragraph. He fixes the marriage at six, the consummation at nine, and the Prophet's ﷺ death when she was eighteen. He sets her own death in 58 AH, when she had come close to seventy. The answer to the entire charge begins inside the very text that states it.

Run Ibn Qutayba's own numbers. Nine years old seven months into 1 AH puts her birth about eight years before the Hijra. The same birth year makes her nineteen, close enough to his eighteen, at the Prophet's ﷺ death in 11 AH. It makes her sixty-six at her death in 58 AH. Ibn Qutayba does not say sixty-six. He says she had come close to seventy. A single paragraph cannot give one woman two birth years. Nothing of doctrine rests on the death notice. Doctrine rests on the round numbers six and nine, and figures that carry a doctrine must meet a stricter test. This internal crack matters because it shows the six-and-nine figures were never fixed history even in the tradition's own books. The sister's dates break them open completely.

2. The Arithmetic of Her Sister Asma

The internal crack opened the question of her birth year, and her sister's dates close it. Asma, Aisha's elder sister, has the best-anchored dates of any woman in this family, and the anchoring comes from the Sunni tradition's own biographers.

The Birth Date That Checks Itself

Source: Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Isaba fi Tamyiz al-Sahaba, vol. 8, entry 10804, p. 14; Abu Nu'aym al-Isbahani, Ma'rifat al-Sahaba, vol. 5, p. 182

Claim: Asma was born twenty-seven years before the Hijra and died at one hundred

Ibn Hajar's entry for Asma holds the two anchors, her birth before the Hijra and her age at death, in one place.

وقال هشام بن عروة عن أبيه بلغت أسماء مائة سنة لم يسقط لها سن ولم ينكر لها عقل وقال أبو نعيم الأصبهاني ولدت قبل الهجرة بسبع وعشرين سنة وعاشت إلى أوائل سنة أربع وعشرين

"Hisham ibn Urwa said, from his father, Asma reached a hundred years old, without losing a tooth or her mind failing."

"Abu Nu'aym al-Isbahani said, she was born twenty-seven years before the Hijra, and she lived to the beginning of the year twenty-four."

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, al-Isaba fi Tamyiz al-Sahaba, vol. 8, entry 10804, p. 14

Abu Nu'aym carries the death notice in his own collection, dated to the year and the event that fixed it.

توفيت أسماء سنة ثلاث وسبعين بمكة بعد قتل ابنها عبد الله ابن الزبير بأيام ، ولها مائة سنة وقد ذهب بصرها

"Asma died in the year seventy-three, in Mecca, a few days after her son Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr was killed, at a hundred years of age, her eyesight having failed."

- Abu Nu'aym al-Isbahani, Ma'rifat al-Sahaba, vol. 5, p. 182

Twenty-seven years before the Hijra plus seventy-three years after it equals exactly one hundred. Abu Nuaym's notice and Hisham's report agree on the birth year and the death year, and the two numbers confirm each other arithmetically. This is the strongest form of confirmation chronological literature can offer. One copy of al-Isaba miswrites the death year as twenty-four. The next exhibit tests that slip and refuses it. Asma died in 73, days after her son Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr was killed. A death in 24 AH would place her grave half a century before the bereavement that killed her.

The Ten-Year Gap

Source: al-Dhahabi, Siyar A'lam al-Nubala, vol. 3, p. 380; Ibn Asakir, Tarikh Madinat Dimashq, vol. 69, pp. 8 and 10

Speaker: Ibn Abi al-Zinad, in al-Dhahabi's entry for Asma

Claim: Asma was ten years older than Aisha

The age distance between the sisters is fixed in a report al-Dhahabi preserves inside Asma's own entry.

قال ابن أبي الزناد : كانت أكبر من عائشة بعشر سنين . قلت : فعلى هذا يكون عمرها إحدى وتسعين سنة . وأما هشام بن عروة ، فقال : عاشت مئة سنة ، ولم يسقط لها سن .

"Ibn Abi al-Zinad said, she was ten years older than Aisha. I say, on this basis her age would be ninety-one."

"As for Hisham ibn Urwa, he said, she lived a hundred years, without losing a tooth."

- al-Dhahabi, Siyar A'lam al-Nubala, vol. 3, p. 380

Ibn Asakir carries the same ten-year report through two separately worded paths back to the same original source. Both paths end at Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi al-Zinad. Al-Dhahabi is visibly doing the arithmetic in his own critical voice and flagging that it does not close. A leading Sunni historian registers the instability from inside the tradition, generations before any modern polemic found it useful. The ninety-one figure carries its own answer in its wording. Al-Dhahabi says, on this basis, and the basis is the ten-year gap applied to the birth year the age reports assume. It is arithmetic in his own voice, computed from the number now on trial, and no death notice carries it. The notices carry a hundred, twice, through two separate lines. The report of the hundred comes from Hisham in Medina about his own grandmother Asma. That is family material from his accepted Medina years. The Iraq transmission the critics flagged is a different body of reports about a different subject. The ninety-one is what six-and-nine forces the death year to become, and the death year refuses. The notices of Aisha's own age at death disagree among themselves, sixty-six by the six-and-nine arithmetic and close to seventy by Ibn Qutayba, so they cannot arbitrate the birth year either way. The self-checking anchors remain the sister's doubly attested dates.

Apply the attested gap to the confirmed birth year. Asma born twenty-seven years before the Hijra, Aisha ten years younger, puts Aisha's birth seventeen years before the Hijra. Seven months into 1 AH, the date Ibn Qutayba's own paragraph assigns to the consummation, Aisha was about eighteen. The tradition's own numbers, added plainly, delete the nine.

A Young Girl When Mecca First Heard Revelation

Source: al-Nasa'i, al-Sunan al-Kubra, vol. 5, p. 6

Speaker: Aisha

Claim: Surat al-Qamar came down in Mecca while Aisha was a young girl playing

Aisha's own memory of the early revelation dates her birth by another route.

وإنه أنزلت والساعة أدهى وأمر بمكة وانى جارية ألعب على محمد وما نزلت سورة البقرة والنساء إلا وأنا عنده

"The Hour is more terrible and more bitter was revealed in Mecca, and I was a young girl playing, in the days of Muhammad. Surah al-Baqarah and Surah al-Nisa came down only when I was already with him."

- Aisha on the revelation of Surat al-Qamar, al-Nasa'i, al-Sunan al-Kubra, vol. 5, p. 6

This is Aisha's own memory, transmitted in the same collections that carry the age reports. Qamar is a Meccan sura, and the report places her playing in Mecca while it came down. So a Meccan sura found Aisha already born and old enough to play, and the long Medinan suras found her married. The Meccan years stretch from the first revelation to the Hijra, so the memory alone fixes no year. It carries weight in combination. Her sister's dates fix her birth about seventeen years before the Hijra. A birth around 605, seventeen years before the Hijra, is what that memory fits. She would be a girl of five or six when the first revelations came, playing in Mecca while they came down.

A Marriage Before the Marriage

Source: Ibn Sa'd, al-Tabaqat al-Kubra (Dar Sadir ed.), vol. 8, p. 59

Speaker: Abdullah ibn Abi Mulaykah

Claim: Aisha was promised to Mut'im's son Jubayr and extracted by divorce before the Prophet's ﷺ proposal

The Qamar memory left her playing in Mecca in the mission's first years. Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat adds a bond from the same years, a prior arrangement that needed a formal divorce to dissolve.

أخبرنا عبد الله بن نمير عن الأجلح عن عبد الله بن أبي ملكية قال خطب رسول الله ﵌ عائشة بنت أبي بكر الصديق فقال إني كنت أعطيتها مطعما لابنه جبير فدعني حتى أسلها منهم فاستسلها منهم فطلقها فتزوجها رسول الله ﵌

"Abdullah ibn Numayr informed us, from al-Ajlah, from Abdullah ibn Abi Mulaykah, who said, the Messenger of Allah asked for Aisha bint Abi Bakr's hand."

"Abu Bakr said, I had already given her to Mut'im, for his son Jubayr, so let me extract her from them. He extracted her from them, they divorced her, and the Messenger of Allah then married her."

- Ibn Sa'd, al-Tabaqat al-Kubra (Dar Sadir ed.), vol. 8, p. 59

Two words carry the weight. Asalaha means to extract her from a bond already in place. Tallaqaha is divorce, the word for dissolving an actual marriage. Mut'im ibn Adi never accepted Islam and died before Badr, so the arrangement belongs to the Meccan years. A family does not contract a real marriage, needing formal release, for a child of five or six. On this report Aisha was already marriageable in Mecca before the Prophet's ﷺ proposal. The six-year betrothal date cannot survive that.

Five independent lines now converge. The death-notice arithmetic, the sister's birth year, the ten-year gap, the Meccan-revelation memory, and the prior marriage all push the same direction. None of them depends on the others. What they jointly rule out, on the tradition's own terms, is nine.

3. Every Chain Leads to Kufa

What Muslim's Own Page Shows

Source: Sahih Muslim, vol. 4, p. 142

Claim: the three route families of the age report, six through Hisham and seven through al-Zuhri

The sister arithmetic fixed her birth before the revelation began, and that birth year removes the nine by itself. The next question is how the number reached the books at all. The defense of the number rests on many chains, so the chains deserve a direct look, and Muslim's own page carries all three families in one place. The first runs through Hisham from his father from Aisha. The second runs through al-Zuhri from Urwa from Aisha. The third runs through al-A'mash from Ibrahim from al-Aswad from Aisha.

(وحدثنا) يحيى بن يحيى أخبرنا أبو معاوية عن هشام ابن عروة ح وحدثنا ابن نمير واللفظ له حدثنا عبدة هو ابن سليمان عن هشام عن أبيه عن عائشة قالت تزوجني النبي ﵌ وانا بنت ست سنين وبنى بي وانا بنت تسع سنين

"Yahya ibn Yahya told us that Abu Muawiyah informed him from Hisham ibn Urwah. Ibn Numayr also told us, using these words, Abdah ibn Sulayman told us from Hisham, from his father, from Aisha. Aisha said, "The Prophet married me when I was six years old, and he began living with me when I was nine years old.""

- Sahih Muslim, vol. 4, p. 142, the route through Ibn Numayr

(وحدثنا) عبد بن حميد أخبرنا عبد الرزاق أخبرنا معمر عن الزهري عن عروة عن عائشة ان النبي ﵌ تزوجها وهي بنت سبع سنين وزفت إليه وهي بنت تسع سنين ولعبها معها ومات عنها وهي بنت ثمان عشرة

"Abd ibn Humayd reports that Abd al-Razzaq reported from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Urwah, from Aisha, that the Prophet married her when she was seven years old. She was brought to him as a bride when she was nine. Her dolls were with her. He died when she was eighteen."

- Sahih Muslim, vol. 4, p. 142, the route through al-Zuhri

قال يحيى وإسحاق أخبرنا وقال الآخران حدثنا أبو معاوية عن الأعمش عن إبراهيم عن الأسود عن عائشة قالت تزوجها رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم وهي بنت ست وبنى بها وهي بنت تسع ومات عنها وهي بنت ثمان عشرة

"Yahya and Ishaq said, Abu Muawiyah informed us. The other two said, Abu Muawiyah narrated to us from al-Amash, from Ibrahim, from al-Aswad, from Aisha. Aisha said, the Messenger of Allah married her when she was six, he consummated the marriage when she was nine, and he died when she was eighteen."

- Sahih Muslim, vol. 4, p. 142, the route through al-A'mash

Six arrives through Hisham. Seven arrives through al-Zuhri. The two families agree that a number was said, and they contradict each other about which number it was. Chains that disagree on the figure do not confirm the figure. They confirm that a story about a figure was circulating, in numbers that moved. The same instability al-Dhahabi flagged in Asma's dates, the age reports carry in their own numbers.

One objection deserves a direct answer. All three routes agree on nine at consummation, so the agreement looks like corroboration. It is one telling of the number counted three times. Two routes carry it through Aisha's nephew Urwa, and the third through al-Aswad's memory of her, a memory that reached writing in Kufa decades after her death. The eighteen at the Prophet's ﷺ death is the story's own arithmetic restated, a nine-year-old bride and nine counted years of marriage. Her death notices, sixty-six in one arithmetic and close to seventy in Ibn Qutayba's, contradict that package and each other, so the derived eighteen certifies nothing outside the story. The Zuhri route escapes Hisham, and it says seven where Hisham's circle says six. Two routes handing down different numbers do not corroborate each other. Where the story circulated is the next question, and the map of the narrators answers it.

The Men Who Carry the Number

Source: the death and birth notices of the narrators, collected from al-Mizzi's Tahdhib al-Kamal and the other rijal works

Claim: every lower link in every age chain lived and taught in Kufa or its Iraqi and Yemeni relay, and below the one elder who knew her, the Kufan chain holds a child of eight and men born after her death

Aisha died in 58 AH. Urwa, her nephew and the family's transmitter, died in 94. From that point the chains divide, and geography decides everything below them.

Below Hisham on his routes stand Kufans. Abd Allah ibn Numayr died in 199. Abda ibn Sulayman died in 179. Abu Muawiya died in 185. Ali ibn Mushir, who also carries Hisham's material into Ibn Majah, died in 158. All of them heard Hisham in Iraq at the end of his life, in the very years the critics flagged. The route through al-Zuhri left Medina as well. Muslim received it through Abd al-Razzaq of Sanaa, from Ma'mar of Basra, who transmitted al-Zuhri's material in Yemen, and Ma'mar himself also studied under Hisham.

The third route never touches Medina at all. Al-Aswad ibn Yazid of Kufa died in 75. Ibrahim al-Nakhai of Kufa died in 96, and he was born around 50, which made him eight years old when Aisha died. Al-A'mash of Kufa died in 148, and he was born around 61, three years after her death. The Kufan route's one living link to Aisha is al-Aswad, who knew her in Medina. What he passed on reached Kufa as a child's memory under Ibrahim, retold decades later by men who never saw Medina.

Set the deaths in a row and the pattern stands out. Every mouth that names the number, below Urwa, belongs to Kufa or its Yemeni relay, and all of it reaches the books in the second century. The first century of Medina, the city where Aisha lived and taught for forty years, produced no number at all. Medina's own first books print no age at all. Hisham stands at the head of the Kufan routes, and his own critics left a precise verdict on which of his years to trust.

His Own School Flagged the Iraq Years

Source: al-Mizzi, Tahdhib al-Kamal, vol. 30, pp. 238-240, the entry on Hisham ibn Urwa

Claim: Malik accepted only Hisham's Medina transmission, and the rijal critics, the scholars of the narrators, restricted their criticism to his Iraq years, where the isnad's own wording, the chain's claim of hearing, weakened

The classical record never calls Hisham a fabricator, and the argument here needs him to be nothing of the kind. It does something sharper. It splits his career in two and asks which half the age report came from. Malik of Medina drew the line in a story Yahya ibn Sa'id preserved, and Yahya told it as a dream he saw. The verdict inside the dream matches what the waking critics were saying.

رأيت مالك بن أنس في النوم فسألته عن هشام بن عروة ، فقال : أما ما حدث به وهو عندنا فهو - أي كأنه يصححه - ، وما حدث به بعدما خرج من عندنا ، فكأنه يوهنه

"I saw Malik ibn Anas in a dream and asked him about Hisham ibn Urwah. Malik replied, "What he narrated while he was with us is sound. What he narrated after he left us is weak.""

- al-Mizzi, Tahdhib al-Kamal, vol. 30, p. 238, the entry on Hisham ibn Urwa

What Hisham transmitted in Medina holds. What he transmitted after leaving weakens. Yaqub ibn Shayba records the split in his waking voice, that nothing drew criticism until Iraq, and that the people of Hisham's own city were the ones who criticized what he began transmitting there.

وقال يعقوب بن شيبة : ثبت ، ثقة ، لم ينكر عليه شئ إلا بعدما صار إلى العراق فإنه انبسط في الرواية عن أبيه ، فأنكر ذلك عليه أهل بلده ، والذي يرى أن هشاما يسهل لأهل العراق أنه كان لا يحدث عن أبيه إلا بما سمعه منه فكان تسهله أنه أرسل عن أبيه مما كان يسمعه من غير أبيه عن أبيه

"Yaqub ibn Shaybah said, "He was established and reliable. No one criticized him until he went to Iraq. There, he became freer in narrating from his father. The scholars of his town criticized him for this."

"Those who thought Hisham became lenient with the people of Iraq held that he only narrated from his father what he had heard directly from him. His leniency was that he reported from his father without a direct link, using material he had heard from others who reported it from his father.""

- al-Mizzi, Tahdhib al-Kamal, vol. 30, p. 238, the entry on Hisham ibn Urwa

The criticism is exact. In Medina he reported from his father only what he had heard directly. In Iraq he loosened the form, and his own townspeople noticed. Ibn Khirash then preserved the collapse in the chain's own wording, and his record is the sharpest evidence this question has.

وقال عبد الرحمان بن يوسف بن خراش : كان مالك لا يرضاه ، وكان هشام صدوقا تدخل أخباره في الصحيح . بلغني أن مالكا نقم عليه حديثه لأهل العراق ، قدم الكوفة ثلاث مرات ، قدمة كان يقول : حدثني أبي ، قال سمعت عائشة ، وقدم الثانية فكان يقول : أخبرني أبي عن عائشة ، وقدم الثالثة فكان يقول : أبي عن عائشة . سمع منه بأخرة وكيع ، وابن نمير ، ومحاضر

"Abd al-Rahman ibn Yusuf ibn Khirash said, "Malik did not approve of him, though Hisham was trustworthy and his reports belong in authentic collections. I heard that Malik criticized his reports to the people of Iraq. Hisham came to Kufa three times."

"On his first visit, he said, 'My father told me that he heard Aisha.' On his second visit, he said, 'My father informed me from Aisha.' On his third visit, he said, 'My father from Aisha.' Waki, Ibn Numayr, and Muhadir heard from him late in his life.""

- al-Mizzi, Tahdhib al-Kamal, vol. 30, p. 239, the entry on Hisham ibn Urwa

First Kufan visit, my father told me that he heard Aisha. Second visit, my father informed me from Aisha. Third visit, the bare form, my father from Aisha, claiming nothing. The isnad itself dissolves in Iraq visit by visit, and the men who heard the last form are named. Waki. Ibn Numayr. Muhadir. Muslim's own route to the number rides Ibn Numayr, as the first route above showed.

وقال أبو نعيم ، وأبو موسى محمد بن المثنى : مات سنة خمس وأربعين ومئة . وقال عبد الله بن داود الخريبي ، والهيثم بن عدي ، وعبدة ابن سليمان ، وخليفة بن خياط ، والزبير بن بكار : مات سنة ست وأربعين ومئة . قال الهيثم : ببغداد . وقال الزبير : بمدينة السلام عند أمير المؤمنين أبي جعفر المنصور في صحابته

"Abu Nuaym and Abu Musa Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna said that he died in 145 AH. Abd Allah ibn Dawud al-Khuraybi, al-Haytham ibn Adi, Abdah ibn Sulayman, Khalifah ibn Khayyat, and al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar said that he died in 146 AH. Al-Haytham said that he died in Baghdad."

"Al-Zubayr said that he died in the City of Peace, with the Commander of the Faithful, Abu Jafar al-Mansur, among his companions."

- al-Mizzi, Tahdhib al-Kamal, vol. 30, p. 240, the entry on Hisham ibn Urwa

Hisham died in 145 or 146 AH in Baghdad, in the company of the caliph al-Mansur. He was born in 61, the year al-Husayn ﵈ was killed at Karbala. The number therefore entered writing through a man who left Medina for the caliphal court in Iraq, and whose formal claims to hearing weakened while he was there. The age reports belong to those final Iraqi years, or they belong to nothing. The Kufan chains disagree among themselves anyway, and even the scenes refuse to sit in one city.

The Places Still Do Not Agree

Source: Ibn Sa'd, al-Tabaqat al-Kubra; al-Kurani, al-Ma' al-Jari fi Ghusl al-Bukhari, p. 197

Claim: the collections disagree on the number and on the place of the marriage scenes

The tradition offers six at betrothal, or seven, or a betrothal three years before the Hijra at seven with consummation at nine. Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat even preserves Aisha's own list of her privileges with the numbers seven and nine side by side inside one self-praising sentence. A self-praising list is evidence to check twice, and the tradition's own scholars check it. And the reports place the intimacy in Mecca in one version and the contract in Medina in another, a contradiction al-Kurani presses.

وقد وصفت عائشة هذا الزواج المأساوي المزعوم ، فقال البخاري (4 / 251) : (باب تزويج النبي ﵌ عائشة وقدومها المدينة وبنائه بها : عن عائشة ﵂ قالت : تزوجني النبي ﵌ وأنا بنت ست سنين فقدمنا المدينة ، فنزلنا في بني الحرث بن خزرج ، فوعكت فتمرق شعري ، فوفى جميمة (تمرط ثم نبت) فأتتني أمي أم رومان وإني لفي أرجوحة ومعي صواحب لي فصرخت بي فأتيتها لا أدري ما تريد بي ، فأخذت بيدي حتى أوقفتني على باب الدار ، وإني لأنهج (أبكيبشدة) حتى سكن بعض نفسي

"Aisha described this supposed tragic marriage herself, as Bukhari said (4 / 251), the chapter on the Prophet's marriage to Aisha, her arrival in Medina, and his building with her."

"Aisha said, the Prophet married me when I was six years old. We arrived in Medina and stayed with Banu al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj. I fell ill and my hair fell out, then it grew back full."

"My mother Umm Ruman came to me while I was on a swing with my friends. She called me, and I came to her not knowing what she wanted with me."

"She took me by the hand and stood me at the door of the house, and I was panting until my breathing settled."

- al-Kurani, al-Ma' al-Jari fi Ghusl al-Bukhari, p. 197

Numbers, places, and chains all moved in the telling. The standard that decides what to do with a moving report was set by the Imams ﵈ themselves. Every report is measured against the Book of Allah first, and what conflicts is set aside. The two weighty things the Prophet ﷺ left are the Book and his House, whose memory is the standing witness against a lone narrator's figure. A report missing from the family's own transmission, arriving late, through flagged channels, in a number its own sisters contradict, does not clear that bar. It cannot carry a moral verdict on a prophet.

4. The Witness Herself

The Honey Plot in Her Own Words

Source: Sahih al-Bukhari, vol. 6, p. 167, the tafsir of Surat al-Tahrim

Speaker: Aisha

Claim: she and Hafsa agreed to tell the Prophet ﷺ a false statement about his breath

The chains of the number end at one mouth, whichever family they ride. Every route, Hisham's, al-Zuhri's, al-A'mash's, closes with Aisha herself saying a figure about her own body. The defenders of the number spend their strength grading the men below her and leave the witness unexamined. The Qur'an did not leave her unexamined. The plot is recorded by Aisha herself, in the very collection the charge appeals to.

إن النبي ﵌ كان يمكث عند زينب ابنة جحش ويشرب عندها عسلا فتواصيت أنا وحفصة ان ائتنا دخل عليها النبي ﵌ فلتقل إني لأجد منك ريح مغافير أكلت مغافير فدخل على إحداهما فقالت له ذلك فقال لا بل شربت عسلا عند زينب بنت جحش ولن أعود له فنزلت يا أيها النبي لم تحرم ما أحل الله لك

"The Prophet stayed with Zaynab bint Jahsh and drank honey at her home. Hafsa and I agreed that, whenever the Prophet visited either of us, she would say, "I smell maghafir on you. Did you eat maghafir?" He visited one of them, and she said this to him."

"He replied, "I drank honey at Zaynab bint Jahsh's home, and I will never drink it again." Then these words came down, "Prophet, why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful for you?""

- Sahih al-Bukhari, vol. 6, p. 167, the tafsir of Surat al-Tahrim

يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّبِىُّ لِمَ تُحَرِّمُ مَآ أَحَلَّ ٱللَّهُ لَكَ ۖ تَبْتَغِى مَرْضَاتَ أَزْوَٰجِكَ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌۭ رَّحِيمٌۭ

ya ayyuha al-nabiyyu lima tuharrimu ma ahalla llahu lak, tabtaghi mardata azwajik, wa-llahu ghafurun rahim.

"Prophet, why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful for you, seeking to please your wives? Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."

- Qur'an 66:1

The Prophet ﷺ used to drink honey in Zaynab's company. Aisha and Hafsa agreed that whichever of them he visited would tell him she smelled maghafir on him, a foul smelling resin, and they knew the statement was false. The verse came down about that plot. Why do you prohibit what Allah made lawful to you. The Book itself filed the plot under prohibiting what Allah allowed. A witness who plotted a false statement against the Prophet ﷺ in his own house, and recorded the plotting herself, has answered a question about her word that no isnad grade can undo. The occasion of the verse goes deeper than honey, and the school's own tafsir of this sura names it.

The Verses That Command Her Repentance

Source: Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 2, pp. 375-377, on Qur'an 66:1-5

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, and Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi

Claim: Aisha and Hafsa spied on the Prophet ﷺ, and the sura answers their plot with a command to repent

The school reads the sura's opening through the Imam ﵇.

قال اطلعت عائشة وحفصة على النبي ﵌ وهو مع مارية ، فقال النبي ﵌ والله ما أقربها ، فأمره الله ان يكفر يمينه

"Aisha and Hafsa saw the Prophet while he was with Mariya. The Prophet said, "By Allah, I will not go near her." Allah then commanded him to make atonement for his oath."

- Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 2, p. 375, on Qur'an 66:1

Ali ibn Ibrahim preserves the fuller occasion. The Prophet ﷺ was with Mariya in Hafsa's quarters on Hafsa's day. Hafsa confronted him, he forbade Mariya on himself to calm her, and he confided to Hafsa a secret about the succession, binding her silence. Hafsa told Aisha the same day. Aisha told Abu Bakr.

فتناول رسول الله مارية ، فعلمت حفصة بذلك فغضبت وأقبلت على رسول الله ﵌ وقالت يا رسول الله هذا في يومي وفي داري وعلى فراشي فاستحيا رسول الله منها ، فقال كفى فقد حرمت مارية على نفسي ولا أطأها بعد هذا ابدا وأنا أفضي إليك سرا فان أنت أخبرت به فعليك لعنة الله والملائكة والناس أجمعين

"The Messenger of Allah was with Mariya, and Hafsa learned of it. She became angry, went to the Messenger of Allah, and said, "the Messenger of Allah, this happened on my day, in my home, and on my bed." The Messenger of Allah felt ashamed before her. He said, "Enough."

"I have forbidden Mariya to myself, and I will never sleep with her again. I will also tell you a secret. If you reveal it, Allah, the angels, and all people will curse you.""

- Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 2, p. 376, on Qur'an 66:1

فقالت نعم ما هو ؟ فقال إن أبا بكر يلي الخلافة بعدي ثم من بعده أبوك فقالت من أخبرك بهذا قال الله أخبرني فأخبرت حفصة عائشة من يومها ذلك وأخبرت عائشة أبا بكر ، فجاء أبو بكر إلى عمر فقال له ان عائشة أخبرتني عن حفصة بشئ ولا أثق بقولها فاسأل أنت حفصة

"Hafsa asked, "What is it?" He replied, "Abu Bakr will rule as caliph after me. Then your father will rule after him." Hafsa asked, "Who told you this?" He replied, "Allah told me." Hafsa told Aisha that same day, and Aisha told Abu Bakr. Abu Bakr came to Umar and said, "Aisha told me something from Hafsa, yet I do not trust her account."

"Ask Hafsa yourself.""

- Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 2, p. 376, on Qur'an 66:1

Abu Bakr's own reaction belongs to the record. He heard the report through Aisha and told Umar he did not trust her word. The first caliph of the opposing school, her own father, treated her transmission as unreliable on the day of this sura's events. The Qur'an then turned to the two women directly.

إِن تَتُوبَآ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ فَقَدْ صَغَتْ قُلُوبُكُمَا ۖ وَإِن تَظَٰهَرَا عَلَيْهِ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ مَوْلَىٰهُ وَجِبْرِيلُ وَصَٰلِحُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ ۖ وَٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ بَعْدَ ذَٰلِكَ ظَهِيرٌ

in tatuba ila llahi fa-qad saghat qulubukuma, wa-in tazahara alayhi fa-inna llaha huwa mawlahu wa-jibrilu wa-salihu al-muminin, wa-al-malaikatu badha dhalika zahir.

"If you both repent to Allah, your hearts have strayed. If you support each other against him, Allah is his Protector, as are Gabriel, the righteous believers, and the angels after that, his supporters."

- Qur'an 66:4

وإذا أسر النبي إلى بعض أزواجه حديثا فلما نبأت به) اي أخبرت به (واظهره الله) يعني اظهر الله نبيه على ما أخبرت به وما هموا به (عرف بعضه) اي أخبرها وقال لم أخبرت بما أخبرتك وقوله (وأعرض عن بعض) قال لم يخبرهم بما علم مما هموا به (قالت من أنبأك هذا قال نبأني العليم الخبير ان تنويا إلى الله فقد صغت قلوبكما وان تظاهرا عليه فان الله هو مولاه وجبريل وصالح المؤمنين) يعني أمير المؤمنين

""When the Prophet told one of his wives a matter in secret, and she disclosed it," means that she told it. "Allah made it known to him," means that Allah informed His Prophet about what she had told and what they had planned."

""He made known part of it" means that he told her, "Why did you reveal what I told you?" "He passed over part of it" means that he did not tell them all that he knew of their plans. She asked, "Who told you this?" He replied, "The All-Knowing, the All-Aware told me." "If you both turn to Allah, your hearts have turned away."

"If you support one another against him, Allah is his protector, along with Gabriel and the righteous believers." This means the Commander of the Faithful ﵇."

- Tafsir al-Qummi, vol. 2, pp. 376-377, on Qur'an 66:3-4

The Imamite reading names the defender the verse raises for the Prophet ﷺ. Allah, Gabriel, and salih al-muminin, and the school reads that phrase as Ali ﵇, the Commander of the Faithful ﵇. The sura then closes its threat over the wives.

عَسَىٰ رَبُّهُۥٓ إِن طَلَّقَكُنَّ أَن يُبْدِلَهُۥٓ أَزْوَٰجًا خَيْرًۭا مِّنكُنَّ مُسْلِمَٰتٍۢ مُّؤْمِنَٰتٍۢ قَٰنِتَٰتٍۢ تَٰٓئِبَٰتٍ عَٰبِدَٰتٍۢ سَٰٓئِحَٰتٍۢ ثَيِّبَٰتٍۢ وَأَبْكَارًۭا

asa rabbuhu in tallaqakunna an yubdilahu azwajan khayran minkunna muslimatin muminatin qanitatin taibatin abidatin saihatin thayyibatin wa-abkaran.

"It may be that, if he divorces you, his Lord will give him wives better than you, women who submit to Allah, believe, are devout, repent, worship, fast, have been married before, and are virgins."

- Qur'an 66:5

The words are revelation's own. If you both repent to Allah, your hearts have deviated. The verse does not say their words deviated. It says their hearts did, and the sura answers with a threat of replacement. A book that testifies about the state of a witness's heart settles a standing question about that witness, and the answer stands against every report she carries alone. One command of this sura she broke within a generation.

The Command She Broke at Basra

Source: Qur'an 33:33; al-Shakiri, Thumma Uqira al-Jamal, p. 68

Claim: the Qur'an commanded the Prophet's wives to stay in their houses, and Aisha left hers to lead an army against the Imam of her time

Surat al-Ahzab gave the Prophet's ﷺ wives a command in their own name.

وَقَرْنَ فِى بُيُوتِكُنَّ وَلَا تَبَرَّجْنَ تَبَرُّجَ ٱلْجَٰهِلِيَّةِ ٱلْأُولَىٰ ۖ وَأَقِمْنَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتِينَ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَأَطِعْنَ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥٓ ۚ إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ ٱلرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ ٱلْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًۭا

wa-qarna fi buyutikunna wa-la tabarrajna tabarruja al-jahiliyyati al-ula, wa-aqimna al-salata wa-atina al-zakata wa-atiina llaha wa-rasulahu, innama yuridu llahu li-yudhhiba ankumu al-rijsa ahla al-bayti wa-yutahhirakum tathira.

"Remain in your homes. Do not display yourselves as women did in the former age of ignorance. Establish prayer, give zakah, and obey Allah and His Messenger. Allah desires to remove impurity from you, People of the House, and purify you completely."

- Qur'an 33:33

Remain in your houses. The command stands in the sura that also purified the House, and it was addressed to the wives as wives. Around thirty years later Aisha left her house at the head of an army, rode a camel to Basra, and made war on the Imam of her time. The Battle of the Camel killed thousands of Muslims, and the school holds her answerable for the blood.

ونادى الإمام : " يا محمد بن أبي بكر ، سلها هل وصل إليها شئ من الرماح والسهام ؟ " فسألها ، فقالت : نعم ، وصل إلي سهم ، خدش رأسي وسلمت من غيره ، الله بيني وبينكم

"The Imam called out, "Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, ask her whether any spears or arrows reached her." Muhammad asked her. She replied, "Yes. An arrow reached me and scratched my head. I escaped all else unharmed. Allah will judge between us.""

- al-Shakiri, Thumma Uqira al-Jamal, p. 68

فقال محمد : الله ليحكمن عليك يوم القيامة ما كان بينك وبين أمير المؤمنين حين تخرجين عليه وتؤلبين الناس على قتاله وتنبذين كتاب الله وراء ظهرك . فقالت عائشة : دعنا يا محمد وقل لصاحبك يحرسني

"Muhammad said, "Allah will judge you on the Day of Resurrection for what stood between you and the Commander of the Faithful ﵇. You rose against him, urged people to fight him, and cast Allah's Book behind your back." Aisha replied, "Leave us, Muhammad. Tell your companion to guard me.""

- al-Shakiri, Thumma Uqira al-Jamal, p. 68

The scene at the camel carries the whole ruling. Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, her own brother, tells her she threw the Book of Allah behind her back and stirred people to fight the Commander of the Faithful ﵇. Her answer asks for Ali's ﵇ guard. The woman who needed the Imam's ﵇ safe conduct at Basra is the same single mouth from which the age number reaches Kufa. The school sets such a report aside, and it leaves the witness herself to Allah.

What the School Does With Such a Witness

The Imami standard measures every report against the Book first. A report from a single witness, arriving late, through flagged chains, about the Prophet's ﷺ own household, meets a Book that testifies about the witness's heart. Such a report does not clear the bar, and the school sets it aside without calling the witness a liar in general. The second section quotes her own memory for a reason. Her testimony is taken there at full value, and even at full value it contradicts the number. The question here is narrower, whether her solitary word can carry the number alone when the Book testifies about her heart.

One boundary keeps this honest. The school does not use the slander affair against her. The tafsir of the school reads the verses of the Ifk as revealed about Mariya, with the mob's charge aimed at another, and the argument leaves the slander exactly where the school leaves it. The case here stands on what the school's own books document, the honey plot, the commanded repentance, the deviated hearts, and Basra. There remains the earliest layer of the record, and it never mentions a number at all.

5. The Earliest Biography Gives No Number

A Muslim While She Was Young

Source: the report of Asma bint Abi Bakr, preserved in the opposing school's books and relayed in Majallat Turathuna, vol. 21, p. 407

Speaker: Asma bint Abi Bakr

Claim: the whole family accepted Islam together, Aisha among them while still young

The witness question settled within its boundary, the record's earliest layer remains, and it is silent on the age. The first biography of the Prophet ﷺ, Ibn Ishaq's Sira in the recension of Ibn Hisham, never gives her a marriage age. The record it does preserve is more useful. Asma herself, the sister whose dates fixed the birth year in the second section, reports what happened the day her father accepted Islam.

قد روى المخالفون عن أسماء بنت أبي بكر أنها قالت : لما أسلم أبي جاء إلى منزله ، فما قام حتى أسلمنا ، وأسلمت عائشة وهي صغيرة

"The opposing school reports from Asma bint Abi Bakr that she said, "When my father accepted Islam, he came home. Before he stood up, we accepted Islam. Aisha accepted Islam while she was young.""

- Majallat Turathuna, vol. 21, p. 407, relaying the report of Asma in the opposing school's books

Abu Bakr accepted Islam in the opening years of the mission, among the very first. Asma says the household entered Islam with him, before he rose from his place, and that Aisha entered it while young. A girl old enough to be counted among the family's Muslims in the first years of the mission was born before the mission began. That is the same birth the sister arithmetic fixed, seventeen years before the Hijra. It also demolishes the birth the six-and-nine arithmetic itself requires, a birth years into the mission, since a girl counted among the family's first Muslims is older than that. On that anchor she entered Islam as an infant in arms, while the record counts her among conscious believers. The number that the Sira does carry sits elsewhere, in an appendix, and it fails to agree with itself.

The Addendum That Grew a Number

Source: Ibn Hisham, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, vol. 4, p. 210, the addenda

Dating: Ibn Hisham died 218 AH

Claim: Ibn Hisham's own appended note gives seven at marriage and nine or ten at consummation, without a chain

The number that the Sira does carry sits in Ibn Hisham's own appendix, appended around two centuries after the events, without any chain at all.

وتزوج رسول الله ﵌ عائشة بنت أبي بكر الصديق بمكة وهي بنت سبع سنين ، وبنى بها بالمدينة ، وهي بنت تسع سنين أو عشر ، ولم يتزوج رسول الله ﵌ بكرا غيرها ، زوجه إياها أبوها أبو بكر وأصدقها رسول الله ﵌ أربع مئة درهم

"The Messenger of Allah married Aisha, daughter of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, in Mecca when she was seven years old. He began living with her in Medina when she was nine or ten years old. She was the only virgin whom the Messenger of Allah married. Her father, Abu Bakr, gave her to him in marriage."

"The Messenger of Allah gave her a bridal gift of four hundred dirhams."

- Ibn Hisham, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, vol. 4, p. 210, the addenda

Seven at the contract, in Mecca, and nine or ten at consummation. The collections say six. The appendix says seven and leaves the upper figure open. Read against the map of the third section, the trail of the number now runs complete. No age in Ibn Ishaq. A chainless seven and nine or ten in Ibn Hisham. The fixed six and nine in the Kufan hadith of Hisham's last years. The number hardened as it traveled, and it hardened in Iraq. One prop of the charge remains, the dolls, and the dolls date themselves.

6. The Dolls That Date the Marriage Later

The Attack's Own Strongest Witness

Source: al-Baghawi's Masabih (hasan), quoted in al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq, vol. 4, pp. 68-69

Speaker: Aisha

Dating: the return from Tabuk (9 AH) or Hunayn (8 AH)

Claim: the doll play happened after one of the two expeditions

The addendum closed the trail of the number, and one prop of the charge remains, the dolls. The prepubescence claim rests on them. Aisha played with dolls, the argument runs, and girls who play with dolls have not reached puberty. She played with them after her wedding, therefore the wedding preceded her puberty. The whole inference hangs on when the playing happened. The report the attack itself quotes fixes the date.

ففي مصابيح البغوي من الحسان ، في باب عشرة النساء ، من كتاب النكاح ، عن عائشة قالت : « قدم رسول اللَّه صلَّى اللَّه عليه واله وسلَّم من غزوة تبوك أو حنين ، وفي بهوتها [ 2 ] ستر ، فهبّت ريح فكشفت ناحية الستر عن بنات لعائشة تلعب بها . فقال : ما هذه يا عائشة ؟ ! قالت : بناتي .

ورأى بينهنّ فرسا له جناحان من رقاع ، فقال : وما هذا الذي أرى وسطهنّ ؟ ! قالت : فرس . قال : وما هذا الذي عليه ؟ ! قالت : جناحان . قال : الفرس يكون له جناحان ؟ ! قالت : أما سمعت أنّ لسليمان خيلا لها أجنحة ؟ ! قالت : فضحك حتّى رأيت نواجذه » [ 1 ] .

"In al-Baghawi's Masabih, among the sound reports, in the chapter on living with women, from Aisha, who said, the Messenger of Allah returned from the expedition to Tabuk or Hunayn."

"A curtain covered a recess in her room. The wind lifted a corner of the curtain and uncovered some dolls of Aisha's she played with. He asked, what are these, Aisha? She answered, my daughters."

"He saw among them a horse with two wings of cloth. He asked, what is this I see among them? She answered, a horse. He asked, and what is this upon it? She answered, two wings."

"He asked, a horse with two wings? She answered, have you not heard that Solomon had horses with wings? She said, he laughed until I could see his molar teeth."

- al-Baghawi's Masabih (hasan), quoted in al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq, vol. 4, pp. 68-69

Tabuk is 9 AH. Hunayn is 8 AH. On the attack's own arithmetic, which makes her nine in 1 AH, the doll scene happens when she is sixteen or seventeen. The dolls stop proving prepubescence at the wedding and start proving something else. A young woman raised in the Prophet's ﷺ house kept her toys, and he laughed with her about them.

The School's Own Commentary on This Very Text

Source: al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq li-Nahj al-Haqq, vol. 4, pp. 69-70

Dating: 14th century AH

Claim: the report dates her playing after the conquest of Mecca, when she was about seventeen

Al-Muzaffar, writing the school's systematic answer, draws the conclusion from the same report.

فإنّها صريحة في لعبها بعد إحدى الغزاتين ، وهما كانتا بعد فتح مكَّة ، ومنه يعلم ما في قوله أخيرا : « ولعب عائشة كان في أوائل الهجرة » .

"The report is explicit that her playing came after one of the two expeditions, and both of them came after the conquest of Mecca."

"From this the flaw in his final statement is known, that Aisha's playing was in the first years of the Hijra."

- al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq li-Nahj al-Haqq, vol. 4, p. 69

ففيه : إنّ الصورة هي : الشكل ، كما في القاموس [ 1 ] ، فتكون الهيئة منها ، وتعليله لا وجه له ؛ لأنّ عائشة لم تكن صغيرة حين اللعب بالأفراس ، بل كانت بنت سبع عشرة تقريبا على رأيهم ، لما سبق من تصريح رواية البغوي بلعبها بها بعد إحدى الغزاتين .

"In it is this, the image is the shape, as in al-Qamus, so the form is one."

"His explanation has no basis, because Aisha was not a young child when she played with the horses. She was about seventeen by their own reckoning, because al-Baghawi's report explicitly dates her playing with them after one of the two expeditions."

- al-Muzaffar, Dala'il al-Sidq li-Nahj al-Haqq, vol. 4, p. 70

Al-Muzaffar even catches a Sunni commentator trimming the report, dropping the word dolls from the winged-horse scene so the dating problem disappears. The school's literature had already worked this ground centuries ago.

The Other Doll Reports Do Not Escape

Source: Bukhari and Muslim, the earlier doll report; al-Khattabi's gloss; al-Nasa'i, al-Sunan al-Kubra, the racing report

Claim: the other doll reports carry no date, and the puberty gloss is a jurist's inference

The al-Zuhri route adds that her dolls came with her to the wedding scene. That detail belongs to the same bridal tableau that says seven where the others say six, so it dates nothing beyond the story that carries it. The earlier doll report, the one in Bukhari and Muslim about her playmates hiding when the Prophet ﷺ entered, carries no date at all. The gloss the attack quotes from al-Khattabi, that dolls were permitted because she had not reached puberty, is a jurist's inference. No witness states it. The racing report in al-Nasa'i's larger collection shows the word the argument leans on, jariya, applied to Aisha as a married woman on a journey. She raced the Prophet ﷺ on foot and beat him, then lost a rematch years later after she had gained weight. The word names a young wife in that usage. The dolls argument, examined on its own evidence, dissolves.

7. The Jewish Law the Attack Quotes

The Higher Floor the Attack Appeals To

The dolls dissolved, and the last support the attack reaches for is a moral floor it claims an older scripture held. The attack's appendix on Jewish culture argues that Judaism set a higher standard, girls at twelve and a half, and that Muhammad ﷺ fell below it. The sources it cites are real, but they say far more than the appendix repeats, and what they say removes the floor the argument stands on.

The appendix's own bibliography points to the Mishnah, and the Mishnah legislates in plain words, in Mishnah Niddah 5:4. A girl of three years and one day whose father has arranged her betrothal is betrothed through intercourse, with the full legal consequences attached. The Babylonian Talmud, Niddah 45a, discusses the same threshold and records the empress Yusteni's words, that she married at six and gave birth at seven. The Gemara debates whether a girl that young can conceive, without disputing that the marriage law stands. Mishnah Ketubot 4:1, the very text the appendix cites for the twelve-and-a-half framework, is about the father's property rights over his daughter's compensation awards. A father administering his minor daughter's affairs is the world the age of twelve and a half actually regulated.

What the Cited Texts Legislate
Source Provision it states
Mishnah Niddah 5:4 Betrothal by intercourse valid from three years and one day
Bavli Niddah 45a Marriage at six and birth at seven recorded in discussion of that law
Mishnah Ketubot 4:1 Father holds the young woman's awards until maturity at twelve and a half

This matters for one reason. A writer who measures the Prophet ﷺ by a moral floor must first show the floor existed. The tradition the attack's own appendix appeals to legislated for marriage with girls far younger than nine, in its founding legal texts. Those texts were codified centuries before Islam and stayed authoritative centuries after. The argument from a higher Jewish standard collapses, and with it the claim that the Prophet ﷺ broke a norm the surrounding scriptures had established. The Qur'an's own standard is a different matter, and a stricter one, and it is the school's.

8. The Qur'an's Readiness Test

The Verse the Attack Reads as a License

The higher floor collapsed, and one test remains, the Book's own. The attack reads Qur'an 65:4 as a license to marry prepubescent girls, a reading the site has already answered in the Imami reading of Qur'an 65:4. There the wa- construction of the verse carries the doubt of the previous category forward, and the third category names women past the age whose menses are uncertain. The school does not read the verse as the attack needs it read.

The Readiness Test the Book States

The Qur'an's own marriage test runs the other way.

وَابْتَلُوا الْيَتَامَى حَتَّىٰ إِذَا بَلَغُوا النِّكَاحَ فَإِنْ آنَسْتُمْ مِنْهُمْ رُشْدًا فَادْفَعُوا إِلَيْهِمْ أَمْوَالَهُمْ

wa-ibtalū al-yatāmā ḥattā idhā balaghū al-nikāḥa fa-in ānastum minhum rushdan fa-dfaʿū ilayhim amwālahum

"Test the orphans until, when they reach the capacity for marriage, if you perceive in them sound judgment, hand over their property to them."

- Qur'an 4:6

The verse pairs two conditions. Reaching the capacity for marriage, and perceiving sound judgment. The verse's own subject is the property of orphans, and its contested phrase drew the tradition's own survey.

فقيل : إنه بلوغ الحلم ، عن الشعبي ، وقيل : هو أن يبلغ ثماني عشرة سنة . وقال السدي : هو أن يبلغ ثلاثين سنة ، ثم نسخها قوله ( حتى إذا بلغوا النكاح ) الآية . وقال أبو حنيفة : إذا بلغ خمسا وعشرين سنة ، دفع المال إليه ، وقبل ذلك يمنع منه إذا لم يؤنس منه الرشد ، وقيل : إنه لا حد له .

بل هو أن يبلغ ويكمل عقله ، ويؤنس منه الرشد ، فيسلم إليه ماله ، وهذا أقوى الوجوه

"Some said it means reaching puberty, based on al-Shabi's view. Others said it means reaching eighteen years of age. Al-Suddi said it means reaching thirty years. Then the verse, "until they reach marriageable age," abrogated this view. Abu Hanifa said that a person's property is handed over at twenty-five. Before that age, it is withheld if sound judgment has not appeared. Others said there is no fixed age. The person must reach maturity, have a fully developed mind, and show sound judgment. Then the property is handed over. This is the strongest view."

- al-Tabrisi, Majma al-Bayan, vol. 4, p. 194, on Qur'an 4:6

Tabrisi runs the readings from puberty to eighteen to thirty to twenty-five, and his strongest reading has no fixed age at all. The capacity arrives with judgment and is certified by it. A birthday count alone certifies nothing. Whatever any report claims about a marriage, the Book the school submits every report to legislates a readiness test here. The Imams ﵈ set the procedure that follows from it. Present the report to the Book, and what conflicts with the Book is set aside.

So the Qur'an licenses nothing the attack describes, the six-and-nine report fails the joint test the Book states, and the chronological record sets the marriage near eighteen. That is the whole case, assembled.

Final Verdict

The charge that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ consummated a marriage with a nine-year-old Aisha fails on four independent grounds, any one of which is sufficient.

First, the chronology. The Sunni tradition's own biographical numbers, Asma's doubly confirmed birth and death years and the attested ten-year gap between the sisters, fix Aisha's birth seventeen years before the Hijra. On those numbers she was near eighteen at the consummation. Ibn Qutayba's own death notice already contradicts his marriage notice before any external evidence is consulted. Asma's own report adds that the family accepted Islam together in the mission's first years, with Aisha young but counting, which pins the same birth year from a second direction.

Second, the transmission. The number surfaces only in second century Iraq. Muslim's own page shows three route families that contradict each other on the figure, six against seven. Every lower link is Kufan or its Yemeni relay, and below the one elder who knew her, the Kufan chain holds a child of eight and men born after her death. Malik of Medina accepted only what Hisham transmitted before leaving, Ibn Khirash recorded the chain's wording collapsing across Hisham's three Kufan visits, and Hisham died in Baghdad in the caliph's company in 145 or 146. The earliest biography carries no number at all, and the isnad-less addendum that finally gives one says seven and nine or ten.

Third, the witness. Every chain ends at Aisha alone. The Qur'an commands her and Hafsa to repent and testifies that their hearts deviated, threatens the wives with replacement, and names Ali ﵇ among the Prophet's ﷺ defenders against the two of them. She records the honey plot against the Prophet ﷺ in her own words. Her own father said he did not trust her word. She broke the command to stay in her house and made war on the Imam of her time at Basra.

Fourth, the supporting evidence. The dolls argument, the only route to prepubescence, dates itself to 8 or 9 AH in its own words. That dating makes Aisha sixteen or seventeen in the doll scene on the attack's arithmetic and dissolves the inference entirely. And the Jewish law invoked as the higher floor legislates for betrothal by intercourse from three years and one day, in the Mishnah and Talmud the attack's own appendix cites.

The certain conclusion is that age nine is not established history. The best-supported figure from the tradition's own arithmetic is eighteen. A charge that must stand on a witness the Qur'an questioned, through chains that all converge on one late century in Kufa, has lost its case. It stands against the arithmetic of two independent biographical lines, and it rests on a doll dated seven years past the wedding.
