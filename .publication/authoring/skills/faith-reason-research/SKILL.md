# Faith & Reason Research

Use this skill as the intellectual layer before layout, HTML, publishing, or mechanical style validation.
Read `~/.agents/prose/contract.md` and `drafting.md` before composing prose.

Read the supplied note or article in full before drafting. Do not begin by rewriting sentences or searching isolated keywords.

## 1. Select the mode

Read `~/.agents/prose/article-structure.md` for the governing category and
anatomy. This research module supplies the evidence and reasoning; it does not
create a second draft or publish independently. The shared article workflow
owns corpus collection, pagination/coverage and verification commands.

## 2. Reconstruct the problem before researching

Create an internal research map before collecting new evidence.

Identify:

1. the exact question;
2. the article's intended thesis;
3. the strongest opposing thesis, when one exists;
4. the audience and premises the argument is allowed to assume;
5. the exact school, author, denomination, or historical group to which each disputed claim belongs;
6. the conclusions that would be too broad for the evidence;
7. the terms, verses, reports, or historical claims carrying the dispute.

For debate mode, formalize the opponent's strongest case into numbered premises and a conclusion. Do not rebut a slogan if the source supplies a stronger argument.

Run a **scope audit**. If the evidence establishes a claim about Ash'arism, Calvinism, one tafsir, one narrator, or one school, do not silently widen it to Islam, Christianity, Sunnism, or all scholars.

## 2a. Record corpus coverage

Follow the research and citation stages in
`~/.agents/prose/article-workflow.md`, including source inventories,
continuation batches and recorded dispositions. Do not maintain a second
retrieval procedure here.

## 3. Build the claim-evidence map

Before prose, make an internal matrix with these columns:

`Claim | Type | Evidence needed | Best source | Qualifier | Opponent reply | Status`

Claim types include lexical, grammatical, Qur'anic, riwa'i, historical, theological, philosophical, and logical.

Every major paragraph in the final article must correspond to a claim in this map. Every load-bearing claim must have evidence or be explicitly marked as inference.

Do not draft until the main chain can be stated as:

`question -> evidence -> conclusion -> next question`

Read `references/research-framework.md` for the full evidence order.

## 4. Ground contested language first

Use lexical research only when the argument turns on a word, construction, or translation.

For each contested term:

1. identify the exact form and construction in the passage, not only the root;
2. consult at least two classical lexicons when the sense is genuinely disputed;
3. test the proposed sense against classical poetry or early usage when useful evidence exists;
4. survey Qur'anic cross-usage without assuming that one occurrence fixes every other occurrence;
5. state the semantic range first, then argue which sense the local context selects.

Never claim that a dictionary makes figurative, technical, or theological usage impossible. Lexicons establish the available language and burden of proof. Context establishes the intended sense.

If poetry does not provide a clean witness, say so. Never bend a verse to fit.

## 5. Build the Imami reading in authority order

When the article asks what the school of Ahl al-Bayt teaches, use this default order unless the subject requires another:

1. **Qur'an**: full local context plus relevant cross-passages.
2. **Riwa'i tafsir of the Ahl al-Bayt**: reports explaining the verse or concept.
3. **Hadith and doctrinal reports**: especially reports that define the theological distinction at issue.
4. **Early Imami scholars**: such as al-Saduq, al-Mufid, al-Tusi, al-Tabrisi when relevant.
5. **Later Imami scholarship and philosophy**: use to clarify, systematize, or expose legitimate internal variation.

Do not flatten disagreement. If sources differ in explanation but share a narrower boundary, state the convergence at that narrower level.

Distinguish clearly between:

- what the Qur'an explicitly says;
- what a narration explicitly says;
- how an exegete reads it;
- how a theologian systematizes it;
- what the article itself infers.

## 6. Preserve source records and translate them fully

Never reduce a useful report to a rhetorically convenient fragment.

For each primary record preserve, when supplied and relevant:

- speaker;
- narrator or chain;
- complete passage needed for the argument;
- source title;
- volume, page, chapter, or report number;
- grading or attribution caveat;
- nearby clause that limits or explains the quoted line.

Translations must account for the full Arabic quoted on the page. Do not quote three Arabic sentences and translate one.

Translate into natural modern English while preserving technical distinctions. Define a technical term on first use when one English word would erase a necessary distinction.

Do not silently treat `created`, `originated`, `uncaused`, `eternal`, `fabricated`, `will`, `command`, `speech`, `knowledge`, or similar terms as synonyms.

Read `~/.agents/prose/translation.md` for translation operations and
`~/.agents/prose/drafting.md` for authored prose.

## 7. Represent the opposing position from its own sources

In debate mode, distinguish three levels where relevant:

1. the popular polemical form;
2. the strongest classical or scholarly form;
3. a sophisticated reply to the obvious objection.

Use the opponent's own primary or authoritative sources for what they believe. Finding aids may locate sources but do not become the cited authority.

Do not attribute one denomination's doctrine to the entire religion.

When the opponent's argument attacks a doctrine your school does not hold, make that scope error explicit before answering the broader philosophical issue.

## 8. Stress-test the argument after the evidence is built

Do not begin with fallacy labels. First reconstruct the inference fairly.

Test each load-bearing step for:

- ambiguous middle or equivocation;
- hidden or missing premise;
- invalid inference or non sequitur;
- scope shift;
- category mistake;
- circularity or imported premise;
- inconsistency with another accepted premise;
- counterexample;
- over-generation;
- reductio consequence;
- causal-role-to-essence inference;
- modal confusion between necessity, certainty, foreknowledge, decree, ability, and compulsion;
- conflation of capacity, knowledge, intention, will, act, and effect;
- burden shifting.

Use a named fallacy only when the label is technically correct. Otherwise call the problem what it is.

For every debate test give four things:

1. the inference being tested;
2. the problem;
3. the question to ask the opponent;
4. what the opponent must establish to continue.

Build branches for likely replies. A strong debate section should tell the reader where to go when the opponent answers yes, no, or introduces a new distinction.

Read `references/debate-mode.md` for the full pressure-test method.

## 9. Draft only after the chain is complete

Write the article as a continuous line of thought.

Order sections by the subject and reasoning. Independent findings may stand alone.
Explain a relationship where it is needed, without inventing a question to hand
off to the next section. Apply the shared deletion test after each section.

Each paragraph should normally do one job:

1. state a claim or question;
2. present or interpret the evidence;
3. explain what follows;
4. supply a qualification needed to interpret the claim.

Avoid commentary that merely announces the document's movement. Use content itself as the transition.

Do not force every article into lexicon -> poetry -> Qur'an -> hadith if a layer is irrelevant. The framework is an evidence order, not a checklist of decorative sections.

## 10. Mode-specific close

Use `~/.agents/prose/article-structure.md`. Conclusions stay within the evidence;
distinguish an argument's failure from proof that an opposing doctrine is false.

## 11. Full-article audit before delivery

Reread the complete draft from beginning to end. Do not audit only changed sentences.

Locate and fix concrete defects; use `council-article.md` for final council review:

- **Readability**: does every paragraph make sense on first read?
- **Connectedness**: does the relationship between sections remain understandable without forced transitions?
- **Comprehensiveness**: are all load-bearing premises and obvious objections covered?
- **Source fidelity**: are records, qualifications, and translations complete?
- **Argument strength**: do conclusions follow from the evidence?
- **Difficulty to escape** for debate: can an informed opponent exit through an unaddressed distinction, scope problem, or missing premise?

For debate, identify the three strongest escape routes and either answer them in the article or narrow the conclusion so they no longer defeat it.

If the prose reads like research notes stitched together, rewrite the structure rather than adding more transition phrases.

## 12. Handoff to the publishing skill

Within either entry point, return the research dossier to the shared article
workflow. The master is reviewed there before any website rendering.

Provide the publishing layer with:

- mode/category;
- exact thesis;
- opponent and scope when applicable;
- section order;
- claim-evidence map;
- contested terms;
- complete source records and translations;
- debate pressure tests when applicable;
- unresolved verification items.

The publishing skill should format and verify this research product. It should not invent the article's intellectual architecture while rendering HTML.
