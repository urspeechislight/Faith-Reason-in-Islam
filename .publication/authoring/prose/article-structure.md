# Article structure

Read before planning or drafting any canonical master. This file owns category
selection and article anatomy for both destinations. Format references map this
structure into Markdown or HTML; they do not invent a different article.

## Category and close

Choose the governing purpose, not the presence of hadith or a polemical source.

| Category | Purpose | Anatomy |
|---|---|---|
| debate | Defend a thesis against an opposing claim | Opening with actual premises and conclusion, The Facts table, evidence and strongest objection, optional glossary, Final Verdict |
| exegesis | Explain the school's reading of one passage | Verse and question, The Facts table, evidence by reading move, The Reading |
| narration | Present one sourced story in its sequence | Faithful narrative, one separate Commentary section, a content-named ending if needed, source footer |
| commentary | Investigate a question across reports or scholars | Supported answer and necessary context, evidence with interpretation beside each source, alternatives and limits; synthesis only if needed |

Debate alone carries a Final Verdict. Exegesis has no premise block. Narration
has no thesis or Facts table. Commentary requires none of those and must not
repeat its argument in a separate Commentary section. A survey of several
unrelated reports is commentary, not one narration. State category and section
plan and proceed when purpose is clear; ask only about material ambiguity.

For debate, record the actual opponent and scope in the research plan and
master frontmatter. Website metadata uses christian, sunni, orientalist, or
secular when applicable. Consult the research framework for evidence order and
`references/debate-mode.md` in faith-reason-research for pressure tests.

## Sections and facts

Build and check one evidence section at a time against the claim-evidence map
and source records. Preserve arguments and evidence when converting supplied
notes; a scoped research article need not include every search result. Record
material exclusions in its dossier. Write the opening after the evidence and
apply `drafting.md`; do not add a second abstract of every section.
For debate, let the compact premises and conclusion provide the opening
argument. Do not precede them with prose repeating every premise. The subtitle
gives the answer briefly; facts locate sources; the body develops the reasoning.
Do not retell the same argument in all four places. Required comparison criteria
can guide the analysis without a paragraph announcing the evaluation procedure.

Debate/exegesis use content-named H2 sections; avoid redundant H3/H5 headings
inside those sections, since the website uses a single heading layer there.
Narration commentary uses content-named H3 cards with live links from the
relevant narrative passage. Headings mark a real division, never a fixed
Introduction / Example / Implication / Conclusion sequence.

Debate/exegesis evidence units and narration commentary cards carry terse
key-value facts immediately before their first quotation, after any necessary
introduction. Useful keys are Source, Speaker, Dating, Claim, Verse, Locus.
Do not fabricate missing fields or duplicate an entire prose paragraph in a
card. Commentary may use a card or table where it helps; neither is obligatory.

Debate/exegesis place `The Facts` directly after the opening. Use real tables:

- Debate: Claim | Actor | Date | Place | Source | Qualifier | ↗.
- Exegesis: Reading | Basis | Qualifier | ↗.

Omit genuinely unavailable fields; split by fact type if needed. Place contains
location alone. Qualifier and the detail link are required. Extract only from
claims already grounded and explained in the article. Use stated, reported,
attributed, likely, or disputed as appropriate, retaining any more precise
qualification needed from the prose. Never turn a report into an established
fact by putting it in a table. Every row links to its supporting heading.
The table indexes the argument; the card identifies its immediate evidence.

## Narration and serial articles

Translate the story in its source sequence. Preserve quoted speech at its
actual place; never substitute a summary, invented bridge event, motive, or
scene. Resolve speakers only from evidence, following `translate-style.md`.
Separate narrator framing from extended direct speech using `paragraphs.md`.

Establish provenance together in a leading commentary card and source metadata:
book, compiler, chain, date, relay loci and state of the text. Dialogue from
that continuous source need not repeat its citation in every speech tag;
separate evidence quotations remain cited. Scripture embedded in the story
cites the passage as preserved there, with its transmission source. Do not
replace a historical speaker's wording with a different modern Bible edition.
Cluster related interpretation into one commentary card per narrative movement.
Finish the story before commentary; do not turn it into an unrequested debate.

Long narrations split at narrative boundaries into a hub and parts, with Part N
of M, previous/next links and a hub link. Update the hub when adding parts.
Keep a consolidated timeline on the hub; appendices can be linked siblings.

## Titles and headings

Use a plain statement or direct question that the evidence supports. Avoid
citation-shaped titles such as The Hadith of X on Y, unsupported originality,
compressed scholarly labels and pipeline jargon. Follow the shared prose
contract's punctuation rules. The summary/subtitle is a complete sentence;
put necessary provenance after the content, not in a provenance-first opener.
The same standard applies to the catalogue blurb and meta description. Read
all headings and summaries aloud and remove duplicate heading/body statements.

## Conditional debate structure

For a debate explicitly answering Christian arguments, ground contested terms
first when relevant, establish the requested Islamic reading from its sources,
then state the actual Christian position from its primary holders. The research
framework owns the linguistic method and Imami authority order. A finding aid
is not evidence of a doctrine. Preserve denominational and historical scope.

Include a content-named comparison section before the verdict, with a fact card
identifying its Christian sources. Compare the established positions on ethical,
rational, textual-fidelity and economy grounds. State the criteria and apply
them fairly in both directions; a required section does not predetermine which
position survives. Use only evidence established and cited in the master.
Do not introduce an opponent or this anatomy into a non-adversarial survey.

A contested-words summary identifies who reads which word how; it is not a
word list or Method label. Its section must carry the relevant lexicon evidence.
Lexicons establish semantic range; context and usage support the selected sense.
