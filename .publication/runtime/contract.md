# Prose contract

Applies to explanatory prose, articles, notes, and their editorial revisions.
Keep routine chat brief. For substantial writing, give enough explanation for
an intelligent reader unfamiliar with the subject to follow on the first read.
A requested literary register controls tone; source fidelity always governs.

## Priorities

Preserve facts, source wording, attribution, and the author's argument first.
Make the reasoning understandable second. Improve rhythm and economy third.
Never change a qualification, negation, condition, quantifier, or degree of
certainty to satisfy a style preference or readability score. Do not turn
most into all, some into none, an attributed report into an established event,
or evidence of a belief into proof that the belief is true.

Keep primary-source statements, a commentator's reading, and the author's own
inferences distinguishable. Explain the step from evidence to conclusion.
Use proves only when the evidence entails that conclusion. Give the date of
an attributed speaker separately from the date of the surviving source when
that difference affects the argument. A citation locates evidence; reliability
requires its own assessment.

## Write the substance

State who did what, what the source says, or why the evidence supports the
claim. Explain an unfamiliar term through its meaning or a concrete example
before relying on it. Use ordinary verbs, complete sentences, and enough
words to carry the whole thought. Vary sentence length to suit the meaning.
Short sentences and low reading-grade scores do not establish clarity.

Each paragraph should contribute a claim, evidence, reasoning, or a necessary
qualification. Explain a quotation's relevance where the reader needs it;
one explanation can cover closely related quotations. Keep the explanation
beside the evidence, even when the quotation itself is collapsed.

Connect ideas where the relationship needs explanation. Do not require an
emphatic ending, a backward reference, or a preview after every exhibit.
A self-contained argument is useful. Give it a descriptive title, its evidence,
a complete citation, and the qualification needed to understand it alone.
Do not repeat a conclusion simply to create emphasis.

Replace argumentative theater and decorative metaphors with the exact claim.
An argument does not need to close a door, carry a burden, silence an opponent,
or complete a journey. Do not announce what the document will do, praise the
method, or describe a claim as devastating. Show the reasoning. Avoid generic
one-line verdicts, abstract noun stacks, forced triads, and mirrored paragraph
endings. Rewrite the sentence or paragraph from its meaning; synonym swaps
usually preserve the defective structure.

Avoid decorative not-X-but-Y framing, litotes, irony, sarcasm, and rhetorical
questions that merely introduce their own answers. Preserve a genuine contrast
or negation when it carries the claim. Never remove a source's actual contrast
or uncertainty. Use no em dashes in authored explanatory prose. Avoid
explanatory parentheses when a separate clause is clearer. Required source
apparatus and verbatim quotations keep their own punctuation.

## Economy and internal process

An authored sentence must add a fact, evidence, explanation, inference, necessary
qualification or useful locator. Delete empty introductions, descriptions of how
an argument unfolds, ritual concessions and repeated conclusions. Do not replace
an empty sentence with a smoother empty sentence. Read first and last sentences
of paragraphs together to catch a recurring announce/explain/recap pattern.

Keep research instructions, source-selection strategy and review deliberations
in the working dossier. A source's identity or reliability belongs in the article
when it affects a claim; boasting that evidence comes from an opponent's own
tradition does not. Cite actual dependence and uncertainty honestly.

Do not introduce litotes, ironic asides or decorative contrasts in new prose.
For necessary contrasts and negations, state the exact distinction. Preserve
source wording and force in quotations and faithful translations. A prohibition
on decorative framing never authorizes strengthening a qualified claim.

For articles and research notes, read `~/.agents/prose/drafting.md` before drafting.
Its deletion test applies section by section, including openings and closings.

## Protected material and review

An editorial pass preserves quotations, translations paired with source text,
source metadata, citations, links and anchors, numbers, and table data.
Authored prose in tables and cards remains editable and reviewable; preserve
the numerical data and cited source wording while correcting that prose.
Here "source metadata" means bibliographic identity, edition, locus and
transmission data. Authored Claim cards and evaluative descriptions of speakers
are reviewable prose; edit their wording while preserving their factual claims.
Translation changes require a separate source-aligned fidelity pass. Do not
invent narrative bridges, motives, dialogue tone, or unnamed speakers.
A literal grammatical gloss that makes no sense in English needs source-aware
translation review, not cosmetic polishing.

Before delivering substantial prose, read every paragraph for meaning. Ask
what it tells the reader, which evidence supports it, which reasoning remains
unstated, and whether the claim changed during revision. Report an apparent
factual or logical defect separately rather than silently changing the author's
position. Use exact examples and evidence, not self-awarded quality scores.

For Islamic-note and Faith & Reason publication work, follow the shared
editorial procedure at ~/.agents/prose/editorial.md and run its artifact check
before filing or publishing. Routine messages do not need review files.
This contract owns prose judgment; the destination skill owns formatting,
source verification, and rendering. A formatting rule cannot override fidelity.
