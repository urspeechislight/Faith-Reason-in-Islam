The Argument Under Examination

A common Christian argument presents the Qur'an with a dilemma. The Qur'an tells the People of the Book that the Torah and the Gospel contain guidance. It tells them to judge by what God revealed in them. Qur'an 7:157 also says that Jews and Christians find the Prophet ﷺ written with them in the Torah and the Gospel.

Premise 1. The Qur'an commands the Book's people to judge by their books

The Qur'an calls the Torah and the Gospel guidance and light. It tells Jews and Christians to judge by them. It says the People of the Book find the Prophet ﷺ written in them, at Qur'an 5:43 to 5:48 and 7:157.

Premise 2. The seventh century books carried the crucifixion and the sonship

Gospel manuscripts copied centuries before the Prophet ﷺ already carry the crucifixion narratives and the doctrines the church taught alongside them.

Premise 3. Either the books were reliable or they were corrupt

If those books were reliable enough to identify Muhammad ﷺ, why should their testimony about the crucifixion and other Christian doctrines be rejected? But if they had already been corrupted, how could the Qur'an appeal to them at all?

The objection also has five companion forms. One stands on Qur'an 6:34, there is no changer for the words of Allah. One reads Qur'an 10:94, ask those who read the Book before you, as a certificate of the codices. One says the corrupt Injil is a book nobody held. One dates the corruption doctrine to Ibn Hazm of Cordoba. One reads Qur'an 3:55 as a promise of Christian standing until the Day of Resurrection.

The dilemma depends on one assumption. If a book still contains genuine revelation, the whole book must therefore be textually and doctrinally reliable. The Qur'an does not make that assumption. It describes the earlier scriptures as containing truth that remains recognizable while also accusing some of their custodians of concealing, distorting, or altering what they received. The two claims appear side by side.

The question is therefore not whether the Torah and Gospel contained truth in the seventh century. The Qur'an plainly says they did. The question is whether affirming surviving truth requires affirming everything found in the books that carried it. That is the question this article examines.

The Facts

The Qur'an does not require complete preservation of earlier scripture. It affirms surviving truth and accuses custodians of alteration in the same passages.

Early Muslim interpretation understood Qur'an 7:157 primarily through description and recognition, not through a surviving literal name line.

Muslim sources simultaneously cite surviving biblical material and accuse the same scriptural communities of alteration or concealment.

Citation of surviving material does not amount to certification of an entire codex.

1. What Qur'an 7:157 Actually Claims

Qur'an 7:157 describes the Prophet ﷺ as the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. The verse does not say, they find the name Muhammad written. Its wording is broader. They find him as someone described or recorded in the scriptures they possess.

The Arabic word maktuban, written, describes the condition in which they find him. In ordinary English the idea is close to saying, they find him described in their books. That still leaves an open question. What did those descriptions contain.

They might include a name. They might include his characteristics, location, office, conduct, or other identifying signs. The grammar alone does not decide that question. The early commentaries do, and the recognition reports add their witness. Both are examined in the sections that follow.

One parallel shows the pattern. At Qur'an 18:77 the two travelers find a wall about to collapse, and the wall's state is part of what they find. The verb to find takes its object in a described state. Qur'an 7:157 works the same way. They find him written, and written names the state in which he is met.

The conclusion is modest and decisive. Qur'an 7:157 does not make its claim depend upon one surviving sentence containing the name Muhammad. It claims that the Prophet ﷺ was recognizable from what remained in their scriptural tradition. What that remainder contained is the work of the commentaries and the recognition reports that follow.

2. How Early Muslims Understood the Written Description

The verse leaves open what the written material contained. A consistent pattern appears across the early commentaries. Recognition is explained through description and prophetic identity, sometimes including the name. None of these glosses requires the further conclusion that the entire codex was textually reliable.

On the Shia side the earliest layer reads the phrase through Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇.

Source: Muhammad ibn Masud al-Ayyashi (d.320)

87 - عن الثمالي عن أبي جعفر ﵇ قال : في قوله : (يجدونه) يعنى اليهود والنصارى صفة محمد واسمه مكتوبا عندهم في التورية والإنجيل ، يأمرهم بالمعروف وينهاهم عن المنكر .

"From al-Thumali, from Abu Jafar peace be upon him, on His saying "they find him." He means the Jews and the Christians find the description of Muhammad God's blessings and peace on him and his family and his name written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. He commands them with honor and forbids them the dishonorable."

- Muhammad ibn Masud al-Ayyashi (d.320), Tafsir al-Ayyashi, v02 p31, hadith 87, with parallels in Bihar al-Anwar v15 p229 and al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an v02 p594

The school's own gloss at the verse itself comes from al-Tabrisi.

Source: al-Tabrisi (d.548)

"As for "whom they find," it means they find his description, those of the Children of Israel who follow him."

- al-Tabrisi (d.548), Jawami al-Jami, v01 p710

Source: al-Fayd al-Kashani (d.1091)

إِنِّي قَرَأْتُ نَعْتَكَ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ : مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ ، وُلِدَ مُولَدُهُ بِمَكَّةَ ، وَمُهَاجَرُهُ بِطِيبَةَ ، لَيْسَ بِفَظٍّ وَلَا غَلِيظٍ وَلَا سَخَّابٍ وَلَا مُتَرَنِّنٍ بِالْفَحْشِ

"I read your description in the Torah. Muhammad son of Abdullah God's blessings and peace on him and his family, his birth at Mecca, his emigration to Tayba. He is not harsh, not coarse, not loud, and not versed in filth."

- al-Fayd al-Kashani (d.1091), al-Safi, v02 p242, from Majalis al-Mufid, chain to Ibn Abbas

Name, birthplace, emigration place, and four traits of character. That is the content in the mouth of a Jewish reader, and the form matches every other item in the table.

The Sunni layer starts with Qatada, a generation after the Companions.

Source: Ibn Abi Hatim al-Razi (d.327)

حدثنا محمد بن يحيي ، أنبأ العباس بن الوليد ثنا يزيد ، عن سعيد ، عن قتادة قال : الَّذِي يَجِدُونَه مَكْتُوباً عِنْدَهُمْ فِي التَّوْراةِ قال : يَجِدُونَ نَعْتَهُ وَأَمْرَهُ وَنُبُوَّتَهُ مَكْتُوباً عِنْدَهُمْ .

"Ibn Abi Hatim transmitted to us, from al-Abbas ibn al-Walid, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada, on "whom they find written with them in the Torah." They find his description, his affair, and his prophethood written with them."

- Ibn Abi Hatim al-Razi (d.327), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Azim, v05 p190, hadith 8336

A report attributed to Malik ibn Anas, the jurist of Medina, adds location and motive.

Source: Malik ibn Anas (d.179)

قال ابن وهب قال مالك بلغني أن طائفة من اليهود نزلوا المدينة طائفة خيبر وطائفة فدك لما كانوا يسمعون من صفة النبي وخروجه في أرض بين حرتين ورجوا أن يكون منهم فأخلفهم الله ذلك وقد كانوا يجدونه مكتوبا عندهم في التوراة والإنجيل بأسمائه وصفاته

"Ibn Wahb said, Malik said, it reached me that a party of the Jews settled in Medina, a party in Khaybar, and a party in Fadak, because of what they used to hear of the Prophet's description God's blessings and peace on him and his family and his coming forth in a land between two lava fields. They hoped he would be from them, and God disappointed them. And they used to find him written with them in the Torah and the Gospel, with his names and his descriptions."

- Malik ibn Anas (d.179), as transmitted by Ibn Wahb, carried in Ibn al-Arabi, Ahkam al-Qur'an, v02 p324

Medina sits between two lava fields. Malik reports that Jews settled there, and in Khaybar, and in Fadak, because their books described a prophet arising in exactly that land. The pattern matters more than the count. Early interpreters did not reduce Qur'an 7:157 to a literal name appearing in an intact codex. They understood recognition through description, office, prophethood, place, and sometimes the name. Some of these glosses are quoted below in the original.

One Shia gloss widens what is found past the Prophet ﷺ himself, and it returns in the fourth section.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

ثم قال : « يَجِدُونَهُ مَكْتُوباً عِنْدَهُمْ فِي التَّوْراةِ وَالْإِنْجِيلِ » يَعْنِي النَّبِيَّ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَآلِهِ والْوَصِيَّ والْقَائِمَ « يَأْمُرُهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ » إِذَا قَامَ « وَيَنْهاهُمْ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ » والْمُنْكَرُ مَنْ أَنْكَرَ فَضْلَ الْإِمَامِ وَجَحَدَهُ

"Then He said, they find him written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. He means the Prophet God's blessings and peace on him and his family, and the Trustee, and the Riser. He commands them with honor when he rises, and he forbids them the dishonorable, and the dishonorable is whoever denies the Imam's excellence and rejects it."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v01 p477 and v02 p406, on Qur'an 7:156 to 7:157

The finding includes the Trustee, meaning Ali ﵇, the one entrusted with the Prophet's ﷺ succession, and the Riser, meaning the awaited Imam from his line. The written content is an office and a description, and the Imams of the House are inside it. In this Imami reading, the written material extends beyond the Prophet's ﷺ name to his office, his succession, and the awaited Imam from his line.

So the phrase means this. The People of the Book meet the Prophet ﷺ in their books as a described man, in a named office, from a named line, arising in a named land. Their own scriptures commanded them to keep his name traditions. The next section shows what those same scriptures, on the Qur'an's own telling, did with that trust.

3. Recognition and Corruption in the Same Passage

The central question is whether the Qur'an contradicts itself. It does not. The Qur'an asserts two things side by side, in the same passages, about the same keepers.

The two claims stand side by side. A. Something recognizable remains. They recognize him. They find him written. The Qur'an confirms truth present with them. B. Some custodians corrupt, conceal, distort, or misrepresent. They distort words. They conceal truth knowingly. They write things and attribute them to God.

The corruption side of that address, at Qur'an 2:75, asks whether the hearers still hope these people will believe. A faction of them used to hear the word of Allah and then change it knowingly after they had understood it. The recognition side, at Qur'an 2:89 and 2:146, says they know him as they know their own sons, and a faction of them conceals the truth while they know. The same address calls the Qur'an a confirmer of what the People of the Book have with them, at Qur'an 3:3 and inside the 2:89 passage itself. Confirmation of content is the third face of the same structure. The books hold truth the Qur'an confirms, the keepers concealed part of it, and neither statement certifies the codex.

The Shia corpus explains how both can be true of the same books, and the explanation is the erasure report.

Source: Ikrima from Ibn Abbas

وفي رواية عكرمة عن ابن عباس قال : إن أحبار اليهود وجدوا صفة النبي ﷺ مكتوبة في التوراة : أكحل أعين ربعة حسن الوجه ، فمحوه من التوراة حسدا وبغيا فأتاهم نفر من قريش فقالوا : أتجدون في التوراة نبيا منا ؟ قالوا : نعم نجده طويلا أزرق سبط الشعر ذكره الواحدي بإسناده في الوسيط .

"In a report of Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, the Jewish rabbis found the Prophet's description God's blessings and peace on him and his family written in the Torah, kohl eyed, of middle height, handsome of face. So they erased it from the Torah out of envy and spite. Then a group of Quraysh came to them and asked, do you find in the Torah a prophet from among us? They said, yes, we find him tall, blue eyed, with lank hair. Al-Wahidi recorded it with his chain in al-Wasit."

- Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, carried in Bihar al-Anwar v09 p67

Four verbs govern one object. The rabbis found the description. It was written. They erased it. And then they misreported it to a delegation that asked. The report does not describe total loss of the prophetic tradition. The rabbis know what they erased, and they can still substitute a false description when questioned. The knowledge survives even where the wording was removed.

The sequel in the same passage of Bihar shows the two assertions working together in one generation.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v09 p67

كانت اليهود يستفتحون أي يستنصرون على الأوس والخزرج برسول الله ﷺ قبل مبعثه ، فلما بعثه الله من العرب ولم يكن من بني إسرائيل كفروا به وجحدوا ما كانوا يقولونه فيه ، فقال لهم معاذ بن جبل وبشر بن البراء بن معرور : يا معشر اليهود اتقوا الله وأسلموا ، فقد كنتم تستفتحون علينا بمحمد ونحن أهل الشرك وتصفونه وتذكرون أنه مبعوث ، فقال سلام بن مسلم أخو بني النضير : ما جاءنا بشئ نعرفه وما هو بالذي كنا نذكر لكم ، فأنزل الله تعالى هذه الآية .

"The Jews used to seek victory, that is, ask for triumph, against the Aws and the Khazraj by the Messenger of Allah God's blessings and peace on him and his family before his mission. When God sent him from the Arabs, and he was not of the Children of Israel, they disbelieved in him and denied what they had been saying about him. Muadh ibn Jabal and Bishr ibn al-Bara told them, company of Jews, fear God and submit. You used to ask victory over us by Muhammad while we were polytheists, and you would describe him and say he would be sent. Salam ibn Muslim of Banu al-Nadir replied, he has brought nothing we recognize, and he is not the one we used to mention to you. So God sent down this verse."

- Bihar al-Anwar v09 p67, on Qur'an 2:89

A description publicly invoked against their Arab rivals while the prophet was still awaited, then denied the moment he appeared outside their line. Recognition first, rejection of the recognized after. Recognition and alteration describe this one sequence, and neither cancels the other.

The Sunnis hold the same joint assertion. Al-Maturidi, commenting on the verse about those who write the book with their hands, says the rabbis altered the Prophet's description itself.

Source: al-Maturidi (d.333)

والثاني : بما قد حرفوا من كتبهم ، وبدلوا من أحكامهم ، وحرفوا من صفته ونعته ونعت أمته

"Second, by what they have altered of their books, changed of their rulings, and distorted of his description, his characteristic, and his community's description."

- al-Maturidi (d.333), Tawilat Ahl al-Sunna, v02 p398, on Qur'an 2:79

And the Medinan case shows the concealment working while the text stayed on the page. The Jewish scholars of Medina held a Torah scroll that still contained the stoning verse. One of them read around it with his hand covering the line. Abdullah ibn Salam, a rabbi who had accepted Islam, ordered the hand lifted. The wording, the scroll, and the concealment all appear in the reports of Malik's Muwatta and Ibn Abd al-Barr's al-Tamhid.

The conclusion, and its limit. The Qur'an's own model is partial preservation, not a choice between perfect preservation and total disappearance. The books held findable content, and the keepers tampered with that content, by erasure, by misreport, and by concealment under a hand. This answers the internal contradiction charge. It does not establish which specific wording was altered in any surviving manuscript.

4. How the Recognition Traditions Work

The recognition reports show how Muslim tradition understood this claim in practice. Not every report proves the same proposition, and the reports sort into four types by the marker that carried the recognition.

Recognition by explicit description. Recognition by place and physical sign. Recognition by continuity of revelation. And Imami reports about broader written prophecy, including the Prophet ﷺ together with his House. One strong example carries each type in the body below, and the remaining reports sit in the attestations box at the end.

Recognition by explicit description

The Qur'anic simile emphasizes how certain the recognition was. In the Ibn Salam tradition, that certainty is explicitly tied to the Prophet's description.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v15 p182

تفسير علي بن إبراهيم : (الذين آتيناهم الكتاب يعرفونه كما يعرفون أبناءهم) الآية ، فإن عمر بن الخطاب قال لعبد الله بن سلام : هل تعرفون محمدا في كتابكم ؟ قال : نعم والله نعرفه بالنعت الذي نعته الله لنا إذا رأيناه فيكم ، كما يعرف أحدنا ابنه إذا رآه مع الغلمان ، والذي يحلف به ابن سلام لأنا بمحمد هذا أشد معرفة مني بابني

"Tafsir of Ali ibn Ibrahim, on those We gave the Book know him as they know their own sons. Umar ibn al-Khattab said to Abdullah ibn Salam, do you find Muhammad in your Book? He said, yes, by God, we know him by the description God set down for us when we see him among you, as one of us knows his son when he sees him among the boys. By the One in whose name Ibn Salam swears, we know this Muhammad God's blessings and peace on him and his family more surely than I know my own son."

- Bihar al-Anwar v15 p182, from Tafsir al-Qummi, on Qur'an 2:146

The Qur'anic comparison expresses certainty of recognition. Ibn Salam's report then explains what that recognition rested on, the description he believed God had given in scripture. Malik's report, quoted in the second section, shows the same type, where Jews settle Medina itself because their books describe a prophet arising in that land.

Recognition by place and physical sign

Salmān al-Fārisī received identifying markers from the last Christian teacher he served, and the markers named a place and a sign.

Source: Musnad Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d.241)

قَالَ : أَيْ بُنَيَّ ، وَاللَّهِ مَا أَعْلَمُهُ أَصْبَحَ عَلَى مَا كُنَّا عَلَيْهِ أَحَدٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ آمُرُكَ أَنْ تَأْتِيَهُ ، وَلَكِنَّهُ قَدْ أَظَلَّكَ زَمَانُ نَبِيٍّ هُوَ مَبْعُوثٌ بِدِينِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ يَخْرُجُ بِأَرْضِ الْعَرَبِ ، مُهَاجِرًا إِلَى أَرْضٍ بَيْنَ حَرَّتَيْنِ بَيْنَهُمَا نَخْلٌ ، بِهِ عَلَامَاتٌ لَا تَخْفَى : يَأْكُلُ الْهَدِيَّةَ ، وَلَا يَأْكُلُ الصَّدَقَةَ ، بَيْنَ كَتِفَيْهِ خَاتَمُ النُّبُوَّةِ ، فَإِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ أَنْ تَلْحَقَ بِتِلِكَ الْبِلَادِ فَافْعَلْ

"He said, my son, by God I know of no one left upon what we were upon that I could tell you to go to. But the era of a prophet has dawned over you, sent with the religion of Abraham, coming forth in the land of the Arabs, emigrating to a land between two lava fields with palms between them. In him are signs that do not hide. He accepts the gift and does not eat the alms tax. Between his shoulder blades is the seal of prophethood. If you can reach that land, do so."

- Musnad Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d.241), al-Risala edition, v39 p144, the Salman narration

Two locations, Arabia and the land between the lava fields, one character test, and one physical mark, the seal of prophethood. The same narration carries the moment Salmān saw the town and recognized it by his teacher's description. The Bahīrā report belongs to this type as well, with the seal between the shoulders as the master marker.

Recognition by continuity of revelation

Waraqa ibn Nawfal recognized the event by its parallel with Moses, and the Negus of Abyssinia recognized by the kinship of what he heard with what Jesus brought.

Source: Ibn Hibban (d.354)

به قال نعم فدعا النجاشي أساقفته فنشروا المصاحف حوله فقرأ عليهم جعفر بن أبي طالب كهيعص فبكى النجاشي حتى اخضل لحيته وبكت أساقفته حتى اخضلوا مصاحفهم ثم قال إن هذا والذي جاء به عيسى يخرج من مشكاة واحدة انطلقا فلعمر الله لا أرسلهم معكما ولا أكاد ولا هم

"He said yes, and the Negus called his bishops and they spread their scriptures around him, and Jaafar ibn Abi Talib peace be upon him recited to them from Maryam. The Negus wept until his beard was soaked, and his bishops wept until their scriptures were soaked. Then he said, this and what Jesus peace be upon him brought come out from one lamp. Go, for by the life of God, I will never send them back with you, and I will not mistreat them."

- Ibn Hibban (d.354), al-Thiqat, v01 p66, with parallels in Musnad Ahmad, al-Risala edition, v29 p314, and Ibn Abi al-Hadid, Sharh Nahj al-Balagha, v06 p310

This type works differently from the first two. The Negus identifies no description and consults no marker. He hears recitation and rules that it comes from the same source as Jesus's message. Waraqa's report, in the attestations box, works the same way through the Greatest Nāmūs, the companion of revelation who used to come to Moses.

Imami reports about broader written prophecy

The Imami reading widens the written content past the Prophet ﷺ himself, to the succession, and the name.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

أوصيك يا موسى وصية الشفيق المشفق بابن البتول عيسى ابن مريم صاحب الأتان والبرنس والزيت والزيتون والمحراب ، ومن بعده بصاحب الجمل الأحمر الطيب الطاهر المطهر ، فمثله في كتابك أنه مؤمن مهيمن على الكتب كلها وأنه راكع ساجد ، راغب ، راهب ، إخوانه المساكين وأنصاره قوم آخرون ويكون في زمانه أزل وزلزال وقتل ، وقلة من المال ، اسمه أحمد ، محمد الأمين من الباقين من ثلة الأولين الماضين … فمر ظلمة بني إسرائيل أن لا يدرسوا اسمه ولا يخذلوه وإنهم لفاعلون ، وحبه لي حسنة ، فأنا معه وأنا من حزبه وهو من حزبي وحزبهم الغالبون ، فتمت كلماتي لأظهرن دينه على الأديان كلها

"I bequeath to you, Moses peace be upon him, the bequest of the tender and anxious one. The son of the virgin, Jesus son of Mary peace be upon him, master of the she-ass, the cloak, the oil, the olive, and the prayer niche. And after him, the master of the red camel, the good, the pure, the purified. His likeness in your book is that he is a believer, a guardian over all the books, bowing, prostrating, devout, a worshipper. 5. If No One Can Change God's Words, What Was Altered?

The objection sets two verses of the Qur'an against each other. If none can change the words of Allah, and the Torah and the Gospel are Allah's words, then the accusation of alteration is closed by the Qur'an's own mouth. The objection only works if the words of Allah in those verses means the codex.

Several of the passages that say none can change God's words concern his decrees, promises, judgments, and promised victory. When the Qur'an says God's words cannot be changed, the natural reading is that no one can thwart what God has decreed. Meanwhile the Qur'an separately accuses humans of mishandling revealed material. No one can thwart God's word does not naturally mean no human can ever modify the wording of a manuscript.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

قال : في يونس قول الله ههنا : الذين آمنوا وكانوا يتقون لهم البشرى في الحياة الدنيا وفي الآخرة لا تبديل لكلمات الله ذلك هو الفوز العظيم

"He said, in Yunus is God's word here. Those who believed and were God fearing, theirs is the glad tidings in the present life and the next. No changer for the words of Allah. That is the great triumph."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v03 p133, applied to the glad tidings given to the dying believer

At Qur'an 7:137 the same usage stands in the open. The good word that God completed upon the Children of Israel, because they bore patiently, is the promise of the land, fulfilled.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

فأنزل الله «وَتَمَّتْ كَلِمَةُ رَبِّكَ الْحُسْنى عَلى بَنِي إِسْرائِيلَ بِما صَبَرُوا»

"God sent down, and your Lord's good word was completed upon the Children of Israel because they bore patiently."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v02 p88

And the long address to Moses ﵇ already quoted in the fourth section carries the same usage inside it, my words are completed, I shall make his religion manifest. The words that cannot be replaced are the decrees and the promises. The displaced thing, in the verses that charge displacement, is another object under another verb. The lexical evidence supports the contextual answer. The verses on changing God's words use the verb tabdil, replacing, over the words that are decrees and promises. The verses that charge displacement use a different verb, tahrif, over the word moved from its places, at Qur'an 2:75, 4:46, and 5:13. The two verbs never cross in the same construction.

Ibn Taymiyya, the Sunni authority the debaters themselves quote on other matters, reads the verses on changing God's words with the completion language. His reading matches this structure, the words completed truthfully and justly, not a codex under seal.

6. Does Qur'an 10:94 Tell Muhammad to Trust the Codices

The objection rests on Qur'an 10:94. If you are in doubt, ask those who read the Book before you. The primary answer does not depend on identifying who is meant to be asked. Even if the verse directs consultation toward knowledgeable People of the Book, consultation about a proposition does not certify every proposition in every book they possess. If someone says, ask a physicist whether the Earth is spherical, that does not certify everything in the physicist's library. A limited appeal to scriptural witnesses can authenticate the point for which they are consulted without granting complete textual inerrancy. Transmitted Muslim interpretations additionally deny that the Prophet ﷺ ever actually doubted, and the Shia corpus carries reports to that effect from the Imams ﵇.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v17 p90

عن إبراهيم بن عمير رفعه إلى أحدهما في قول الله لنبيه ﷺ : فإن كنت في شك مما أنزلنا إليك فأسأل الذين يقرؤون الكتاب من قبلك قال : قال رسول الله ﷺ : لاشك ولا أشك

"From Ibrahim ibn Umayr, raised to one of the two peace be upon them both, on God's word to His Prophet God's blessings and peace on him and his family, if you are in doubt about what We sent down to you, ask those who read the Book before you. The Messenger of Allah God's blessings and peace on him and his family said, there is no doubt, and I do not doubt."

- Bihar al-Anwar v17 p90, from Ilal al-Sharai

Al-Dhahabi transmits the same verdict in the Sunni record, he did not doubt and he did not ask. Al-Nahhas reads the address as spoken to the community through the Prophet ﷺ. That is the standard figure of the Qur'an's addresses. Al-Kashani records the occasion and the purpose. The objectors had said, what, a messenger who eats food and walks in the markets. The verse sent the Prophet ﷺ to ask them, in front of the ignorant, whether God ever sent a messenger who did not. The conditional stands for the sake of the invitation, as the mubahala verse offers the challenge no one is expected to need. And the Shia school adds its own resolution of who the asked are.

7. What Does the Qur'an Mean by the Injil

The objection offers a choice. Either the Injil is the four Gospels, and then the accusation of alteration indicts the Qur'an's own praise of it. Or the Injil is a lost single book given to Jesus ﵇, and then the Qur'an commands people to judge by a book nobody ever held. The choice assumes the school's practice, and the practice refutes it. The corpus quotes the Injil the way it quotes the Torah, by formula, as a present, citable, contested book.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

قَالَ : « فِي التَّوْرَاةِ مَكْتُوبٌ : يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ ، تَفَرَّغْ لِعِبَادَتِي ؛ أَمْلَأْ قَلْبَكَ غِنًى وَلَا أَكِلْكَ إِلى طَلَبِكَ ، وَعَلَيَّ أَنْ أَسُدَّ فَاقَتَكَ ، وَأَمْلَأَ قَلْبَكَ خَوْفاً مِنِّي ، وَإِنْ لَاتَفَرَّغْ لِعِبَادَتِي ، أَمْلَأْ قَلْبَكَ شُغُلًا بِالدُّنْيَا ، ثُمَّ لَاأَسُدَّ فَاقَتَكَ ، وَأَكِلْكَ إِلى طَلَبِكَ

The practice never treats the Torah as either certified or lost. It treats it as a book that still carries surviving truth alongside alteration.

The Injil receives the same treatment, and the strongest witness is the majlis of Imam al-Rida ﵇ with the Jewish exponent.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v16 p92

- Bihar al-Anwar v16 p92, hadith 21, from Uyun Akhbar al-Rida, with a parallel in al-Rayshahri, Nabi al-Rahma, p67

The Imam quotes a named passage of the Injil to the man who keeps that book, and the keeper confirms the passage. The same corpus charges those keepers with displacing words from their places. A third possibility exists besides those two. The Qur'anic Injil is the revelation God gave Jesus ﵇. By the Prophet's time, Christians possessed books and traditions that the Islamic sources treat as carrying some surviving Injil material alongside alteration and later transmission. Qur'an 5:47 tells the People of the Book to judge by what God sent down in it. The verse identifies the normative object as what God sent down. It does not separately state that every proposition currently associated with the Gospel is itself revelation. Another verse used in this objection is Qur'an 5:68, which tells the People of the Book they stand on nothing until they uphold the Torah and the Gospel. It carries the same answer. Upholding the surviving revelation content is not certifying a codex. The same surah charges the keepers with displacement in the same breath.

Two outside facts complete the picture. The first is a report in al-Kafi itself about who keeps the books.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

"He asked him about God's word, and We wrote in the Psalms after the Remembrance, what is the Psalms and what is the Remembrance. He said, the Remembrance is with God, and the Psalms are what was sent down to David peace be upon him. And every book that came down is with the People of Knowledge, and we are they."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, Dar al-Hadith edition, v01 p802, from Abdullah ibn Sinan, from Imam Jafar al-Sadiq ﵇

The singular name itself settles nothing about physical volumes. Singular names often refer to a scriptural revelation or corpus without telling us how many physical books or literary compositions carry it. The Qur'an can refer to divine Injil material as present and usable without identifying every surviving Christian composition with the original revelation given to Jesus ﵇.

8. Did Muslims Invent Textual Corruption After Ibn Hazm

Explicit Muslim accusations of textual alteration predate Ibn Hazm of Cordoba, who died in 456 of the Hijra. The earliest named practitioner whose book survives in quotation is Ali ibn Rabban al-Tabari, a Nestorian physician who accepted Islam and died in 247 of the Hijra, two centuries earlier.

He wrote the Book of Religion and Empire and quoted Isaiah and Habakkuk with the name in the text. Earlier Muslims citing Biblical material does not mean they considered the entire Bible intact. Citation of selected material alongside accusations of alteration is exactly the pattern already shown, and the historical record reinforces it.

Source: quoted in al-Zindani

جاء في سفر أشعيا : «إني جعلت اسمك محمدا ، يا محمد يا قدوس الرب ، اسمك موجود من الأبد» … وجاء في سفر حبقوق : «إن اللّه جاء من التيمان ، والقدوس من جبل فاران ، لقد أضاءت السماء من بهاء محمد ، وامتلأت الأرض من حمده» ذكره علي بن ربن الطبري في كتابه (الدين والدولة)

"From the scroll of Isaiah, I have made your name Muhammad. O Muhammad, O holy one of the Lord, your name exists from eternity. And from the scroll of Habakkuk, God came from Tayman, and the Holy One from Mount Faran. The heaven shone with the splendor of Muhammad God's blessings and peace on him and his family, and the earth was filled with his praise. Ali ibn Rabban al-Tabari recorded it in his book, the Book of Religion and Empire."

- quoted in al-Zindani, Bayyinat al-Rasul wa-Mu jazatih, p32, from Ali ibn Rabban al-Tabari (d.247), al-Din wa-l-Dawla

The report reaches us through a modern carrier, and that provenance is stated plainly. A convert who had been a Christian scholar, writing two centuries before the supposed concession, quotes Habakkuk's Faran passage with the name of Muhammad ﷺ in it. This agrees in essence with the majlis of Imam al-Rida ﵇ quoted in the eleventh section, where the same Habakkuk text stands with the name Ahmad. These are two Muslim transmissions of a similar prophetic reading, not independently established witnesses.

The accusation of alteration in the Imami corpus is as old as the corpus itself, standing beside the citation practice from the first layer. Al-Kafi cites the Torah and the Injil by formula, and reports the erasure of the description. The majlis quotes the Injil's Paraclete passage to its own keeper, and the same corpus charges the keepers with displacement. Citation and alteration were held together from the beginning, and the doctrine was not born of a debate in eleventh century Cordoba.

9. What Do Pre-Islamic Crucifixion Manuscripts Actually Establish

The strongest modern form of the dilemma sets the name question aside and turns to manuscript dating. Gospel manuscripts older than Islam by centuries carry the crucifixion. If the Qur'an denies what the oldest manuscripts affirm, the Qur'an is falsified by the very books it praised.

The objection only works if the Qur'an denies what the manuscripts report. Read the verse as its own context presents it, clause by clause. The verse makes two denials, then closes with a statement about what the killers knew. Qur'an 4:157 answers a list of Jewish boasts, that they killed the Messiah, that they crucified him, boasts set beside their slander of Mary ﵍. The first clause pair denies the boast outright. They did not kill him and did not crucify him. On the school's own reports the pair is true. The one killed and crucified was a likeness bearer, not Jesus ﵇. The closing qualifier denies something further. It denies that the killers possessed certainty of identity in what they did. A crucifixion of a likeness occurred. The Qur'an denies the killing of Jesus ﵇, and denies that the actors knew with certainty whom they had. The two denials sit in one verse, and the manuscript objection has to defeat both.

Al-Tabari glosses that last phrase in his commentary.

Source: al-Tabari (d.310)

ما كان لهم بمن قتلوه من علم ، ولكنهم اتبعوا ظنهم فقتلوه ، ظنا منهم أنه عيسى ، وأنه الذي يريدون قتله ، ولم يكن به = وما قتلوه يقينًا ، يقول : وما قتلوا هذا الذي اتبعوه في المقتول الذي قتلوه وهم يحسبونه عيسى ، يقينًا أنه عيسى ولا أنه غيره ، ولكنهم كانوا منه على ظن وشبهة . وهذا كقول الرجل للرجل : ما قتلت هذا الأمر علمًا ، وما قتلته يقينًا ، إذا تكلم فيه بالظن على غير يقين علم

"They had no knowledge of the one they killed. They followed their conjecture and killed him, supposing him to be Jesus peace be upon him, supposing him to be the one they meant to kill, and he was not. And they did not kill him with certainty, meaning, they did not kill this one they pursued and killed while reckoning him Jesus, with certainty that it was Jesus or that it was other than him. They were upon conjecture and doubt. It is like a man saying to a man, you did not kill this matter by knowledge, nor by certainty, when he speaks of it by conjecture without certain knowledge."

- al-Tabari (d.310), Jami al-Bayan, Shakir edition, v09 p377

Someone, on al-Tabari's reading, was killed, on a guess, in the dark, by people who could not be sure who they had. The verse denies certain knowledge of the killing of Jesus ﵇, inside an event of semblance, and affirms his raising. The Shia corpus carries the same structure in the long report of the competing claims.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v14 p338

حتى طلبته اليهود وادعت أنها عذبته ودفنته في الأرض حيا وادعى بعضهم أنهم قتلوه وصلبوه ، وما كان الله ليجعل لهم سلطانا عليه ، وإنما شبه لهم وما قدروا على عذابه ودفنه ولا على قتله وصلبه … فلما أراد أن يرفعه أوحى إليه أن استودع نور الله وحكمته وعلم كتابه شمعون بن حمون الصفا

"Until the Jews sought him, and one party claimed they tortured him and buried him alive in the earth, and another party claimed they killed and crucified him. And God would not give them authority over him. It was made a likeness for them, and they could not reach him, not to torture, not to bury, not to kill, not to crucify. And when God willed to raise him, He revealed to him, deposit the light of God and His wisdom and the knowledge of His Book with Simon son of Hamon al-Safa."

- Bihar al-Anwar v14 p338

The parties to the event itself could not agree on what they had done, torture and burial in one telling, killing and crucifixion in another. The verse answers a boast with a denial of certainty. Pre-Islamic manuscripts establish that Christians were reporting and believing the crucifixion before Islam. They do not, simply by being old manuscripts, establish that the Qur'an endorses that report or that the Qur'an regards the transmitters as possessing certain knowledge of Jesus's identity. Their existence does not create an internal contradiction in the Qur'an, because the Qur'an's own model allows an earlier community to transmit a claim that the Qur'an later rejects. On the standard scholarly dating, the Greek Gospels are narratives composed decades after the event. Luke presents his account as an orderly compilation based on traditions handed down by those he describes as eyewitnesses from the beginning. The oldest complete copies preserve that inherited testimony, and inherited testimony about an event of semblance is precisely what the verse says the disagreeing parties had. This is not an independent historical proof of substitution. It answers the narrower charge that the Qur'an contradicts itself by acknowledging earlier scripture while denying the crucifixion. The objection's premise also lists the divine sonship. Divine sonship and crucifixion are logically distinct doctrines, and they are answered separately. The same Qur'anic criterion applies to the sonship on its own. The Qur'an rejects it regardless of its presence in surviving Christian texts.

10. Who Is Above Whom in Qur'an 3:55

The last companion objection concerns a promise read into Qur'an 3:55. God tells Jesus ﵇, I am taking you and raising you to Me and purifying you from those who disbelieved. And I am setting those who follow you above those who disbelieved until the Day of Resurrection. On that reading the Christian church keeps dominance to the end of time, and no later religion can supersede it.

The verse answers from its own occasion, and the Shia corpus transmits the occasion in full.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329)

إن عيسى وعد أصحابه ليلة رفعه الله إليه ، فاجتمعوا إليه عند المساء وهم اثنا عشر رجلا فأدخلهم بيتا ثم خرج عليهم من عين في زاوية البيت وهو ينقض رأسه من الماء ، فقال إن الله أوحى إلي انه رافعي إليه الساعة ومطهري من اليهود فأيكم يلقى عليه شبحي فيقتل ويصلب ويكون معي في درجتي ، فقال شاب منهم انا يا روح الله قال فأنت هوذا

"Jesus peace be upon him promised his companions the night God raised him to Himself. They gathered to him at evening, twelve men, and he brought them into a house, then came out upon them from a spring in the corner of the house, wringing the water from his head, and said, God has revealed to me that this hour He raises me to Himself and purifies me from the Jews. Which of you will have my likeness cast upon him, to be killed and crucified, and be with me in my rank? A young man among them said, I, O Spirit of God. He said, then you are he."

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329), Tafsir al-Qummi, v01 p129, from Humran ibn Ayan, from Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇, on Qur'an 3:55

The verse's drama is the raising and the escape. The people Jesus ﵇ is purified from are the Jews who came to kill him. Those are the disbelievers of the verse, the ones who disbelieved in him, not the Muslims, who believe in him as a messenger of God. Two transmitted readings divide over who the followers are. The Imami reading takes them as Jesus's immediate faithful followers. An alternative classical reading takes the aboveness historically, the Christians above the Jews. On either reading, the verse compares the followers of Jesus with those who rejected Jesus.

Source: Ibn Zayd

قال الذين كفروا من بني إسرائيل . وجاعل الذين اتبعوك قال : الذين آمنوا به من بني إسرائيل وغيرهم ، فوق الذين كفروا النصارى فوقَ اليهود إلى يوم القيامة ، قال : فليس بلد فيه أحد من النصارى إلا وهم فوق يهود في شرق ولا غرب ، هم في البلدان كلها مستذلون

"The disbelievers are those of the Children of Israel. As for those who follow you, they are those who believed in him of the Children of Israel and others, above those who disbelieved, the Nasara above the Jews until the Day of Resurrection. He said, there is no town containing Christians where they are not above the Jews, east or west. In all the lands the Jews are humbled."

- Ibn Zayd, transmitted by Yunus from Ibn Wahb, in al-Tabari, Jami al-Bayan, Shakir edition, v06 p463

Neither reading certifies Nicene Christianity against Islam. Even on the temporal reading, the verse promises Christians standing above the Jews who disbelieved in Jesus ﵇, and no later Christian doctrine follows from that standing.

The sister verse at Qur'an 61:14 names the followers precisely, a faction of the Children of Israel believed and a faction disbelieved, and God aided the believers against their enemy. Al-Qummi places the two factions at the event of the likeness itself.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329)

(فآمنت طائفة من بني إسرائيل وكفرت طائفة) قال : التي كفرت هي التي قتلت شبيه عيسى وصلبته والتي آمنت هي التي قبلت شبيه عيسى حتى لا يقتل ( فقتلت الطائفة التي قتلته وصلبته وهو قوله : فأيدنا الذين آمنوا على عدوهم فأصبحوا ظاهرين )

"On a faction of the Children of Israel believed and a faction disbelieved, he said, the disbelieving faction is the one that killed the likeness of Jesus peace be upon him and crucified it, and the believing faction is the one that accepted the matter of the likeness so he would not be killed. The believing faction prevailed over the faction that killed and crucified, and that is His word, so We gave the believers victory over their enemy and they became manifest."

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329), Tafsir al-Qummi, v02 p366, on Qur'an 61:14

In al-Qummi's reading, the followers are the believers of Jesus's ﵇ own generation, the men and women who knew the crucified one was a likeness and said so, and their victory is historical. And the school reads the pattern forward. The Kafi report on the same verse says the apostles of Jesus ﵇ were his partisans, and our partisans are our apostles. The line continues in the partisans of the House ﵇. On every reading, the verse's until the Day of Resurrection qualifies the aboveness of whichever party the verse means. The objection needs three claims at once, that the aboveness is temporal, that the followers are the Nicene church, and that standing above the Jews proves doctrine true. Each claim fails inside the verse, and the objection needs all three.

11. A Secondary Question: Possible Biblical Correlates

The Islamic Dilemma is already answered on the internal evidence of the earlier sections, and nothing in the case so far depends on what follows. The secondary question is whether surviving biblical texts contain material Muslims can reasonably connect to Muhammad ﷺ. The claim of Qur'an 7:157 stands on the evidence already exhibited in the earlier sections, and it needs no biblical verse.

First, the like of Moses. This parallel is Qur'anic before it is biblical, and the recognition stories already quoted carry it. The Qur'an itself declares the parallel.

Source: Qur'an 73:15

إِنَّا أَرْسَلْنا إِلَيْكُمْ رَسُولاً شاهِداً عَلَيْكُمْ كَما أَرْسَلْنا إِلى فِرْعَوْنَ رَسُولاً

inna arsalna ilaykum rasulan shahidan alaykum kama arsalna ila firawna rasula

"We have sent to you a messenger, a witness over you, as We sent to Pharaoh a messenger."

- Qur'an 73:15

Qur'an 73:15 establishes an Islamic Moses and Muhammad parallel. It does not by itself establish that Deuteronomy 18 predicts Muhammad ﷺ. The biblical passage, if discussed, must be treated independently. Within the Islamic record, the People of the Book who accepted recognized by this marker. Waraqa knew the Greatest Namus that came to Moses ﵇, and the Negus used the same formula. Both are carried in the strong graded strata of the Sunni school.

Second, the Faran cluster. One caveat comes first. Muslim authors report that earlier scriptural readers identified Faran with Mecca. That report is evidence for the history of Muslim interpretation, and not by itself proof of Jewish or Christian consensus. Within that record, the Torah's blessing in Deuteronomy 33 names three places for three theophanies. The Lord came from Sinai, shone from Seir, and was manifest from the mountains of Faran. The corpus identifies the three as the Torah to Moses ﵇, the Gospel to Jesus ﵇ of Seir's Nazareth, and the Qur'an to Muhammad ﷺ from Faran.

Source: al-Nuwayri (d.733)

فأما ما اتفقوا عليه مما جاء في التوراة وترجموه بالعربية ورضوا ترجمته فمن ذلك قوله : «جاء اللَّهُ من طُورِ سِيناءَ ، وأشْرَقَ لنا من ساعِيرَ ، واسْتَعْلى من جِبالِ فاران» … قال العلماء : وفي هذا تصريح بنبوّة محمد ﷺ … وفاران : هي مكة

"As for what they agreed upon of what came in the Torah, and translated into Arabic, and accepted the translation of, among it is His saying, God came from Mount Sinai, shone upon us from Seir, and was manifest from the mountains of Faran. The scholars said, in this is an explicit declaration of the prophethood of Muhammad God's blessings and peace on him and his family. And Faran is Mecca."

- al-Nuwayri (d.733), Nihayat al-Arab, v16 p110

Al-Maqrizi presses the same record further, saying there is no disagreement between the Muslims and the People of the Book that Faran is Mecca, and challenging any who claim otherwise to bring their claim. The agreement the corpus reports is itself reported by Muslims, a fact stated plainly. What carries weight is that a Muslim encyclopedist of the fourteenth century could present the verse as a text whose Arabic translation the Book people themselves had accepted. He set it alongside his whole apparatus of sources, without fear of contradiction on the identification. The case for this reading is strong, with that attribution stated.

Third, the two riders and the mountain of Faran, in the majlis. Imam al-Rida ﵇ quoted Isaiah and Habakkuk to the Jewish exponent as the latter's own books.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v14 p165

وقال شعيا النبي فيما تقول أنت وأصحابك في التوراة : « رأيت راكبين أضاء لهما الأرض أحدهما على حمار والآخر على جمل » … قال : أما راكب الحمار فعيسى وأما راكب الجمل فمحمد ﷺ ، أتنكر هذا من التوراة ؟ قال : لا ما أنكره . ثم قال الرضا ﵇ : … « جاء الله بالبيان من جبل فاران ، وامتلأت السماوات من تسبيح أحمد وأمته ، يحمل خيله في البحر كما يحمل في البر ، يأتينا بكتاب جديد بعد خراب بيت المقدس » يعني بالكتاب القرآن

"The prophet Isaiah said, in what you and your companions hold in the Torah, I saw two riders, for whom the earth lit up, one upon a donkey and one upon a camel. He said, as for the rider of the donkey, it is Jesus peace be upon him, and as for the rider of the camel, it is Muhammad God's blessings and peace on him and his family. Do you deny this is in the Torah? He said, no, I do not deny it. Then al-Rida peace be upon him said, God came with the exposition from Mount Faran, and the heavens were filled with the praise of Ahmad and his community. His horses are borne on the sea as they are borne on the land. He comes to us with a new book after the ruining of the Temple, meaning by the book, the Qur'an."

- Bihar al-Anwar v14 p165, with a parallel in Nur al-Thaqalayn, v02 p80, which glosses Faran as a mountain of Mecca a day's distance from it

Two images, two riders, and the name Ahmad, quoted against the keeper of the book, who affirmed he did not deny it. The dialogue reaches us through Muslim transmitters, and it is ranked accordingly, strong inside the tradition's record, offered as Imami testimony and not as neutral external evidence.

Fourth, Kedar. Isaiah's new song passage, in the Arabic carrying books, sends the praise to the villages that Qaydar inhabits, and Qaydar is a name in the Prophet's ﷺ own line, on both schools' genealogical record.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v35 p143

بن نبت بن قيدار بن إسماعيل ، وإسماعيل أول من فتق لسانه بالعربية المبينة التي نزل بها القرآن

"Son of Nabt, son of Qaydar, son of Ishmael peace be upon him. And Ishmael peace be upon him was the first whose tongue opened with the clear Arabic in which the Qur'an came down."

- Bihar al-Anwar v35 p143, the Prophet's ﷺ genealogy, with a parallel in Ibn Qutayba, al-Marif

The villages of Kedar praising with a new song, in a line the corpus itself names as the Prophet's ﷺ ancestry, is a reading of medium strength, dependent on the carried translation.

Fifth, the Paraclete. The majlis passage quoted in the seventh section already carries the Imam's ﵇ citation of the departing son of the virgin and the coming Barqlita who interprets all things, affirmed by the Exilarch. The corpus's Bihar carries the Gospel's promise of another Fardaqlit who remains with them the whole of the latter age. The school's identification of the promised one with Muhammad ﷺ rests on these passages as carried. The periklytos reading, the claim that the Greek text itself once read the praised one in place of the advocate, is rejected. That reading is a guess about a word, carried by no early manuscript, and the identification does not need it.

Four claims are excluded. The machmadim claim, which reads the Song of Songs' phrase about him who is altogether desirable as a hidden name of Muhammad ﷺ, is wordplay on a plural noun, and it is left out. The Arabic Infancy Gospel's naming claim fails at the standard scholarly reference, whose complete summary of the text contains no such prophecy, and whose manuscripts are all from 1299 or later. The extant Gospel of Barnabas is a medieval composition. The sixth century list that condemns a gospel under Barnabas's name preserves no quotation to tie it to the extant book, and the mention proves a title and nothing more. The valley of Baca bridge, tying Psalm 84 to Bakkah of Qur'an 3:96, appears nowhere in the corpus.

12. Remaining Objections

Three objections remain.

Objection 1. What if the corruption was interpretive rather than textual?

Some Muslim apologists answer the accusation of alteration by saying the text never changed, only the reading. Some Qur'anic passages do concern concealment or oral distortion. The reciters twisted the Book with their tongues while the page stayed whole, and the Qur'an itself names that mode in Qur'an 3:78. But the sources examined above do not limit tahrif to interpretation. They also transmit explicit claims of erased descriptions and altered wording. The Medinan case shows the concealment working while the text stayed on the page, the stoning verse read around under a hand. The Islamic case therefore does not depend on proving that every charge of tahrif is textual. It only needs to show that the Islamic model allows both surviving truth and alteration.

Objection 2. What about the Gospel authors' certainty?

Luke distinguishes his own ordered account from the testimony handed down by those described as eyewitnesses from the beginning. John's closing chapters claim eyewitness testimony directly, at John 19:35 and 21:24. The objection says these authors were certain, so the verse's report of conjecture is refuted. John's assertion of certainty does not create an internal contradiction in the Qur'an, because Qur'an 4:157 explicitly denies that the relevant certainty was justified. The participants in the event are the very party whose texts affirm certainty, and the verse describes that party's knowledge as conjecture about a semblance. Luke shows the genre includes compilers. John shows the genre includes certainty claims from participants. The verse addresses the participants' knowledge. This is not an independent historical proof of substitution. It answers the narrower charge that the Qur'an contradicts itself by acknowledging earlier scripture while denying the crucifixion. The limit is this. A reader who does not accept the Qur'an's account will weigh the eyewitness claims as history and will not be moved by this internal fit.

Objection 3. If Muslims quote the Bible, are they not authenticating it?

This objection is aimed at the Muslim reader. You Muslims cite our Bible as prophecy of your Prophet ﷺ, says the debater, so you have certified our book, and your charge of corruption contradicts your own dawah. The answer is one doctrine, stated in two sentences. Quoting one passage as surviving truth does not amount to declaring the entire textual tradition free from alteration. The Imami sources do both at once, citing passages they regard as genuine and accusing the same scriptural communities of distortion elsewhere. The criterion for which is which is not the school's whim. It is the Qur'an itself. The Qur'an names itself a guardian over the earlier books, muhaymin, at 5:48. That verse stands in the very passage the objection quotes. What the earlier books carry in agreement with the Qur'an is the surviving content. What contradicts it is the displaced or altered material, exactly as the Qur'an tests the keepers' claims in the majlis. For the internal consistency question addressed here, this criterion is legitimate, because the objection itself begins by asking whether the Qur'an contradicts its own claims. Recognition and tampering are not two doctrines forced together. They are the joint assertion the Qur'an itself makes in the same address. Every citation this school makes of the Torah and the Gospel is a citation of surviving content, exactly like the Imam's ﵇ citation of the Paraclete passage to the Exilarch. Every corruption charge is a charge about displacement, exactly like the erasure report. The objection assumes the school holds an all or nothing premise about the codex, and the school never held it.

With the third objection answered, the verse stands in its plain form. Whom they find written with them. The Prophet ﷺ is met by description, character, office, place, and mark. The scriptures that carried him had commanded his keepers to preserve his name, and had warned that the keepers would efface it. People recognized him across every generation these reports record, precisely because recognition never depended on the page staying whole.

Glossary of Key Terms

Tahrif is displacement of received wording or meaning, the charge the Qur'an makes at 2:75, 4:46, and 5:13.

Tabdil is replacing, the verb of the verses that say none can change God's words. It governs God's decrees and promises, never the codex.

Na't and sifa both mean description, the portrait given in words. Most recognition accounts in these reports work through a description. The Negus report is the exception, where recognition comes from the continuity of revelation.

Muhaymin means guardian or witness, the Qur'an's word at 5:48 for its own authority over the earlier books.

Namus is the word Waraqa uses of the angel who came to Moses ﵇, the bearer of revelation.

Shubbiha lahum means it was made to appear so to them, the Qur'an's word at 4:157 for the semblance at the crucifixion. Imami and many classical reports explain that appearance through a likeness or substitute.

Yaqin means certain knowledge, and the verse denies the killers had it.

Injil is the Gospel, the revelation given to Jesus ﵇. The Imami sources treat surviving Christian scriptural tradition as containing material from that revelation while also allowing alteration elsewhere.

Hal is the Arabic grammatical state, the accusative of circumstance, the construction of written in 7:157.

Final Verdict

The Islamic Dilemma ties the Qur'an's praise of the earlier scriptures to the seventh century codices. That assumption is the objection's whole strength, and its whole weakness, because the Qur'an itself refuses it. The Qur'an praises what the People of the Book still find in their books and charges their custodians with tampering in the same address. Recognition and alteration are the same story told twice, once as the keepers' treasure and once as the keepers' crime.

The remaining objections are answered on the same ground. The verses that say none can change God's words protect his decrees and promises, not the custodians' codices. Qur'an 10:94 licenses a limited consultation, and a consultation does not certify every proposition in the book consulted. The Injil is cited as a present book whose keeper affirmed the cited passage, neither certified whole nor dismissed as lost. The accusation of alteration predates Ibn Hazm by two centuries. The crucifixion manuscripts show Christians reporting the crucifixion before Islam. They do not create a contradiction inside the Qur'an, because the Qur'an's own model allows an earlier community to transmit a claim the Qur'an later rejects. Qur'an 3:55 compares the followers of Jesus ﵇ with those who rejected him, and it does not certify later Christian doctrine.

Surviving revelation does not entail total codex reliability, and the whole dilemma rests on assuming that it does.
