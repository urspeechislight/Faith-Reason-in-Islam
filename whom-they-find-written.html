Whom They Find Written With Them

A Christian trap reads the Qur'an's praise of the Torah and the Gospel as a certificate of the seventh century codices. Either the Bible is reliable and the crucifixion stands, or the Bible is corrupt and no one could find the Prophet ﷺ written in it. The Qur'an itself pairs the recognition claim with the corruption charge, in the same passages, and that pairing removes the plank the trap walks on.

The Argument Under Examination

Christian debaters press one Qur'anic phrase into a two horned trap. The phrase is the ending of Qur'an 7:157. The Messenger ﷺ is the prophet whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. The trap runs three steps.

Premise 1. The Qur'an commands the Book's people to judge by their books

The Qur'an calls the Torah and the Gospel guidance and light. It tells Jews and Christians to judge by them. It says the People of the Book find the Prophet ﷺ written in them, at Qur'an 5:43 to 5:48 and 7:157.

Premise 2. The seventh century books carried the crucifixion and the sonship

The only Torah and Gospel in circulation were the Masoretic text and the four Gospels. Manuscript evidence places the crucifixion and the divine sonship inside those books centuries before the Prophet ﷺ.

Premise 3. Either the books were reliable or they were corrupt

If reliable, Islam's denial of the crucifixion and the sonship falls. If already corrupt, no one could find anyone written in them, and the Qur'an's own proof appeal fails.

Five companion arguments patrol the exits. The no changer argument quotes Qur'an 6:34 against the corruption escape. The ask the readers argument reads Qur'an 10:94 as a certificate of the codices. The phantom Gospel argument says the corrupt Injil is a book nobody held. The concession argument dates the corruption doctrine to Ibn Hazm of Cordoba. The dominance argument reads Qur'an 3:55 as a promise of Christian standing until the Day of Resurrection.

Every one of these arguments walks across one bridge plank. The plank says that if something is findable in a book, the whole book is certified as reliable. The answer removes that plank. The answer is written for readers who accept the Qur'an as Allah's word, and it names that ground here so the third exit can recall it. The Qur'an itself pairs the finding claim with the corruption charge, in the same passages, against the same communities. Eight classical exegetes of both schools gloss the finding as description, and eleven recognition stories run on description, character, place, and a physical mark. Not one turns on a bare name line.

1. What Found Written Means

The verb and the construction come first, because the dilemma hangs its whole weight on them. The Qur'an uses the verb "find" with a described object in a state, over and over, and it never means a name read off a page.

The construction at 7:157 has three parts. The finders find him, the found one is in the state of being written, and the writing sits in their keeping. The Qur'an itself uses exactly this shape elsewhere, and each time the found thing is met in a described condition, not extracted as a label.

Source: Qur'an 18:77

Claim: Exhibit quoted below with citation

فَوَجَدا فِيها جِداراً يُرِيدُ أَنْ يَنْقُضَّ فَأَقامَهُ

fa-wajadā fīhā jidāran yurīdu an yanqaḍḍa fa-aqāmahu

"And the two found in it a wall about to collapse, and he set it up."

- Qur'an 18:77

Moses and his companion find a wall. The wall is found in a state, about to fall. The participle carries the content of the discovery. Nobody reads the wall.

Source: Qur'an 24:39

Claim: Exhibit quoted below with citation

لَمْ يَجِدْهُ شَيْئاً وَوَجَدَ اللَّهَ عِنْدَهُ فَوَفَّاهُ حِسابَهُ

lam yajidhu shayʾan wa-wajada Allāha ʿindahu fa-waffāhu ḥisābahu

"He does not find it anything, and he finds Allah at it, and Allah pays him his account in full."

- Qur'an 24:39

The finder at the mirage finds his hoped-for water as nothing, and finds Allah present at the spot. The construction with "at" or "with" carries a judgment met in a place, and the found thing can even be found as its own opposite.

Source: Qur'an 28:23

Claim: Exhibit quoted below with citation

وَوَجَدَ مِنْ دُونِهِمُ امْرَأَتَيْنِ تَذُودانِ

wa-wajada min dūnihim imraʾatayni tadhūdān

"And he found, apart from them, two women holding back their flock."

- Qur'an 28:23

Moses finds two women in a place set off from the crowd, in the state of holding their flock back. Place and state, found together. So "they find him written with them in the Torah and the Gospel" tells the finder in what state and in what keeping the found one is met. It does not say, and cannot be made to say, that a verbatim name line survives on a page.

2. The Eight Glosses

The classical glosses of both schools decide what the finding holds. The count across both schools is eight, and every one of them says description, or description together with a name, and none of them says the codex stands certified.

On the Shia side the earliest layer reads the phrase through Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇.

Source: Muhammad ibn Masud al-Ayyashi (d.320)

Claim: Exhibit quoted below with citation

87 - عن الثمالي عن أبي جعفر ﵇ قال : في قوله : (يجدونه) يعنى اليهود والنصارى صفة محمد واسمه مكتوبا عندهم في التورية والإنجيل ، يأمرهم بالمعروف وينهاهم عن المنكر .

"From al-Thumali, from Abu Jafar peace be upon him, on His saying "they find him." He means the Jews and the Christians find the description of Muhammad God's blessings and peace on him and his family and his name written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. He commands them with honor and forbids them the dishonorable."

- Muhammad ibn Masud al-Ayyashi (d.320), Tafsir al-Ayyashi, v02 p31, hadith 87, with parallels in Bihar al-Anwar v15 p229 and al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an v02 p594

The school's own gloss at the verse itself comes from al-Tabrisi.

Source: al-Tabrisi (d.548)

(الَّذِي يَجِدُونَهُ) أَيْ : يَجِدُونَ نَعْتَهُ أُولَئِكَ الَّذِينَ يَتَّبَعُونَهُ مِنْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ

"As for "whom they find," it means they find his description, those of the Children of Israel who follow him."

- al-Tabrisi (d.548), Jawami al-Jami, v01 p710

Al-Kashani's al-Safi fuses the two, "by his name and his description," and carries a report from the Majalis of al-Mufid in which a Jew recites the whole package to the Prophet ﷺ directly.

Source: al-Fayd al-Kashani (d.1091)

Claim: Exhibit quoted below with citation

إِنِّي قَرَأْتُ نَعْتَكَ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ : مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ ، وُلِدَ مُولَدُهُ بِمَكَّةَ ، وَمُهَاجَرُهُ بِطِيبَةَ ، لَيْسَ بِفَظٍّ وَلَا غَلِيظٍ وَلَا سَخَّابٍ وَلَا مُتَرَنِّنٍ بِالْفَحْشِ

"I read your description in the Torah. Muhammad son of Abdullah God's blessings and peace on him and his family, his birth at Mecca, his emigration to Tayba. He is not harsh, not coarse, not loud, and not versed in filth."

- al-Fayd al-Kashani (d.1091), al-Safi, v02 p242, from Majalis al-Mufid, chain to Ibn Abbas

Name, birthplace, emigration place, and four traits of character. That is the finding's content in the mouth of a Jewish reader, and the form matches every other item in the note.

"Ibn Abi Hatim transmitted to us, from al-Abbas ibn al-Walid, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada, on "whom they find written with them in the Torah." They find his description, his affair, and his prophethood written with them."

- Ibn Abi Hatim al-Razi (d.327), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Azim, v05 p190, hadith 8336

And the strongest corroboration of all comes from the jurist of Medina, in a report that adds the two things the debaters say cannot be there, location and motive.

قال ابن وهب قال مالك بلغني أن طائفة من اليهود نزلوا المدينة طائفة خيبر وطائفة فدك لما كانوا يسمعون من صفة النبي وخروجه في أرض بين حرتين ورجوا أن يكون منهم فأخلفهم الله ذلك وقد كانوا يجدونه مكتوبا عندهم في التوراة والإنجيل بأسمائه وصفاته

"Ibn Wahb said, Malik said, it reached me that a party of the Jews settled in Medina, a party in Khaybar, and a party in Fadak, because of what they used to hear of the Prophet's description God's blessings and peace on him and his family and his coming forth in a land between two lava fields. - Malik ibn Anas (d.179), as transmitted by Ibn Wahb, carried in Ibn al-Arabi, Ahkam al-Qur'an, v02 p324

Medina sits between two lava fields. Malik reports that Jews settled there, and in Khaybar, and in Fadak, because their books described a prophet arising in exactly that land. Recognition by description and by place, with names attached, from the most authoritative jurist of the Sunni school, a century before the earliest Shia corpus books were compiled.

Count the glosses. Al-Ayyashi says description and name. Al-Tabrisi says description. Al-Kashani says name and description. Qatada says description, affair, and prophethood. Malik says names and descriptions, plus the location. Al-Maturidi says what is written is that he is a sent prophet and unlettered. The Baydawi tradition says name and description, and al-Khafaji's commentary on it spells the pair out, by name and by description. Eight glosses across both schools, and not one of them reads "written" as a certificate of the codex's integrity.

One Shia gloss widens the finding past the Prophet ﷺ himself, and it will matter later in the note.

"Then He said, they find him written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. He means the Prophet God's blessings and peace on him and his family, and the Trustee, and the Riser. He commands them with honor when he rises, and he forbids them the dishonorable, and the dishonorable is whoever denies the Imam's excellence and rejects it."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v01 p477 and v02 p406, on Qur'an 7:156 to 7:157

The finding includes the Trustee, meaning Ali ﵇, the one entrusted with the Prophet's ﷺ succession, and the Riser, meaning the awaited Imam from his line. The written content is an office and a description, and the Imams of the House are inside it. A Christian debater who prepares for a name hunt meets instead a claim about prophethood, trusteeship, and succession, which is the claim the Shia school actually makes from the verse.

So the phrase means this. The People of the Book meet the Prophet ﷺ in their books as a described man, in a named office, from a named line, arising in a named land. Their own scriptures commanded them to keep his name traditions. The next section shows what those same scriptures, on the Qur'an's own telling, did with that trust.

3. Recognition and Alteration in One Breath

The dilemma needs the finding claim and the corruption charge to be enemies. The Qur'an will not cooperate. It asserts both, in the same passages, about the same keepers.

Open surah al-Baqara at the place where the Qur'an addresses the Children of Israel. Within a few verses the Qur'an says that a group of them displaces words from their places. It says they know the Messenger ﷺ the way a man knows his own son. It says they used to pray for victory by him before he came. Displacement, recognition, and the liturgical use of the recognition, all in one continuous address.

The corruption side of that address, at Qur'an 2:75, asks whether the hearers still hope these people will believe. A faction of them used to hear the word of Allah and then change it knowingly after they had understood it. The recognition side, at Qur'an 2:89 and 2:146, says they know him as they know their own sons, and a faction of them conceals the truth while they know. The same address calls the Qur'an a confirmer of what the People of the Book have with them, at Qur'an 3:3 and inside the 2:89 passage itself. Confirmation of content is the third face of the same structure. The books hold truth the Qur'an confirms, the keepers concealed part of it, and neither statement certifies the codex.

The Shia corpus explains how both can be true of the same books, and the explanation is the erasure report.

Source: Ikrima from Ibn Abbas

Claim: Exhibit quoted below with citation

وفي رواية عكرمة عن ابن عباس قال : إن أحبار اليهود وجدوا صفة النبي ﷺ مكتوبة في التوراة : أكحل أعين ربعة حسن الوجه ، فمحوه من التوراة حسدا وبغيا فأتاهم نفر من قريش فقالوا : أتجدون في التوراة نبيا منا ؟ قالوا : نعم نجده طويلا أزرق سبط الشعر ذكره الواحدي بإسناده في الوسيط .

"In a report of Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, the Jewish rabbis found the Prophet's description God's blessings and peace on him and his family written in the Torah, kohl eyed, of middle height, handsome of face. So they erased it from the Torah out of envy and spite. Then a group of Quraysh came to them and asked, do you find in the Torah a prophet from among us? They said, yes, we find him tall, blue eyed, with lank hair. Al-Wahidi recorded it with his chain in al-Wasit."

- Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, carried in Bihar al-Anwar v09 p67

Four verbs govern one object. The rabbis found the description. It was written. They erased it. And then they misreported it to a delegation that asked. The same book holds the findable content and the tampering hands, and the tampering is aimed at outsiders while the keepers know what they erased. This is not corruption as loss. It is corruption as concealment of a known thing.

The sequel in the same passage of Bihar shows the two assertions working together in one generation.

Muadh ibn Jabal and Bishr ibn al-Bara told them, company of Jews, fear God and submit. - Bihar al-Anwar v09 p67, on Qur'an 2:89

A description publicly invoked against their Arab rivals while the prophet was still awaited, then denied the moment he appeared outside their line. Recognition first, rejection of the recognized after. The finding claim and the corruption charge describe this one sequence, and neither cancels the other.

The Sunnis hold the same joint assertion. Al-Maturidi, commenting on the verse about those who write the book with their hands, says the rabbis altered the Prophet's description itself.

"Second, by what they have altered of their books, changed of their rulings, and distorted of his description, his characteristic, and his community's description."

- al-Maturidi (d.333), Tawilat Ahl al-Sunna, v02 p398, on Qur'an 2:79

And the Medinan case shows the concealment working while the text stayed on the page. The Jewish scholars of Medina held a Torah scroll that still contained the stoning verse. One of them read around it with his hand covering the line. Abdullah ibn Salam, a rabbi who had accepted Islam, ordered the hand lifted. The wording, the scroll, and the concealment all appear in the reports of Malik's Muwatta and Ibn Abd al-Barr's al-Tamhid.

So the joint assertion stands. The books held findable content, and the keepers tampered with the finding, by erasure, by misreport, and by concealment under a hand. A claim that the finding certifies the codex must now explain why the Qur'an praises the finding and charges the tampering in the same breath. Either the pairing is incoherent, which no debater wants to argue from a book he is quoting, or the finding certifies the found content and not the container. That is the Shia reading, and the bridge plank of the dilemma is gone.

4. The Recognition Catalogue

The catalogue settles what the finding held in practice. Story by story, in both schools' sources, the People of the Book recognized the Prophet ﷺ before and at his appearing. In no story does anyone find him by reading a bare name off a page. The readers who read their books read portraits, whole descriptions, and the portraits did the work. Description, character tests, places, and a physical mark are the medium every time.

The Qur'an's own simile for this recognition is a father picking his son out of a crowd of boys.

5. The Companion Arguments

The main horn of the dilemma has had its plank removed. The companion arguments patrol the exits, and each is answered in turn, with the dominance promise taking a full section after them because its occasion needs the room.

The no-changer argument quotes the Qur'an against itself. If none can change the words of Allah, and the Torah and the Gospel are Allah's words, then the corruption escape is closed by the Qur'an's own mouth. The argument only works if "the words of Allah" in those verses means the codex. The Qur'an uses two different verbs over two different objects, and neither pair crosses.

The no-change verses use the verb of replacing, over the words that are decrees and promises. At Qur'an 6:34 the context is the denied messengers of old, that God's help arrived at its appointed term, and no one replaces His words, the appointed help. At Qur'an 6:115 the word of the Lord is completed truthfully and justly. At Qur'an 10:64 the words are the promise itself, and the corpus applies them exactly so.

The strongest modern form of the dilemma drops the name argument and goes to the dating laboratories. Gospel manuscripts older than Islam by centuries carry the crucifixion. If the Qur'an denies what the oldest manuscripts affirm, the Qur'an is falsified by the very books it praised.

The argument needs the Qur'an to be denying what the manuscripts report. Read the verse as its own context presents it, clause by clause. The clause pair and the closing qualifier do different work. Qur'an 4:157 answers a list of Jewish boasts, that they killed the Messiah, that they crucified him, boasts set beside their slander of Mary ﵍. The first clause pair denies the boast outright. They did not kill him and did not crucify him. On the school's own reports the pair is true. The one killed and crucified was a likeness bearer, not Jesus ﵇. The closing qualifier denies something further. It denies that the killers possessed certainty of identity in what they did. A crucifixion of a likeness occurred. The Qur'an denies the killing of Jesus ﵇, and denies that the actors knew with certainty whom they had. The two denials sit in one verse, and the manuscript argument has to defeat both.

The chief exegete of the Sunni school fixes the meaning of that last phrase.

Now the manuscripts take their turn, and they cannot carry the weight the argument assigns them. A manuscript tradition can be stable and still be the stable record of a semblance. The Greek Gospels are narratives composed a lifetime and more after the event, and their own prologue says so. Luke's opening concedes that he writes what was handed down by those who were eyewitnesses from the beginning. He was not one of them. The oldest complete copies preserve that inherited testimony, and inherited testimony about an event of semblance is precisely what the verse says the disagreeing parties had. John's closing chapters make it from inside the party the verse says followed conjecture. That is the affirmation the verse predicts, not evidence of the fact. Manuscript dating measures the age of the witness. It cannot convert a participant's claim or a compiler's order into certainty. The corpus charges the keepers with displacing the word and the description. The epistemic answer was carried on the crucifixion, because the manuscript evidence is pressed there. The sonship falls with the same criterion once the crucifixion answer stands.

8. The Exits Closed

A determined debater leaves any note by three doors. This section closes them.

The first door says the corruption was interpretive only, that the text never changed, only the reading. The door is opened by Muslim apologists themselves, and it cannot lead where they want. If the books were textually whole and only misread, then the tongue distortion of Qur'an 3:78 governs them. The reciters twisted the Book with their tongues while the page stayed whole. The Qur'an itself names that mode. A book whose keepers misread it aloud to every hearer cannot ground the certain knowledge of the crucifixion that the first horn needs. The debater who walks through this door escapes the corruption charge by confirming the stronger charge, that the community's teaching left the text behind. And the Medinan case stands at the door. The stoning verse was on the page, under a hand, misread around. Textual presence with communal misreport is exactly the corpus's account of the condition.

The second door says the Gospel writers were certain, whatever the verse's epistemics. The door has a handle, and it opens into the same room. Luke's prologue announces a narrative compiled from what was handed down by the eyewitnesses and ministers of the word. He offers Theophilus certainty in place of what he has been taught by word. This is the writing of a compiler, the claim of order and diligence, and pointedly the claim of a man who was not there. John's closing chapters do claim the testimony of the eyewitness, at John 19:35 and 21:24. That claim is a certainty claim made from inside the party the verse describes as following conjecture about a semblance. The verse predicts affirmations of certainty from exactly that quarter, so the affirmation is the confirmation, not the refutation. Luke shows the genre includes compilers, John shows the genre includes certainty claims from participants, and the verse addresses the participants' knowledge, which it says was conjecture. The pincer requires the verses to supply a certainty their own texts show to be compiler's order and participant's claim.

The third door is the sharpest, and it is aimed at the reader of the note more than at the note. You Muslims cite our Bible as prophecy of your Prophet ﷺ, says the debater, so you have certified our book, and your corruption charge contradicts your own dawah. The door closes on the note's whole doctrine, stated once as one sentence. The school cites what survives and charges what was displaced, because the finding certifies the found content and never the container. And the criterion for which is which is not the school's whim. It is the Qur'an itself. The Qur'an names itself a guardian over the earlier books, muhaymin, at 5:48. That verse stands in the very passage the debater quotes. What the earlier books carry in agreement with the Qur'an is the surviving content. What contradicts it is the displaced or altered material, exactly as the Qur'an is the tester of the keepers' claims in the majlis. Within the audience named at the start, readers who accept the Qur'an as Allah's word, the criterion is stated and non-circular. The two uses are one doctrine, the joint assertion the Qur'an itself makes, recognition and tampering in the same address. The debater needs the school to hold an all or nothing premise about the codex, and the school never held it. Every citation this school makes of the Torah and the Gospel is a citation of surviving content, exactly like the Imam's ﵇ citation of the Paraclete passage to the Exilarch. And every corruption charge is a charge about displacement, exactly like the erasure report. The consistency fork fails because it assumes two doctrines where there has only ever been one.

And with the third door closed, the finding claim stands in its final form. Whom they find written with them. The found one is met by description, character, office, place, and mark, inside books his own scriptures commanded to keep his name and warned they would efface it. The finding has worked, in every generation recorded here, precisely because it never depended on the page staying whole.

Glossary of Key Terms

Tahrif is displacement of received wording or meaning, the charge the Qur'an makes at 2:75, 4:46, and 5:13.

Tabdil is replacing, the verb of the no change verses, which governs God's decrees and promises and never the codex.

Na't and sifa both mean description, the portrait given in words. Every recognition account in the article runs on one.

Muhaymin means guardian or witness, the Qur'an's word at 5:48 for its own authority over the earlier books.

Namus is the Arabic form of a Greek word for the companion of revelation. Waraqa and the Negus use it of the angel who came to Moses ﵇.

Shubbiha lahum means it was made a likeness to them, the Qur'an's word at 4:157 for the semblance at the crucifixion.

Yaqin means certain knowledge, and the verse denies the killers had it.

Injil is the Gospel, the revelation given to Jesus ﵇. It names the content God gave him, carried in the community's book.

Hal is the Arabic grammatical state, the accusative of circumstance, the construction of written in 7:157.

Final Verdict

The Islamic Dilemma welds the Qur'an's praise of the earlier scriptures to their seventh century codices. The weld is the argument's whole strength, and its whole weakness, because the Qur'an itself refuses it. It praises the finding and charges the tampering in one address. Finding and tampering are the same story told twice, once as the keepers' treasure and once as the keepers' crime.

The companion arguments fall with the weld. The no change verses protect the decrees. The ask the readers verse is counterfactual, and its consultees are the prophets of the heavens. The Gospel is neither certified nor lost but cited, as a present book whose keeper affirmed the cited passage. The corruption doctrine is older than the Andalusian flip by two centuries. The crucifixion verse denies the killing of Jesus ﵇ and denies the killers' certainty, so the oldest manuscripts refute nothing the verse asserts. The dominance promise sits between Jesus's ﵇ followers and his own people's disbelievers, and the Muslims stand with the followers.

The finding worked because it was never a wager on any codex, and the dilemma fails because it was never anything else.
