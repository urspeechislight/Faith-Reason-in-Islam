The Argument Under Examination

A common Christian argument presents the Qur'an with a dilemma, and the objection begins with real Qur'anic statements. The Qur'an speaks of the Torah.

إِنَّا أَنْزَلْنَا التَّوْرَاةَ فِيهَا هُدًى وَنُورٌ

"Indeed, We sent down the Torah, in which was guidance and light..."

- Qur'an 5:44

It speaks of the Gospel.

وَلْيَحْكُمْ أَهْلُ الْإِنْجِيلِ بِمَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ فِيهِ

"Let the People of the Gospel judge by what Allah revealed in it..."

- Qur'an 5:47

And it speaks of Muhammad ﷺ.

النَّبِيَّ الْأُمِّيَّ الَّذِي يَجِدُونَهُ مَكْتُوبًا عِنْدَهُمْ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ وَالْإِنْجِيلِ

"...the unlettered Prophet whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel..."

- Qur'an 7:157

The Christian objection asks what appears to be an obvious question. If these books contained enough divine truth to be consulted and to identify Muhammad ﷺ, how can Islam reject what those same books say about Jesus?

Premise 1. The Qur'an commands the Book's people to judge by their books

The Qur'an calls the Torah and the Gospel guidance and light. It tells Jews and Christians to judge by them. It says the People of the Book find the Prophet ﷺ written in them, at Qur'an 5:43 to 5:48 and 7:157.

Premise 2. The seventh century books carried the crucifixion and the sonship

Gospel manuscripts copied centuries before the Prophet ﷺ already carry the crucifixion narratives and the doctrines the church taught alongside them.

Premise 3. Either the books were reliable or they were corrupt

If those books were reliable enough to identify Muhammad ﷺ, why should their testimony about the crucifixion and other Christian doctrines be rejected? But if they had already been corrupted, how could the Qur'an appeal to them at all?

The objection also has five companion forms. One stands on Qur'an 6:34, there is no changer for the words of Allah. One reads Qur'an 10:94, ask those who read the Book before you, as a certificate of the biblical collections. One says the corrupt Injil is a book nobody held. One dates the corruption doctrine to Ibn Hazm of Cordoba. One reads Qur'an 3:55 as a promise of Christian standing until the Day of Resurrection.

The dilemma depends on one assumption. If a book still contains genuine revelation, the whole book must therefore be textually and doctrinally reliable. The Qur'an does not make that assumption. It describes the earlier scriptures as containing truth that remains recognizable while also accusing some of their custodians of concealing, distorting, or altering what they received. The two claims appear side by side.

The question is therefore not whether the Torah and Gospel contained truth in the seventh century. The Qur'an plainly says they did. The central answer comes first. The Qur'an's own model is partial preservation, not a choice between perfect preservation and total disappearance. The books held findable content, and some custodians concealed, distorted, or erased parts of it. Affirming surviving truth does not require affirming everything in the books that carried it. The question is whether affirming surviving truth requires affirming everything found in the books that carried it. That is the question this article examines.

Evidence at a Glance

In brief. The Qur'an does not require complete preservation of earlier scripture. It affirms surviving truth and accuses custodians of alteration in the same passages. Early Muslim interpretation understood Qur'an 7:157 primarily through description and recognition, not through a surviving literal name line. Muslim sources simultaneously cite surviving biblical material and accuse the same scriptural communities of alteration or concealment. Citation of surviving material does not amount to certification of an entire biblical collection.

1. Judge by What God Revealed in Them

The objection stands on the passages where the Qur'an tells the People of the Book to judge by their books. This section quotes those passages in order and states what each one says.

وَكَيْفَ يُحَكِّمُونَكَ وَعِنْدَهُمُ التَّوْرَاةُ فِيهَا حُكْمُ اللَّهِ

"Yet how is it that they come to you for judgment, while the Torah is with them, containing the judgment of God..."

- Qur'an 5:43

The words that matter are with them and containing. The book is present in the community's keeping, and it holds God's judgment. The verse asks why they seek judgment elsewhere while this book is with them.

إِنَّا أَنْزَلْنَا التَّوْرَاةَ فِيهَا هُدًى وَنُورٌ

"Indeed, We sent down the Torah, in which was guidance and light..."

- Qur'an 5:44

The Torah is guidance and light, and the prophets, the rabbis, and the scholars judge by it, entrusted with the Book of God. The verse praises content and assigns custodians. It does not state that the seventh century biblical collection is textually reliable as a whole.

وَلْيَحْكُمْ أَهْلُ الْإِنْجِيلِ بِمَا أَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ فِيهِ

"Let the People of the Gospel judge by what Allah revealed in it..."

- Qur'an 5:47

The Gospel command names its object precisely. The People of the Gospel judge by what God revealed in the Gospel. The verse does not separately state that every proposition associated with the Gospel is itself revelation.

وَأَنْزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ بِالْحَقِّ مُصَدِّقًا لِمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَمُهَيْمِنًا عَلَيْهِ

"And We sent down to you the Book in truth, confirming what is before it of the Book, and a guardian over it."

- Qur'an 5:48

The word that matters is guardian, muhaymin. The Qur'an confirms what is before it of the Book and stands guardian over it. This verse supplies the criterion this page uses for what survives, and the criterion is examined again where the objection from quoting the Bible is answered.

قُلْ يَا أَهْلَ الْكِتَابِ لَسْتُمْ عَلَى شَيْءٍ حَتَّى تُقِيمُوا التَّوْرَاةَ وَالْإِنْجِيلَ

"Say, O People of the Book, you stand on nothing until you uphold the Torah and the Gospel..."

- Qur'an 5:68

The same passage tells the People of the Book they stand on nothing until they uphold the Torah and the Gospel. Upholding the surviving revelation is not certifying a whole collection.

A report of al-Kafi answers the question of custody from inside the tradition.

2. Recognition and Corruption in the Same Passage

The central question is whether the Qur'an contradicts itself. It does not. The Qur'an asserts two things side by side, in the same passages, about the same keepers.

Something remained recognizable

الَّذِينَ آَتَيْنَاهُمُ الْكِتَابَ يَعْرِفُونَهُ كَمَا يَعْرِفُونَ أَبْنَاءَهُمْ

"Those to whom We gave the Book recognize him as they recognize their own sons..."

- Qur'an 2:146

فَلَمَّا جَاءَهُمْ مَا عَرَفُوا كَفَرُوا بِهِ

"...when there came to them what they recognized, they disbelieved in it..."

- Qur'an 2:89

The word that matters is recognize. The Qur'an says they knew whom they were looking at, with the certainty of a father picking out his son, and 2:89 adds that some disbelieved in the very thing they had recognized.

Yet alteration and concealment also occurred

يَسْمَعُونَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا عَقَلُوهُ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ

"...a group of them used to hear the word of Allah and then distort it after they had understood it, knowingly."

- Qur'an 2:75

وَإِنَّ فَرِيقًا مِنْهُمْ لَيَكْتُمُونَ الْحَقَّ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ

"...a group of them conceal the truth while they know."

- Qur'an 2:146

يَلْوُونَ أَلْسِنَتَهُمْ بِالْكِتَابِ لِتَحْسَبُوهُ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَمَا هُوَ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَيَقُولُونَ هُوَ مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ وَمَا هُوَ مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ وَيَقُولُونَ عَلَى اللَّهِ الْكَذِبَ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ

"And a party of them twist their tongues with the Book, that you may suppose it to be from the Book when it is not. And they say, it is from God, when it is not. And they speak against God the lie while they know."

- Qur'an 3:78

The words that matter are knowingly and while they know. The Qur'an charges deliberate distortion and deliberate concealment, not passive loss. At Qur'an 3:78 the alteration is spoken aloud, recited as though it were the Book itself.

These are not two theological systems being forced together centuries later. They are statements the Qur'an itself makes about the same scriptural communities. Something remained recognizable, while something was also concealed or distorted.

The same address calls the Qur'an a confirmer of what the People of the Book have with them, at Qur'an 3:3 and inside the 2:89 passage itself. Confirmation of content is the third face of the same structure. The books hold truth the Qur'an confirms, the keepers concealed part of it, and neither statement certifies the whole biblical collection.

The Shia sources cited here explain how both can be true of the same books, and the explanation is the erasure report.

The knowledge survives even where the wording was removed.

The wording, the scroll, and the concealment all appear in the reports of Malik's Muwatta and Ibn Abd al-Barr's al-Tamhid.

The conclusion, and its limit. The Qur'an's own model is partial preservation, not a choice between perfect preservation and total disappearance. The books held findable content, and the keepers tampered with that content, by erasure, by misreport, and by concealment under a hand. This answers the internal contradiction charge. It does not establish which specific wording was altered in any surviving manuscript.

3. What Qur'an 7:157 Actually Claims

Qur'an 7:157 describes the Prophet ﷺ as the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. The verse does not say, they find the name Muhammad written. Its wording is broader. They find him as someone described or recorded in the scriptures they possess.

The Arabic word maktuban, written, describes the condition in which they find him. In ordinary English the idea is close to saying, they find him described in their books. That still leaves an open question. What did those descriptions contain.

They might include a name. They might include his characteristics, location, office, conduct, or other identifying signs. The grammar alone does not decide that question. The early commentaries do, and the recognition reports add their witness.

One parallel shows the pattern. At Qur'an 18:77 the two travelers find a wall about to collapse, and the wall's state is part of what they find. The verb to find takes its object in a described state. Qur'an 7:157 works the same way. They find him written, and written names the state in which he is met.

Qur'an 7:157 does not make its claim depend upon one surviving sentence containing the name Muhammad. It claims that the Prophet ﷺ was recognizable from what remained in their scriptural tradition. What that remainder contained is the work of the commentaries and the recognition reports that follow.

4. How Early Muslims Understood the Written Description

The verse leaves open what the written material contained. A consistent pattern appears across the early commentaries. Recognition is explained through description and prophetic identity, sometimes including the name. None of these glosses requires the further conclusion that the entire biblical collection was textually reliable.

On the Shia side the earliest layer reads the phrase through Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇.

Source: Muhammad ibn Masud al-Ayyashi (d.320)

87 - عن الثمالي عن أبي جعفر ﵇ قال : في قوله : (يجدونه) يعنى اليهود والنصارى صفة محمد واسمه مكتوبا عندهم في التورية والإنجيل ، يأمرهم بالمعروف وينهاهم عن المنكر .

"Al-Thumali reported from Abu Jafar ﶇ. On His saying, they find him, he said, He means the Jews and the Christians find the description of Muhammad ﷺ and his name written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. He commands them with honor and forbids them the dishonorable."

- Muhammad ibn Masud al-Ayyashi (d.320), Tafsir al-Ayyashi, v02 p31, hadith 87, with parallels in Bihar al-Anwar v15 p229 and al-Burhan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an v02 p594

- al-Tabrisi (d.548), Jawami al-Jami, v01 p710

إِنِّي قَرَأْتُ نَعْتَكَ فِي التَّوْرَاةِ : مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ ، وُلِدَ مُولَدُهُ بِمَكَّةَ ، وَمُهَاجَرُهُ بِطِيبَةَ ، لَيْسَ بِفَظٍّ وَلَا غَلِيظٍ وَلَا سَخَّابٍ وَلَا مُتَرَنِّنٍ بِالْفَحْشِ

- al-Fayd al-Kashani (d.1091), al-Safi, v02 p242, from Majalis al-Mufid, chain to Ibn Abbas

Name, birthplace, emigration place, and four traits of character. That is the content in the mouth of a Jewish reader, and the form matches every other item in the table.

The Sunni layer starts with Qatada, a generation after the Companions.

Source: Ibn Abi Hatim al-Razi (d.327)

حدثنا محمد بن يحيي ، أنبأ العباس بن الوليد ثنا يزيد ، عن سعيد ، عن قتادة قال : الَّذِي يَجِدُونَه مَكْتُوباً عِنْدَهُمْ فِي التَّوْراةِ قال : يَجِدُونَ نَعْتَهُ وَأَمْرَهُ وَنُبُوَّتَهُ مَكْتُوباً عِنْدَهُمْ .

"Ibn Abi Hatim transmitted to us, from al-Abbas ibn al-Walid, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada, on "whom they find written with them in the Torah." They find his description, his affair, and his prophethood written with them."

- Ibn Abi Hatim al-Razi (d.327), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Azim, v05 p190, hadith 8336

A report attributed to Malik ibn Anas, the jurist of Medina, adds location and motive.

Source: Malik ibn Anas (d.179)

قال ابن وهب قال مالك بلغني أن طائفة من اليهود نزلوا المدينة طائفة خيبر وطائفة فدك لما كانوا يسمعون من صفة النبي وخروجه في أرض بين حرتين ورجوا أن يكون منهم فأخلفهم الله ذلك وقد كانوا يجدونه مكتوبا عندهم في التوراة والإنجيل بأسمائه وصفاته

They used to find him written with them in the Torah and the Gospel, with his names and his descriptions."

- Malik ibn Anas (d.179), as transmitted by Ibn Wahb, recorded in Ibn al-Arabi, Ahkam al-Qur'an, v02 p324

Medina sits between two lava fields. Malik reports that Jews settled there, and in Khaybar, and in Fadak, because their books described a prophet arising in exactly that land. Early interpreters did not reduce Qur'an 7:157 to a literal name appearing in an intact manuscript tradition. Across the glosses below, recognition runs through description, office, prophethood, place, and sometimes the name. Some of these glosses are quoted below in the original.

One Shia gloss widens what is found past the Prophet ﷺ himself, and it returns in the fifth section.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

ثم قال : « يَجِدُونَهُ مَكْتُوباً عِنْدَهُمْ فِي التَّوْراةِ وَالْإِنْجِيلِ » يَعْنِي النَّبِيَّ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَآلِهِ والْوَصِيَّ والْقَائِمَ « يَأْمُرُهُمْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ » إِذَا قَامَ « وَيَنْهاهُمْ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ » والْمُنْكَرُ مَنْ أَنْكَرَ فَضْلَ الْإِمَامِ وَجَحَدَهُ

"Then He said, they find him written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. He means the Prophet ﷺ, and the Trustee, and the Riser. He commands them with honor when he rises, and he forbids them the dishonorable, and the dishonorable is whoever denies the Imam's excellence and rejects it."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v01 p477 and v02 p406, on Qur'an 7:156 to 7:157

The finding includes the Trustee, meaning Ali ﵇, the one entrusted with the Prophet's ﷺ succession, and the Riser, meaning the awaited Imam from his line. The written content is an office and a description, and the Imams of the House are inside it. In this Imami reading, the written material extends beyond the Prophet's ﷺ name to his office, his succession, and the awaited Imam from his line.

So the phrase means this. The People of the Book meet the Prophet ﷺ in their books as a described man, in a named office, from a named line, arising in a named land. One Imami report, examined in the fifth section, says their own scriptures commanded them to keep his name traditions. The second section shows what those same scriptures, on the Qur'an's own telling, did with that trust.

5. How the Recognition Traditions Work

The recognition reports show how Muslim tradition understood this claim in practice. Not every report proves the same proposition, and the reports sort into four types by the marker that carried the recognition.

Recognition by explicit description. Recognition by place and physical sign. Recognition by continuity of revelation. And Imami reports about broader written prophecy, including the Prophet ﷺ together with his House. One strong example carries each type in the body below, and the remaining reports sit in the attestations box at the end.

Recognition by explicit description

The Qur'anic simile emphasizes how certain the recognition was. In the Ibn Salam tradition, that certainty is explicitly tied to the Prophet's description.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v15 p182

تفسير علي بن إبراهيم : (الذين آتيناهم الكتاب يعرفونه كما يعرفون أبناءهم) الآية ، فإن عمر بن الخطاب قال لعبد الله بن سلام : هل تعرفون محمدا في كتابكم ؟ قال : نعم والله نعرفه بالنعت الذي نعته الله لنا إذا رأيناه فيكم ، كما يعرف أحدنا ابنه إذا رآه مع الغلمان ، والذي يحلف به ابن سلام لأنا بمحمد هذا أشد معرفة مني بابني

"Ali ibn Ibrahim's Tafsir, on those We gave the Book know him as they know their own sons. Umar ibn al-Khattab said to Abdullah ibn Salam, do you find Muhammad in your Book? He said, yes, by God, we know him by the description God set down for us when we see him among you. It is as one of us knows his son when he sees him among the boys. By the One in whose name Ibn Salam swears, we know this Muhammad ﷺ more surely than I know my own son."

- Bihar al-Anwar v15 p182, from Tafsir al-Qummi, on Qur'an 2:146

The Qur'anic comparison expresses certainty of recognition. Ibn Salam's report then explains what that recognition rested on, the description he believed God had given in scripture. Malik's report, quoted in the fourth section, shows the same type, where Jews settle Medina itself because their books describe a prophet arising in that land.

Recognition by place and physical sign

Salmān al-Fārisī received identifying markers from the last Christian teacher he served, and the markers named a place and a sign.

Source: Musnad Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d.241)

قَالَ : أَيْ بُنَيَّ ، وَاللَّهِ مَا أَعْلَمُهُ أَصْبَحَ عَلَى مَا كُنَّا عَلَيْهِ أَحَدٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ آمُرُكَ أَنْ تَأْتِيَهُ ، وَلَكِنَّهُ قَدْ أَظَلَّكَ زَمَانُ نَبِيٍّ هُوَ مَبْعُوثٌ بِدِينِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ يَخْرُجُ بِأَرْضِ الْعَرَبِ ، مُهَاجِرًا إِلَى أَرْضٍ بَيْنَ حَرَّتَيْنِ بَيْنَهُمَا نَخْلٌ ، بِهِ عَلَامَاتٌ لَا تَخْفَى : يَأْكُلُ الْهَدِيَّةَ ، وَلَا يَأْكُلُ الصَّدَقَةَ ، بَيْنَ كَتِفَيْهِ خَاتَمُ النُّبُوَّةِ ، فَإِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ أَنْ تَلْحَقَ بِتِلِكَ الْبِلَادِ فَافْعَلْ

"He said, my son, by God I know of no one left upon what we were upon that I could tell you to go to. But the era of a prophet has dawned over you, sent with the religion of Abraham, coming forth in the land of the Arabs, emigrating to a land between two lava fields with palms between them. In him are signs that do not hide. He accepts the gift and does not eat the alms tax. Between his shoulder blades is the seal of prophethood. If you can reach that land, do so."

- Musnad Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d.241), al-Risala edition, v39 p144, the Salman narration

Two locations, Arabia and the land between the lava fields, one character test, and one physical mark, the seal of prophethood. The same narration carries the moment Salmān saw the town and recognized it by his teacher's description. The Bahīrā report belongs to this type as well, with the seal between the shoulders as the master marker.

Recognition by continuity of revelation

Waraqa ibn Nawfal recognized the event by its parallel with Moses, and the Negus of Abyssinia recognized by the kinship of what he heard with what Jesus brought.

Source: Ibn Hibban (d.354)

به قال نعم فدعا النجاشي أساقفته فنشروا المصاحف حوله فقرأ عليهم جعفر بن أبي طالب كهيعص فبكى النجاشي حتى اخضل لحيته وبكت أساقفته حتى اخضلوا مصاحفهم ثم قال إن هذا والذي جاء به عيسى يخرج من مشكاة واحدة انطلقا فلعمر الله لا أرسلهم معكما ولا أكاد ولا هم

"He said yes, and the Negus called his bishops and they spread their scriptures around him, and Jaafar ibn Abi Talib ﶇ recited to them from Maryam. The Negus wept until his beard was soaked, and his bishops wept until their scriptures were soaked. Then he said, this and what Jesus ﶇ brought come out from one lamp. Go, for by the life of God, I will never send them back with you, and I will not mistreat them."

- Ibn Hibban (d.354), al-Thiqat, v01 p66, with parallels in Musnad Ahmad, al-Risala edition, v29 p314, and Ibn Abi al-Hadid, Sharh Nahj al-Balagha, v06 p310

This type works differently from the first two. The Negus identifies no description and consults no marker. He hears recitation and rules that it comes from the same source as Jesus's message. Waraqa's report, in the attestations box, works the same way through the Greatest Nāmūs, the companion of revelation who used to come to Moses.

Imami reports about broader written prophecy

The Imami reading widens the written content past the Prophet ﷺ himself, to the succession, and the name.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

"I bequeath to you, O Moses ﶇ, the bequest of the tender and anxious one. It concerns the son of the virgin, Jesus son of Mary ﶇ, master of the she-ass, the cloak, the oil, the olive, and the prayer niche. After him comes the master of the red camel, the good, the pure, the purified. His likeness in your book is that he is a believer, a guardian over all the books, bowing, prostrating, devout, a worshipper. His brothers are the poor, his helpers are another people, and in his time there will be hardship, earthquake, killing, and little wealth. His name is Ahmad, Muhammad the Trustworthy, remaining of the band of the former ones who passed. Command the wrongdoers of the Children of Israel not to let his name be effaced and not to forsake him, and yet they will do it. Love of him is a good deed with Me. I am with him, I am of his party, he is of Mine, and his party are the overcoming. My words are completed. I shall make his religion manifest over all religions."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v08 p67, with a parallel in Bihar al-Anwar v13 p335, from Tuhaf al-Uqul

This report shows the tradition's own explanation for the absence of an explicit name in surviving scripture. It transmitted reports in which preservation of the name was commanded and its later effacement foretold. That shows the doctrine is internally coherent and early. It is not external historical evidence of a particular textual deletion.

6. If No One Can Change God's Words, What Was Altered?

The objection sets two verses of the Qur'an against each other. If none can change the words of Allah, and the Torah and the Gospel are Allah's words, then the accusation of alteration is closed by the Qur'an's own mouth. The objection only works if the words of Allah in those verses means the physical books.

وَتَمَّتْ كَلِمَةُ رَبِّكَ صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ

"And the word of your Lord is fulfilled, in truth and in justice. None can change His words. He is the Hearing, the Knowing."

- Qur'an 6:115

The words that matter are fulfilled and none can change His words. The statement sits on God completing what he has decreed, and its parallels, at Qur'an 6:34 and 18:27, carry the same sense. Several of the passages that say none can change God's words concern his decrees, promises, judgments, and promised victory. When the Qur'an says God's words cannot be changed, the natural reading is that no one can thwart what God has decreed. Meanwhile the Qur'an separately accuses humans of mishandling revealed material. No one can thwart God's word does not naturally mean no human can ever modify the wording of a manuscript.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

قال : في يونس قول الله ههنا : الذين آمنوا وكانوا يتقون لهم البشرى في الحياة الدنيا وفي الآخرة لا تبديل لكلمات الله ذلك هو الفوز العظيم

"He said, in Yunus is God's word here. Those who believed and were God fearing, theirs is the glad tidings in the present life and the next. No changer for the words of Allah. That is the great triumph."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v03 p133, applied to the glad tidings given to the dying believer

At Qur'an 7:137 the same usage stands in the open. The good word that God completed upon the Children of Israel, because they bore patiently, is the promise of the land, fulfilled.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

فأنزل الله «وَتَمَّتْ كَلِمَةُ رَبِّكَ الْحُسْنى عَلى بَنِي إِسْرائِيلَ بِما صَبَرُوا»

"God sent down, and your Lord's good word was completed upon the Children of Israel because they bore patiently."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v02 p88

And the long address to Moses ﵇ already quoted in the fifth section carries the same usage inside it, my words are completed, I shall make his religion manifest. The words that cannot be replaced are the decrees and the promises. The displaced thing, in the verses that charge displacement, is another object under another verb. The lexical evidence supports the contextual answer. The verses on changing God's words use the verb tabdil, replacing, over the words that are decrees and promises. The verses that charge displacement use a different verb, tahrif, over the word moved from its places, at Qur'an 2:75, 4:46, and 5:13. That is the pattern in the passages at issue here.

Ibn Taymiyya, the Sunni authority the debaters themselves quote on other matters, reads the verses on changing God's words with the completion language. His reading matches this structure, the words completed truthfully and justly, not a sealed manuscript.

An Imami ta’wil of the displaced word

Al-Qummi, on the verse where God hardens hearts against those who displace the word from its places, names the displaced word itself. This is an Imami interpretive layer, recorded here as part of the school’s tafsir tradition.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329)

(وجعلنا قلوبهم قاسية يحرفون الكلم عن مواضعه) قال : من نحي أمير المؤمنين عن موضعه ، والدليل على أن الكلم أمير المؤمنين ، قوله : وجَعَلَها كَلِمَةً باقِيَةً فِي عَقِبِه يعني الإمامة

"On the verse, We made their hearts hard, they displace the word from its places, he said, it means whoever removed Ali ibn Abi Talib ﶇ from his place. The proof that the word is Ali ﶇ is His saying, and He made it an enduring word in his progeny, meaning the Imamate."

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329), Tafsir al-Qummi, v01 p189, on Qur'an 5:13, from al-Sadiq ﵇

In this reading the word whose displacement the Qur'an charges is the enduring word of the Imamate, announced to Abraham ﵇ and standing in his progeny, and the men who displaced it are the men who removed the Trustee from his place. The primary contextual answer above does not depend on this layer.

7. Does Qur'an 10:94 Tell Muhammad to Trust the Biblical Collections

The objection rests on Qur'an 10:94.

فَإِنْ كُنْتَ فِي شَكٍّ مِمَّا أَنْزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ فَاسْأَلِ الَّذِينَ يَقْرَءُونَ الْكِتَابَ مِنْ قَبْلِكَ

"So if you are in doubt concerning what We have revealed to you, ask those who have been reading the Book before you..."

- Qur'an 10:94

The primary answer does not depend on identifying who is meant to be asked. Even if the verse directs consultation toward knowledgeable People of the Book, consultation about a proposition does not certify every proposition in every book they possess. If someone says, ask a physicist whether the Earth is spherical, that does not certify everything in the physicist's library. A limited appeal to scriptural witnesses can authenticate the point for which they are consulted without granting complete textual inerrancy. Transmitted Muslim interpretations additionally deny that the Prophet ﷺ ever actually doubted, and the Shia sources carry reports to that effect from the Imams ﵇.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v17 p90

عن إبراهيم بن عمير رفعه إلى أحدهما في قول الله لنبيه ﷺ : فإن كنت في شك مما أنزلنا إليك فأسأل الذين يقرؤون الكتاب من قبلك قال : قال رسول الله ﷺ : لاشك ولا أشك

"Ibrahim ibn Umayr reported, raised to one of the two Imams ﶇ. It concerns God's word to His Prophet ﷺ, if you are in doubt about what We sent down to you, ask those who read the Book before you. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, there is no doubt, and I do not doubt."

- Bihar al-Anwar v17 p90, from Ilal al-Sharai

Al-Dhahabi transmits the same verdict in the Sunni record, he did not doubt and he did not ask. Al-Nahhas reads the address as spoken to the community through the Prophet ﷺ. That is the standard figure of the Qur'an's addresses. Al-Kashani records the occasion and the purpose. The objectors had said, what, a messenger who eats food and walks in the markets. The verse sent the Prophet ﷺ to ask them, in front of the ignorant, whether God ever sent a messenger who did not. The conditional stands for the sake of the invitation, as the mubahala verse, the mutual curse challenge of Qur'an 3:61, offers the challenge no one is expected to need. And the Shia school adds its own resolution of who the asked are.

Source: Furat ibn Ibrahim al-Kufi (d.352)

عن زرارة بن أعين قال : قلت لأبي جعفر ﵇ … قلت : قوله : «فإن كنتَ في شكّ مما أنزلنا إليك فأسأل الذّين يقرؤون الكتاب من قبلك» من هؤلاء الذين أمر رسول الله ﷺ بسؤالهم ؟ فقال : إن رسول الله ﷺ لما أسري به إلى السماء … جمع الله لي النبيّين والصدّيقين والملائكة ، فأذّن جبرئيل وأقام الصلاة ، ثم تقدّم رسول الله فصلّى بهم ، فلما انصرف قال : بم تشهدون ؟

"Zurara ibn Ayan said, I said to Abu Jafar ﶇ, as for His saying, if you are in doubt, ask those who read the Book before you. Who are those the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was commanded to ask? He said, when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was taken by night to the heavens, God gathered for me the prophets, the truthful, and the angels. Gabriel called the prayer and stood it, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ stepped forward and led them in it. When he turned he said, what do you testify to?"

- Furat ibn Ibrahim al-Kufi (d.352), Tafsir Furat al-Kufi, p182, and al-Hajj Sa'id Abu Ma'ash, Wasiyyat Amir al-Muminin, p354, from Zurara ibn Ayan

In this Imami report, those asked are identified as the prophets themselves, gathered in the fourth heaven, the scriptures' actual recipients, questioned at the source of revelation. A verse about consulting heaven's own bearers certifies no earthly book.

8. What Does the Qur'an Mean by the Injil

The objection offers a choice. Either the Injil is the four Gospels, and then the accusation of alteration indicts the Qur'an's own praise of it. Or the Injil is a lost single book given to Jesus ﵇, and then the Qur'an commands people to judge by a book nobody ever held. The choice assumes the school's practice, and the practice refutes it. The sources cited here quote the Injil the way they quote the Torah, by formula, as a present, citable, contested book.

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329)

قَالَ : « فِي التَّوْرَاةِ مَكْتُوبٌ : يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ ، تَفَرَّغْ لِعِبَادَتِي ؛ أَمْلَأْ قَلْبَكَ غِنًى وَلَا أَكِلْكَ إِلى طَلَبِكَ ، وَعَلَيَّ أَنْ أَسُدَّ فَاقَتَكَ ، وَأَمْلَأَ قَلْبَكَ خَوْفاً مِنِّي ، وَإِنْ لَاتَفَرَّغْ لِعِبَادَتِي ، أَمْلَأْ قَلْبَكَ شُغُلًا بِالدُّنْيَا ، ثُمَّ لَاأَسُدَّ فَاقَتَكَ ، وَأَكِلْكَ إِلى طَلَبِكَ

"In the Torah is written, son of Adam, be vacant for My worship and I will fill your heart with richness and I leave you not to your own seeking. Upon Me is to close your want, and to fill your heart with fear of Me. And if you are not vacant for My worship, I fill your heart with the occupation of the world, then I do not close your want, and I leave you to your own seeking."

- al-Kulayni (d.329), al-Kafi, v03 p214, from al-Sadiq ﵇

The Imams ﵇ cite, and the Prophet ﷺ himself cites with the Hebrew in his mouth. The first thing written in the Torah is Muhammad the Messenger of Allah. In the Hebrew tongue it is tab. The practice never treats the Torah as either certified or lost. It treats it as a book that still carries surviving truth alongside alteration.

The Injil receives the same treatment, and the strongest witness is the majlis of Imam al-Rida ﵇ with the Jewish exponent.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v16 p92

في حديث طويل عن الرضا في مناظرته مع أصحاب المقالات قال لرأس الجالوت : في الإنجيل مكتوب : ابن البَرَّة ذاهب ، والبارقليطا جاء من بعده ، وهو يخفف الآصار ، ويفسر لكم كل شئ ، ويشهد لي كما شهدت له ، أنا جئتكم بالأمثال وهو يأتيكم بالتأويل ، أتؤمن بهذا في الإنجيل ؟ قال : نعم لا أنكره

"From a long report of al-Rida's ﶇ disputation with the holders of the creeds. He said to Ras al-Jalut, in the Injil is written, the son of the virgin is departing, and the Barqlita has come after him. He lightens the burdens, he interprets everything for you, and he testifies for me as I testified for him. I came to you in parables and he comes to you with the interpretation. Do you believe this is in the Injil? He said, yes, I do not deny it."

- Bihar al-Anwar v16 p92, hadith 21, from Uyun Akhbar al-Rida, with a parallel in al-Rayshahri, Nabi al-Rahma, p67

The Imam quotes a passage he identifies as coming from the Injil, and Ras al-Jalut, the person speaking with him, replies that he does not deny it. The same sources cited here charge those communities with displacing words from their places. A third possibility exists besides those two. The Qur'anic Injil is the revelation God gave Jesus ﵇. By the Prophet's time, Christians possessed books and traditions that the Islamic sources treat as carrying some surviving Injil material alongside alteration and later transmission. The verses about judging by what God revealed in them are examined in the first section. The same surah charges the keepers with displacement in the same breath.

The singular name itself settles nothing about physical volumes. Singular names often refer to a scriptural revelation or collection without telling us how many physical books or literary compositions carry it. The Qur'an can refer to divine Injil material as present and usable without identifying every surviving Christian composition with the original revelation given to Jesus ﵇.

9. Did Muslims Invent Textual Corruption After Ibn Hazm

Al-Maturidi, who died in 333 of the Hijra, accuses the Book's people in his commentary on Qur'an 2:79 of altering their books and changing their rulings. His book survives from the fourth century of the Hijra, and it already carries an explicit accusation of textual alteration. Ibn Hazm of Cordoba, who died in 456, therefore did not invent the accusation. More than a century later, he pressed it further.

Separate from the accusation of alteration stands the earliest named practitioner of biblical citation, Ali ibn Rabban al-Tabari, a Nestorian physician who accepted Islam and died in 247 of the Hijra. His book, the Book of Religion and Empire, quoted Isaiah and Habakkuk with the name in the text. The quotation displayed below concerns prophetic identification, and it does not by itself demonstrate an accusation of textual alteration. It shows that citing biblical material predates Ibn Hazm by two centuries.

Earlier Muslims citing Biblical material does not mean they considered the entire Bible intact. Citation of selected material alongside accusations of alteration is exactly the pattern already shown, and the historical record reinforces it.

Source: quoted in al-Zindani

جاء في سفر أشعيا : «إني جعلت اسمك محمدا ، يا محمد يا قدوس الرب ، اسمك موجود من الأبد» … وجاء في سفر حبقوق : «إن اللّه جاء من التيمان ، والقدوس من جبل فاران ، لقد أضاءت السماء من بهاء محمد ، وامتلأت الأرض من حمده» ذكره علي بن ربن الطبري في كتابه (الدين والدولة)

"From the scroll of Isaiah, I have made your name Muhammad. O Muhammad, O holy one of the Lord, your name exists from eternity. And from the scroll of Habakkuk, God came from Tayman, and the Holy One from Mount Faran. The heaven shone with the splendor of Muhammad ﷺ, and the earth was filled with his praise. Ali ibn Rabban al-Tabari recorded it in his book, the Book of Religion and Empire."

- quoted in al-Zindani, Bayyinat al-Rasul wa-Mu jazatih, p32, from Ali ibn Rabban al-Tabari (d.247), al-Din wa-l-Dawla

The claim that alteration predates Ibn Hazm is already established by the early material discussed above, the erasure report recorded in Bihar, the name-preservation report of al-Kafi, and the accusation verses in the commentaries. Ibn Rabban is additional evidence. Because the particular wording quoted here reaches us through a later carrier, it is not load-bearing. A convert who had been a Christian scholar, writing two centuries before the supposed concession, quotes Habakkuk's Faran passage with the name of Muhammad ﷺ in it. This agrees in essence with the majlis of Imam al-Rida ﵇ quoted in the twelfth section, where the same Habakkuk text stands with the name Ahmad. These are two Muslim transmissions of a similar prophetic reading, not independently established witnesses.

The accusation of alteration appears in the earliest Imami sources, standing beside the citation of the Bible from the first layer. Al-Kafi cites the Torah and the Injil by formula, and the sources transmit the erasure of the description. The majlis quotes the Injil's Paraclete passage to Ras al-Jalut, and the same sources charge those communities with displacement. Citation and alteration were held together from the beginning, and the doctrine was not born of a debate in eleventh century Cordoba.

10. What Do Pre-Islamic Crucifixion Manuscripts Actually Establish

The strongest modern form of the dilemma sets the name question aside and turns to manuscript dating. Gospel manuscripts older than Islam by centuries carry the crucifixion. If the Qur'an denies what the oldest manuscripts affirm, the Qur'an is falsified by the very books it praised.

What the manuscripts establish is that Christians were reporting the crucifixion long before Islam. What they do not establish is that the Qur'an's affirmation of surviving earlier revelation certifies that particular report as true. The verse at the center of the objection reads.

وَقَوْلِهِمْ إِنَّا قَتَلْنَا الْمَسِيحَ عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ وَمَا قَتَلُوهُ وَمَا صَلَبُوهُ وَلَكِنْ شُبِّهَ لَهُمْ وَإِنَّ الَّذِينَ اخْتَلَفُوا فِيهِ لَفِي شَكٍّ مِنْهُ مَا لَهُمْ بِهِ مِنْ عِلْمٍ إِلَّا اتِّبَاعَ الظَّنِّ

"...and for their saying, we killed the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, the messenger of God. They did not kill him and did not crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them. And those who differ over it are in doubt of it, having no knowledge of it beyond following conjecture..."

- Qur'an 4:157

Read the verse as its own context presents it, clause by clause. The verse makes two denials, then closes with a statement about what the killers knew. Qur'an 4:157 answers a list of Jewish boasts, that they killed the Messiah, that they crucified him, boasts set beside their slander of Mary ﵍. The first clause pair denies the boast outright. They did not kill him and did not crucify him. On the school's own reports the pair is true. The one killed and crucified was a likeness bearer, not Jesus ﵇. The closing qualifier denies something further. It denies that the killers possessed certainty of identity in what they did. A crucifixion of a likeness occurred. The Qur'an denies the killing of Jesus ﵇, and denies that the actors knew with certainty whom they had. The two denials sit in one verse, and the manuscript objection has to defeat both.

Al-Tabari glosses that last phrase in his commentary.

Source: al-Tabari (d.310)

ما كان لهم بمن قتلوه من علم ، ولكنهم اتبعوا ظنهم فقتلوه ، ظنا منهم أنه عيسى ، وأنه الذي يريدون قتله ، ولم يكن به = وما قتلوه يقينًا ، يقول : وما قتلوا هذا الذي اتبعوه في المقتول الذي قتلوه وهم يحسبونه عيسى ، يقينًا أنه عيسى ولا أنه غيره ، ولكنهم كانوا منه على ظن وشبهة . وهذا كقول الرجل للرجل : ما قتلت هذا الأمر علمًا ، وما قتلته يقينًا ، إذا تكلم فيه بالظن على غير يقين علم

"They had no knowledge of the one they killed. They followed their conjecture and killed him, supposing him to be Jesus ﶇ, supposing him to be the one they meant to kill, and he was not. They did not kill him with certainty. That is, they did not kill this one they pursued and killed while reckoning him Jesus, with certainty that it was Jesus or that it was other than him. They were upon conjecture and doubt. It is like a man saying to a man, you did not kill this matter by knowledge, nor by certainty, when he speaks of it by conjecture without certain knowledge."

- al-Tabari (d.310), Jami al-Bayan, Shakir edition, v09 p377

Someone, on al-Tabari's reading, was killed on a guess, by people who could not be sure who they had. The verse denies certain knowledge of the killing of Jesus ﵇, inside an event of semblance, and affirms his raising. The Shia sources carry the same structure in the long report of the competing claims.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v14 p338

حتى طلبته اليهود وادعت أنها عذبته ودفنته في الأرض حيا وادعى بعضهم أنهم قتلوه وصلبوه ، وما كان الله ليجعل لهم سلطانا عليه ، وإنما شبه لهم وما قدروا على عذابه ودفنه ولا على قتله وصلبه … فلما أراد أن يرفعه أوحى إليه أن استودع نور الله وحكمته وعلم كتابه شمعون بن حمون الصفا

"Until the Jews sought him, and one party claimed they tortured him and buried him alive in the earth, and another party claimed they killed and crucified him. And God would not give them authority over him. It was made a likeness for them, and they could not reach him, not to torture, not to bury, not to kill, not to crucify. And when God willed to raise him, He revealed to him, deposit the light of God and His wisdom and the knowledge of His Book with Simon son of Hamon al-Safa."

- Bihar al-Anwar v14 p338

The parties to the event itself could not agree on what they had done, torture and burial in one telling, killing and crucifixion in another. The verse answers a boast with a denial of certainty. Pre-Islamic manuscripts establish that Christians were reporting and believing the crucifixion before Islam. They do not, simply by being old manuscripts, establish that the Qur'an endorses that report or that the Qur'an regards the transmitters as possessing certain knowledge of Jesus's identity. Their existence does not create an internal contradiction in the Qur'an, because the Qur'an's own model allows an earlier community to transmit a claim that the Qur'an later rejects. On the standard scholarly dating, the Greek Gospels are narratives composed decades after the event. Luke presents his account as an orderly compilation based on traditions handed down by those he describes as eyewitnesses from the beginning. The oldest complete copies preserve that inherited testimony, and inherited testimony about an event of semblance is precisely what the verse says the disagreeing parties had. This is not an independent historical proof of substitution. It answers the narrower charge that the Qur'an contradicts itself by acknowledging earlier scripture while denying the crucifixion. The objection's premise also lists the divine sonship. Divine sonship and crucifixion are logically distinct doctrines, and they are answered separately. The same Qur'anic criterion applies to the sonship on its own. The Qur'an rejects it regardless of its presence in surviving Christian texts.

11. Who Is Above Whom in Qur'an 3:55

The last companion objection concerns a promise read into Qur'an 3:55. God tells Jesus ﵇, I am taking you and raising you to Me and purifying you from those who disbelieved. And I am setting those who follow you above those who disbelieved until the Day of Resurrection. On that reading the Christian church keeps dominance to the end of time, and no later religion can supersede it.

The verse answers from its own occasion, and the Shia sources transmit the occasion in full.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329)

إن عيسى وعد أصحابه ليلة رفعه الله إليه ، فاجتمعوا إليه عند المساء وهم اثنا عشر رجلا فأدخلهم بيتا ثم خرج عليهم من عين في زاوية البيت وهو ينقض رأسه من الماء ، فقال إن الله أوحى إلي انه رافعي إليه الساعة ومطهري من اليهود فأيكم يلقى عليه شبحي فيقتل ويصلب ويكون معي في درجتي ، فقال شاب منهم انا يا روح الله قال فأنت هوذا

"Jesus ﶇ promised his companions the night God raised him to Himself. They gathered to him at evening, twelve men, and he brought them into a house, then came out upon them from a spring in the corner of the house, wringing the water from his head. He said, God has revealed to me that this hour He raises me to Himself and purifies me from the Jews. Which of you will have my likeness cast upon him, to be killed and crucified, and be with me in my rank? A young man among them said, I, O Spirit of God. He said, then you are he."

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329), Tafsir al-Qummi, v01 p129, from Humran ibn Ayan, from Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ﵇, on Qur'an 3:55

يَا عِيسَى إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ وَمُطَهِّرُكَ مِنَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَجَاعِلُ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوكَ فَوْقَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا إِلَى يَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ

"O Jesus, I am taking you and raising you to Myself, and purifying you from those who disbelieved, and placing those who followed you above those who disbelieved until the Day of Resurrection..."

- Qur'an 3:55

The verse's drama is the raising and the escape. The people Jesus ﵇ is purified from are the Jews who came to kill him. Those are the disbelievers of the verse, the ones who disbelieved in him, not the Muslims, who believe in him as a messenger of God. Two transmitted readings divide over who the followers are. The Imami reading takes them as Jesus's immediate faithful followers. An alternative classical reading takes the superiority historically, the Christians above the Jews. On either reading, the verse compares the followers of Jesus with those who rejected Jesus.

Source: Ibn Zayd

قال الذين كفروا من بني إسرائيل . وجاعل الذين اتبعوك قال : الذين آمنوا به من بني إسرائيل وغيرهم ، فوق الذين كفروا النصارى فوقَ اليهود إلى يوم القيامة ، قال : فليس بلد فيه أحد من النصارى إلا وهم فوق يهود في شرق ولا غرب ، هم في البلدان كلها مستذلون

"The disbelievers are those of the Children of Israel. As for those who follow you, they are those who believed in him of the Children of Israel and others, above those who disbelieved, the Nasara above the Jews until the Day of Resurrection. He said, there is no town containing Christians where they are not above the Jews, east or west. In all the lands the Jews are humbled."

- Ibn Zayd, transmitted by Yunus from Ibn Wahb, in al-Tabari, Jami al-Bayan, Shakir edition, v06 p463

Neither reading certifies Nicene Christianity against Islam. Even on the temporal reading, the verse promises Christians standing above the Jews who disbelieved in Jesus ﵇, and no later Christian doctrine follows from that standing.

The sister verse at Qur'an 61:14 names the followers precisely, a faction of the Children of Israel believed and a faction disbelieved, and God aided the believers against their enemy. Al-Qummi places the two factions at the event of the likeness itself.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329)

(فآمنت طائفة من بني إسرائيل وكفرت طائفة) قال : التي كفرت هي التي قتلت شبيه عيسى وصلبته والتي آمنت هي التي قبلت شبيه عيسى حتى لا يقتل ( فقتلت الطائفة التي قتلته وصلبته وهو قوله : فأيدنا الذين آمنوا على عدوهم فأصبحوا ظاهرين )

"On a faction of the Children of Israel believed and a faction disbelieved, he said, the disbelieving faction is the one that killed the likeness of Jesus ﶇ and crucified it. The believing faction is the one that accepted the matter of the likeness so he would not be killed. The believing faction prevailed over the faction that killed and crucified, and that is His word, so We gave the believers victory over their enemy and they became manifest."

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329), Tafsir al-Qummi, v02 p366, on Qur'an 61:14

In al-Qummi's reading, the followers are the believers of Jesus's ﵇ own generation, the men and women who knew the crucified one was a likeness and said so, and their victory is historical. And the school reads the pattern forward. The Kafi report on the same verse says the apostles of Jesus ﵇ were his partisans, and our partisans are our apostles. The line continues in the partisans of the House ﵇. On every reading, the verse's until the Day of Resurrection qualifies the superiority of whichever party the verse means. Even if the superiority is understood historically and continues until the Day of Resurrection, the objection still has to show two things. It has to show that those who follow you means the later Nicene church, and that historical superiority proves its doctrines true. The verse establishes neither conclusion.

12. A Secondary Question: Possible Biblical Correlates

The Islamic Dilemma is already answered on the internal evidence of the earlier sections, and nothing in the case so far depends on what follows. The secondary question is whether surviving biblical texts contain material Muslims can reasonably connect to Muhammad ﷺ. The claim of Qur'an 7:157 stands on the evidence already exhibited in the earlier sections, and it needs no biblical verse.

First, the like of Moses. This parallel is Qur'anic before it is biblical, and the recognition stories already quoted carry it. The Qur'an itself declares the parallel.

Source: Qur'an 73:15

إِنَّا أَرْسَلْنا إِلَيْكُمْ رَسُولاً شاهِداً عَلَيْكُمْ كَما أَرْسَلْنا إِلى فِرْعَوْنَ رَسُولاً

inna arsalna ilaykum rasulan shahidan alaykum kama arsalna ila firawna rasula

"We have sent to you a messenger, a witness over you, as We sent to Pharaoh a messenger."

- Qur'an 73:15

Qur'an 73:15 establishes an Islamic Moses and Muhammad parallel. It does not by itself establish that Deuteronomy 18 predicts Muhammad ﷺ. The biblical passage, if discussed, must be treated independently. Within the Islamic record, the People of the Book who accepted recognized by this marker. Waraqa knew the Greatest Namus that came to Moses ﵇, and the Negus said the Qur'an and the message of Jesus come from one lamp. The two reports support the continuity of revelation in different ways. Both are recorded in the strongly graded hadith collections of the Sunni school.

Second, the Faran cluster. One caveat comes first. Muslim authors report that earlier scriptural readers identified Faran with Mecca. That report is evidence for the history of Muslim interpretation, and not by itself proof of Jewish or Christian consensus. Within that record, the Torah's blessing in Deuteronomy 33 names three places for three theophanies. The Lord came from Sinai, shone from Seir, and was manifest from the mountains of Faran. The Muslim sources cited here identify the three as the Torah to Moses ﵇, the Gospel to Jesus ﵇ of Seir's Nazareth, and the Qur'an to Muhammad ﷺ from Faran.

Source: al-Nuwayri (d.733)

فأما ما اتفقوا عليه مما جاء في التوراة وترجموه بالعربية ورضوا ترجمته فمن ذلك قوله : «جاء اللَّهُ من طُورِ سِيناءَ ، وأشْرَقَ لنا من ساعِيرَ ، واسْتَعْلى من جِبالِ فاران» … قال العلماء : وفي هذا تصريح بنبوّة محمد ﷺ … وفاران : هي مكة

"As for what they agreed upon of what came in the Torah, and translated into Arabic, and accepted the translation of, among it is His saying, God came from Mount Sinai, shone upon us from Seir, and was manifest from the mountains of Faran. The scholars said, in this is an explicit declaration of the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ. And Faran is Mecca."

- al-Nuwayri (d.733), Nihayat al-Arab, v16 p110

Al-Maqrizi presses the same record further, saying there is no disagreement between the Muslims and the People of the Book that Faran is Mecca, and challenging any who claim otherwise to bring their claim. The agreement the Muslim sources report is itself reported by Muslims, a fact stated plainly. What carries weight is that a Muslim encyclopedist of the fourteenth century could present the verse as a text whose Arabic translation the Book people themselves had accepted. He set it alongside his whole apparatus of sources, without fear of contradiction on the identification. The identification is well attested within the Muslim interpretive tradition, while the claim of broader Jewish-Christian agreement requires independent evidence.

Third, the two riders and the mountain of Faran, in the majlis. Imam al-Rida ﵇ quoted Isaiah and Habakkuk to the Jewish exponent as the latter's own books.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v14 p165

وقال شعيا النبي فيما تقول أنت وأصحابك في التوراة : « رأيت راكبين أضاء لهما الأرض أحدهما على حمار والآخر على جمل » … قال : أما راكب الحمار فعيسى وأما راكب الجمل فمحمد ﷺ ، أتنكر هذا من التوراة ؟ قال : لا ما أنكره . ثم قال الرضا ﵇ : … « جاء الله بالبيان من جبل فاران ، وامتلأت السماوات من تسبيح أحمد وأمته ، يحمل خيله في البحر كما يحمل في البر ، يأتينا بكتاب جديد بعد خراب بيت المقدس » يعني بالكتاب القرآن

"The prophet Isaiah said, in what you and your companions hold in the Torah, I saw two riders, for whom the earth lit up, one upon a donkey and one upon a camel. He said, as for the rider of the donkey, it is Jesus ﶇ, and as for the rider of the camel, it is Muhammad ﷺ. Do you deny this is in the Torah? He said, no, I do not deny it. Then al-Rida ﶇ said, God came with the exposition from Mount Faran, and the heavens were filled with the praise of Ahmad and his community. His horses are borne on the sea as they are borne on the land. He comes to us with a new book after the ruining of the Temple, meaning by the book, the Qur'an."

- Bihar al-Anwar v14 p165, with a parallel in Nur al-Thaqalayn, v02 p80, which glosses Faran as a mountain of Mecca a day's distance from it

Two images, two riders, and the name Ahmad, quoted against the Exilarch, who affirmed he did not deny it. The dialogue reaches us through Muslim transmitters, and it is ranked accordingly, strong inside the tradition's record, offered as Imami testimony and not as neutral external evidence.

Fourth, Kedar. Isaiah's new song passage, in the Arabic carrying books, sends the praise to the villages that Qaydar inhabits, and Qaydar is a name in the Prophet's ﷺ own line, on both schools' genealogical record.

Source: Bihar al-Anwar v35 p143

بن نبت بن قيدار بن إسماعيل ، وإسماعيل أول من فتق لسانه بالعربية المبينة التي نزل بها القرآن

"Son of Nabt, son of Qaydar, son of Ishmael ﶇ. Ishmael ﶇ was the first whose tongue opened with the clear Arabic in which the Qur'an came down."

- Bihar al-Anwar v35 p143, the Prophet's ﷺ genealogy, with a parallel in Ibn Qutayba, al-Marif

The villages of Kedar praising with a new song, in a line the sources themselves name as the Prophet's ﷺ ancestry, is a reading of medium strength, dependent on the carried translation.

Fifth, the Paraclete. The majlis passage quoted in the eighth section already carries the Imam's ﵇ citation of the departing son of the virgin and the coming Barqlita who interprets all things, affirmed by the Exilarch. Bihar al-Anwar carries the Gospel's promise of another Fardaqlit who remains with them the whole of the latter age. The school's identification of the promised one with Muhammad ﷺ rests on these passages as carried. The periklytos reading, the claim that the Greek text itself once read the praised one in place of the advocate, is rejected. That reading is a guess about a word, recorded in no early manuscript, and the identification does not need it.

Four claims are excluded. The machmadim claim, which reads the Song of Songs' phrase about him who is altogether desirable as a hidden name of Muhammad ﷺ, is wordplay on a plural noun, and it is left out. The Arabic Infancy Gospel's naming claim fails at the standard scholarly reference, whose complete summary of the text contains no such prophecy, and whose manuscripts are all from 1299 or later. The extant Gospel of Barnabas is a medieval composition. The sixth century list that condemns a gospel under Barnabas's name preserves no quotation to tie it to the extant book, and the mention proves a title and nothing more. The valley of Baca bridge, tying Psalm 84 to Bakkah of Qur'an 3:96, appears nowhere in the Muslim sources cited here.

13. Remaining Objections

Three objections remain.

Objection 1. What if the corruption was interpretive rather than textual?

Some Muslim apologists answer the accusation of alteration by saying the text never changed, only the reading. Some Qur'anic passages do concern concealment or oral distortion. The reciters twisted the Book with their tongues while the page stayed whole, and the Qur'an itself names that mode in Qur'an 3:78. But the sources examined above do not limit tahrif to interpretation. They also transmit explicit claims of erased descriptions and altered wording. The Medinan case shows the concealment working while the text stayed on the page, the stoning verse read around under a hand. The Islamic case therefore does not depend on proving that every charge of tahrif is textual. It only needs to show that the Islamic model allows both surviving truth and alteration.

Objection 2. What about the Gospel authors' certainty?

Luke distinguishes his own ordered account from the testimony handed down by those described as eyewitnesses from the beginning. John's closing chapters claim eyewitness testimony directly, at John 19:35 and 21:24. The objection says these authors were certain, so the verse's report of conjecture is refuted. The surviving Christian texts preserve claims of certainty about the crucifixion, while Qur'an 4:157 denies that the relevant people possessed warranted certainty about Jesus's identity. The certainty claims therefore create no internal contradiction in the Qur'an. Luke shows the genre includes compilers. John shows the genre includes certainty claims from participants. The verse addresses the participants' knowledge. This is not an independent historical proof of substitution. It answers the narrower charge that the Qur'an contradicts itself by acknowledging earlier scripture while denying the crucifixion. The limit is this. A reader who does not accept the Qur'an's account will weigh the eyewitness claims as history and will not be moved by this internal fit.

Objection 3. If Muslims quote the Bible, are they not authenticating it?

This objection is aimed at the Muslim reader. You Muslims cite our Bible as prophecy of your Prophet ﷺ, says the debater, so you have certified our book, and your charge of corruption contradicts your own dawah. The answer is one doctrine, stated in two sentences. Quoting one passage as surviving truth does not amount to declaring the entire textual tradition free from alteration. The Imami sources do both at once, citing passages they regard as genuine and accusing the same scriptural communities of distortion elsewhere. The criterion for which is which is not the school's whim. It is the Qur'an itself.

وَأَنْزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ بِالْحَقِّ مُصَدِّقًا لِمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ وَمُهَيْمِنًا عَلَيْهِ

"And We sent down to you the Book in truth, confirming what is before it of the Book, and a guardian over it."

- Qur'an 5:48

The word that matters is guardian, muhaymin. The Qur'an names itself the guardian over the earlier books in that verse, and the verse stands in the very passage the objection quotes. What the earlier books carry in agreement with the Qur'an is the surviving content. What contradicts it is the displaced or altered material, exactly as the Qur'an tests the keepers' claims in the majlis. For the internal consistency question addressed here, this criterion is legitimate, because the objection itself begins by asking whether the Qur'an contradicts its own claims. Recognition and tampering are not two doctrines forced together. They are the joint assertion the Qur'an itself makes in the same address. The citations examined here of the Torah and the Gospel are citations of surviving content, exactly like the Imam's ﵇ citation of the Paraclete passage to the Exilarch. Every corruption charge is a charge about displacement, exactly like the erasure report. The objection assumes the school holds an all or nothing premise about the biblical collections, and the school never held it.

With the third objection answered, the verse stands in its plain form. Whom they find written with them. The Prophet ﷺ is met by description, character, office, place, and mark. One Imami report goes further, saying that preservation of his name had been commanded and that its later effacement was foretold. People recognized him across every generation these reports record, precisely because recognition never depended on the page staying whole.

Glossary of Key Terms

Tahrif is displacement of received wording or meaning, the charge the Qur'an makes at 2:75, 4:46, and 5:13.

Tabdil is replacing, the verb of the verses that say none can change God's words. In the no changer passages examined here, it concerns God's decrees and promises.

Na't and sifa both mean description, the portrait given in words. Most recognition accounts in these reports work through a description. The Negus report is the exception, where recognition comes from the continuity of revelation.

Muhaymin means guardian or witness, the Qur'an's word at 5:48 for its own authority over the earlier books.

Namus is the word Waraqa uses of the angel who came to Moses ﵇, the bearer of revelation.

Shubbiha lahum means it was made to appear so to them, the Qur'an's word at 4:157 for the semblance at the crucifixion. Imami and many classical reports explain that appearance through a likeness or substitute.

Yaqin means certain knowledge, and the verse denies the killers had it.

Injil is the Gospel, the revelation given to Jesus ﵇. The Imami sources treat surviving Christian scriptural tradition as containing material from that revelation while also allowing alteration elsewhere.

Hal is the Arabic grammatical state, the accusative of circumstance, the construction of written in 7:157.

Final Verdict

The Islamic Dilemma ties the Qur'an's praise of the earlier scriptures to the seventh century biblical collections. That assumption is the objection's whole strength, and its whole weakness, because the Qur'an itself refuses it. The Qur'an praises what the People of the Book still find in their books and charges their custodians with tampering in the same address. Recognition and alteration are the same story told twice, once as the keepers' treasure and once as the keepers' crime.

The remaining objections are answered on the same ground. The verses that say none can change God's words protect his decrees and promises, not the custodians' books. Qur'an 10:94 licenses a limited consultation, and a consultation does not certify every proposition in the book consulted. The Injil is cited as a present book, and the person speaking with the Imam affirmed the cited passage, neither certified whole nor dismissed as lost. The accusation of alteration predates Ibn Hazm. The crucifixion manuscripts show Christians reporting the crucifixion before Islam. They do not create a contradiction inside the Qur'an, because the Qur'an's own model allows an earlier community to transmit a claim the Qur'an later rejects. Qur'an 3:55 compares the followers of Jesus ﵇ with those who rejected him, and it does not certify later Christian doctrine.

Surviving revelation does not entail total reliability of the whole book, and the whole dilemma rests on assuming that it does.
