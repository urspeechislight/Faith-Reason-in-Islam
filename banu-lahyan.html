The Expedition of Banu Lahyan

Two years after the men of al-Raji' were betrayed and killed, the Prophet ﷺ marched into the Hudhayl highlands to take their blood, found the canyons empty, and came home reciting the words of return with no battle fought. Part 20 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Empty Highlands

The debt of al-Raji' stood unpaid. Khubayb ibn Adi and his companions had been lured to their deaths by the tribes they were sent to teach, and Lahyan of Hudhayl held the blood.

Between the truce of Hudaybiyya and the march on Khaybar, the Prophet ﷺ took the army into their highlands. The Lahyan read the size of the column from their ridges and emptied the ground before it. The army came home without a strike.▼ commentary▼ commentary

وكان بين صلح الحديبية ( سنة ست من الهجرة ) وبين غزوة خيبر غزوة بني لحيان ، وغزوة ذي قرد (4) ، خرج فيهما رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم واستعمل ابن أمّ مكتوم على المدينة .

وكان سبب الأولى طلب بأصحاب الرجيع خبيب بن عديّ وأصحابه .

وسبب الثانية إغارة المشركين على لقاح لرسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم بالغابة ، وقتل رجل من بني غفار ، واحتمال امرأته مع اللّ

"Between the truce of Hudaybiyya, in the sixth year, and the expedition of Khaybar came the expedition of Banu Lahyan and the expedition of Dhi Qard, and the Messenger of Allah went out in both, and put Ibn Umm Maktum in charge of Medina. The cause of the first was blood for the men of al-Raji', Khubayb ibn Adi and his companions. The cause of the second was the raid of the idolaters on the milk camels of the Messenger of Allah at al-Ghaba, the killing of a man of Ghifar, and the carrying off of his wife with the camels."

- al-Nadwi, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, p.418

""He found them fortified on the tops of the mountains, and when the enemy he wanted escaped him, they said, let us go down to Usfan and show the people of Mecca that we came to it. So he went out with two hundred riders of his companions and stopped at Usfan, then sent two riders on until they reached Kara al-Ghamim, and they returned at evening going home. Jabir ibn Abd Allah used to say, 'I heard the Messenger of Allah say, Repentant, returning, if Allah wills, praising our Lord, worshiping Him. "Ka'b ibn Malik said, on the expedition of Banu Lahyan, 'Had the Lahyan but waited to face them, they would have met a host in their homeland, sure of its promise, a swift one whose terror fills the raiding party, before a crusher like a thundercloud in the van. But they were a people scattered, fleeing the passes of the Hijaz with nothing settled.'"

- Ka'b ibn Malik's verse, in al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unaf, vol.6, p.366

Commentary

The Sources for Lahyan

Source spine: al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unaf, vol.6, p.366, carrying the return prayer from Jabir and Ka'b ibn Malik's verse

Other carriers: al-Kharkushi, Sharaf al-Mustafa, vol.3, p.49. al-Nadwi, al-Sira al-Nabawiyya, p.418, on the cause and the calendar. Ibn Sa'd, al-Tabaqat al-Kubra, vol.2, p.79, on the heights, the feint, and the prayer

Citation note: the serial note placed the return prayer in al-Suhayli at a page the corpus holds for the verse alone, so the prayer is quoted from Ibn Sa'd, where it grounds with its march context

Dating note: al-Nadwi places the march between the Hudaybiyya truce and Khaybar, while the series index follows al-Waqidi's list, which dates it Rabi' al-Awwal of year six, before Hudaybiyya. The disagreement stays open

Deputy over Medina: Ibn Umm Maktum, shared with the Dhi Qard march

The march sits between the Hudaybiyya truce and Khaybar in the calendar, and its cause is named as blood for the betrayed men of al-Raji'. The works record no fighting, and Ka'b's verse is itself the record, a tribe gone from its own valleys before the column arrived.

A Debt Taken to the Highlands

Cause: the killing of Khubayb ibn Adi and his companions at al-Raji'

Commander: the Prophet ﷺ, with Ibn Umm Maktum over Medina

Ground: the Hudhayl highlands, the Lahyan canyons

Contact: none. The tribe scattered, and the army returned

Adjacent: the Dhi Qard raid answered the camel theft at al-Ghaba on the same watch

The march could not restore the dead of al-Raji', and the works do not pretend it meant to. What it did was carry the debt to the debtor's own ground and let the highlands read the price of another betrayal. Ka'b's verse holds the mirror up, a people who fled their own settled home because a column of sure promise was in the passes. No one was killed, and the return prayer Jabir preserved marks the march as a return, repentant and worshiping.

The strategic outcome ran together with the next march. Within weeks the Dhi Qard pursuit answered the camel raid on al-Ghaba, and the year closed with Hudaybiyya signed and Khaybar ahead, the highlands quiet on both flanks of the road.

Was the Expedition of Banu Lahyan Justified

The cause was the murder of envoys, teachers sent out to tribes who had asked for them and betrayed by other hands for a bounty, and the blood was laid to Lahyan of Hudhayl. The march was retribution carried to the murderers' ground, and it cost the Lahyan their homeland's peace and nothing else. No assault on a settlement, no pursuit into the scattering, no killing is recorded by any work quoted here.

Measured against the law of that world, a blood debt collected by a march that spills no blood is restraint. The record shows the march ending in a feint toward Mecca and a homegoing prayer, with no pursuit. The Qurayza, judged months before under the full rigor of the same law, stand in the same series as the measure of what the Prophet's ﷺ hand could have done at Lahyan and did not.

The reprisal could have been prevented. The prevention lay two years back, at al-Raji', in the hands of the men who sold their guests. After the betrayal, only the debt's collection or its abandonment were on the table, and a state that abandons its envoys' blood raises the price on the next ten.
