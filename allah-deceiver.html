A Christian Charge Weighed Against the Lexicons

Allah Repays the Plot, He Does Not Start It

The charge that the Qur'an worships 'the best of deceivers' collapses on the word makr itself, because the lexicons split the word into a praised class and a blamed class and fix Allah's makr as repayment of the plotters' own makr, every makr verse names the human plot first and joins Allah's act to it as its answer, the superlative crowns the very class of plotters the verse convicts, and the attack's own Bible keeps the same grammar of answered delusion in 2 Thessalonians 2, Ezekiel 14, 1 Kings 22, Jeremiah 20, and Titus 1. The Argument Under Examination

Premise 1. The Lexicons Split the Word

The attack hangs on one English word. Makr, it says, means deceit, so a verse that says Allah makara must call Allah a deceiver. The dictionaries refuse to certify that reading. Raghib's al-Mufradat, the lexicon of Qur'anic words, splits makr into a praiseworthy class and a blameworthy class, and it files "Allah is the best of makirin" under the praiseworthy class at the verse itself. Lisan al-Arab records the settled reading of the interpreters. Makr from Allah is repayment, named after the makr it answers, exactly as "the repayment of an evil deed is an evil deed like it" names a punishment after the sin. Al-Misbah and Maqayis al-Lughah say the same. The translator who prints "deceiver" has already chosen the blamed class for a word the lexicons place in the praised class.

Premise 2. Every Verse Names the Human Plot First

The Qur'an's usage carries the same frame. In every verse where makr is said of Allah, the human plot comes first and Allah's act is joined to it with wa- as its answer. Quraysh met at Dar al-Nadwa to hold, kill, or expel the Prophet , and Qur'an 8:30 answers their council verse by verse. The parties who plotted to kill Jesus heard "they plotted, and Allah plotted" (Qur'an 3:54). The verse about feeling secure from Allah's makr warns people already at ease in sin (Qur'an 7:99), the verse that says all makr belongs to Allah follows a list of earlier plotters it topples (Qur'an 13:42), and the verse the attack calls permission to scheme carries its own verdict in its own breath, "they scheme only against themselves" (Qur'an 6:123). The reciter's rule the corpus preserves says it plainly. Cut the second clause loose from the first and you falsely hand makr to Allah without qualification. Keep the join, and the clause reads as repayment.

Premise 3. The Imams Answer, Repayment

The school's own Imams said what the word carries. Asked about Qur'an 3:54, Imam al-Rida answered that Allah does not scheme, He repays scheming with its like. The school's frame for Allah's makr and kayd is istidraj, respite that draws the sinner on. Tafsir al-Qummi glosses "We will draw them on" as renewing blessings on people while they sin, and "My kayd is firm" as "My punishment is severe." The word the attack flattens into guile is, in the school's reading, patience that ends in justice.

Premise 4. The Superlative Crowns the Convicted Class

The superlative crowns a class, and the verse itself names the class. "Best of the makirin" ranks Allah among the plotters the same verse convicts, the council at Dar al-Nadwa, and the report the school reads on the phrase fills it with the vindication of the plotted-against, one rider at Badr, twelve thousand at the Conquest. The prayer the attack itself quotes, "scheme for me and do not scheme against me," is glossed inside Lisan al-Arab as a plea to direct Allah's requital at enemies, which is the class-indexed reading of the word in a single line.

Premise 5. Their Own Canon Keeps the Same Grammar

The contrast the attack draws with its own Bible does not survive the Bible. Titus 1:2 says God cannot lie, and the same canon records God sending strong delusion on those who first refused the love of the truth, 2 Thessalonians 2:10-11, answering the idol-bearing prophet according to the multitude of his idols, Ezekiel 14:4-9, permitting a lying spirit after asking who would persuade a king who hated the truth-teller, 1 Kings 22:20-22, and hearing Jeremiah accuse Him with a verb the English margin renders "enticed", Jeremiah 20:7. The grammar of answered delusion belongs to both books. A charge that indicts one indicts the other, and a reading that spares one must spare the other.

Conclusion. The Word the Attack Flattened Carries a Frame

The lexicons class-index the word, the Qur'an's construction joins Allah's act to the plot it answers, the Imams name the act repayment, the superlative crowns the class of plotters the verse convicts, and the attack's own canon shares the grammar it calls damning. The god the attack convicts of deceit is a god the dictionaries, the verse construction, the school, and the attack's own scripture all decline to name.

What Raghib Splits in Two

The attack quotes Lane's lexicon and stops. Lane's entry describes makr between men, one man planning another's harm in secret, and that much is real. But the attack never opens the Arabic lexicons that carry the word's whole range, and the first of them refuses the attack's reading at the exact phrase at issue.

المكر صرف الغير عما يقصده بحيلة وذلك ضربان : مكر محمود وذلك أن يتحرى بذلك فعل جميل على ذلك قال (والله خير الماكرين) ومذموم وهو أن يتحرى به فعل قبيح ، قال (ولا يحيق المكر السيئ إلا بأهله - وإذ يمكر بك الذين كفروا - فانظر كيف كان عاقبة مكرهم) وقال في الامرين (ومكروا مكرا ومكرنا مكرا) وقال بعضهم : من مكر الله إمهال العبد وتمكينه من أعراض الدنيا ولذلك قال أمير المؤمنين ﵁ : من وسع عليه دنياه ولم يعلم أنه مكر به فهو مخدوع عن عقله

al-makr. ṣarf al-ghayri ʿammā yaqṣuduhu bi-ḥīla, wa-dhālika ḍarbān...

"Deception turns someone away from what he intends through a scheme. It has two forms. Praiseworthy deception seeks a good act. Allah says, 'Allah is the best of planners.' Blameworthy deception seeks an evil act.

Allah says, 'The evil scheme surrounds only its own people,' 'When those who disbelieved plotted against you,' and 'See how their plotting ended.' Allah says of both, 'They plotted a scheme, and We plotted a scheme.' Some said that Allah's scheme is to give a servant time and let him enjoy worldly pleasures.

The Commander of the Faithful said, 'Whoever receives abundance in his worldly life and does not know that he is being drawn into a scheme has been deceived about his own mind.'"

al-Raghib al-Isfahani, al-Mufradat fi Gharib al-Qur'an, entry makr

The dictionary does one thing the attack cannot survive. It splits the word. Makr has a praiseworthy class, and Raghib places "Allah is the best of the makirin" inside that class, at Qur'an 3:54, by name. The blamed class belongs to the plotters, and the verse Raghib attaches to it, "the evil scheme surrounds only its own people" (Qur'an 35:43), already names where makr lands, on its own people. The entry even carries the istidraj reading, that Allah's makr is respite and provision, and it closes with the Commander of the Faithful ﵇ calling unchecked abundance a scheme that deceives its recipient about his own mind. The lexicon of Qur'anic words, read whole, hands the attack its answer inside one entry.

The dictionaries agree on the point the attack denies. Makr said of Allah is repayment that borrows the name of the act it answers. The floor of two lexicons is met four times over, and no lexicon the corpus carries says otherwise.

The Third Word the Attack Flattens

The attack adds khada' from Qur'an 4:142, "they seek to deceive Allah, while He is their deceiver," as a second conviction. The word's own form refuses the reading. Khada'a in the third pattern, mufa'ala, is the pattern of two parties locked on each other, and the grammarians say the form needs both sides.

يخادعون الله وهو خادعهم

yukhādiʿūna Allāha wa-huwa khādiʿuhum

"They seek to deceive Allah, and He deceives them."

Qur'an 4:142, as carried in Lisan al-Arab, vol. 5, p. 183

وقرأ الباقون * ( وما يخدعون ) * لأن المخادعة لا تتصور إلا بين اثنين

"The others recited, 'And they do not deceive,' because mutual deception can only exist between two people."

Shaykh Zada's commentary on al-Baydawi, vol. 1, p. 269, on Qur'an 2:9

وكذلك قوله تعالى : يخادعون الله ، وهو خادعهم ؛ والمُخادَعة من هؤلاء فيما يخيل إِليهم حقيقة ، وهي من الله سبحانه مجاز ، إِنما الاستهزاء والخَدع من الله ﷿ ، مكافأَة لهم ؛ ومنه قول عمرو بن كلثوم : أَلا لا يَجْهَلَنْ أَحدٌ علينا ، فنَجْهَلَ فوقَ جَهْلِ الجاهِلينا أَي إِنما نكافئهُم على جَهْلهم كقوله تعالى : فمن اعتدى عليكم فاعتدوا عليه بمثل ما اعتدى عليكم ؛ وهو باب واسع كبير

wa-l-mukhādaʿatu min hā'ulā'i ḥaqīqatun, wa-hiya mina Allāhi majāzun, makāfa'atan lahum...

"Allah also says, 'They seek to deceive Allah, while He deceives them.' Their attempt at deception is real in what they imagine. Allah's response is figurative. Allah repays them through mockery and deception.

Amr ibn Kulthum said, 'Let no one act foolishly against us, lest we answer with greater foolishness than the foolish.' He meant that they repay people for their foolishness.

This is like Allah's words, 'Whoever attacks you, attack him in the same way he attacked you.' This is a broad and important subject."

Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol. 3, p. 177, on the verses that pair a human act with Allah's answer

The form builds the frame the attack erases. One party tries the outwitting, and the other party turns it back, and the Arabic verb shape itself, the mufa'ala pattern of mutual engagement, will not let the second party be an initiator. Even the poem Lisan quotes as the parallel, Amr ibn Kulthum's line about answering foolishness with foolishness, is pre-Islamic Arabia's own plain statement of repayment that borrows the offender's word.

The Join That Carries the Meaning

One construction fact holds every makr verse of the Qur'an together, and the corpus preserves the reciters' rule that states it.

إنما جاز إسناد الاستهزاء والمكر إلى الله تعالى على معنى الجزاء عليهما ، وذلك على سبيل المزاوجة ، فإذا استأنفت وقطعت الثاني من الأول أوهم أنك تسنده إلى الله مطلقا والحكم في صفاته سبحانه أن تصان عن الوهم

"Mockery and makr may be attributed to Allah only as repayment for them. This follows the matching of words. When the second phrase is separated from the first, it may suggest that these are attributed to Allah without qualification. His attributes must be protected from such misunderstanding."

al-Zarkashi, al-Burhan, vol. 1, p. 367, relaying Abu Hatim al-Sijistani

The rule is a rule about recitation. "They plot" and "and Allah plots" are one joined clause, wa- fastening the second member to the first, and the grammarian warns that anyone who breaks the join and reads Allah's clause standing alone has manufactured the very reading the attack prints in English. The join is the meaning. Every translation that begins Allah's clause with a fresh capital letter, as a new sentence about a new subject, has cut exactly where Abu Hatim said not to cut.

How the Qur'an Places the Word

With the lexicons fixed and the construction fixed, the Qur'an's own placement settles the survey. The master verse first.

وَإِذْ يَمْكُرُ بِكَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا لِيُثْبِتُوكَ أَوْ يَقْتُلُوكَ أَوْ يُخْرِجُوكَ ويَمْكُرُونَ ويَمْكُرُ اللَّه واللَّه خَيْرُ الْماكِرِينَ

wa-idh yamkuru bika alladhīna kafarū li-yuthbitūka aw yaqtulūka aw yukhrijūka, wa-yamkurūna wa-yamkuru Allāhu, wa-Allāhu khayru al-mākirīn

"When those who disbelieved plotted against you to restrain you, kill you, or drive you out. They plotted, and Allah planned. Allah is the best of planners."

Qur'an 8:30

Three acts of men fill the verse's first breath, restrain, kill, expel, and Allah's clause is joined to theirs by wa- as the answer. The verse about Jesus ﵇ runs the same shape with the plot to kill him.

وَمَكَرُوا وَمَكَرَ اللَّهُ وَاللَّهُ خَيْرُ الْماكِرِينَ

wa-makarū wa-makara Allāhu, wa-Allāhu khayru al-mākirīn

"They plotted, and Allah planned. Allah is the best of planners."

Qur'an 3:54

The two verses the attack reads as initiation warnings carry the requital frame inside their own words.

أَ فَأَمِنُوا مَكْرَ اللَّهِ فَلا يَأْمَنُ مَكْرَ اللَّهِ إِلَّا الْقَوْمُ الْخاسِرُونَ

"Do they feel secure from Allah's plan? Only the losing people feel secure from Allah's plan."

Qur'an 7:99

The verse addresses people already at ease in sin, and it names them the losers before it finishes the sentence. Al-Tabataba'i's al-Mizan reads it exactly so, that makr from Allah is valid only as repayment, and that feeling safe from it is itself the trap. His line on the next verse in the attack's list matches.

وَقَدْ مَكَرَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِهِمْ فَلِلَّهِ الْمَكْرُ جَمِيعاً

Qur'an 13:42

فالله سبحانه يملك المكر كله ويبطله ويرده إلى أهله فليعتبروا

"Allah possesses all devious planning. He cancels it and sends it back upon those who use it. Let them take heed."

al-Tabataba'i, al-Mizan, vol. 11, p. 379, on Qur'an 13:42

Possession is the verse's word, and al-Tabataba'i names what possession does. Allah owns the plotters' makr, holds it powerless, and returns it to its senders. The verse the attack calls a license to scheme carries its verdict in its own tail.

وَكَذَلِكَ جَعَلْنَا فِي كُلِّ قَرْيَةٍ أَكَابِرَ مُجْرِمِيهَا لِيَمْكُرُوا فِيهَا وَمَا يَمْكُرُونَ إِلَّا بِأَنْفُسِهِمْ وَمَا يَشْعُرُونَ

"Thus We made the leading criminals in every town scheme within it. They scheme only against themselves, though they do not realize it."

Qur'an 6:123

The attackers cut the verse before its second half. God's act in the first half is to let a town's own leading criminals rise and scheme, and the verse then says, in the same breath, that their scheming lands on no one else. The Ant verse adds the end of the story, and Lisan's own quotation of it carries the whole arc.

ومكروا مكراً ومكرنا مكراً وهم لا يشعرون

"They devised a plot, and We devised a plot, though they did not realize it."

Qur'an 27:50

Every verse in the survey, 3:54, 8:30, 7:99, 10:21 in its warning to the exultant, 13:42, 27:50-51, 6:123, and the Hypocrites' own attempt in Qur'an 2:8-9, places the human act first and Allah's act joined to it as its repayment, and the two earliest of them, 3:54 and 8:30, name the human plots outright, the council against the Prophet ﷺ and the killing plan against Jesus ﵇. The dictionaries defined the word, the construction joined the clauses, and the Qur'an's own sequence carried both. What the attack still needs is a verse where Allah's makr comes first, with no plot before it, and that verse the Qur'an does not have.

A deception, by definition, conceals its end from the one it works on. Istidraj publishes its end in the verse that institutes it.

3. Best of Plotters Names the Class the Verse Convicts

Sources. al-Mizan, vol. 8, pp. 202 and 357; Lisan al-Arab, vol. 5, p. 183; al-Mufradat, entry makr

Grammars. the elative khayr plus a definite plural ranks within a named class

Claim. the superlative ranks Allah against the plotters the verse names, the Muslim prayer the attack quotes proves the class-index in one line, and the fear of Allah's makr the attack mocks is the fear of earned repayment

Who the Makirin Are

The elative construction fixes the class before any theology is discussed. Khayr al-makirin is the Arabic shape of "best among the makirin," best within a class, exactly as khayr al-hakimin is best among judges and khayr al-razzaqin is best among providers. The class is supplied by the clause's own context, and in Qur'an 8:30 the context is the council that planned murder, and in Qur'an 3:54 it is the parties that planned the killing of Jesus ﵇. Al-Tabataba'i's survey of the Qur'an's divine names lists khayr al-makirin in one row with khayr al-hakimin, khayr al-fasilin, khayr al-razzaqin, khayr al-ghafirin, and khayr al-warithin, every one of them a superlative of an attribute exercised inside a frame where others also act. The judges judge badly, and Allah judges best. The plotters plot murder, and Allah plots best, which the verse then defines as rescue, vindication, and repayment.

The attack's reading needs the superlative to mean "most deceitful in betrayal," a sense the lexicons place in the blamed class and refuse for Allah, and it needs the class of makirin to be everyone, so that "best of deceivers" crowns Allah over a world of victims. The verse supplies its own class instead, the men in the room, and crowns Allah against exactly them. Al-Mizan states the doctrinal boundary in one line.

وهو إنما يصح منه تعالى إذا كان على نحو المجازاة كان يأتي الانسان بالمعصية فيؤاخذه الله بالعذاب من حيث لا يشعر أو يفعل به ما يسوقه إلى العذاب وهو لا يشعر

وأما المكر الابتدائي من غير تحقق معصية سابقة فمما يمتنع عليه تعالى

"This applies to Allah only as recompense. A person commits a sin, then Allah seizes him with punishment from where he does not expect. Or Allah does what leads him to punishment without his knowing. Devious planning begun before any prior sin has occurred is impossible for Allah."

al-Tabataba'i, al-Mizan, vol. 8, p. 202, on Qur'an 7:99

Makr that initiates, with no prior offence to answer, is impossible for Allah. That is the school's boundary, stated in the school's chief commentary, and every verse in the attack's list sits inside it.

The Prayer the Attack Quotes Against Itself

The attack ends its case with a prayer it considers damning, the Prophet's ﷺ supplication the collections carry as "scheme for me and do not scheme against me," and Abu Bakr's saying that he would not feel safe from Allah's makr even with one foot in Paradise. The first is the class-index in a single line, and the second is the fear of earned repayment. Lisan al-Arab carries the prayer with its gloss.

وفي حديث الدعاء : اللهم امْكُرْ لي ولا تَمْكُرْ بي ؛ قال ابن الأَثير : مَكْرُ الله إِيقاعُ بلائه بأَعدائه دون أَوليائه ، وقيل : هو استدراج العبد بالطاعات فَيُتَوَهَّمُ أَنها مقبولة وهي مردودة ، المعنى : أَلْحِقْ مَكْرَكَ بِأَعْدائي لا بي

Allāhumma-mkur lī wa-lā tamkur bī... al-maʿnā, alḥiq makraka bi-aʿdā'ī wa-lā bī

"A supplication says, 'O Allah, plan for me and do not plan against me.' Ibn al-Athir said, 'Allah's makr is His sending of affliction upon His enemies while sparing His allies.

It has also been said that He leads a servant on through acts of obedience, so the servant thinks they are accepted when they have been rejected. The meaning is, direct Your plan against my enemies, not against me.'"

Ibn Manzur, Lisan al-Arab, vol. 5, p. 183, relaying the supplication and Ibn al-Athir's gloss

Read the prayer's own words. The one praying asks Allah to deploy makr for him and against his enemies, which only makes sense if the word names a class Allah can righteously exercise, the repayment class the lexicons filed. Ibn al-Athir's gloss says the same in plainer terms, Allah's makr lands on His enemies and spares His allies, and the gloss ends with the paraphrase, direct Your makr at my enemies, not at me. A deceit whose definition forbids victims from knowing they are targeted cannot be prayed for by its intended beneficiaries. And Abu Bakr's fear, quoted from the attack's own modern source, is the fear of a man who knew his own record, which is the fear the verse at Qur'an 7:99 commands, that only the losing people feel safe from Allah's makr. The attack treats that fear as evidence of an untrustworthy god. The prayer, the gloss, and the fear together evidence the exact opposite, a god whose makr is so exactly just that a guilty conscience prays about it.

Two facts of the case decide it. The enemy was in fact few against a much larger force, nine hundred and some against three hundred and some, so the dream showed the side that was smaller as smaller. And the verse names its own purpose, steadying, since a count shown at full size would have broken resolve before the decree it served could run, and the next verse ends the pair with "so that Allah might settle a matter that was to be done" (Qur'an 8:44). A dream that shows the truth, aimed at mercy and courage for the man it was sent to, is a kindness, and the attack reached "deception of believers" only by deciding, without evidence, that the appearance was false.

The Likeness the Killers Mistook

The last exhibit is Qur'an 4:157, the claim that Allah "duped the followers of Christ" with a substituted likeness.

الذي القي عليه شبه عيسى ﵇ فقال اللّه رداً عليهم : وَما قَتَلُوهُ وَما صَلَبُوهُ وَلكِنْ شُبِّهَ لَهُمْ

"The one who was made to resemble Jesus . Allah replied to them, 'They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, though it was made to seem so to them.'"

Mukhtasar Tafsir al-Qummi, p. 134, on Qur'an 4:157

The verse's own subject settles who was fooled. It was made to appear so to them, to the men holding the warrants, the same "they" who boasted "we killed the Messiah" in the verse before it, and the tafsir relay names the mechanism, the likeness was cast on another man and the killers struck him by mistake. The rescue of Jesus ﵇ was Allah's answer to the plot of 3:54, where the same sura's cast planned his death, and the people the attack calls Allah's victims are the people who spent the boast. On the report's own words the disciples saw what the execution party saw, and the Qur'an says of everyone who argues about the event that they have no certain knowledge of it and follow only conjecture (Qur'an 4:157). A claim whose only evidence is a killing party's after-action report is weak ground for charging anyone with mass deception, and the school's reading asks less of the text than the attack's, since the attack must explain why a deceiver god left the truth standing in his own book, verse by verse, in the very sura he was deceiving about.

The claim is real and the school does not dispute it, since the Qur'an makes the same claim in its own idiom, "the truth has come and falsehood cannot originate nor repeat it" (Qur'an 34:49). The question is what the same canon does next.

The energy of delusion, energeian planēs, is sent by God in the Textus Receptus, and the passage fixes its recipients before it fixes the act. They are the perishing, and the ground is stated as a verdict, they received not the love of the truth. The delusion answers a refusal already made. That is the Qur'an's frame for makr, God's act joined by wa- to a human act already committed, stated in the canon the attack calls the clean contrast.

The Comparison on the Merits

The comparison now runs on the criteria the attack itself must accept. Consistency with the book's own frame. The Qur'an states its frame in the open, the joined clause, the named plot, the announced respite, and the canon's frame appears in 2 Thessalonians itself, the delusion sent on those who refused the love of the truth. Both books act on the same rule, God's answering act follows man's settled refusal, and the Qur'an says so with more grammar and less narrative than Kings or Ezekiel, not less honesty. What the texts demand of the reader. The Qur'an's makr verses demand the wa- be respected and the class named, and Ezekiel 14 demands the idols in the heart be read before the deception of the prophet is judged. The demands are equal. Which reading indicts whom. A polemic that prints "Allah is the best of deceivers" in English, and prints "God shall send them strong delusion" in English beside it, and convicts only the Arabic, has not compared the two gods. It has compared two translations of its own choosing. The same courtesy it extends to Ezekiel's named rule and Paul's refusing Thessalonians, the dictionaries, the reciters' join, and the school's Imams ﵈ extend to the Qur'an, and the attack extends that courtesy nowhere in its own case.

The attack's first article pushes the Satan accusation toward a god who authors unbelief, and the school's kalam answers with the station between compulsion and delegation.

Deceit Belongs to the Weak

The school's commentator closes the doctrinal file with the argument from power.

وأمّا المكر والخداع فلأنّهما انّما يصدران من العاجز والخائف الذي لا يعارض الخصم كفاحا

"Plotting and deception come from someone weak and afraid, who cannot face an opponent openly."

Qadi Sa'id al-Qummi, Sharh Tawhid al-Saduq, vol. 2, p. 714

Deceit is the weapon of the cornered. The one who hides his end does so because the other side could stop him if it saw the plan, and this is why the lexicons' blamed class exists at all, it is makr from a position of weakness. The Qur'an's Allah speaks His ends out loud. He announces the drawing-on in the verse that institutes it, names the Day the repayment lands, publishes the rule that the plot returns to its sender, and commands the believers to recite both halves of the warning aloud. A deceiver conceals the outcome from the target. The Qur'an's god recites the outcome to the target, in first person, and then does it. The attack read that publication record as a confession.

Final Verdict

The word the attack flattened carries a frame the attack dropped, and every layer of evidence returned the same verdict.

The dictionaries split makr into a praised class and a blamed class, and they place "best of the makirin" in the praised class at the verse itself. Raghib says so, Lisan al-Arab says so through the interpreters' settled reading, and al-Misbah with Maqayis say so in the grammar of named repayment, the punishment that borrows the offender's word. The construction carries the frame in its joints. The wa- that joins "and Allah plotted" to "they plotted" is the reciters' protected join, and the rule the corpus preserves warns that cutting it manufactures, from exactly this verse, the reading the attack printed in English. The usage survey found no verse where Allah's makr comes first. The councils of Dar al-Nadwa and the parties against Jesus ﵇ come first, in the text, by name.

The school's Imams ﵈ were asked, and answered. Allah does not scheme, He repays scheming, said al-Rida ﵇, and his report in al-Tawhid files mockery, scheming, and deceit under the same rule. Istidraj, the respite that draws the sinner on, is announced in the verse that institutes it, glossed by the school's tafsir as blessings renewed while men sin, taught by the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ as the reading of a believer's own abundance. The superlative crowns the class the verse convicts, the vindication report fills it with Badr and the Conquest, and the prayer the attack quoted as a confession is the class-index asked for out loud, scheme for me, direct it at my enemies. Satan's accusation was classed with the excuses of the damned by the Imam ﵇ the school trusts, withdrawn by Satan himself in the Qur'an's own courtroom, and bounded by a word, sultan, authority he does not have.

And the contrast the attack drew with its own Bible indicted its own Bible. The God who cannot lie in Titus is the God who sends the energy of delusion on those who refused the love of the truth, who answers the idol-bearing prophet according to the multitude of his idols, who permits the lying spirit a truth-hating king asked his courts for, and who hears His own prophet open a lament with the same verb the King James margin softens to enticed. The canon's harmonizers read those texts as requital and permission, and the attack extends that reading to its own book freely, while refusing the dictionaries, the reciters' join, and the named Imams ﵈ of the school the same courtesy for one Arabic word.

The verdict is the lexicons' verdict, the construction's verdict, the Imams' ﵇ verdict, and, read on its own terms, the attack's own canon's verdict, delivered in one line. Allah repays the plot. He does not start it.
