Battle Narration

The Expedition of Tabuk

Word came of a Roman gathering in the north, and in Rajab of the ninth year the Prophet ﷺ marched through the heat to Tabuk. The enemy never came, and the march itself did the work, with Ali ﵇ left over Medina and the last of the wars closed without a sword. The final part, 28 of 28, in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Last March

The last march went north. In Rajab of the ninth year, the Prophet ﷺ ordered the people to make ready for the war on the Romans. They were the furthest and largest enemy the state had faced. The season was the worst it could have been, the fruits ripe on the trees and the heat at its height. The army marched. No battle was fought. The march itself was the campaign.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"'The expedition of Tabuk fell in Rajab of the ninth year of the Hijra. Allah revealed to His Prophet that he should march to it in person and call out the people to go with him, and He told him he would need no war in it and would not meet an enemy's fighting, and that the affairs would come to him without a sword, and He meant to test his companions by the march with him, that they might be marked out by it and their inmost thoughts shown. The fruits had ripened on the trees, and the heat was at its height on them, and most of them held back, in desire for the present life, and care for its living, and fear of the violence of the heat, and the length of the road, and the meeting with the enemy. And he left the Commander of the Faithful, Ali, over his family and his sons and his wives and his place of emigration, and said to him, "Ali, Medina is not set right but by me and by you." And that was because he knew the ill intent of the Bedouins, and of many of the people of Mecca and those around them of their fools and shedders of blood, and he feared they would seek Medina in his absence from it, since there was none in it'"

- Sharaf al-Din al-Musawi, collected works, vol.5, p.407

Commentary

The Sources for Tabuk

Source spine: Ibn Ishaq via al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unuf, vol.7, p.304

Shia line: Sharaf al-Din al-Musawi, collected works, vol.5, p.407, on the revelation's terms, the holdbacks, and the deputy. Al-Iskafi, al-Mi'yar wa-l-Muwazana, p.219, on the Manzila hadith

Sunni line: al-Bayhaqi, Dala'il al-Nubuwwa, vol.5, p.249, carrying Ibn Ishaq on the Ayla treaty and Jarba and Adhruh. Al-Suyuti, al-Durr al-Manthur, vol.3, p.274, on the three who were left behind

Year: Rajab of the ninth, with the year closing on the delegations

Honest limits: the reports gathered here give the order, the terms, the treaties, the three, and the deputy's word, and do not narrate the road itself stage by stage

The frame is shared across the lines, the Roman threat, the hard season, the march, the absence of battle, and the treaties the march collected at the frontier. Ayla, Jarba, and Adhruh entered the faith's protection on the jizya while the army stood at Tabuk.

The March That Was the Victory

Enemy: the Romans, the gathering reported in the north

Season: Rajab, the fruits ripe, the heat at its height, the road long

The test: the march itself was meant to mark out the believers, and the holdbacks are named as a class, in desire for the season and fear of the road

The deputy: Ali ﵇, over the family and the wives and Medina, with the Prophet's ﷺ word, Medina is not set right but by me and by you

The outcome: no battle, the enemy not met, and the march returned having done its work

Read the march as its own reports read it. The revelation's terms, kept in the Shia line, promised no war and no fighting. The march was a mobilization, the whole state put on the road in the worst season, to answer a threat with presence. The holdbacks are part of the record the march was designed to produce, and the year that followed, the year of the delegations, is the ledger of what it proved.

What the Frontier Yielded

Ayla: Yuhanna ibn Ru'ba came out and made peace on the jizya, and the Prophet's ﷺ document for him opens with the security of Allah and His Messenger

Jarba and Adhruh: came and entered the same terms

The three: Ka'b ibn Malik, Marara ibn al-Rabi', and Hilal ibn Umayya, whose repentance was deferred forty days, booths pitched, wives withdrawn, until the verse came down on them, and Umm Salama carried the news to Ka'b

The frontier did not fight, it signed. The treaty text the reports preserve is a written security for the port, on the payment of the jizya, and the northern wall was settled by document where the sword had been expected. The three who were left behind are the march's other monument, the Qur'an's own record of a community holding its absent men to account for forty days.

The Series' Last Ledger

Tactical: no engagement, the frontier reached, the return without loss in these reports

Strategic: the northern threat answered by demonstration, the frontier settled by treaty, the year of the delegations opened, and the nine years of the expeditions closed

Command: the Prophet ﷺ in person, with Ali ﵇ holding Medina

The strategic outcome closed the serial this catalog has tracked. The state that marched seventy men to Waddan in its second month marched an army to the Roman frontier in its ninth year. The difference between the two is the whole of the record.

Was the March to Tabuk Justified

The cause was a reported Roman gathering at the frontier, the empire itself, the enemy beyond every enemy this series has counted. A state with Medina's web of alliances could not leave a Roman concentration unanswered. The answer it gave was the most expensive and least bloody in the whole record, an army on the road, and no sword drawn.

The conduct of the march is its own justification. The reports record the heat, the season, and the road, and the army went anyway, and the reports record no engagement, no booty, and no pursuit. The march tested the community, as its own terms said it would, and the community that marched was the one the year of the delegations then carried.

Prevention belonged to the empire whose gathering was reported, in not gathering. The march's own answer to the threat was the prevention of the war it did not fight.
