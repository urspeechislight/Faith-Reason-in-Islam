Quranic Analysis

And Those Who Have Not Menstruated: The Imami Reading of Qur'an 65:4

The whole dispute over this verse sits inside one clause. That clause, read in its own grammar and against the Imams' law, speaks of women at the age of menstruation who have not menstruated

The Actual Question

The Verse

وَاللَّائِي يَئِسْنَ مِنَ الْمَحِيضِ مِنْ نِسَائِكُمْ إِنِ ارْتَبْتُمْ فَعِدَّتُهُنَّ ثَلَاثَةُ أَشْهُرٍ وَاللَّائِي لَمْ يَحِضْنَ ۚ وَأُولَاتُ الْأَحْمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَنْ يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ

Wa-allā'ī ya'isna min al-maḥīḍi min nisā'ikum in irtabtum fa-ʿiddatuhunna thalāthatu ashhurin, wa-allā'ī lam yaḥiḍna; wa-ulātu al-aḥmāli ajaluhunna an yaḍaʿna ḥamlahunn.

"And those of your women who have despaired of menstruation, if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months; and those who have not menstruated. And those who are pregnant, their term is that they deliver their burden."

- The Qur'an, Surat al-Talaq, 65:4

Where the Dispute Sits

No one disputes the first class: "those who have despaired of menstruation" are women past its age. No one disputes the third: the pregnant wait until delivery. The entire controversy over this verse sits inside the middle clause alone: wa-allā'ī lam yaḥiḍna, "and those who have not menstruated." The majority of exegetes, and within the school al-Murtada and Ibn Zuhra, read it as reaching down to the pre-pubescent girl. Al-Mizan and the sounder line of Imami jurisprudence read it as the woman at the age of menstruation whose courses have never come or have never settled. This page argues the second reading, and it argues it only from what bears on this clause: its conjunction and its governing condition, the grammar of its negation, the classical lexicon of its verb, al-Mizan, and the Imams' own rulings on the pre-pubescent divorcee.

From Within the Clause

What Licenses the Conjunction: The Waw of Co-Sharing

The clause opens with a wāw of conjunction. Before any meaning is drawn from that conjunction, the grammar books must first answer: what does the conjoining wāw do? Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani, in the foundational work of Arabic meaning-theory, gives the rule with precision:

وليس للواو معنى سوى الإشراك في الحكم الذي يقتضيه الإعراب الذي أتبعت فيه الثاني الأول . فإذا قلت : جاءني زيد وعمرو ، لم تقد بالواو شيئا أكثر من إشراك عمرو في المجيء الذي أثبته لزيد ، والجمع بينه وبينه

"The wāw has no meaning whatsoever beyond co-sharing in the ruling (al-ishrāk fī al-ḥukm) required by the inflection in which you made the second follow the first. When you say 'Zayd and Amr came to me,' you have asserted by the wāw nothing more than Amr's co-sharing in the coming you affirmed of Zayd, and his being joined with him in it."

- Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani (d.471 AH), Dala'il al-I'jaz fi 'Ilm al-Ma'ani, p224

The conjoined element shares the judgment of the element it is conjoined to. That is the entire license the wāw carries. And the commentators on Ibn Malik's Alfiyya, the grammar's most memorized text, use this very verse as the standing example of how much of the frame the conjunct inherits. Ibn Aqil's commentary, in the chapter on the elided predicate:

وقد يحذف الجزءان ، أعني المبتدأ والخبر ، للدلالة عليهما ، كقوله تعالى : واللائي يئسن من المحيض من نسائكم إن ارتبتم فعدتهن ثلاثة أشهر ، واللائي لم يحضن ، أي : فعدتهن ثلاثة أشهر ، فحذف المبتدأ والخبر لدلالة ما قبله عليه . . . وقوله : واللائي لم يحضن معطوف على واللائي يئسن

"And both parts, the subject and the predicate, may be elided when something indicates them, as in His saying: 'And those who have despaired of menstruation among your women, if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months; and those who have not menstruated' — that is, 'their waiting period is three months.' The subject and predicate were elided because what precedes indicates them... And His saying 'and those who have not menstruated' is conjoined to 'and those who have despaired.'"

- Ibn Aqil (d.769 AH), Sharh Alfiyyat Ibn Malik, vol.01, p246

His own taqdīr of the elided text is "wa-allā'ī lam yaḥiḍna kadhālik" — "and those who have not menstruated, likewise." That word is the grammar speaking. The conjunct is judged with the whole judgment of its conjunctee: the condition and the ruling together, "if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months." Written out by the grammarians' own rule, the verse reads: "those who have despaired of menstruation, if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months; and those who have not menstruated, likewise, if you are in doubt, three months." The doubt is not decoration around the first class; by the law of conjunction it sits inside the second class too. What that doubt is, and to whom it can belong, is the next question.

The Doubt Governs Both Classes, and Doubt Is an Adult Question

The verse does not say "and those who have not menstruated, their waiting period is three months" as a free-standing rule. It says "those who have despaired of menstruation, if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months, and those who have not menstruated." The doubt clause reaches across the conjunction to both classes. And doubt about menstruation, its cessation, or its non-arrival is a question that can only be asked of a woman at the age of menstruation. The contemporary Imami jurist al-Irwani states the argument in its exact form:

وأما قوله : واللائي لم يحضن فليس من البعيد أن يكون المراد منه : واللائي لم يحضن إن ارتبتم فعدتهن ثلاثة أشهر . والارتياب لا يتصور في حق التي لم تحض إلا إذا فرض أنها في سن من تحيض ولم تحض . وعليه فالآية الكريمة أجنبية عن الصغيرة التي هي ليست في سن من تحيض

"As for His saying 'and those who have not menstruated': it is not far-fetched that the meaning is, 'and those who have not menstruated, if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months.' And doubt is inconceivable in the case of one who has not menstruated except on the supposition that she is at the age of those who menstruate and has not menstruated. On this reading, the noble verse has nothing to do with the pre-pubescent girl, who is not at the age of those who menstruate."

- Shaykh Muhammad Baqir al-Irwani, Durus Tamhidiyya fi al-Fiqh al-Istidlali, vol.02, p424

Of the small child there is no doubt to resolve. She has not menstruated because her age has not come, and no one waits to see whether it has. Doubt exists precisely where menstruation is due and absent: the grown woman whose cycle has stopped without explanation or never began.

The Imam's Own Gloss of the Doubt: A Missed Month

Al-Halabi asked Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ what the irtiyab of this verse is. The answer defines the verse's entire population:

عدة المرأة التي لا تحيض والمستحاضة التي لا تطهر ثلاثة أشهر ، وعدة التي تحيض ويستقيم حيضها ثلاثة قروء ، وسألته عن قول الله : إن ارتبتم ، ما الريبة ؟ فقال : ما زاد على شهر فهو ريبة فلتعتد ثلاثة أشهر وليترك الحيض

"The waiting period of the woman who does not menstruate, and of the woman with chronic bleeding who is never clear, is three months; and the waiting period of the woman who menstruates regularly is three cycles. And I asked him about God's saying 'if you are in doubt': what is the doubt? He said: 'What exceeds a month [without a period] is the doubt; let her keep a waiting period of three months and disregard the cycles.'"

- Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇, via al-Halabi, in al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, cited in al-Mizan, vol.19, p321

The "woman who does not menstruate" of the Imam's ruling is the adult whose period has stopped; the doubt is a cycle that has run past a month. That is the verse's "those who have not menstruated" read by the verse's own companions in the tradition: women of age, not girls below it.

The Grammar of Lam Yahidna

The particle lam with the jussive does not deny a thing absolutely; it denies its occurrence up to the present, of something whose occurrence is live. Makki ibn Abi Talib's formulation, preserved in the Quranic-philological tradition:

لم تنفي الماضي وترد المستقبل ماضيا

"Lam negates the past and refers the future back into the past."

- Makki ibn Abi Talib (d.437 AH), cited in al-Lubab fi Ulum al-Kitab, vol.18, p235

Lam yaḥiḍna is "they have not menstruated," an absence reckoned now, of an act awaited. The register fits the woman from whom menstruation is due. It is not the register in which the classical jurist or lexicographer describes the small child, whose non-menstruation is not an awaited event at all but simply her age.

The Lexicon of the Verb: Menstruation Belongs to the Age of Majority

The classical dictionaries predicate the verb of the verse of the grown female, the mar'a, and cite poetry to prove the usage:

حاضت المرأة تحيض حيضا ومحيضا ، فهي حائض وحائضة أيضا ، عن الفراء . وأنشد : كحائضة يزنى بها غير طاهر . ونساء حيض وحوائض

"The woman menstruated (ḥāḍat al-mar'a), she menstruates, menstruation; she is a menstruant, from al-Farra'. And he cited the verse: 'like a menstruating woman taken by one not pure.' And 'women menstruating' (nisā'un ḥayḍun wa-ḥawā'iḍ)."

- al-Jawhari (d.393 AH), al-Sihah, root ḥ-y-ḍ, vol.03, p213

The poetry al-Jawhari cites as the usage-witness is adult by construction. And Ibn Manzur's entry fixes the boundary of the whole word-field in its gloss on the well-known hadith:

حاضت المرأة وتحيضت ودرست وعركت تحيض حيضا ومحاضا ومحيضا إذا سال الدم منها في أوقات معلومة . . . لا تقبل صلاة حائض إلا بخمار ، أي بلغت سن المحيض وجرى عليها القلم

"The woman menstruated... when the blood flows from her at known times... 'The prayer of a menstruant is not accepted unless she is veiled' — that is, she has reached the age of menstruation (balaghat sinna al-maḥīḍ) and the Pen's record has begun against her."

- Ibn Manzur (d.711 AH), Lisan al-Arab, root ḥ-y-ḍ, vol.07, p143

The hā'iḍ is, by the dictionary's own gloss, "she who has reached the age of menstruation." A clause sorting women by the presence or absence of ḥayḍ sorts the population to which ḥayḍ belongs: females at the age, some flowing, some ceased, some not yet. The child is not within the word's jurisdiction.

Al-Mizan's Reading

Allamah Tabataba'i reads the clause on exactly these lines. First, the doubt:

المراد بالارتياب الشك في يأسهن من المحيض أهو لكبر أم لعارض ، فالمعنى : واللائي يئسن من المحيض من نسائكم وشككتم في أمر يأسهن أهو لبلوغ سنهن سن اليأس أم لعارض فعدتهن ثلاثة أشهر

"What is meant by the doubt is doubt about their despair of menstruation, whether it is from advanced age or from an incidental cause. The meaning is: those of your women who have despaired of menstruation, and you are unsure whether their despair is from reaching the age of despair or from an incidental cause, their waiting period is three months."

- Allamah Tabataba'i (d.1402 AH), al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, vol.19, p316

Then the contested clause itself. The conjunction he invokes is not a homiletic suggestion; it is the grammarians' own parsing of the verse, established above from al-Jurjani's rule of co-sharing and Ibn Aqil's taqdīr. On that license, the conjunction does the decisive work:

وقوله : واللائي لم يحضن عطف على قوله : واللائي يئسن الخ ، والمعنى : واللائي لم يحضن وهن في سن من تحيض فعدتهن ثلاثة أشهر

"And His saying 'and those who have not menstruated' is conjoined to His saying 'and those who have despaired' and so forth. The meaning is: those who have not menstruated while being at the age of women who menstruate (wahunna fī sinni man taḥīḍ), their waiting period is three months."

- Allamah Tabataba'i (d.1402 AH), al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, vol.19, p316

At the age of women who menstruate. The clause is an ʿaṭf on the menopausal class and shares its frame: one continuous adult population, sorted by what menstruation is doing — ceased, absent, or suspended by pregnancy. The pre-pubescent girl is not a coordinate on that map.

The Imami Legal Tradition

The School's Own Category: "She Reached the Age of Menstruation and Has Not Menstruated"

The Imami jurists did not need to invent the adult reading of the clause; they already legislated with the clause's own words for a distinct adult class. Al-Allama al-Hilli, in the rules of istibrā':

ولو كانت من ذوات الأقراء استبرأت بحيضة ، وإن بلغت سن الحيض ولم تحض فبخمسة وأربعين يوما

"If she is one of those with cycles, she is cleared by one menstruation; and if she has reached the age of menstruation and has not menstruated, then by forty-five days."

- al-Allama al-Hilli (d.726 AH), Qawa'id al-Ahkam, vol.03, p148

"She who has reached the age of menstruation and has not menstruated" is a standing technical category of Imami law, the school's own gloss on the verse's phrase. The adult non-menstruant has her own rules; she is a woman, of age, whose ḥayḍ is absent.

The Jurists State the Reading Outright, and Exclude the Child

Al Asfur's commentary on the Mafatih states the verse's meaning with full explicitness, and names the dissenters inside the school:

والمراد بمن لم يحضن من النساء ما لم تر الحيض رأسا وهي في سنه ، فإن عدتها ثلاثة أشهر . . . وليس المراد باللائي يئسن هي البالغة سن اليأس بغير ريبة ولا باللائي لم يحضن الصغيرة ، فإن هؤلاء لا عدة عليهن في الأصح ولسن من أهل الاعتداد ، كما ذهب إليه المخالفون وتبعهم عليه بعض علمائنا كالمرتضى وابن زهرة

"What is meant by 'those who have not menstruated' among women is she who has never seen menstruation at all while being at its age; her waiting period is three months... And what is not meant by 'those who have despaired' is she who has reached the age of despair without doubt, nor by 'those who have not menstruated' the pre-pubescent girl, for these have no waiting period upon them in the soundest view (fī al-aṣaḥḥ) and are not among those subject to a waiting period, as the opponents held, followed in that by some of our own scholars, such as al-Murtada and Ibn Zuhra."

- Shaykh Husayn Al Asfur (d.1216 AH), al-Anwar al-Lawami' fi Sharh Mafatih al-Shara'i', vol.10, p108

"She who has never seen menstruation at all while being at its age." This is the school's classical formulation: the clause covers the grown woman whose courses have never come, and excludes the ṣaghīra, who "has no waiting period in the soundest view and is not among those subject to one."

The Imams' Rulings: No Idda for the Pre-Pubescent, No Consummation With Her

The hadith corpus settles the child's status from both ends. On the waiting period, al-Kafi devotes a chapter to "the divorce of the one who has not come of age and the one who has despaired of menstruation":

في الرجل يطلق الصبية التي لم تبلغ ، ولا تحمل مثلها ، وقد كان دخل بها ، والمرأة التي قد يئست من المحيض ، وارتفع حيضها ، فلا تلد مثلها ، قال : ليس عليهما عدة وإن دخل بهما

"Concerning the man who divorces the young girl who has not come of age, whose like does not bear children, though he has consummated with her, and the woman who has despaired of menstruation, whose courses have ceased, so that her like does not give birth, he said: 'Neither of them has any waiting period, even if he has consummated with them.'"

- Imam al-Baqir or al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, Furu', vol.11, p543

If the verse assigned the pre-pubescent girl three months, the Imam would not rule that she has no ʿidda at all, even after consummation. On consummation itself, Zurara reports from Imam al-Baqir ﵇:

لا تدخل بالجارية حتى يتم لها تسع سنين أو عشر سنين

"Do not consummate with the young girl until she has completed nine years, or ten years."

- Imam al-Baqir ﵇, via Zurara ibn A'yan, in al-Saduq (d.381 AH), al-Khisal, p434; also al-Kafi; Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p214

And the Da'a'im:

من تزوج جارية صغيرة ، فلا يطأها حتى تبلغ تسع سنين

"Whoever marries a small girl shall not lie with her until she reaches nine years."

- al-Qadi al-Nu'man (d.363 AH), Da'a'im al-Islam, via Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p214

Al-Nuri's chapter heading states the rule with its teeth:

باب أنه لا يجوز الدخول بالزوجة حتى تبلغ تسع سنين ، فإن فعل قبل ذلك فعيب أو أفضاها ضمن

"Chapter: it is not permitted to consummate with the wife until she reaches nine years; if he does so before that and injures her, or tears her [the injury of ifḍā'], he is liable."

- al-Nuri al-Tabarsi (d.1320 AH), Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p214 (chapter heading)

In the Imams' law the pre-pubescent girl is not a consummatable wife and not a bearer of ʿidda. A verse legislating the waiting periods of consummated marriages has no occasion to speak of her, and on the Imami reading, it does not.

Why Nine Years: The Girl's Threshold of Majority

The number is not an arbitrary fence. In the Imams' reports, nine years is the boundary at which the girl legally exits minority. Humran asked Imam al-Baqir ﵇ when full legal liability descends on the boy and the girl, and the answer separates their two paths:

إن الجارية ليست مثل الغلام ؛ إن الجارية إذا تزوجت ودخل بها ولها تسع سنين ، ذهب عنها اليتم ، ودفع إليها مالها ، وجاز أمرها في الشراء والبيع ، وأقيمت عليها الحدود التامة ، وأخذ لها وبها . والغلام لا يجوز أمره في الشراء والبيع ، ولا يخرج من اليتم حتى يبلغ خمس عشرة سنة ، أو يحتلم ، أو يشعر ، أو ينبت قبل ذلك

"The girl is not like the boy. When the girl is married and consummated with at nine years, minority leaves her, her property is handed over to her, her transactions in buying and selling take effect, and the full penalties are established against her and for her. The boy's transactions do not take effect, nor does he exit minority, until he reaches fifteen years, or experiences nocturnal emission, or grows hair before that."

- Imam al-Baqir ﵇, via Humran ibn A'yan, in al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, vol.14, p71; also vol.07, p198; al-Tusi, Tahdhib al-Ahkam, vol.10, p38

And the muwaththaqa of Abd Allah ibn Sinan from Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ places the reason inside the number itself:

إذا بلغت الجارية تسع سنين فكذلك ، وذلك أنها تحيض لتسع سنين

"When the girl reaches nine years, the same applies [deeds are recorded for her and against her, and she is liable to punishment], and that is because she menstruates at nine years."

- Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, muwaththaqat Abd Allah ibn Sinan, in al-Hurr al-Amili (d.1104 AH), Wasa'il al-Shi'a, Abwab al-Wasaya, b.44; cited in Bahuth fi al-Fiqh al-Mu'asir, vol.06, p92

The Imam's own rationale ties the number to menarche: the ninth year is when the law presumes the capacity of ḥayḍ to have arrived. The jurists note that actual menarche at nine is rare and take the rationale as the onset of the maturity that makes menarche possible; the boundary stands either way. The classical rule seals it from the other side: "any blood she sees before the nine is not ḥayḍ" — before nine there is no ḥayḍ in law at all (recorded in Kitab al-Tahara, vol.01, p85, as the rule of the classical jurists). So within the Imams' law, "the age of those who menstruate" begins at nine. The girl below it has no ḥayḍ to cease, to await, or to be doubted, and a clause sorting women by ḥayḍ has, once again, no occasion to speak of her.

The Minority Report Inside the School

The school knew the other reading and recorded it honestly. Al-Murtada held that the menopausal and the pre-pubescent both carry a three-month ʿidda in every case, reading "those who have not menstruated" as "those who have not reached it"; Ibn Zuhra called that the ruling caution requires. Al Asfur, quoted above, identifies this as following the opponents' reading, against the aṣaḥḥ of the school. And where the verse's syntax pressed, some Imami commentators proposed the ellipsis rather than the child: that the clause "and those who have not menstruated" carries an elided ruling of its own, "no waiting period is upon them," the elision signaled by the context, precisely because carrying "their waiting period is three months" across the conjunction would empty "if you are in doubt" of its point (al-Yanabi' al-Fiqhiyya, vol.20, p203). The tradition debated the clause with its own tools. The reading that survived as the soundest is the adult one.

Conclusion

The Clause Speaks of Women at the Age

The dispute over 65:4 was never about the menopausal or the pregnant; it sits entirely inside wa-allā'ī lam yaḥiḍna. Read in its own construction, the clause is conjoined to the class past the age and governed by a doubt, and the Imam glossed that doubt as a missed month, a state of the grown woman. Read in its grammar, lam negates an awaited act. Read in its lexicon, ḥayḍ is the attribute of she who has reached its age, as the dictionaries state and their poetry witnesses. Read in al-Mizan, it is "those who have not menstruated while being at the age of women who menstruate." Read in the school's law, "she reached the age of menstruation and has not menstruated" is a standing adult category, the pre-pubescent girl has no ʿidda even after a reported consummation, and consummation with her is prohibited outright. The majority reading that reaches down to the child is attested, and the school recorded it in al-Murtada and Ibn Zuhra, but the soundest line of the Imami tradition, from the Imams' rulings to al-Allama's categories to al-Mizan's bayan, confines the clause to the woman at the age of menstruation who has not menstruated. The pre-pubescent girl enters the verse only through an assumption the clause's own words do not make.
