Quranic Analysis

Those Who Have Not Menstruated: Reading Qur'an 65:4 in Classical Arabic

The verse's grammar, the Quran's own usage, the classical lexicons and poetry, al-Mizan, and the Imams' law converge on one frame: women of menstruating age, not pre-pubescent girls

The Question

The Thesis

Qur'an 65:4 legislates the waiting period (idda) of three classes of divorced women: those who have despaired of menstruation, those who have not menstruated, and those who are pregnant. The dominant reading takes "those who have not menstruated" to include pre-pubescent girls. The Imami reading of Allamah Tabataba'i takes it as women at the age of menstruation who have not menstruated. This analysis argues the second reading from the verse's own words: its conjunction, its negation, its noun. Then from the Quran's usage of nisa'. Then from the classical lexicons and the poetry they cite. Then from the Imams' law, which gives the pre-pubescent girl no idda at all and forbids consummation with her.

The Verse

وَاللَّائِي يَئِسْنَ مِنَ الْمَحِيضِ مِنْ نِسَائِكُمْ إِنِ ارْتَبْتُمْ فَعِدَّتُهُنَّ ثَلَاثَةُ أَشْهُرٍ وَاللَّائِي لَمْ يَحِضْنَ ۚ وَأُولَاتُ الْأَحْمَالِ أَجَلُهُنَّ أَنْ يَضَعْنَ حَمْلَهُنَّ

Wa-allā'ī ya'isna min al-maḥīḍi min nisā'ikum in irtabtum fa-ʿiddatuhunna thalāthatu ashhurin, wa-allā'ī lam yaḥiḍna; wa-ulātu al-aḥmāli ajaluhunna an yaḍaʿna ḥamlahunn.

"And those of your women who have despaired of menstruation, if you are in doubt, their waiting period is three months; and those who have not menstruated. And those who are pregnant, their term is that they deliver their burden."

- The Qur'an, Surat al-Talaq, 65:4

Everything turns on two words: lam yaḥiḍna, "have not menstruated." Do they reach down to the child who has never approached puberty, or only across to the grown woman whose courses have never come or have ceased without explanation? The verse's own construction answers first.

From Within the Verse

Min Nisa'ikum: The Class Named Is "Your Women"

The verse locates its first class, and by conjunction all three classes, inside one noun: your women (nisa'ikum). The Quran's own usage treats nisa' as a class apart from children. When it legislates whom a woman may uncover before, it names the child separately: or the children who have not yet become aware of women's private parts. When it lists the helpless, it counts three classes, not two: men, women, and children.

أَوِ الطِّفْلِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يَظْهَرُوا عَلَىٰ عَوْرَاتِ النِّسَاءِ

"Or the children who have not yet become aware of women's private parts."

- The Qur'an, Surat al-Nur, 24:31

إِلَّا الْمُسْتَضْعَفِينَ مِنَ الرِّجَالِ وَالنِّسَاءِ وَالْوِلْدَانِ

"Except the helpless among the men, the women, and the children."

- The Qur'an, Surat al-Nisa', 4:98

In the Quran's lexicon the ṭifl and the walad stand outside the nisa'. A verse that assigns waiting periods to "your women" addresses itself to the adult class.

Ya'isna min al-Mahid: Despair Presupposes Expectation

The first class is defined by a verb of loss. Al-Jawhari's entry is terse:

اليأس : القنوط . وقد يئس من الشئ ييأس

"Al-ya's is despair, the loss of hope. One says: he despaired of a thing (ya'isa min al-shay')."

- al-Jawhari (d.393 AH), al-Sihah, root y-hamza-s, vol.03, p132

One despairs of what one once hoped for or possessed. The classical usage makes the age-frame explicit, in a report al-Jawhari preserves from Ibn Mas'ud:

ما من مصلى لامرأة أفضل من أشد مكانا في بيتها ظلمة ، إلا امرأة قد يئست من البعولة ، فهي في منقليها

"No prayer-place of a woman is better than the darkest corner of her house, except for a woman who has despaired of husbands (ya'isat min al-ba'ula); she may pray in either of her halls."

- Ibn Mas'ud, cited in al-Jawhari (d.393 AH), al-Sihah, vol.05, p114

A woman who has "despaired of husbands" is a woman who has passed the age of marriage, not a girl who has never reached it. So with the verse: women who have despaired of menstruation are women whose age has carried them past it. The ya'isa is defined by having been on the far side of the thing lost.

Hadat al-Mar'a: Menstruation Belongs to the Grown Woman

The lexicons predicate the verb of menstruation of the grown female, the mar'a:

حاضت المرأة تحيض حيضا ومحيضا ، فهي حائض وحائضة أيضا ، عن الفراء . وأنشد : كحائضة يزنى بها غير طاهر . ونساء حيض وحوائض

"The woman menstruated (ḥāḍat al-mar'a), she menstruates, menstruation; she is a menstruant, from al-Farra'. And he cited the verse: 'like a menstruating woman taken by one not pure.' And 'women menstruating' (nisā'un ḥayḍun wa-ḥawā'iḍ)."

- al-Jawhari (d.393 AH), al-Sihah, root ḥ-y-ḍ, vol.03, p213

The poetry al-Jawhari cites as the usage-witness is adult by construction: the menstruating woman of the line is a sexual subject. Ibn Manzur's fuller entry carries the same definition and, in its gloss on the well-known hadith, fixes the boundary explicitly:

حاضت المرأة وتحيضت ودرست وعركت تحيض حيضا ومحاضا ومحيضا إذا سال الدم منها في أوقات معلومة . . . لا تقبل صلاة حائض إلا بخمار ، أي بلغت سن المحيض وجرى عليها القلم

"The woman menstruated... when the blood flows from her at known times... 'The prayer of a menstruant is not accepted unless she is veiled' — that is, she has reached the age of menstruation (balaghat sinna al-maḥīḍ) and the Pen's record has begun against her."

- Ibn Manzur (d.711 AH), Lisan al-Arab, root ḥ-y-ḍ, vol.07, p143

The hā'iḍ is the woman who has "reached the age of menstruation and come under legal responsibility." Menstruation, in the dictionaries, is the marker of the grown woman; its vocabulary is predicated of her, not of the child.

Lam Yahidna: "Have Not Menstruated" Is Said of One Expected To

The grammarians define the particle lam as a negation that runs up to the present moment. Makki ibn Abi Talib's formulation, preserved in the Quranic-philological tradition:

لم تنفي الماضي وترد المستقبل ماضيا

"Lam negates the past and refers the future back into the past."

- Makki ibn Abi Talib (d.437 AH), cited in al-Lubab fi Ulum al-Kitab, vol.18, p235

Lam yaḥiḍna is "they have not menstruated," an absence reckoned at the moment of speech, of something whose occurrence is live and expected. The idiom fits the grown woman whose courses have not come. Of the small child one does not say "she has not menstruated" in this register at all; the expectation that the particle carries has not yet attached to her.

The Conjunction Decides

Finally the syntax itself. "Those who have not menstruated" is conjoined directly to "those who have despaired of menstruation," sharing its article, its pronoun, and its ruling. A conjunct inherits the frame of its conjunct: one continuous population of women defined by the state of their cycles, those whose courses have ceased and those whose courses have not come. The verse then adds the third class, the pregnant, whose term is delivery. Read straight, the verse is a complete map of the adult possibilities: menstruation ended, menstruation absent, pregnancy in progress. The pre-pubescent child is not a coordinate on that map.

Al-Mizan's Reading

Allamah Tabataba'i reads the verse exactly along these lines. First, he fixes what the "doubt" of in irtabtum is about:

المراد بالارتياب الشك في يأسهن من المحيض أهو لكبر أم لعارض ، فالمعنى : واللائي يئسن من المحيض من نسائكم وشككتم في أمر يأسهن أهو لبلوغ سنهن سن اليأس أم لعارض فعدتهن ثلاثة أشهر

"What is meant by the doubt is doubt about their despair of menstruation, whether it is from advanced age or from an incidental cause. The meaning is: those of your women who have despaired of menstruation, and you are unsure whether their despair is from reaching the age of despair or from an incidental cause, their waiting period is three months."

- Allamah Tabataba'i (d.1402 AH), al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, vol.19, p316

The verse's scenario is a woman whose periods have stopped with the cause unknown: menopause or some transient interruption. That is a question that can only arise for a woman of menstruating age. The second class extends the same frame:

وقوله : واللائي لم يحضن عطف على قوله : واللائي يئسن الخ ، والمعنى : واللائي لم يحضن وهن في سن من تحيض فعدتهن ثلاثة أشهر

"And His saying 'and those who have not menstruated' is conjoined to His saying 'and those who have despaired' and so forth. The meaning is: those who have not menstruated while being at the age of women who menstruate (wahunna fī sinni man taḥīḍ), their waiting period is three months."

- Allamah Tabataba'i (d.1402 AH), al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, vol.19, p316

At the age of women who menstruate. The conjunction with the menopausal class does the work: as the first class is women past the age, the second is women at the age whose menstruation has not occurred. The child stands outside both.

The Imams' Law Confirms the Frame

The Pre-Pubescent Girl Has No Idda at All

If the verse's "those who have not menstruated" reached down to the pre-pubescent girl, her waiting period would be three months. The Imams' ruling is the opposite: she has no waiting period whatsoever. Al-Kafi devotes a chapter to "the divorce of the one who has not come of age and the one who has despaired of menstruation":

في الرجل يطلق الصبية التي لم تبلغ ، ولا تحمل مثلها ، وقد كان دخل بها ، والمرأة التي قد يئست من المحيض ، وارتفع حيضها ، فلا تلد مثلها ، قال : ليس عليهما عدة وإن دخل بهما

"Concerning the man who divorces the young girl who has not come of age, whose like does not bear children, though he has consummated with her, and the woman who has despaired of menstruation, whose courses have ceased, so that her like does not give birth, he said: 'Neither of them has any waiting period, even if he has consummated with them.'"

- Imam al-Baqir or al-Sadiq ﵇, in al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, Furu', vol.11, p543

The pre-pubescent divorcee waits nothing. She is outside the verse's legislation, which is precisely what the Imami reading of the verse predicts.

The "Doubt" Is a Missed Month

Al-Halabi asked Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ directly what the irtiyab of the verse is. The answer locates the whole passage among adult women whose cycles have been interrupted:

عدة المرأة التي لا تحيض والمستحاضة التي لا تطهر ثلاثة أشهر ، وعدة التي تحيض ويستقيم حيضها ثلاثة قروء ، وسألته عن قول الله : إن ارتبتم ، ما الريبة ؟ فقال : ما زاد على شهر فهو ريبة فلتعتد ثلاثة أشهر وليترك الحيض

"The waiting period of the woman who does not menstruate, and of the woman with chronic bleeding who is never clear, is three months; and the waiting period of the woman who menstruates regularly is three cycles. And I asked him about God's saying 'if you are in doubt': what is the doubt? He said: 'What exceeds a month [without a period] is the doubt; let her keep a waiting period of three months and disregard the cycles.'"

- Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇, via al-Halabi, in al-Kulayni, al-Kafi, cited in al-Mizan, vol.19, p321

The woman "who does not menstruate" of the Imams' law is the grown woman whose period has stopped, the mustaḥāḍa beside her. The "doubt" is a cycle that has run past a month. Every term of the verse, in the Imams' reading, presupposes a woman of age.

No Consummation With the Pre-Pubescent

The tradition is equally explicit on the act that a waiting period presupposes. Zurara reports from Imam al-Baqir ﵇:

لا تدخل بالجارية حتى يتم لها تسع سنين أو عشر سنين

"Do not consummate with the young girl until she has completed nine years, or ten years."

- Imam al-Baqir ﵇, via Zurara ibn A'yan, in al-Saduq (d.381 AH), al-Khisal, p434; also al-Kafi; Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p214

And the Da'a'im:

من تزوج جارية صغيرة ، فلا يطأها حتى تبلغ تسع سنين

"Whoever marries a small girl shall not lie with her until she reaches nine years."

- al-Qadi al-Nu'man (d.363 AH), Da'a'im al-Islam, via Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p214

Al-Nuri's chapter heading states the fiqh rule and its teeth: "Chapter: it is not permitted to consummate with the wife until she reaches nine years; if he does so before that and injures her or destroys her [through the injury of ifḍā'], he is liable" (Mustadrak al-Wasa'il, vol.14, p214). In the Imams' law the pre-pubescent girl is not a consummatable wife: no consummation, and no waiting period after divorce. A verse legislating the waiting periods of consummated marriages has no occasion to speak of her.

Conclusion

The Frame Is Adult

Read in its own Arabic, Qur'an 65:4 legislates a single population: your women, a class the Quran itself holds apart from ṭifl and wildān; women who have despaired of menstruation, a verb that presupposes expectation; women who have not menstruated, a negation that anticipates the act; women carrying pregnancies. Al-Mizan states the construal in one line: those who have not menstruated while being at the age of women who menstruate. The Imams' law completes the circle from the other side: the pre-pubescent girl has no ʿidda even after a reported consummation, the "doubt" of the verse is a missed month in a grown woman, and consummation with the small girl is prohibited outright. The majority of exegetes, Sunni and Shia, have read "those who have not menstruated" as reaching the child; this analysis does not conceal that. It shows that the verse's grammar, the Quran's usage, the classical lexicons and their poetry, the school's greatest modern commentary, and the Imams' own rulings all support the narrower, adult frame, and that the child enters the verse only by an assumption the text never states.
