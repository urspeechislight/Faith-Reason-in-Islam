Hadith Methodology

Why Shia Scholars Do Not Accept Every Hadith in Their Books

The Argument Under Examination

The Charge

The Shia hadith corpus contradicts itself, opponents said, and a wise Lord cannot command a religion whose reports collide. Shaykh al-Ta’ifa al-Tusi opened his largest work by quoting the charge in full and agreeing with its premise. Almost every report has another beside it that opposes it. The school’s answer was never denial. From the earliest collectors onward it built gates a report must pass, and its own scholars grade the collections by those gates today.

Premise 1: The Content Faces the Quran First

The machinery al-Tusi promised opens with content, because content stands before the Book before any chain is weighed. The Prophet’s ﷺ sermon at Mina and three reports from Imam al-Sadiq ﵇ fix the rule. Whatever agrees with the Quran was said, whatever conflicts with it was never said, and a conflicting hadith is zukhruf, glitter with no substance. This gate is the tradition’s foundation, announced publicly and taught repeatedly.

Premise 2: Conflicting Reports Follow a Fixed Ladder

The Imams were asked directly what to do with two conflicting reports, and the answers came in fixed order. The famous over the obscure, then the firmer narrator, then the report that departs from the common position, then precaution, and at the end free choice or standing down. The ladder is hadith, not jurists’ invention, and the school inherited it as an order.

Premise 3: The Chain Faces the Narrator Science

Every narrator is weighed individually. Trustworthiness, not sectarian membership, is the operative test in the dominant modern school, and every proposed mass authentication has been dismantled, from the companions of consensus to the 4,000 students of al-Sadiq ﵇. The conclusion lands on the school’s own greatest book. Not every report in al-Kafi is established as authentic.

Premise 4: The Departure Rule Has a Stated Reason

The most distinctive rung, preferring the report that contradicts the common position, comes with its history in the sources. The community fabricated counter rulings against Ali to break his cause, and the rule answers that campaign. Its scope is debated inside the school today, with al-Sistani’s school arguing most narrations cannot be attributed to taqiyya at all.

Conclusion: The Book Is a Repository, Not a Verdict

A school that admits the contradictions, commands the testing, and grades its own collections by the tests is not a school that accepts every hadith in its books. The verdict belongs to the scholar who runs the gates, and the gates were commanded by the Imams themselves.

1. The Charge the School Answers

The objection comes from outside and from inside. Opponents called the corpus self-contradictory, and the school’s own scholars agreed the reports collide. The answer was never denial. It was a testing apparatus, built early, applied for a thousand years.

The Opponent’s Opening Was a Book Request

Source: al-Tusi (d.460 AH), Tahdhib al-Ahkam, vol.1, p52 (ed. Dar al-Kutub al-Islamiyya)

Speaker: al-Tusi, in his introduction

Dating: compiled between 414 and 436 AH

Claim: almost every Shia report has another beside it that opposes it, and the opponents made this their strongest attack on the school

The Admission and the Charge Together

Shaykh al-Ta’ifa al-Tusi opened his Tahdhib al-Ahkam with the problem stated at full strength. A colleague had challenged him over the contradictions in the Shia corpus, and al-Tusi recorded the challenge without softening it.

ذاكرني بعض الأصدقاء أيده الله ممن أوجب حقه ( علينا ) (1) بأحاديث أصحابنا أيدهم الله ورحم السلف منهم ، وما وقع فيها من الاختلاف والتباين والمنافاة والتضاد ، حتى لا يكاد يتفق خبر إلا وبإزائه ما يضاده ولا يسلم حديث إلا وفي مقابلته ما ينافيه ، حتى جعل مخالفونا ذلك من أعظم الطعون على مذهبنا ، وتطرقوا بذلك إلى إبطال معتقدنا ، وذكروا أنه لم يزل شيوخكم السلف والخلف يطعنون على مخالفيهم بالاختلاف الذي يدينون الله تعالى به ويشنعون عليهم بافتراق كلمتهم في الفروع ، ويذكرون أن هذا مما لا يجوز أن يتعبد به الحكيم ، ولا أن يبيح العمل به العليم ، وقد وجدناكم أشد اختلافا من مخالفيكم وأكثر تباينا من مباينيكم ، ووجود هذا الاختلاف منكم مع اعتقادكم بطلان ذلك دليل على فساد الأصل حتى دخل (2) على جماعة ممن ليس لهم قوة في العلم ولا بصيرة بوجوه النظر ومعاني الألفاظ شبهة ، وكثير منهم رجع عن اعتقاد الحق لما اشتبه عليه الوجه في ذلك ، وعجز عن حل الشبهة فيه ، سمعت شيخنا أبا عبد الله أيده الله يذكر أن أبا الحسين الهاروني (3) العلوي كان يعتقد الحق ويدين بالإمامة فرجع عنها لما التبس عليه الامر في اختلفا الأحاديث وترك المذهب

A friend, may Allah support him, whose right over us I must honor, discussed the reports of our companions with me. May Allah support the living among them and have mercy on those who came before them.

He spoke of the differences, contradictions, and conflicts within them. Almost every report has another beside it that opposes it.

Almost every hadith has another standing against it. Our opponents have made this one of their strongest attacks on our school.

They use it to invalidate our beliefs. They say, your early and later scholars always attacked their opponents for the differences in their religion.

They condemned them because they divided over secondary matters. They said that a wise Lord could not command such a religion, and an All-Knowing Lord could not permit people to act by it.

Yet we find that you differ more than your opponents and disagree more than those who disagree with you. Since you believe such disagreement is false, its presence among you proves that your foundation is corrupt.

This doubt entered the minds of people who lacked strength in knowledge and insight into methods of reasoning and the meanings of words. Many of them abandoned the true belief because this matter confused them and they could not answer the doubt.

I heard our teacher Abu Abd Allah say that Abu al-Husayn al-Haruni, an Alid, had believed in the truth and held to the doctrine of the Imamate. He left it when the conflicting hadiths confused him, and he abandoned the school.

- al-Tusi (d.460 AH), Tahdhib al-Ahkam, vol.1, p52

The charge here is the real one, in its strongest form, and al-Tusi wrote it himself. A wise Lord, the opponents argued, cannot command a religion whose reports contradict each other, and the presence of such contradiction among a school that condemns contradiction proves the foundation corrupt. The doubt cost real adherents. Al-Tusi names Abu al-Husayn al-Haruni, an Alid who held the Imamite doctrine and left it because the conflicting hadiths confused him. The school’s own greatest systematizer therefore opened his largest work by facing the problem. His answer was not to deny the contradictions. It was to build the machinery the record shows, and his Tahdhib and al-Istibsar are that machinery at work.

2. The Quran Gate

Before any chain is examined, the content stands before the Book. The school grounds this gate in the Prophet’s own sermon and in the Imam’s repeated teaching, and its greatest collector opened his book with it.

Whatever Agrees with the Book, I Said It

Source: al-Ayyashi (d.320 AH), Tafsir, from Hisham ibn al-Hakam, as preserved in al-Nuri al-Tabrisi, Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, p304, no.21415

Speaker: the Prophet ﷺ, in a sermon at Mina or Mecca, relayed by Imam al-Sadiq ﵇

Dating: the farewell pilgrimage period

Claim: whatever is attributed to the Prophet that agrees with the Quran, he said. Whatever conflicts with it, he never said

The Standard in the Prophet’s Own Words

The first gate any report must pass is the Book itself. The rule is attributed to the Prophet ﷺ in a sermon, relayed through Hisham ibn al-Hakam from Imam al-Sadiq ﵇.

محمد بن مسعود العياشي في تفسيره : عن هشام بن الحكم ، عن أبي عبد الله (﵇) ، قال : " قال رسول الله (صلى الله عليه وآله) ، في خطبة بمنى أو بمكة : يا أيها الناس ، ما جاءكم عني يوافق القرآن فأنا قلته ، وما جاءكم عني لا يوافق القرآن فلم أقله " .

Muhammad ibn Masud al-Ayyashi reports in his commentary from Hisham ibn al-Hakam, from Abu Abd Allah, the Messenger of Allah said in a sermon at Mina or Mecca, people, whatever comes to you from me that agrees with the Quran, I said it. Whatever comes to you from me that does not agree with the Quran, I did not say it.

- al-Ayyashi, Tafsir, via al-Nuri al-Tabrisi, Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, p304

This is the gate the whole tradition stands on. A report attributed to the Prophet ﷺ that carries a meaning the Quran contradicts is returned to its sender, whatever its chain looks like. The sermon setting matters. The rule was announced publicly, at Mina, before the whole pilgrimage, not whispered to a narrator.

Zukhruf, the Glitter That Is Not Gold

Source: al-Ayyashi, Tafsir, from Muhammad ibn Muslim, Ayyub ibn al-Hurr, and Kulayb al-Asadi, via Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, p304-305, nos.21416-21418

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, in three separate reports

Dating: the Imam’s teaching circle in Medina

Claim: everything is referred back to the Book and the Sunna, and any hadith that does not agree with the Book of Allah is zukhruf, glitter without substance

Three Narrators, One Rule

The rule is not a lone line. Al-Ayyashi’s commentary carries it from three independent narrators, and al-Nuri gathered them in one chapter.

وعن محمد بن مسلم قال : قال أبو عبد الله (﵇) : " يا محمد ، ما جاءك في رواية - من بر أو فاجر - يوافق القرآن فخذ به ، وما جاءك في رواية - من بر أو فاجر - يخالف القرآن فلا تأخذ به " . [21417] 6 - وعن أيوب بن الحر قال : سمعت أبا عبد الله (﵇) ، يقول : " كل شئ مردود إلى الكتاب والسنة ، وكل حديث لا يوافق القرآن فهو زخرف " . [21418] 7 - وعن كليب الأسدي قال : سمعت أبا عبد الله (﵇) ، يقول : " ما أتاكم عنا من حديث لا يصدقه كتاب الله فهو زخرف (1) " .

Muhammad ibn Muslim said that Abu Abd Allah said, Muhammad, when a report reaches you from a righteous person or a sinful person, follow it if it agrees with the Quran. Do not follow it if it conflicts with the Quran.

Ayyub ibn al-Hurr said, I heard Abu Abd Allah say, everything returns to the Book and the Sunnah. Every hadith that does not agree with the Quran is false adornment.

Kulayb al-Asadi said, I heard Abu Abd Allah say, any hadith that comes to you from us and is not confirmed by the Book of Allah is false adornment.

- al-Ayyashi, Tafsir, via Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, pp304-305

The word the Imam ﵇ chooses is zukhruf, the ornament, the surface glitter. A contradictory hadith may sit in a book with a chain beside it, and it is still glitter, because the substance belongs to the Book. The middle report carries the widest gate of all. Whatever comes in a report, from a righteous man or a sinful one, is judged only by the Quran it agrees with or conflicts with.

The Collector Set the Rules Himself

Source: al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, vol.1, muqaddima, p56

Speaker: the Imam, in the three principles al-Kulayni quotes

Dating: compiled circa 322 AH in Baghdad

Claim: present the reports to the Book of Allah, take what agrees and return what conflicts, leave what agrees with the opposing camp, and hold to what is unanimously agreed

Al-Kulayni’s Three Filters

The widest gate of all, judging any report by its agreement with the Quran whatever the narrator, now meets the collector himself. The compiler of al-Kafi stated his filters in his own introduction, quoting the Imam, and then added a sentence his opponents rarely quote.

اعرضوها على كتاب الله فما وافى كتاب الله ﷿ فخذوه ، وما خالف كتاب الله فردوه " وقوله ﵇ : " دعوا ما وافق القوم فإن الرشد في خلافهم " وقوله ﵇ " خذوا بالمجمع عليه ، فإن المجمع عليه لا ريب فيه " ونحن لا نعرف من جميع ذلك إلا أقله (1) ولا نجد شيئا " أحوط ولا أوسع من رد علم ذلك كله إلى العالم ﵇ وقبول ما وسع من الأمر فيه بقوله ﵇ : " بأيما أخذتم من باب التسليم وسعكم " .

Present them to the Book of Allah. Take whatever agrees with the Book of Allah, and reject whatever conflicts with the Book of Allah.

He also said, Leave whatever agrees with the people, for guidance lies in differing from them. He also said, Follow what is widely agreed upon, for what is widely agreed upon carries no doubt.

We know only a small part of all this. We find nothing safer or broader than leaving knowledge of all of it to the Imam and accepting the latitude he gave in this matter when he said, Whichever one you follow in a spirit of submission will suffice for you.

- al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, vol.1, muqaddima

The three filters run in order. The Quran first. Then the departure from the opposing camp. Then the unanimous. And then al-Kulayni says what the Akhbari later tradition wished he had not. Of all this, we know only the least of it. The collector who is accused of swallowing every report whole says plainly that his filters reach certainty only rarely, and that the safe course is to return the whole matter to the Imam. The introduction is a manual of doubt, not a claim of blanket authenticity.

3. When Two Hadiths Collide

Conflict is the corpus’s normal condition, so the school’s Imams were asked the question directly and answered in fixed order. The exhibits below carry the ladder itself, precaution as its fourth rung, and standing down at the end.

Two Judges, Two Rulings

Source: al-Tabrisi (d.548 AH), al-Ihtijaj, from Umar ibn Hanzala, via al-Nuri al-Tabrisi, Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, p302-303, no.21412

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, answering Umar ibn Hanzala

Dating: the Imam’s Medina

Claim: between two qualified arbiters who differ, follow the more upright, the more learned in hadith, and the more scrupulous, not the other

The Arbiter Standard

Al-Kulayni closed his filters by returning the whole matter to the Imam, and the next report shows what that return looks like in practice. Two Shia arbitrators hear a case and rule differently, and the question reaches the Imam.

الطبرسي في الاحتجاج : عن عمر بن حنظلة ، قال : سألت أبا عبد الله (﵇) ، عن رجلين من أصحابنا بينهما منازعة في دين أو ميراث ، فتحاكما إلى السلطان - إلى أن قال - فإن كان كل واحد اختار رجلا من أصحابنا فرضيا أن يكونا الناظرين في حقهما ، فاختلفا (1) فيما حكما ، فان الحكمين اختلفا في حديثكم ، قل : " إن الحكم ما حكم به أعدلهما وأفقهها في الحديث وأورعهما ، ولا يلتفت إلى ما يحكم به الآخر " قلت : فإنهما عدلان مرضيان عرفا بذلك ، لا يفضل أحدهما صاحبه ، قال : " ينظر الآن إلى ما كان من روايتهما عنا في ذلك الذي حكما ، الجمع عليه بين أصحابك فيؤخذ به من حكمهما ، ويترك الشاذ الذي ليس بمشهور عند أصحابك ، فإن المجمع عليه لا ريب فيه " - إلى أن قال - قلت : فإن كان الخبران عنكم مشهورين ، قد رواهما الثقات عنكم ، قال : " ينظر ما كان حكمه حكم الكتاب والسنة وخالف العامة فيؤخذ به ، ويترك ما خالف حكمه حكم الكتاب والسنة ووافق العامة " .

قلت : جعلت فداك [أرأيت] (2) إن كان الفقيهان عرفا حكمه من الكتاب والسنة ؟ ثم وجدنا أحد الخبرين يوافق العامة ، والآخر يخالف ، بأيهما نأخذ من الخبرين ؟ قال : " ينظر إلى ما هم إليه يميلون ، فان ما خالف العامة ففيه الرشاد " قلت : جعلت فداك ، فان وافقهم الخبران جميعا ؟ قال : " انظروا إلى ما يميل إليه حكامهم وقضاتهم فاتركوه جانبا ، وخذوا بغيره " قلت : فان وافق حكامهم الخبرين جميعا ؟ قال : " إذا كان كذلك فارجه وقف عنده حتى تلقى إمامك ، فان الوقوف عند الشبهات خير من الاقتحام في الهلكات

Al-Tabrisi reports in al-Ihtijaj from Umar ibn Hanzala. He asked Abu Abd Allah about two of their companions who disputed over a debt or inheritance and took their case to the ruler.

He later asked, If each man chooses one of our companions and accepts them as judges in their case, yet the two judges give different rulings because they differ over your hadiths, what should they do? Abu Abd Allah replied, Follow the ruling of the one who is more just, more learned in hadith, and more careful in religion.

Do not consider the ruling of the other. Umar said, Both are just and accepted.

Both are known for these qualities, and neither surpasses the other. Abu Abd Allah replied, Look at the reports from us concerning the matter they judged.

Follow the ruling based on the report that your companions widely accept. Leave the isolated report that is not well known among your companions.

What is widely agreed upon carries no doubt. Umar later asked, What if both reports from you are well known and trustworthy people have narrated both? Abu Abd Allah replied, Follow the one whose ruling agrees with the Book and the Sunnah and differs from the common people.

Leave the one whose ruling conflicts with the Book and the Sunnah and agrees with the common people. Umar said, May I be your ransom.

Suppose the two jurists know its ruling from the Book and the Sunnah, then we find that one report agrees with the common people and the other differs from them. Which report should we follow? Abu Abd Allah replied, Look at what they incline toward.

Guidance lies in what differs from the common people. Umar said, May I be your ransom.

What if both reports agree with them? Abu Abd Allah replied, Look at the ruling favored by their judges and legal authorities. Set it aside and follow the other report.

Umar said, What if their judges agree with both reports? Abu Abd Allah replied, Delay the matter and stop at it until you meet your Imam. Stopping before doubtful matters is better than rushing into destruction.

- al-Tabrisi, al-Ihtijaj, via Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, pp302-303

The Imam ﵇ builds the ladder one rung at a time as Umar ibn Hanzala keeps raising the hard case. The two judges are equal in justice. Then the report that agrees with the Quran and departs from the common position is followed. When both reports depart from it, both are set aside until the Imam himself can be asked, because standing still at a doubt is better than charging into destruction. The rule of standing down is the school’s last resort, and it is the Imam who commands it.

Zurarah’s Five Steps

Source: al-Allama al-Hilli (d.726 AH), Awali al-Liali, raising it to Zurara ibn A’yan, via al-Nuri al-Tabrisi, Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, p303, no.21413

Speaker: Imam al-Baqir ﵇, answering Zurarah

Dating: early second century AH, Medina

Claim: between conflicting reports, take the well known over the rare, then the more trustworthy narrator, then what departs from the common position, then precaution, then choose

The Hierarchy in the Imam’s Voice

The famous five-step ladder is a hadith, not a jurist’s construction. Zurarah asks the question directly and the Imam ﵇ answers rung by rung.

عوالي اللآلي : روى العلامة مرفوعا إلى زرارة بن أعين ، قال : سألت الباقر (﵇) ، فقلت : جعلت فداك ، يأتي عنكم الخبران أو الحديثان المتعارضان ، فبأيهما آخذ ؟ فقال (﵇) : " يا زرارة ، خذ بما اشتهر بين أصحابك ودع الشاذ النادر " فقلت : يا سيدي ، انهما معا مشهوران مرويان مأثوران عنكم ، فقال (﵇) : " خذ بقول أعدلهما عندك ، وأوثقهما في نفسك " فقلت : إنهما معا عدلان مرضيان موثقان ، فقال (﵇) : " أنظر ما وافق منهما مذهب العامة فاتركه ، وخذ بما خالفهم " قلت : ربما كانا معا موافقين لهم ، أو مخالفين ، فكيف اصنع ؟ فقال : " اذن فخذ بما فيه الحائطة لدينك ، واترك ما خالف الاحتياط " فقلت : انهما معا موافقان للاحتياط أو مخالفان له ، فكيف اصنع ؟ فقال (﵇) : " إذن فتخير أحدهما فتأخذ به وتدع الأخير " وفي رواية انه (﵇) قال : " إذن فارجه حتى تلقى امامك فتسأله "

Al-Allamah reports in Awali al-Liali from Zurara ibn Ayan. Zurara said, I asked al-Baqir, may I be your ransom.

Two conflicting reports or hadiths come to us from you. Which one should I follow? Al-Baqir replied, Zurara, follow what is well known among your companions and leave the rare isolated report.

Zurara said, My master, both are well known, narrated, and transmitted from you. Al-Baqir replied, Follow the statement of the one who is more just in your view and more trustworthy in your heart.

Zurara said, Both are just, accepted, and trustworthy. Al-Baqir replied, Look at the one that agrees with the school of the common people.

Leave it and follow what differs from them. Zurara said, At times both reports agree with them, or both differ from them.

What should I do? He replied, Then follow what provides greater protection for your religion, and leave what conflicts with caution. Zurara said, Both may agree with caution, or both may conflict with it.

What should I do? Al-Baqir replied, Then choose one of them, follow it, and leave the other. Another report says that he said, Then delay the matter until you meet your Imam and ask him.

- Awali al-Liali, via Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, p303

Fame among the companions comes first, then the firmer narrator, then the report that departs from the common position, then the precaution that fences the religion, and only then free choice. The ladder is fixed, and the jurists inherited it as an order, not a menu. When every rung fails, the Imam gives Zurarah the same last resort he gave Umar ibn Hanzala. Wait, and ask the Imam.

4. The Taqiyya Question

The distinctive rung of the ladder, preferring the report that departs from the common position, has a stated reason in the sources. It answers a documented campaign of counter rulings aimed at Ali, and its scope is debated inside the school today.

Why the Departure Rule Exists

Source: al-Saduq (d.381 AH), ‘Ilal al-Shara’i‘, vol.2, p221, bab 315, no.1

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, raised from Abu Ishaq al-Irjani

Dating: the Imam’s Medina

Claim: the community was commanded to take the opposite of the common position because they opposed Ali in every ruling he gave, intending to break his cause

The Reason Behind the Rule

The departure rule is not sectarian reflex. Al-Saduq devotes a chapter of his book of reasons to it, and the Imam ﵇ gives the history.

حدثنا أبي ﵀ قال : حدثنا أحمد بن إدريس عن أبي إسحاق الإرجاني رفعه قال : قال أبو عبد الله (ع) أتدري لم أمرتم بالأخذ بخلاف ما تقول العامة ؟ فقلت : لا ندري ، فقال : ان عليا (ع) لم يكن يدين الله بدين الا خالف عليه الأمة إلى غيره إرادة لابطال أمره وكانوا يسألون أمير المؤمنين (ع) عن الشئ الذي لا يعلمونه فإذا أفتاهم جعلوا له ضدا من عندهم ليلبسوا على الناس

My father narrated from Ahmad ibn Idris, from Abu Ishaq al-Irjani, who raised the report to Abu Abd Allah. Abu Abd Allah said, Do you know why you were commanded to follow what differs from what the common people say? I said, We do not know.

He replied, Ali followed no religion before Allah except that the community opposed him with another religion, seeking to destroy his cause. They would ask the Commander of the Faithful about a matter they did not know.

When he gave them a ruling, they invented its opposite in order to confuse the people.

- al-Saduq (d.381 AH), ‘Ilal al-Shara’i‘, vol.2, p221

Here is the engine of the whole tarjih apparatus. The opponents asked Ali ﵇ about matters they did not know, and when he ruled, they fabricated a counter ruling from their own side to confuse the people. The departure rule answers that campaign. The Imam’s history explains why the jurists bound the rule to cases of two competing reports, where the counter rulings lived, and the jurists themselves state the binding. This is why the ladder in the previous section carries that rung at all.

Take What the Common Camp Rejects

Source: al-Khomeini (d.1989), al-Ta‘adul wa-l-Tarjih, p192-193, gathering the reports of al-Hasan ibn al-Jahm and others from Risalat al-Qutb

Speaker: Imam al-Kazim ﵇, answering al-Hasan ibn al-Jahm

Dating: late second century AH

Claim: when two reports conflict, take what the opposing camp rejects and avoid what they hold

The Rule in the Imam’s Answer

Al-Hasan ibn al-Jahm asks Imam al-Kazim ﵇, called the Righteous Servant, whether submission alone suffices, and the answer opens the conflict question itself.

وعنها بإسناده عن الحسن بن الجهم قال : قلت للعبد الصالح : هل يسعنا فيما ورد علينا منكم إلّا التسليم لكم ؟ فقال : «لا واللَّه ، لا يسعكم إلّا التسليم لنا» . فقلت : فيروى عن أبي عبد اللَّه ﵇ شيء ، ويروى عنه خلافه ، فبأيّهما نأخذ ؟ فقال : «خذ بما خالف القوم ، وما وافق القوم فاجتنبه

Al-Hasan ibn al-Jahm said, I asked the Righteous Servant, must we submit to you in everything that comes to us from you? He replied, By Allah, you must submit to us. I said, Something is narrated from Abu Abd Allah, and another report from him conflicts with it.

Which should we follow? He replied, Follow what differs from the people. Avoid what agrees with the people.

- al-Khomeini, al-Ta‘adul wa-l-Tarjih, pp192-193, from Risalat al-Qutb

Submission to the Imams ﵈ is the frame, and the departure rule is the content given inside it. The same page of Khomeini’s monograph gathers the parallel reports, from Muhammad ibn Abd Allah before Imam al-Rida ﵇ and from Sama‘a before Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, all carrying the same rule. The reason given in the first card of this section attaches the rule to conflict alone, and to nothing else the common camp says.

When No Sunni Jurist Is in Town

Source: al-Saduq (d.381 AH), ‘Uyun Akhbar al-Rida, vol.1, p248-249, no.10, the full chain of the Barqi family and others

Speaker: Imam al-Rida ﵇, answering Ali ibn Asbat

Dating: early third century AH

Claim: when no Shia jurist is available, ask the city’s jurist and take the opposite of his answer, for the truth lies there

The Rule at Its Boldest, With Its Chain

Al-Hasan ibn al-Jahm received the rule inside the frame of submission. Imam al-Rida ﵇ now carries the rule to its boldest form, and al-Saduq records it with a five transmitter chain.

10 - حدثنا علي بن أحمد بن عبد الله بن أحمد بن أبي عبد الله البرقي ومحمد بن موسى البرقي ومحمد بن علي ماجيلويه ومحمد بن علي بن هاشم وعلي بن عيسى المجاور ﵃ قالوا : حدثنا علي بن محمد ماجيلويه عن أحمد بن محمد بن خالد عن أحمد بن محمد السياري عن علي بن أسباط قال : قلت للرضا ﵇ : يحدث الامر لا أجد بدا من معرفته وليس في البلد الذي انا فيه أحد استفتيه من مواليك قال : فقال : ايت فقيه البلد فاستفته في امرك فإذا أفتاك بشئ فخذ بخلافه فإن الحق فيه

Ali ibn Ahmad ibn Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Abd Allah al-Barqi, Muhammad ibn Musa al-Barqi, Muhammad ibn Ali Majiluyah, Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Hashim, and Ali ibn Isa al-Mujawir narrated from Ali ibn Muhammad Majiluyah, from Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Khalid, from Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Sayyari, from Ali ibn Asbat.

Ali ibn Asbat said, I asked al-Rida, a matter arises that I must understand, yet no one from your followers lives in the town where I am whom I can ask for a legal ruling. He replied, Go to the jurist of that town and ask him about your matter.

When he gives you a ruling, follow the opposite of it, for the truth lies there.

- al-Saduq, ‘Uyun Akhbar al-Rida, vol.1, pp248-249

The report addresses the stranded questioner, the man in a town with no Shia jurist to ask. The instruction is blunt, and it belongs to the same historical setting as the fabrication report in the first card of this section. Modern scholars read such reports against their context. Al-Sistani’s school argues that most narrations cannot be attributed to taqiyya at all, since the Imams ﵈ transmitted openly anti-Sunni material throughout the same period, and the four classical arguments for pervasive taqiyya do not hold. The reports stand as they are. Their scope is what the scholars debate.

5. The Imam Answered Differently

Not every contradiction is a problem to be explained away. The sources record the Imam giving different answers to the same question, and the school reads the difference as delegated authority working under pressure.

Three Different Answers in One Sitting

Source: al-Saffar (d.290 AH), Basa’ir al-Darajat, from Musa ibn Ashim, via al-Nuri al-Tabrisi, Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, p305, no.21420

Speaker: Imam al-Sadiq ﵇, to Musa ibn Ashim

Dating: the Imam’s Medina

Claim: the Imam answered three questioners differently in one sitting, then explained that what Allah delegated to Muhammad He delegated to the Imams

The Shock and Its Answer

Some contradictions are not scribal error. They happen inside one majlis, and a witness recorded his shock.

محمد بن الحسن الصفار في البصائر : عن أحمد بن محمد ، عن أبيه ، عن عبد الله بن المغيرة ، عن عبد الله بن سنان ، عن موسى بن أشيم ، قال : دخلت على أبي عبد الله (﵇) ، فسألته عن مسألة فأجابني ، فبينا انا جالس إذ جاءه رجل ، فسأله عنها بعينها فأجابه بخلاف ما أجابني ، ثم جاءه آخر فسأله عنها بعينها ، فأجابه بخلاف ما أجابني وأجاب صاحبي ، ففزعت من ذلك وعظم علي ، فلما خرج القوم نظر إلي فقال : " يا بن أشيم كأنك جزعت ! " قلت : جعلني الله فداك ، إنما جزعت من ثلاثة أقاويل في مسألة واحدة ، فقال : " يا ابن اشيم ، ان الله فوض إلى سليمان بن داود امر ملكه ، فقال تعالى : * (هذا عطاؤنا فامنن أو امسك بغير حساب) * (1) وفوض إلى محمد (﵌) أمر دينه ، فقال : * (وما آتيكم الرسول فخذوه وما نهاكم عنه فانتهوا) * (2) وإن الله ﵎ فوض إلى الأئمة منا وإلينا ما فوض إلى محمد (﵌) ، فلا تجزع " .

Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Saffar reports in Basa'ir from Ahmad ibn Muhammad, from his father, from Abd Allah ibn al-Mughirah, from Abd Allah ibn Sinan, from Musa ibn Ashim. Musa said, I visited Abu Abd Allah and asked him a question.

He answered me. While I was sitting there, a man came and asked him the same question.

He gave him an answer different from mine. Then another man came and asked the same question.

He gave him an answer different from mine and from my companion's. This alarmed me and weighed heavily on me.

When the people left, he looked at me and said, Ibn Ashim, you seem distressed. I said, May Allah make me your ransom.

I was distressed because you gave three different answers to one question. He replied, Ibn Ashim, Allah entrusted Solomon son of David with authority over his kingdom and said, This is Our gift.

Give freely or withhold without account. He entrusted Muhammad with authority over His religion and said, Take what the Messenger gives you, and refrain from what he forbids you.

Allah entrusted us, the Imams, with what He entrusted to Muhammad. Do not be distressed.

- al-Saffar, Basa’ir al-Darajat, via Mustadrak al-Wasa’il, vol.17, p305

Musa ibn Ashim asks a question, receives an answer, then watches two others ask the same question and receive two different answers. The Imam ﵇ reads his face and names the cause. What Allah delegated to Solomon He delegated to Muhammad ﷺ, and what He delegated to Muhammad He delegated to the Imams ﵈. The varying answers are an exercise of delegated authority, this is your gift, so give or withhold. The school’s scholars add the context layer on top. A ruling matching the common position under pressure is read as taqiyya, and al-Sistani’s school reads the transition from al-Baqir’s ﵇ era to al-Sadiq’s ﵇ that way, while the same school holds that most narrations carry no taqiyya at all.

6. The Authority the School Built

The same tradition that admits the contradictions also claims authority for the tested report, and its modern gatekeepers state plainly what fails the test. The two positions are one position.

The Consensus That Carried the Reports

Source: al-Tusi (d.460 AH), ‘Uddat al-Usul, quoted in al-Khoei, Mu‘jam Rijal al-Hadith, vol.1, p113

Speaker: al-Tusi, arguing for the authority of solitary reports

Dating: fifth century AH

Claim: the rightful community acts on these reports, asks the ruling’s source, accepts the trustworthy narrator’s book, and does not dispute this

From Admission to Authority

Delegated authority explained the varying answers. Al-Tusi now takes the next step, from authority exercised to authority transmitted, and his proof for the solitary report is the practice of the community itself.

والذي يدل على ذلك إجماع الفرقة المحقة ، فإني ، وجدتها مجمعة على العمل بهذه الاخبار التي رووها في تصانيفهم ، ودونوها في أصولهم لا يتناكرون ذلك ولا يتدافعونه ، حتى أن واحدا منهم إذا أفتى بشئ لا يعرفونه سألوه من أين قلت هذا ؟ فإذا أحالهم إلى كتاب معروف أو أصل مشهور وروايته ، وكان راويه ثقة لا ينكر حديثه ، سكتوا وسلموا الامر في ذلك ، وقبلوا قوله . .) . وقال بعد ما ذكر جملا من الاعتراض على حجية الخبر وأجاب عنها : (ومما يدل أيضا على جواز العمل بهذه الاخبار التي أشرنا إليها ما ظهر من الفرقة المحقة من الاختلاف الصادر عن العمل بها ، فإني وجدتها مختلفة

What proves this is the consensus of the rightful community. I found that they all act on these reports, which they narrated in their books and recorded in their foundational collections.

They do not reject this or dispute it.

When one of them gives a ruling that the others do not know, they ask him, where did you get this? When he refers them to a known book or a famous foundational collection and its narration, and its narrator is trustworthy and his hadith is not rejected, they remain silent, leave the matter to him, and accept his statement.

After mentioning several objections to the authority of reports and answering them, he said, Another proof that it is permissible to act on these reports is the disagreement that has appeared among the rightful community through acting on them. I found them differing.

- al-Tusi, ‘Uddat al-Usul, quoted in al-Khoei, Mu‘jam Rijal al-Hadith, vol.1, p113

The community that recorded these reports in their books and foundations acted on them, asked after the source, and accepted the answer when it traced to a known book through a trustworthy narrator. The contradiction problem and the authority claim live in the same scholar by design. The reports carry law because the community treated them as carrying law, and the community’s practice included the testing ladder from the day it accepted them.

The Verdict on al-Kafi

Source: al-Khoei (d.1992), Mu‘jam Rijal al-Hadith, vol.1, p110

Speaker: al-Khoei, concluding his discussion of al-Kafi

Dating: twentieth century, Najaf

Claim: not every report in al-Kafi is established as authentic, some are undoubtedly weak, and some can be confidently judged never to have come from the Infallible

The Modern Gatekeeper’s Conclusion

The most referenced modern rijal work closes its discussion of al-Kafi with one paragraph, and it is the opposite of blanket acceptance.

والمتحصل أنه لم تثبت صحة جميع روايات الكافي ، بل لا شك في أن بعضها ضعيفة ، بل إن بعضها يطمأن بعدم صدورها من المعصوم ﵇ . والله أعلم ببواطن الأمور .

In sum, the authenticity of every report in al-Kafi has not been established. Some of its reports are undoubtedly weak. Some of them can be confidently judged never to have come from the Infallible Imam. Allah knows best what lies hidden.

- al-Khoei, Mu‘jam Rijal al-Hadith, vol.1, p110

The community’s practice carried the ladder from the first day, and al-Khoei’s narrator science is that practice’s modern instrument. He required a connected chain and established trustworthiness for every narrator, one man at a time. He rejected every proposed mass authentication, allowing only Kamil al-Ziyarat and Nawadir al-Hikma as wholes, and later scholars debated even those two. His test asks one question of a narrator, does this man lie. A corrupt creed does not itself make a liar, he argued in his Hajj volumes, so the non-Imami narrator whose honesty is established passes, and the report is graded reliable. The dismantling then ran through every candidate. The companions of consensus are reliable in their own persons, he argued, but their teachers are not covered, and he showed the leading companions narrating from men the rijal works grade liars. The 4,000 students of al-Sadiq ﵇ include unreliable men by name. Companionship, agency, licensing, and sheer volume of narration all fail as proofs of trustworthiness, each for a stated reason in his introduction. His conclusion about the school’s greatest collection is the clearest statement available of what the methodology means in practice. The book is a repository. The verdict belongs to the scholar who runs the tests. Al-Sistani, his student, narrowed the gates further, reading the companions of consensus as reliable only in their own persons and weighing a report’s context against the spirit of the law.

Glossary of Key Terms

Khabar al-wahid: a report carried by a chain short of mass transmission. It yields probability, not certainty, and its authority in law is the school’s central usul question.

Tarjih: preferring one of two conflicting reports. The school’s ladder fixes the order of preference.

Ta‘adul: equipollence, when no preference can be found. The jurist then chooses freely or both reports drop.

Taqiyya: precautionary dissimulation under threat. A ruling matching the common position under pressure may be taqiyya, which is why such reports lose when they conflict.

Khilaf al-‘ammah: departure from the common position, the school’s distinctive preference rule, grounded in the fabrication campaign against Ali.

Zukhruf: glitter without substance. The Imam’s word for a hadith that conflicts with the Quran.

Wathaqa: trustworthiness, the narrator test in the dominant modern school. It asks whether the man lies, not what creed he holds.

Akhbari: the traditionalist school that holds the Four Books reliable as a whole and treats later chain grading with suspicion. The Usuli school, which dominates today, is the school of the individual tests described above.

‘Adala: justice, the stricter narrator test that includes right belief. Al-‘Allama al-Hilli’s standard, narrower than al-Khoei’s.

Mutawatir: a report carried at every level by numbers that make collective lying impossible. It yields certainty and stands above every other kind of evidence.

Final Verdict

The school that opponents accused of swallowing contradictions is the school that wrote the tests. The Prophet’s ﷺ sermon fixed the Quran gate before any chain was weighed. The Imams fixed the conflict ladder in their own answers, rung by rung, and named standing down as its last resort. The collectors stated their filters and their limits in their own introductions, and the greatest of them wrote that he knew only the least of what the filters yield. The modern gatekeepers narrowed the gates further, weighing each narrator alone and dismantling every mass authentication proposed to them, and the clearest sentence in the whole literature is al-Khoei’s verdict on the school’s greatest book. Not every report in al-Kafi is proven authentic. A tradition that says this about its own canon, in the voice of its own greatest rijal scholar, has answered the charge. The contradictions are real, the gates rest on the school’s own transmitted commands, and the book remains a repository whose verdict belongs to the scholar who runs the tests.
