The Expedition of Waddan

Twelve months after the Hijra the Prophet ﷺ marched against a Quraysh caravan on the Syria road. He met no enemy and came home with the first treaty of the new state, a written pact with Banu Damra. From Ibn Ishaq, carried by Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, with the Shia line of al-Mufid. Part 1 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The First March

Twelve months after the Hijra, his migration from Mecca to Medina, and twelve nights into the month of Safar, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ marched out of Medina. He rode at the head of the Emigrants, the Muslims of Mecca who had followed him to the city. No Ansari, no native Muslim of Medina, rode with him.

Sa'd ibn Ubada stayed behind to run Medina. The march aimed at two targets, a trade caravan of Quraysh on the caravan road to Syria, and Banu Damra, the tribe that held the wells on that road.▼ commentary

Ibn Ishaq's account, carried by Ibn Sayyid al-Nas with its full chain, opens the story.

روينا عن أبي عروبة ثنا سليمان بن سيف ثنا سعيد بن بريع ثنا محمد بن إسحاق قال خرج رسول الله ﵌ في صفر غازيا على رأس اثنى عشر شهرا من مقدمه المدينة لاثنتي عشرة ليلة مضت من شهر صفر حتى بلغ ودان وكان يريد قريشا وبنى ضمرة وهى غزوة الأبواء ثم رجع إلى المدينة وكان استعمل عليها سعد ابن عبادة فيما ذكره ابن هشام . قال ابن إسحاق فوادعته فيها بنو ضمرة وكان الذي وادعه منهم عليهم مخشى بن عمرو الضمري وكان سيدهم في زمانه ذلك ثم رجع رسول الله ﵌ إلى المدينة ولم يلق كيدا

"We have it from Abu 'Aruba. Sulayman ibn Sayf told us. Sa'id ibn Buray' told us. Muhammad ibn Ishaq said. The Messenger of Allah went out in Safar on campaign, twelve months after his arrival in Medina, when twelve nights of Safar had passed, and marched until he reached Waddan. He had Quraysh and Banu Damra in view. This is the expedition of al-Abwa. Then he returned to Medina. He had put Sa'd ibn Ubada in charge of it, as Ibn Hisham mentions. Ibn Ishaq said that Banu Damra made peace with him there. The one who made peace with him on their behalf was Makhshi ibn Amr al-Damri, their chief at that time. Then the Messenger of Allah returned to Medina and met no hostility."

- Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.1, p.295, carrying Ibn Ishaq with his chain

The same report reached later chroniclers with one added detail. Banu Damra belonged to the wider Kinana confederation.

قال ابن إسحاق وفي صفر على رأس اثنى عشر شهرا من الهجرة غزا صلى اللّه تعالى عليه وآله وسلم غزوة ودّان يريد قريشا وبنى ضمرة من كنانة فوادعه

"Ibn Ishaq said that in Safar, twelve months after the Hijra, he went out on the expedition of Waddan with Quraysh and Banu Damra of Kinana in view, and he made peace with them."

- al-'Amili, Bahja al-Mafatil, vol.1, p.172

The march ran to Waddan, a large village about eight miles from al-Abwa on the Mecca to Medina road.

وغزوة الأبواء تسمي غزوة ودّان ، بفتح الواو وتشديد الدال المهملة آخره نون (1) ، وذلك أنه صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم خرج غازيا حتّى بلغ « ودّان » وهي قرية كبيرة بينها وبين الأبواء نحو ثمانية أميال ، و « الأبواء » قرية بين مكة والمدينة ، وكان خروجه صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم بالمهاجرين يتعرض عير قريش ، ويريد بني ضمرة ، وكان عدد من معه سبعين رجلا من أصحابه ،

"The expedition of al-Abwa is also called the expedition of Waddan, with a fatha on the waw, a doubled dal, and a final nun. The Messenger of Allah went out on campaign until he reached Waddan, a large village about eight miles from al-Abwa, and al-Abwa is a village between Mecca and Medina. He went out with the Emigrants to intercept a caravan of Quraysh, and he had Banu Damra in view. The number with him was seventy men of his companions."

- al-Tahtawi, Nihayat al-Ijaz, p.286, on the two names and the seventy

No caravan crossed his path. No arrow flew and no sword was drawn. The Prophet ﷺ turned to the second aim of the march, the pact. He concluded it with the chief of Banu Damra, and a written document fixed its terms.▼ commentary▼ commentary

وفي هذه الغزوة صالح بنى ضمرة ، فعقد الصلح مع سيدهم حينئذ ، وهو مجدي بن عمرو الجهني ، علي ألا يغزوهم ولا يغزونه ، ولا يكثروا عليه جمعا ، ولا يعينوا عليه عدوا ، وكتب بينه وبينهم كتابا نسخته :

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم :

« هذا كتاب من محمد رسول الله صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم لبني ضمرة بأنهم آمنون علي أموالهم وأنفسهم ، وأن لهم النصرة علي من رامهم ، إلا أن يحاربوا في دين الله ما بلّ بحر صوفة ( أي ما بقي فيه ما يبل الصوفة ) ، وأن النبي صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم إذا دعاهم لنصره أجابوه ، عليهم بذلك ذمة الله وذمة رسوله ( أي أمانهما ) » انتهي .

"On this expedition he made peace with Banu Damra. He concluded the peace with their chief at that time, Majdi ibn Amr al-Juhani. Neither side would raid the other, they would not gather troops against him, and they would not help any enemy against him. He wrote a document between himself and them, and its text follows. In the name of Allah, the Merciful, the Compassionate. This is a document from Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, to Banu Damra. They are safe in their wealth and their persons. They have support against whoever attacks them, unless they fight in the religion of Allah, for as long as the sea wets wool. If the Prophet calls them to his aid, they answer him. The protection of Allah and the protection of His Messenger bind them to this. End of document."

- al-Tahtawi, Nihayat al-Ijaz, p.286, the pact and its written text

The banner went back to Medina with the army, white, carrying the words of Islam.▼ commentary

وكان لواؤه أبيض مكتوبا عليه « لا إله إلا الله محمد رسول الله » ، وكان اللواء مع عمه حمزة ، واستعمل علي المدينة سعد بن عبادة ، وانصرف إلي المدينة ، وكانت غيبته خمس عشرة ليلة .

"His banner was white, with the words 'There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah' written on it. The banner was with his uncle Hamza. He put Sa'd ibn Ubada in charge of Medina and went back to it. His absence lasted fifteen nights."

- al-Tahtawi, Nihayat al-Ijaz, p.286, on the banner and the return

The whole march lasted fifteen nights. No one was killed and no one fled, because no battle happened. The Shia line adds a report on the banner that the Sunni chroniclers do not carry.▼ commentary

وهي أول غزواته (صلى الله عليه واله) بنفسه خرج في ستين راكبا من المهاجرين يريد عيرا لقريش فلم يلق كيدا ووادع في طريقه بني ضمرة وكتب بينه وبينهم كتابا وكانت غيبته خمس عشرة ليلة.

قال ابن سعد: كان لواؤه مع حمزة بن عبد المطلب ولكن المفيد في الارشاد روى بسنده عن أبي البختري القرشي أن النبي (صلى الله عليه واله) اعطى علي بن أبي طالب الراية في غزاة ودان وهي أول غزاة حمل فيها راية في الاسلام مع النبي (صلى الله عليه واله)

"It was the first of his expeditions in person. He went out with sixty riders of the Emigrants, with a caravan of Quraysh in view, and met no hostility. On the way he made peace with Banu Damra and wrote a document between himself and them. His absence lasted fifteen nights. Ibn Sa'd said his banner was with Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib. But al-Mufid, in al-Irshad, transmitted through his chain from Abu al-Bakhtari al-Qurashi that the Prophet gave the banner to Ali ibn Abi Talib on the campaign of Waddan, the first campaign on which he carried a banner in Islam alongside the Prophet."

- Maktabat al-Marja', p.1837, citing Ibn Sa'd and al-Mufid's al-Irshad

Commentary

The Sources for Waddan

Source: Ibn Ishaq, carried by Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.1, p.295

Chain: Abu 'Aruba, then Sulayman ibn Sayf, then Sa'id ibn Buray', then Muhammad ibn Ishaq

Dating: Ibn Sayyid al-Nas died 734 AH

Later carriers: al-'Amili, Bahja al-Mafatil, vol.1, p.172. al-Tahtawi, Nihayat al-Ijaz, p.286. Maktabat al-Marja', p.1837, citing Ibn Sa'd and al-Mufid

The story of Waddan rests on one root, Ibn Ishaq. Every later Sunni chronicler who tells it draws from him. The Shia line adds al-Mufid's al-Irshad as a second root for the banner question.

That is a thin base, and the march is known only through what these carriers actually say. Where they disagree, the disagreement stays in view.

What the Pact Secured

Parties: the Prophet ﷺ and Banu Damra, through their chief

Terms: no raiding either way, no troops gathered against him, no aid to his enemies

Obligation on Banu Damra: answer his call to arms

Obligation on the Muslims: Banu Damra safe in wealth and persons, supported against attackers

Duration: for as long as the sea wets wool, an Arab formula meaning forever

The pact gave the Muslims their first ally on the caravan road that ran from Medina up to Syria. Quraysh caravans used that road, and after Waddan a strong tribe on it stood neutral at worst. The formula about the sea and the wool is an Arab way of saying forever, because the sea never dries. The exception clause, unless they fight in the religion of Allah, keeps the pact from covering a betrayal.

Who Held the Banner

Majority report: the banner was with Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib, per Ibn Sa'd

Shia report: al-Mufid, al-Irshad, from Abu al-Bakhtari al-Qurashi, gives the banner to Ali ﵇

Common ground: the Prophet ﷺ commanded the march in person

Ibn Sa'd puts the white banner in the hand of Hamza. Al-Mufid, through Abu al-Bakhtari, puts it in the hand of Ali ﵇. The two reports agree on everything else. The march happened, it was the first in person, and it ended in a pact.

A reader who holds to Ibn Sa'd loses nothing of the story, and a reader who holds to al-Mufid gains the point that Ali ﵇ stood at the front from the very first march. An honest historian cannot force one answer from these sources.

Why the Numbers Differ

Riders: sixty per Maktabat al-Marja', seventy per Nihayat al-Ijaz

Treaty partner: Makhshi ibn Amr al-Damri per Ibn Hisham, Majdi ibn Amr al-Juhani per Nihayat al-Ijaz

The head counts differ by ten men, and the treaty partner carries two names from two houses, al-Damri and al-Juhani. The gaps sit at the edges of the story, in counts and in names. Every carrier agrees on the core, the march, the bloodless outcome, and the written pact.

Was the March to Waddan Justified

The march was offensive in form and defensive in aim. The Muslims were refugees whom Mecca had driven from their homes for their faith, and the Qur'an's permission to fight names exactly that wrong.

أُذِنَ لِلَّذِينَ يُقَٰتَلُونَ بِأَنَّهُمْ ظُلِمُوا۟ ۚ وَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ نَصْرِهِمْ لَقَدِيرٌ ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ مِن دِيَٰرِهِم بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ إِلَّآ أَن يَقُولُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ ۗ

udhina li-lladhīna yuqātalūna bi-annahum ẓulimū wa-inna llāha ʿalā naṣrihim laqadīr. alladhīna ukhrijū min diyārihim bi-ghayri ḥaqqin illā an yaqūlū rabbunā llāh

"Permission to fight is given to those who are being fought, because they were wronged, and Allah is able to help them. Those who were driven from their homes without right, only because they said our Lord is Allah."

- Qur'an 22:39-40, Uthmani text

The Prophet ﷺ carried that fight to the trade artery of the enemy that had driven them out. He came home without plunder and chose a defensive alliance over retaliation. Had the aim been plunder, the pact with Banu Damra would not have served that aim.

The strategic gain was real. The flank on the caravan road to Syria was secured before the first battle of Islam was ever fought.

No fighting occurred, so no question of conduct in battle arises. No companions are reported to have fled, because there was nothing to flee. No one was killed.

The tactical outcome was no engagement. The strategic outcome was the first treaty of the new state.
