Does the Qur'an Leave the Door Open at 43:81

The Argument Under Examination

Christian readers sometimes quote Qur'an 43:81. They read it as an admission that God could have a son. The verse reads as a conditional. The argument runs that the door is left open. The study ahead tests that reading against the Arabic language, the verse's own context, and the exegetical record of both schools.

Three facts decide the matter. First, the word rendered "worshippers" here has a second classical sense, "those who disdain." The lexicons attest it with a full verse of poetry. Second, the verse sits in a company of verses that close every door the conditional might open. Third, the earliest Ahl al-Bayt exegetes read the conditional as a device of impossibility. Major Sunni exegetes agree with that reading on their own chains.

Premise 1: The word ʿābidīn in Qur'an 43:81 is lexically capable of meaning "the disdainful" and "the deniers," on the authority of al-Jawhari and the poetry he cites.

Premise 2: The conditional in kāna form is a recognized Arabic device for declaring something impossible, used the same way elsewhere in the Qur'an.

Premise 3: The school's exegesis from Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi onward, and the Sunni exegesis from al-Baydawi onward, converge on readings that deny any possibility of a son.

1. The Verse on the Table

The verse in the Uthmani text, fetched from the Qur'an API, reads as follows.

Source: Qur'an 43:81, Uthmani text via alquran.cloud API

Speaker: God, addressing the Prophet

Claim: A conditional joined to a declaration of worship or disdain

قُلْ إِن كَانَ لِلرَّحْمَٰنِ وَلَدٌۭ فَأَنَا۠ أَوَّلُ ٱلْعَٰبِدِينَ

Qul in kāna li-l-raḥmāni waladun fa-anā awwalu l-ʿābidīn.

سُبْحَٰنَ رَبِّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَرْشِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ

Subḥāna rabbi l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi rabbi l-ʿarshi ʿammā yaṣifūn.

Say, if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be the first of the worshippers.

Glory to the Lord of the heavens and the earth, the Lord of the Throne, above what they describe.

- Qur'an 43:81-82, Uthmani text (alquran.cloud)

The word translated "if" is in, and the verb is kāna. Together they set up a condition. The question the study answers is what kind of condition the Arabic sets up here, an open possibility or a closed door.

2. The Word Before the Verse

The crux is the word al-ʿābidīn. In modern reading it is taken as "worshippers." The classical lexicons record another sense for the same root, anger and disdain, and they prove it with a complete verse of the poet al-Farazdaq.

Source: Al-Jawhari (d. 398 AH), Al-Sihah, v2, p67

Speaker: Abu Zayd and Abu Amr, the language authorities

Claim: ʿabada with the doubled middle means to disdain and to be angry, and 43:81 carries that sense

قال الفرزدق : أولئك أحلاسي فجئني بمثلهم (2) * وأعبد أن أهجو كليبا بدارم -

Abu Zayd said, ʿabid with vowelling of the middle means anger and disdain, and the noun is ʿabda like anafa, and he ʿabida means he disdained. Al-Farazdaq said, those are my close kin, so bring me their like, and I disdain to satirize Kulayb with Darim.

- Al-Jawhari, Al-Sihah, v2, p67

The full verse of al-Farazdaq as carried on the same page of Al-Sihah.

Source: Al-Farazdaq (d. 110 AH), quoted in Al-Sihah v2 p67

Speaker: Al-Farazdaq, defending his lineage

Claim: A complete two-hemistich verse using ʿabada in the disdain sense

أولئك أحلاسي فجئني بمثلهم

Ulāʾika aḥlāsī fa-jiʾnī bi-mithlihim,

وأعبد أن أهجو كليبا بدارم

wa-aʿbudu an ahjū Kulayban bi-Dārim.

Those are my close kin, so bring me their like,

and I disdain to satirize Kulayb with Darim.

- Al-Farazdaq, in Al-Jawhari, Al-Sihah, v2, p67

The earlier published version quoted half of this verse. The full verse is restored here from the corpus bytes. Its first half carries the weight. It names the kin whose like the challenger cannot produce. The poetry and the verse use the same conditional device.

No verse of the Qur'an stands alone. The verses around 43:81 and the parallel passages elsewhere close the door the conditional seems to open.

وَأَنَّهُۥ تَعَٰلَىٰ جَدُّ رَبِّنَا مَا ٱتَّخَذَ صَٰحِبَةًۭ وَلَا وَلَدًا

And they said, the Most Merciful has taken a son. Glory to Him. They are rather honored servants.

And He, exalted is the majesty of our Lord, has not taken a consort nor a son.

" وقال تعالى " : قل إن كان للرحمن ولد فأنا أول العابدين * سبحان رب السماوات والأرض رب العرش عما يصفون 81 ، 82 الطور : أم له البنات ولكم البنون 39 النجم : ألكم الذكر وله الأنثى * تلك إذا قسمة ضيزى 21 ، 22 " وقال تعالى " : إن الذين لا يؤمنون بالآخرة ليسمون الملائكة تسمية الأنثى * ومالهم به من علم إن يتبعون إلا الظن وإن الظن لا يغني من الحق شيئا 27 ، 28 الجن : وأنه تعالى جد ربنا ما اتخذ صاحبة ولا ولدا 3

And He, exalted said, say, if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be first of the worshippers. Glory to the Lord of the heavens and the earth, the Lord of the Throne, above what they describe. The Mountain chapter, does He have daughters and you sons. The Star chapter, do you have the male and He the female. Those are then a division unjust. And He exalted said, those who do not believe in the hereafter name the angels with the naming of the female. And the Jinn chapter, and He exalted is the majesty of our Lord, He has not taken a consort nor a son.

- Al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, v3, p258

The chapter of Maryam answers the same claim. There the heavens almost split. The chapter of the Jinn states the negation plainly. Surat al-Ikhlas closes the question with the most direct negation in the canon. Against this company, reading 43:81 as an admission of possibility makes one verse contradict the whole.

4. The Readings of the School

The Ahl al-Bayt record reads the verse through three main lenses. Al-Majlisi preserves all three in Bihar al-Anwar with his lexical commentary.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d. 329 AH), Tafsir, relayed in Bihar v3 p258

Speaker: Al-Qummi

Claim: First of the disdainful, that God should have a son

3 - تفسير علي بن إبراهيم : قوله : قل إن كان للرحمن ولدا فأنا أول العابدين ، يعني أول الآنفين له أن يكون له ولد . (1) بيان : هذا أحد الوجوه في تأويل هذه الآية . قال الجوهري : قال أبو زيد : العبد بالتحريك : الغضب والانف ، والاسم العبدة مثل الانفة ، وقد عبد أي أنف . وقال أبو عمرو : قوله تعالى : فأنا أول العابدين من الانف والغضب انتهى .

Ali ibn Ibrahim said, His saying, say if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be first of the ʿābidīn, means first of the disdainful, that He should have a son. Commentary, this is one of the faces of interpreting the verse. Al-Jawhari said, Abu Zayd said, ʿabid with the vowelled middle is anger and disdain, and the noun is ʿabda like anafa, and he ʿabida, meaning he disdained. And Abu Amr said, His saying, first of the ʿābidīn, is from disdain and anger.

- Al-Qummi, Tafsir, relayed in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, v3, p258

Al-Majlisi then unfolds the remaining faces, including the impossibility reading that the Sunni canon would later adopt.

Source: Al-Majlisi (d. 1111 AH), Bayan on the Qummi relay, Bihar v3 p259

Speaker: Al-Majlisi

Claim: Hanging the impossible on the impossible, and the negation reading of in

تعليق المحال بالمحال أي ليس له ولد ، إذ لو كان له ولد لكنت أول العابدين له ، فإن النبي يكون أعلم بالله وبما يصح له ومالا يصح ، وأولى بتعظيم ما يجب تعظيمه ، ومن حق تعظيم الوالد تعظيم ولده . وثالثها : أن المعنى : إن كان له ولد في زعمكم فأنا أول العابدين لله ، الموحدين له ، المنكرين لقولكم . ورابعها : أن " إن " بمعنى " ما " للنفي ، والمعنى : ما كان للرحمن ولد ، فأنا أول العابدين لله المقرين بذلك .

The second is that it belongs to hanging the impossible on the impossible, meaning He has no son, since were He to have a son I would be first of the worshippers of him, for the Prophet is most knowing of God and of what befits Him and what does not, and most entitled to magnify what must be magnified, and it is of the right of magnifying the father to magnify the son. The third is that the meaning is, if he has a son in your claim, then I am first of the worshippers of God, the ones declaring His oneness, the deniers of your saying. The fourth is that in carries the meaning of negation, so the meaning is, the Most Merciful has no son, and I am first of the worshippers of God, the ones affirming that.

- Al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, v3, p259

Two more readings complete the school's record. The Ihtijaj attributes a direct gloss to Ali himself.

Source: Al-Tabarsi (d. 548 AH), Al-Ihtijaj, v1, p382

Speaker: Ali ibn Abi Talib

Claim: First of the deniers, and the inner meaning opposes the outer

وكذلك قوله : " إن كان للرحمن ولد فإنا أول العابدين " أي : الجاحدين والتأويل في هذا القول باطنه مضاد لظاهره .

And likewise His saying, if the Most Merciful had a son then we are first of the ʿābidīn, meaning the deniers, and the interpretation in this saying is inward against the outward.

- Al-Tabarsi, Al-Ihtijaj, v1, p382

And al-Kafi's covenant chapter ties the verse to the primordial covenant, where the Prophet was first to enter the fire made cool and safe.

Ali ibn Ibrahim, from his father, from Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Nasr, from Aban ibn Uthman, from Muhammad ibn Ali al-Halabi, from Abu Abd Allah, who said, God, when He willed to create Adam, sent water on the clay, then grasped a grasp and kneaded it, then parted it into two portions with His hand, then scattered them, and they were crawling. Then He raised a fire for them and commanded the people of the left to enter it, and they went toward it and feared it and did not enter. Then He commanded the people of the right to enter, and they went and entered, and God commanded the fire and it became coolness and peace upon them. When the people of the left saw that they said, our Lord, retract from us, and He retracted, then He said to them, enter it, and they went and stood upon it and did not enter, so He returned them to clay and created Adam from it. And Abu Abd Allah said, these will never be able to be of those, nor those of these. He said, they consider that the Messenger of God was first of those who entered that fire, therefore His saying, say, if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be first of the ʿābidīn.

- Al-Kulayni, Al-Kafi, v2, p7

Claim: Praise to God who did not beget, so an heir would inherit, and was not begotten, so a partner would share

2 - التوحيد : ماجيلويه ، عن عمه ، عن البرقي ، عن اليقطيني ، عن سليمان بن رشيد ، عن أبيه ، عن المفضل قال : سمعت أبا عبد الله ﵇ يقول : الحمد لله الذي لم يلد فيورث ولم يولد فيشارك .

5. The Lexicons and the Variants

The Sunni lexical tradition carries the same entry. One transmitter records a Tamim variant of the proof verse.

Source: Al-Qurtubi (d. 671 AH), Al-Jami li-Ahkam al-Quran, v16, p121

Speaker: Al-Qurtubi, citing Abu Zayd

Claim: The ʿabida entry with the full Farazdaq verse and the Tamim variant

قال الفرزدق : أولئك أجلاسي فجئني بمثلهم * وأعبد أن أهجو كليبا بدارم وينشد أيضا : أولئك ناس إن هجوني هجوتهم * وأعبد أن يهجى كليب بدارم

It is said, ʿabida yaʿbudu ʿabidan with the vowelled middle, when he disdained and grew angry, so he is ʿabid, and the noun is ʿabda like anafa, on the authority of Abu Zayd. Al-Farazdaq said, those are my close kin so bring me their like, and I disdain to satirize Kulayb with Darim. And it is also recited, those are a people, if they satirize me I satirize them, and I disdain that Kulayb be satirized with Darim.

- Al-Qurtubi, Al-Jami li-Ahkam al-Quran, v16, p121

Al-Bahrani transmits the gloss through the genealogy of narrators.

Source: Al-Bahrani (d. 1107 AH), Ghayat al-Maram, v5, p259

Speaker: The narrator of the report

Claim: ʿabida means he disdained, and Ibn Qutaybah recited the Tamim variant

قال الراوي : عبد : استنكف ، وأنشد ابن قتيبة واعبد ان تهجي تميم بدارم ، أي آنف (2) .

The narrator said, ʿabida, he disdained, and Ibn Qutaybah recited, and I disdain that Tamim be satirized with Darim, meaning I am too proud.

- Al-Bahrani, Ghayat al-Maram, v5, p259

Eight centuries later Sayyid Shubbar still reads the word the same way, through the Qamus.

Source: Sayyid Abd Allah Shubbar (d. 1242 AH), Misbah al-Anwar, v1, p550

Speaker: Shubbar, on the report of al-Hurr al-Amili

Claim: The worship in the report means denial, and the language scholars declared this sense, and 43:81 was read through it

الأوّل : أن يكون المراد بالعبادة في قوله : « لم يعبد الحقّ » الجحود والإنكار ، ويكون المعنى : من عرف الحقّ لم يجحده ولم ينكره ، وهذا المعنى صرّح به أهل اللغة كصاحب القاموس ، وبه فسّر قوله تعالى : « قُلْ إِنْ كانَ لِلرَّحْمنِ وَلَدٌ فَأَنَا أَوَّلُ الْعابِدِينَ » (2) ، أي الجاحدين (3) .

The first is that the intent of worship in His saying, he did not worship the truth, is denial and rejection, and the meaning is, whoever knows the truth does not deny it and does not reject it, and this sense the people of language declared, like the author of the Qamus, and by it He interpreted His saying, say, if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be first of the worshippers, meaning the deniers.

- Sayyid Abd Allah Shubbar, Misbah al-Anwar, v1, p550

6. What the Sunni Canon Says

The Sunni exegetes themselves supply the convergence that decides the question.

Source: Al-Baydawi (d. 685 AH), Anwar al-Tanzil, v5, p97

Speaker: Al-Baydawi

Claim: The conditional denies both the son and its worship, on the strongest negation

منكم فإن النبي صلَّى اللَّه عليه وسلم يكون أعلم باللَّه وبما يصح له وبما لا يصح له ، وأولى بتعظيم ما يوجب تعظيمه ومن تعظيم الوالد تعظيم ولده ، ولا يلزم من ذلك صحة كينونة الولد وعبادته له إذ المحال قد يستلزم المحال

Say, if the Most Merciful had a son from among you, then the Prophet, peace be upon him, would be most knowing of God and of what befits Him and what does not befit Him, and most entitled to magnify what demands magnification, and of the right of magnifying the father is magnifying the son, and it does not follow from that the reality of the son and his worship, for the impossible may entail the impossible. Rather the intent is negating them both in the strongest of ways.

- Al-Baydawi, Anwar al-Tanzil, v5, p97

Source: Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani (d. 852 AH), Fath al-Bari, v8, p437

Speaker: Ibn Hajar, citing the exegetes

Claim: The worshippers are the deniers of what you claimed

وقال آخرون معناه إن كان للرحمن في قولكم ولد فأنا أول العابدين أي الكافرين بذلك والجاحدين لما قلتم والعابدين من عبد بكسر الباء يعبد بفتحها قال الشاعر أولئك قومي إن هجوني هجوتهم * وأعبد أن أهجو كليبا بدارم أي أمتنع وأخرج الطبري أيضا عن يونس بن عبد الأعلى عن بن وهب عبد معناه استنكف ثم ساق قصة عن عمر في ذلك و

Others said its meaning is, if the Most Merciful has in your saying a son, then I am first of the worshippers, meaning the deniers of that and the rejecters of what you claimed. And al-ʿābidīn is from ʿabida with the broken middle, yaʿbudu with the open. The poet said, those are my people, if they satirize me I satirize them, and I disdain to satirize Kulayb with Darim, meaning I refrain.

- Ibn Hajar, Fath al-Bari, v8, p437

Al-Qummi's relay on the Maryam passage frames the doctrine the conditional serves.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d. 329 AH) on Qur'an 19:88-93, Bihar v3 p258

7. The Christian Reading Compared

The Christian claim traces to the creedal line that the Son is begotten, not made. The primary holders are the Nicene Creed of 325 CE and its revision of 381 CE. The Gospel line is John 3:16. There God gave His only Son. Those texts are not quoted from memory here, and the Bible fetch channel stays reserved for the comparison build. The creedal holders are cited below as sources, not quoted.

  • Nicene Creed (325 CE), the line begotten, not made, held at Nicaea. - Nicene Creed, 325 CE, New Advent translation
  • John 3:16, God gave His only Son, in the Gospel of John.
What the comparison will test is the device itself, whether any conditional of the form found at 43:81 asserts possibility.

On the Arabic evidence assembled above, the answer is no. The lexicons give the word a disdain sense proven by a complete verse. The exegetes of both schools read the conditional as a device of negation. The surrounding canon states the negation without condition. The Christian reading of 43:81 asks the verse to carry a meaning its language, its company, and its exegetes all refuse.

Final Verdict

Qur'an 43:81 does not allow divine sonship. The conditional is a known Arabic device that hangs an impossibility on an impossibility. The word ʿābidīn carries the disdain sense in the lexicons with a full proof verse. The Ahl al-Bayt exegetes from al-Qummi to al-Majlisi read the verse as a negation of the son claim. The Sunni exegetes al-Baydawi and Ibn Hajar agree in their own canon. The verse closes the door. It does not open one.
