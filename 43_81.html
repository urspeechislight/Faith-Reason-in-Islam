Does Qur’an 43:81 Allow for Divine Sonship?

The Argument Under Examination

Some Christians say Qur’an 43:81 suggests God could have a son, like Jesus. Here is their argument.

Source: Qur’an 43:81, Uthmani text

Speaker: God, addressing the Prophet ﷺ

Claim: the Christian reading takes the verse as leaving open the possibility of a divine son

قُلْ إِن كَانَ لِلرَّحْمَٰنِ وَلَدٌ فَأَنَا أَوَّلُ الْعَابِدِينَ

Qul in kāna lil-Raḥmāni waladun fa-anā awwalu al-ʿābidīn

“Say, if the All-Merciful had a child, then I would be the first to worship.” Qur’an 43:81

- Qur’an 43:81, Uthmani text

Premise 2: Worship Implies Acceptance

The word “worship” (ʿābidīn) means accepting the child as divine, supporting the idea of Jesus as God’s son.

Premise 3: Fits Christian Belief

This matches the Christian view that Jesus is God’s son, part of the Trinity.

Conclusion

Qur’an 43:81 supports the possibility of God having a son, aligning with Christian theology.

The Christian claim misreads the verse. Qur’an 43:81 uses a special Arabic style called al-sharṭ al-mumteni‘ (impossible condition) to reject the idea of God having a child, targeting Meccan pagans and Christians. We’ll use language, Islamic teachings, history, and logic to prove this, showing the Christian view doesn’t make sense.

1. Language Proof

The word ʿābidīn is the crux of the verse. In modern reading it is taken as “worshippers.” The classical lexicons record another sense for the same root, anger and disdain, and they prove it with a full verse of the poet al-Farazdaq.

Source: Al-Jawhari (d.398 AH), Al-Sihah, vol.2, p67

Speaker: Abu Zayd and Abu Amr, the language authorities

Claim: The verb ʿabida, with the middle letter vowelled, means to disdain and to be angry, and the noun is ʿabda, like anafa

قال الفرزدق : أولئك أحلاسي فجئني بمثلهم (2) * وأعبد أن أهجو كليبا بدارم -

ʿAbidun bi-ḥarakati l-wasati l-ghaḍabu wa-l-anaf, wa-l-ismu l-ʿabdatu mithlu l-anafah, wa-ʿabida yaʿbudu ayi llāha wa-stakbara

Abu Zayd said, ʿabid with the vowelled middle means anger and disdain, and the noun is ʿabda like anafa. And al-Farazdaq said, those are my close kin, so bring me their like, and I disdain to satirize Kulayb with Darim.

- Al-Jawhari (d.398 AH), Al-Sihah, vol.2, p67

The full verse of al-Farazdaq, both halves, carried on the same lexicon page.

Source: Al-Farazdaq (d.110 AH), in Al-Jawhari, Al-Sihah, vol.2, p67

Speaker: Al-Farazdaq, defending his lineage

Claim: A complete two-hemistich verse using the verb in the disdain sense

أولئك أحلاسي فجئني بمثلهم
وأعبد أن أهجو كليبا بدارم

Ulāʾika aḥlāsī fa-jiʾnī bi-mithlihim / wa-aʿbudu an ahjū Kulayban bi-Dārim

“Those are my close kin, so bring me their like, and I disdain to satirize Kulayb with Darim” (al-Farazdaq)

- Al-Farazdaq (d.110 AH), in Al-Jawhari (d.398 AH), Al-Sihah, vol.2, p67

Al-Majlisi gathers the verses of negation under one heading, son and consort alike.

Source: Al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p258

Speaker: Al-Majlisi, compiling the verses of negation

Claim: The verse block on negating son and consort, from Maryam through the Jinn chapter

" وقال تعالى " : قل إن كان للرحمن ولد فأنا أول العابدين * سبحان رب السماوات والأرض رب العرش عما يصفون 81 ، 82 الطور : أم له البنات ولكم البنون 39 النجم : ألكم الذكر وله الأنثى * تلك إذا قسمة ضيزى 21 ، 22 " وقال تعالى " : إن الذين لا يؤمنون بالآخرة ليسمون الملائكة تسمية الأنثى * ومالهم به من علم إن يتبعون إلا الظن وإن الظن لا يغني من الحق شيئا 27 ، 28 الجن : وأنه تعالى جد ربنا ما اتخذ صاحبة ولا ولدا 3

And He, exalted said, say, if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be first of the worshippers. Glory to the Lord of the heavens and the earth, the Lord of the Throne, above what they describe. The Mountain chapter, does He have daughters and you sons. The Star chapter, do you have the male and He the female. Those are then a division unjust. And those who do not believe in the hereafter name the angels with the naming of the female. And the Jinn chapter, and He exalted is the majesty of our Lord, He has not taken a consort nor a son.

- Al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p258

An earlier published form carried only the second half of this verse. The first half carries the weight. It names the kin whose like the challenger cannot produce. The conditional structure of the poetry and the conditional structure of the verse are the same device.

2. The Readings of the School

The Ahl al-Bayt record reads the verse through three main lenses. Al-Majlisi preserves all three in Bihar al-Anwar with his lexical commentary.

Source: Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329 AH), Tafsir, relayed in al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p258

Speaker: Al-Qummi

Claim: First of the disdainful, that God should have a son

3 - تفسير علي بن إبراهيم : قوله : قل إن كان للرحمن ولدا فأنا أول العابدين ، يعني أول الآنفين له أن يكون له ولد . (1) بيان : هذا أحد الوجوه في تأويل هذه الآية . قال الجوهري : قال أبو زيد : العبد بالتحريك : الغضب والانف ، والاسم العبدة مثل الانفة ، وقد عبد أي أنف . وقال أبو عمرو : قوله تعالى : فأنا أول العابدين من الانف والغضب انتهى .

Ali ibn Ibrahim said, His saying, say if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be first of the ʿābidīn, means first of the disdainful, that He should have a son. Commentary, this is one of the faces of interpreting the verse. Al-Jawhari said, Abu Zayd said, ʿabid with the vowelled middle is anger and disdain, and the noun is ʿabda like anafa, and he ʿabida, meaning he disdained. And Abu Amr said, His saying, first of the ʿābidīn, is from disdain and anger.

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi (d.329 AH), Tafsir, relayed in al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p258

The later commentary fixes the meaning of the throne in the verse that follows.

Source: Al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.55, p35

Speaker: Al-Majlisi, on the throne of 43:82

Claim: The throne fitting this place is the throne of holiness, above likeness, example, and sons

والمناسب هنا عرش التقدس والتنزه عن الأشباه والأمثال والأولاد ، فالعرش في كل مقام يراد به معنى يعلمه الراسخون في العلم .

And what fits here is the throne of holiness and transcendence above likenesses, examples, and sons. The throne in every place carries a meaning the firm in knowledge know.

- Al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.55, p35

Source: Al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p259

Speaker: Al-Majlisi, on the faces of the verse

Claim: The second face hangs the impossible on the impossible, and the third and fourth faces deny the son outright

تعليق المحال بالمحال أي ليس له ولد ، إذ لو كان له ولد لكنت أول العابدين له ، فإن النبي يكون أعلم بالله وبما يصح له ومالا يصح ، وأولى بتعظيم ما يجب تعظيمه ، ومن حق تعظيم الوالد تعظيم ولده . وثالثها : أن المعنى : إن كان له ولد في زعمكم فأنا أول العابدين لله ، الموحدين له ، المنكرين لقولكم . ورابعها : أن " إن " بمعنى " ما " للنفي ، والمعنى : ما كان للرحمن ولد ، فأنا أول العابدين لله المقرين بذلك .

The second is that it belongs to hanging the impossible on the impossible, meaning He has no son, since were He to have a son I would be first of the worshippers of him. The third is that the meaning is, if he has a son in your claim, then I am first of the worshippers of God, the ones declaring His oneness, the deniers of your saying. The fourth is that the word in carries the meaning of negation, so the meaning is, the Most Merciful has no son, and I am first of the worshippers.

- Al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p259

The Ihtijaj attributes a direct gloss to Ali ﵇ himself.

Source: Al-Tabarsi (d.548 AH), al-Ihtijaj, vol.1, p382

Speaker: Ali ibn Abi Talib, on the meaning of the verse

Claim: First of the deniers, and the inner meaning stands against the outer claim

وكذلك قوله : " إن كان للرحمن ولد فإنا أول العابدين " أي : الجاحدين والتأويل في هذا القول باطنه مضاد لظاهره .

And likewise His saying, if the Most Merciful had a son then we are first of the ʿābidīn, meaning the deniers, and the interpretation in this saying is inward against the outward.

- Al-Tabarsi (d.548 AH), al-Ihtijaj, vol.1, p382

Al-Kafi ties the verse to the primordial covenant, where the Prophet ﷺ was first to enter the fire that became cool and safe.

Source: Al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, vol.2, p7

Speaker: Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇, to Muhammad ibn Ali al-Halabi

Claim: The Prophet was first to enter that fire, therefore the verse

3 - علي بن إبراهيم ، عن أبيه ، عن أحمد بن محمد بن أبي نصر ، عن أبان بن عثمان عن محمد بن علي الحلبي ، عن أبي عبد الله (﵇) قال : إن الله ﷿ لما أراد أن يخلق آدم (﵇) أرسل الماء على الطين ، ثم قبض قبضة فعركها ثم فرقها فرقتين بيده ثم ذرأهم فإذا هم يدبون ، ثم رفع لهم نارا فأمر أهل الشمال أن يدخلوها فذهبوا إليها فهابوها فلم يدخلوها ثم أمر أهل اليمين أن يدخلوها فذهبوا فدخلوها فأمر الله ﷿ النار فكانت عليهم بردا وسلاما ، فلما رأى ذلك أهل الشمال قالوا : ربنا أقلنا ، فأقالهم ، ثم قال لهم : ادخلوها فذهبوا فقاموا عليها ولم يدخلوها ، فأعادهم طينا (2) وخلق منها آدم (﵇) وقال أبو عبد الله (﵇) : فلن يستطيع هؤلاء أن يكونوا من هؤلاء ولا هؤلاء أن يكونوا من هؤلاء . قال : فيرون أن رسول الله (﵌) أول من دخل تلك النار فلذلك قوله ﷿ : " قل إن كان للرحمن ولد

Ali ibn Ibrahim, from his father, from Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Nasr, from Aban ibn Uthman, from Muhammad ibn Ali al-Halabi, from Abu Abd Allah, who said, God, when He willed to create Adam, sent water on the clay, then grasped a grasp and kneaded it, then parted it into two portions with His hand, then scattered them, and they were crawling. Then He raised a fire for them and commanded the people of the left to enter it, and they went toward it and feared it and did not enter. Then He commanded the people of the right to enter, and they went and entered, and God commanded the fire and it became coolness and peace upon them.

When the people of the left saw that they said, our Lord, retract from us, and He retracted, then He said to them, enter it, and they went and stood upon it and did not enter, so He returned them to clay and created Adam from it. And Abu Abd Allah said, these will never be able to be of those, nor those of these. He said, they consider that the Messenger of God was first of those who entered that fire, therefore His saying, say, if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be first of the ʿābidīn.

- Al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, vol.2, p7

A doxology relayed from Mufaddal frames the doctrine the verse serves.

Source: Al-Saduq (d.381 AH), Tawhid, relayed in al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p258

Speaker: Ja'far al-Sadiq ﵇, heard by al-Mufaddal

Claim: God did not beget an heir and was not begotten a partner

2 - التوحيد : ماجيلويه ، عن عمه ، عن البرقي ، عن اليقطيني ، عن سليمان بن رشيد ، عن أبيه ، عن المفضل قال : سمعت أبا عبد الله ﵇ يقول : الحمد لله الذي لم يلد فيورث ولم يولد فيشارك .

Majilawayh, from his uncle, from al-Barqi, from al-Yaqtini, from Sulayman ibn Rashid, from his father, from al-Mufaddal, who said, I heard Abu Abd Allah saying, praise to God, who did not beget, so an heir would inherit, and was not begotten, so a partner would share.

- Al-Saduq (d.381 AH), Tawhid, relayed in al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p258

3. The Sunni Canon Agrees

The convergence that decides the question comes from the Sunni exegetes themselves.

Source: Al-Baydawi (d.685 AH), Anwar al-Tanzil, vol.5, p97

Speaker: Al-Baydawi, on 43:81

Claim: The conditional denies both the son and its worship, on the strongest form of negation

منكم فإن النبي صلَّى اللَّه عليه وسلم يكون أعلم باللَّه وبما يصح له وبما لا يصح له ، وأولى بتعظيم ما يوجب تعظيمه ومن تعظيم الوالد تعظيم ولده ، ولا يلزم من ذلك صحة كينونة الولد وعبادته له إذ المحال قد يستلزم المحال

Say, if the Most Merciful had a son from among you, then the Prophet, peace be upon him, would be most knowing of God and of what befits Him, and most entitled to magnify what demands magnification. And of the right of magnifying the father is magnifying the son. It does not follow from that the reality of the son and his worship, for the impossible may entail the impossible. Rather the intent is negating them both in the strongest of ways.

- Al-Baydawi (d.685 AH), Anwar al-Tanzil, vol.5, p97

Source: Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani (d.852 AH), Fath al-Bari, vol.8, p437

Speaker: Ibn Hajar, citing the exegetes

Claim: The worshippers are the deniers of what you claimed

وقال آخرون معناه إن كان للرحمن في قولكم ولد فأنا أول العابدين أي الكافرين بذلك والجاحدين لما قلتم والعابدين من عبد بكسر الباء يعبد بفتحها قال الشاعر أولئك قومي إن هجوني هجوتهم * وأعبد أن أهجو كليبا بدارم أي أمتنع وأخرج الطبري أيضا عن يونس بن عبد الأعلى عن بن وهب عبد معناه استنكف ثم ساق قصة عن عمر في ذلك و

Others said its meaning is, if the Most Merciful has in your saying a son, then I am first of the worshippers, meaning the deniers of that and the rejecters of what you claimed. And al-ʿābidīn is from ʿabida with the broken middle, yaʿbudu with the open. The poet said, those are my people, if they satirize me I satirize them, and I disdain to satirize Kulayb with Darim, meaning I refrain.

- Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani (d.852 AH), Fath al-Bari, vol.8, p437

Tafsir of Ali ibn Ibrahim. 4. The Variants and the Verdict

The Sunni lexical tradition carries the same entry, and one transmitter records a Tamim variant of the proof verse.

Source: Al-Qurtubi (d.671 AH), al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an, vol.16, p121

Speaker: Al-Qurtubi, citing Abu Zayd

Claim: The ʿabida entry with the full Farazdaq verse and the Tamim variant

قال الفرزدق : أولئك أجلاسي فجئني بمثلهم * وأعبد أن أهجو كليبا بدارم وينشد أيضا : أولئك ناس إن هجوني هجوتهم * وأعبد أن يهجى كليب بدارم

It is said, ʿabida yaʿbudu ʿabidan with the vowelled middle, when he disdained and grew angry, so he is ʿabid, and the noun is ʿabda like anafa, on the authority of Abu Zayd. Al-Farazdaq said, those are my close kin so bring me their like, and I disdain to satirize Kulayb with Darim. And it is also recited, those are a people, if they satirize me I satirize them, and I disdain that Kulayb be satirized with Darim.

- Al-Qurtubi (d.671 AH), al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an, vol.16, p121

Source: Al-Bahrani (d.1107 AH), Ghayat al-Maram, vol.5, p259

Speaker: The narrator of the report

Claim: ʿAbida means he disdained, and Ibn Qutaybah recited the Tamim variant

قال الراوي : عبد : استنكف ، وأنشد ابن قتيبة واعبد ان تهجي تميم بدارم ، أي آنف (2) .

The narrator said, ʿabida, he disdained, and Ibn Qutaybah recited, and I disdain that Tamim be satirized with Darim, meaning I am too proud.

- Al-Bahrani (d.1107 AH), Ghayat al-Maram, vol.5, p259

Eight centuries after al-Jawhari, Sayyid Shubbar still read the word the same way, through the Qamus.

Source: Sayyid Abd Allah Shubbar (d.1242 AH), Misbah al-Anwar, vol.1, p550

Speaker: Shubbar, on the report of al-Hurr al-Amili

Claim: The worship in the report means denial, and the language scholars declared this sense, and 43:81 was read through it

الأوّل : أن يكون المراد بالعبادة في قوله : « لم يعبد الحقّ » الجحود والإنكار ، ويكون المعنى : من عرف الحقّ لم يجحده ولم ينكره ، وهذا المعنى صرّح به أهل اللغة كصاحب القاموس ، وبه فسّر قوله تعالى : « قُلْ إِنْ كانَ لِلرَّحْمنِ وَلَدٌ فَأَنَا أَوَّلُ الْعابِدِينَ » (2) ، أي الجاحدين (3) .

The first is that the intent of worship in His saying, he did not worship the truth, is denial and rejection, and the meaning is, whoever knows the truth does not deny it and does not reject it. And this sense the people of language declared, like the author of the Qamus. And by it He interpreted His saying, say, if the Most Merciful had a son, I would be first of the worshippers, meaning the deniers.

- Sayyid Abd Allah Shubbar (d.1242 AH), Misbah al-Anwar, vol.1, p550

5. The Christian Reading Compared

The Christian claim rests on the creedal line that the Son is begotten, not made. The primary holders are the Nicene Creed of 325 CE with its Constantinopolitan revision of 381 CE, and the Gospel line that God gave His only Son, John 3:16. The creed is cited here as a source, with no quotation from memory.

Source: Nicene Creed (325 CE), revised at Constantinople (381 CE)

Speaker: the councils of Nicaea and Constantinople

Claim: the Son is begotten, not made, of one essence with the Father

- Nicene Creed (325 CE), New Advent translation

Source: John 3:16, Gospel of John

Speaker: John, on the giving of the Son

Claim: God gave His only Son, that believers may have eternal life

- John 3:16, Gospel of John

Four tests separate the two readings. First, does the Arabic conditional assert possibility? The lexicons say no, and the full Farazdaq verse shows the device at work. Second, does the verse company leave the door open? The canon of negation verses closes it. Third, what does the earliest exegesis say? Al-Qummi and the Ihtijaj read denial. Fourth, do the Sunni exegetes agree? Al-Baydawi and Ibn Hajar read the verse as negation in its strongest form. The Christian reading of 43:81 asks the verse to carry a meaning its language, its company, and its exegetes all refuse.

Final Verdict

Qur’an 43:81 does not allow divine sonship. The conditional is a known Arabic device that hangs an impossibility on an impossibility. The word ʿābidīn carries the disdain sense in the lexicons with a full proof verse. The exegetes of the Ahl al-Bayt school from al-Qummi to al-Majlisi read the verse as a negation of the son claim. The Sunni exegetes al-Baydawi and Ibn Hajar agree in their own canon. The verse closes the door. It does not open one.
