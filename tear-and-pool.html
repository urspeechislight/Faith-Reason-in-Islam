A Narration from Kamil al-Ziyarat

May Allah Have Mercy on Your Tears: The Promise to Those Who Grieve for al-Ḥusayn

Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇ comforts a man of Basra who could not safely visit the grave of al-Ḥusayn ﵇ and promises him mercy for his tears and a drink from al-Kawthar. From Ibn Qulawayh (d.368 AH), Kamil al-Ziyarat, report no. 6.

Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar al-Ḥimyarī narrated the report from his father, from ʿAlī ibn Muḥammad ibn Sālim, from Muḥammad ibn Khālid, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ḥammād al-Baṣrī, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Aṣamm, from Mismaʿ ibn ʿAbd al-Malik Kirdīn al-Baṣrī.▼ commentary Mismaʿ said:

Abū ʿAbd Allāh, Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇, said to me:

"Mismaʿ, you are a man of Iraq. Do you not visit the grave of al-Ḥusayn ﵇?"▼ commentary

Mismaʿ replied:

"No. I am a man well known among the people of Basra. Among us are those who follow the inclinations of this caliph, and we have many enemies among the tribes, the Nāṣibīs and others. I do not feel safe from their reporting my situation to the descendants of Sulaymān, and then they would make an example of me."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ said:

"Do you not remember what was done to him?"▼ commentary

Mismaʿ replied:

"Yes."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ asked:

"Do you grieve over it?"

Mismaʿ replied:

"Indeed, by Allah, I weep over it until my family sees its effect upon me. I refrain from eating until its traces show upon my face."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ said:

"May Allah have mercy upon your tears. Indeed, you are counted among those who grieve intensely for us: those who rejoice at our joy, grieve at our sorrow, fear when we fear, and feel secure when we are secure.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"At the time of your death, you will surely see my forefathers present with you and instructing the Angel of Death concerning you. The good news with which they will greet you will be even greater. The Angel of Death will be gentler and more compassionate toward you than a tender mother is toward her child."▼ commentary

Mismaʿ said: then the Imam ﵇ began to weep, and I wept with him. He then said:

"Praise belongs to Allah, who has favoured us over His creation through mercy and has especially distinguished us, the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈, with mercy."

"Mismaʿ, the earth and the heaven have continued to weep out of compassion for us since the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib ﵇, was killed. Yet those among the angels who have wept for us are even greater in number. The tears of the angels have not ceased since we were killed.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"No one weeps out of compassion for us and for what we have suffered without Allah showing him mercy, even before the tear leaves his eye. When his tears flow down his cheek, if a single drop of them were to fall into Hell, it would extinguish its heat until no heat remained within it.▼ commentary

"Indeed, the person whose heart aches for us will rejoice when he sees us at the time of his death. That joy will remain in his heart until he comes to us at the Pool.▼ commentary

"Indeed, al-Kawthar will rejoice when one who loves us arrives at it. It will offer him such varieties of nourishment that he will wish for no departure from it."

"Whoever drinks one draught from it will never become thirsty afterward, nor will he ever ask for another drink. It possesses the coolness of camphor, the fragrance of musk, and the taste of ginger. It is sweeter than honey, softer than butter, clearer than a tear, and more fragrant than ambergris.▼ commentary

"It issues forth from Tasnīm and passes through the rivers of Paradise. It flows over pebbles of pearls and rubies. The drinking vessels upon it are more numerous than the stars of the sky, and its fragrance can be perceived from a distance of one thousand years' travel."

"Its drinking vessels are made of gold, silver, and every kind of precious jewel. Every delightful fragrance wafts into the face of the one drinking from it, until he says: if only I could remain here. I desire no substitute for this, and I have no wish to be moved away from it."

"Indeed, son of Kirdīn, you are among those who will drink their fill from it. No eye weeps for us without being blessed by looking upon al-Kawthar, and everyone who loves us will be given drink from it."

"The one who drinks from it will be given enjoyment, flavour, and desire for it in proportion to his love for us, beyond what will be given to someone beneath him in love for us."

"At al-Kawthar will stand the Commander of the Faithful ﵇, and in his hand will be a staff made from buckthorn, with which he will drive away our enemies.▼ commentary

"One of them will say: I testify to the two testimonies of faith! He will reply: go to the imam so-and-so whom you followed, and ask him to intercede for you. The man will say: the imam whom you mention has disavowed me. He will then say: go back behind you. Ask the one whom you befriended, followed, and preferred above all creation to intercede for you, if, as you believed, he was the best of creation. For the best of creation is one who intercedes, and it is only fitting that his intercession should be accepted. The man will say: I am perishing from thirst! He will reply: may Allah increase your thirst. May Allah increase your burning thirst."

Mismaʿ said:

"May I be made your ransom. How was he able to approach the Pool when others like him were unable to do so?"

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ replied:

"He refrained from certain shameful acts, and whenever we, the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈, were mentioned, he restrained himself from insulting us. He also abandoned certain acts that others dared to commit. However, he did not do this out of love for us or affection toward us. It was because of his intense exertion in worship, his religiosity, and because he had occupied himself with these things and left off speaking about people. As for his heart, it was hypocritical. His religion was hostility toward us, his way was that of following the Nāṣibīs, and his allegiance was to those who had passed before, preferring the two of them above everyone else..."▼ commentary

Commentary

How Each Promise Is Grounded in the Qur'an

The Source of the Narration

The report is number 6 of Kamil al-Ziyarat, a classical Shiʿi book on visiting the shrines. Ibn Qulawayh compiled it; he died in 368 AH, about 979 CE. The chain runs from Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar al-Ḥimyarī, through five more transmitters, down to Mismaʿ ibn ʿAbd al-Malik Kirdīn al-Baṣrī, a companion of Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇ from Basra.

Ibn Qulawayh wrote about two centuries after Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇, and this report reaches him through a single chain of transmission. Later scholars transmit it in full: al-Majlisi in Bihar al-Anwar, vol.44, p.292, and al-Rayshahri, quoting Kamil al-Ziyarat, in Mawsu'at Imam al-Husayn, vol.6, p.175. The text we have breaks off mid-sentence at the end.

Mismaʿ's own words tell us the danger. The descendants of Sulaymān were the branch of the Abbasid family that governed Basra. The Nāṣibīs were tribesmen who hated ʿAlī ﵇ and his line. A known Shiʿi of Basra could be reported to the governor and made an example of. Mourning al-Ḥusayn ﵇ had to be done in secret, or paid for.

A Visit Too Dangerous to Make

The Imam's ﵇ first question is about the body: the journey to the grave. Mismaʿ's answer shows a heart that loves the Imam's house and fears for its life. So the Imam ﵇ turns to what danger cannot touch, remembering and grieving. The Qur'an lays down the principle of capacity:

لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا

"Allah does not burden any soul beyond its capacity."

- Qur'an 2:286

When the journey is too dangerous, remembrance and grief remain open. The Qur'an asks love for the Prophet's ﷺ near family, and love seeks nearness:

قُل لَّآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا إِلَّا ٱلْمَوَدَّةَ فِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ

"Say: I ask of you no reward for it except love for the near relatives."

- Qur'an 42:23

The verse is disputed. Some exegetes read the near relatives as kinship in general, others as the Prophet's ﷺ own family. Either way, the verse binds love, and that love gathers around a grave beside the Euphrates.

Ziyārah, the visitation of the grave, is one way to draw near, with the feet. Grief and remembrance are another, open even to the believer the authorities watch. The Imam ﵇ blesses the grief Mismaʿ can safely offer, and he leaves aside the journey danger forbids.

Tears of Recognition

Mismaʿ's sorrow is visible. He loses his appetite; his face shows it. In the Qur'an, sincere weeping is the body overflowing what the heart has recognized:

وَإِذَا سَمِعُوا۟ مَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَى ٱلرَّسُولِ تَرَىٰٓ أَعْيُنَهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ ٱلدَّمْعِ مِمَّا عَرَفُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْحَقِّ

"When they hear what was sent down to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflow with tears because of what they have recognized of the truth."

- Qur'an 5:83

Mismaʿ recognizes what was done. Al-Ḥusayn ﵇, son of ʿAlī ﵇ and Fāṭimah ﵍ and grandson of the Prophet ﷺ, was killed at Karbala in 61 AH (680 CE), with his family and companions, by an army of the Umayyad governor of Kufa fighting in the name of the caliph Yazid, after he refused them his allegiance. The killing put truth and falsehood, faithfulness and betrayal, divine authority and worldly power, face to face. Mismaʿ's grief sees that, and answers it.

The Qur'an itself shows a prophet mourning like this. Yaʿqūb ﵇ wept for Yūsuf ﵇ until his sight failed:

وَتَوَلَّىٰ عَنْهُمْ وَقَالَ يَٰٓأَسَفَىٰ عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ وَٱبْيَضَّتْ عَيْنَاهُ مِنَ ٱلْحُزْنِ فَهُوَ كَظِيمٌۭ

"He turned away from them and said: how great is my sorrow for Yūsuf. His eyes turned white from grief, and he was choking down his sorrow."

- Qur'an 12:84

The Qur'an never calls that mourning a rebellion against the decree. Yaʿqūb's ﵇ grief comes from love, and it stays with patience, in his own words at the first loss of Yūsuf ﵇:

فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ

"So patience is beautiful."

- Qur'an 12:18

Grief and patience travel together in the Qur'an. Sacred grief holds fast to what Allah has made sacred, and the prophets ﵈ show the pattern.

The Softened Heart and the Heart of Stone

The Imam ﵇ asks mercy for the tear itself, because the tear comes from a softened heart. A softened heart is exactly what the Qur'an asks for:

أَلَمْ يَأْنِ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ أَن تَخْشَعَ قُلُوبُهُمْ لِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ وَمَا نَزَلَ مِنَ ٱلْحَقِّ

"Has the time not come for those who believe, that their hearts should become humble at the remembrance of Allah and what has descended of the truth?"

- Qur'an 57:16

The true opposite of the weeping heart is the hard heart. The Qur'an describes it after the Children of Israel received their signs:

ثُمَّ قَسَتْ قُلُوبُكُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ فَهِىَ كَٱلْحِجَارَةِ أَوْ أَشَدُّ قَسْوَةًۭ

"Then your hearts hardened after that, so they became like stones, or even harder."

- Qur'an 2:74

Weeping for al-Ḥusayn ﵇ means the most when it breaks that hardness. A man can cry and still be cruel; such tears fall short of what the Imam ﵇ blesses here. The praised tear comes from a heart that recognized the truth, loved it, and was wounded by its violation.

One Heart, One Allegiance

The Imam ﵇ counts Mismaʿ among those who rejoice at the joy of the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ and grieve at their sorrow. This is wilāyah, allegiance of love and loyalty to the Prophet's ﷺ house. The believer's feelings take sides: he rejoices when truth wins, and grieves when Allah's servants are wronged. The Qur'an names the shape of such a heart with an impossible image:

مَّا جَعَلَ ٱللَّهُ لِرَجُلٍۢ مِّن قَلْبَيْنِ فِى جَوْفِهِۦ

"Allah has not placed two hearts within any man's body."

- Qur'an 33:4

In its own setting the verse answers two pre-Islamic customs: zihār, where a husband swore off his wife as though she were his mother, and adoption, which renamed an adopted son. The school takes the image further: one body, one allegiance, now for the loyalties of the heart.

One heart cannot love the oppressed and approve their enemies at the same time. The Qur'an says it directly:

لَّا تَجِدُ قَوْمًۭا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ يُوَآدُّونَ مَنْ حَآدَّ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ

"You will not find a people who believe in Allah and the Last Day loving those who oppose Allah and His Messenger."

- Qur'an 58:22

Love builds loyalty. It shapes what the believer turns from, and it settles which side his heart lives on. The allegiance forbids imitating injustice against anyone, and it refuses to bless the deliberate destruction of truth. To rejoice and grieve with the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ is to enter their world, with a heart that holds together.

The Company at Death

Mismaʿ fears mutilation and death. The Imam ﵇ answers him by turning his eyes to the last moment. Rulers control what happens here; how a believer is received at death belongs to Allah alone. The Qur'an ties security to faith kept clean of wrongdoing:

ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَلَمْ يَلْبِسُوٓا۟ إِيمَٰنَهُم بِظُلْمٍ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمُ ٱلْأَمْنُ وَهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ

"Those who believe and do not clothe their faith with wrongdoing: for them is security, and they are rightly guided."

- Qur'an 6:82

What the Imam ﵇ promises Mismaʿ follows the verse about Allah's steadfast servants:

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَقَٰمُوا۟ تَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ أَلَّا تَخَافُوا۟ وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا۟ وَأَبْشِرُوا۟ بِٱلْجَنَّةِ ٱلَّتِى كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ

"Indeed, those who say: our Lord is Allah, and then remain steadfast: the angels descend upon them: do not fear and do not grieve, but receive the good news of the Garden which you were promised."

- Qur'an 41:30

Fear gives way to reassurance, loneliness to presence, death to good news. Those Mismaʿ grieved for in life will comfort him at his death, and the Angel of Death will treat him more gently than a tender mother treats her child. Souls meet death differently; for the loyal believer it is a passage into company. His fear and his security have moved from himself to Allah: he fears losing his faith more than losing his life, and he finds safety in nearness to Allah, whatever the powerful intend.

Heaven and Earth Bear Witness

Heaven and earth weep for the House. The Qur'an sets the contrast, about the people of Pharaoh, drowned and abandoned:

فَمَا بَكَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلسَّمَآءُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُنظَرِينَ

"So neither heaven nor earth wept over them, nor were they granted respite."

- Qur'an 44:29

Some exegetes hear a plain idiom: nobody mourned Pharaoh's people, and the verse says only that. The school hears a measured denial: some dead are mourned by heaven and earth and some are not; Pharaoh's people earned silence, and the House of the Prophet ﷺ stands among the mourned. Heaven and earth withheld their sorrow from Pharaoh's people. The tyrant thinks the death of a righteous man is a political event he can erase. Creation answers him: the mourning of earth and heaven says that worldly victory and divine worth are two different measures.

So Karbala cannot be measured on the visible field alone. The body was killed, and the crime went through the whole moral structure of creation.

Why Angels May Weep

The weeping angels outnumber even the weeping heavens. The Qur'an calls the angels servants whose obedience is complete:

لَّا يَعْصُونَ ٱللَّهَ مَآ أَمَرَهُمْ وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ

"They do not disobey Allah in what He commands them, and they do what they are commanded."

- Qur'an 66:6

Scripture never says that angels weep; the Imam's ﵇ words say it. What the verse gives is narrower, and enough: the angels never disobey Allah in what He commands them, so if they weep, the weeping falls inside their service. Their tears do not dispute the decree. They carry no passion out of control, no ignorance of the divine wisdom. Their grief is full recognition of how great the crime was, and their mourning agrees with Allah's judgment about what is sacred and about what violated it.

Why One Tear Can Cool the Fire

Mercy before the tear is visible

Allah shows the mourner mercy before the tear even leaves his eye. He already knows the heart it came from:

يَعْلَمُ خَآئِنَةَ ٱلْأَعْيُنِ وَمَا تُخْفِى ٱلصُّدُورُ

"He knows the treachery of the eyes and what the breasts conceal."

- Qur'an 40:19

The act is settled with Allah before anyone can see it, praise it, or reward it. The tear is free of the need to be seen: its value is fixed in secret, beyond the reach of reputation.

Mercy that already encompasses creation

The Qur'an sets Allah's mercy before the mourner and far wider than his act:

وَرَحْمَتِى وَسِعَتْ كُلَّ شَىْءٍۢ

"And My mercy encompasses all things."

- Qur'an 7:156

كَتَبَ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ ٱلرَّحْمَةَ

"He has prescribed mercy for Himself."

- Qur'an 6:12

قُلْ يَٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا

"Say: My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of Allah's mercy. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins."

- Qur'an 39:53

Allah grants this mercy freely, and the tear buys nothing from Him. The tear shows a heart that opened itself to a mercy already wrapping the whole creation.

The drop and the fire

One drop would put out the heat of Hell. The image declares the power of divine mercy. Hell is the end of separation from Allah, of rebellion hardened and injustice left unrepented. The praised tear comes from the opposite condition: tenderness, recognition, loyalty, compassion for Allah's oppressed servants. It carries the sign of mercy into the place of wrath. A tear is small; what it carries can be immense.

What the tear must produce

The promise joins hope to turning back:

إِلَّا مَن تَابَ وَءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ عَمَلًۭا صَٰلِحًۭا فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ يُبَدِّلُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمْ حَسَنَٰتٍۢ

"Except those who repent, believe, and act righteously: for them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good deeds."

- Qur'an 25:70

A tear that truly comes from loyalty to al-Ḥusayn ﵇ moves the weeper away from the conduct of the men who killed him. If cruelty, dishonesty, and injustice in the mourner survive untouched, the tear's claim on that loyalty is still incomplete.

From Grief to the Pool of al-Kawthar

The grief the Imam ﵇ blesses is for a season; its fruit lasts. The bond grief forms opens fully at death. The Qur'an promises the friends of Allah freedom from fear and from grief:

أَلَآ إِنَّ أَوْلِيَآءَ ٱللَّهِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

"Indeed, the friends of Allah: no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve."

- Qur'an 10:62

Whoever accepted grief for truth in this world is promised freedom from grief in the next. The heart's pain purifies, because it refuses peace with oppression. From the tear the promise turns to water, and the water has its own verse:

إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْكَوْثَرَ

"Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar."

- Qur'an 108:1

The mourner gives a drop born of love. Allah receives it with mercy. The mourner is received at the river of abundance, and the thirst of grief is exchanged for a drink after which there is no thirst. Al-Kawthar is nearness to the Prophet ﷺ and his family made visible, and the drink is poured in the measure of the love that sought it.

Even the grading has its verse:

وَلِكُلٍّۢ دَرَجَٰتٌۭ مِّمَّا عَمِلُوا۟ ۚ وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِغَٰفِلٍ عَمَّا يَعْمَلُونَ

"For each are degrees from what they did, and your Lord is fully aware of what they do."

- Qur'an 6:132

The Qur'an grades these degrees by what people did; the Imam ﵇ grades his drink by love. In both, the reward is measured, and love draws the deeper draught.

The Drinks of Paradise

Cooler than camphor, fragrant with musk, tasting of ginger, sweeter than honey, clearer than a tear. The Qur'an's drinks of Paradise answer each detail:

إِنَّ ٱلْأَبْرَارَ يَشْرَبُونَ مِن كَأْسٍۢ كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا كَافُورًا

"Indeed, the righteous will drink from a cup whose mixture is camphor."

- Qur'an 76:5

وَيُسْقَوْنَ فِيهَا كَأْسًۭا كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا زَنجَبِيلًا

"And they will be given there a cup whose mixture is ginger."

- Qur'an 76:17

Even the vessels, gold and silver and every precious jewel, appear in the Qur'an's picture of Paradise:

وَيُطَافُ عَلَيْهِم بِـَٔانِيَةٍۢ مِّن فِضَّةٍۢ وَأَكْوَابٍۢ كَانَتْ قَوَارِيرَا۠ قَوَارِيرَا۟ مِن فِضَّةٍۢ قَدَّرُوهَا تَقْدِيرًۭا

"And circulated among them are vessels of silver and cups that were crystal, crystal of silver, which they measure out in measure."

- Qur'an 76:15-16

وَسَقَىٰهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ شَرَابًۭا طَهُورًا

"And their Lord will give them a purifying drink."

- Qur'an 76:21

And the Pool has a source with a Qur'anic name, Tasnīm, the spring of the righteous in the next life:

وَمِزَاجُهُۥ مِن تَسْنِيمٍ

"And its mixture is of Tasnīm."

- Qur'an 83:27

The musk, too, appears in the Qur'an, in its picture of that same drink:

خِتَٰمُهُۥ مِسْكٌۭ ۚ وَفِى ذَٰلِكَ فَلْيَتَنَافَسِ ٱلْمُتَنَٰفِسُونَ

"Its seal is musk; for that, let the competitors compete."

- Qur'an 83:26

The Pool draws from a spring the Qur'an itself names. The Qur'an calls the drink purifying; the Imam's ﵇ words go further: clearer than a tear. Together they fit the Pool for single-hearted love. The verse of the two hearts (Qur'an 33:4) becomes consolation here: mixed allegiance divides the soul, and al-Kawthar is the home of the undivided heart.

The Testimony That Stayed on the Tongue

At al-Kawthar stands the Commander of the Faithful ﵇, a staff of buckthorn in his hand, driving the enemies of the House away. One of them pleads his faith; he is sent to the leaders he followed, and they disown him. He begs for intercession, and it fails. The last word to him is a prayer that his thirst increase. The Sunni collections tell a story in much the same shape: in the Basin (ḥawḍ) narrations of Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, people are driven back from the Prophet's ﷺ Basin on the Day of Rising, because what they did after him contradicted the confession they brought to the water. A Prophet at the water, turning away those whose lives emptied their words: this is shared ground between the two traditions.

A man walks up to the Pool and says the two testimonies of faith, and the water is refused him. The Qur'an already warns that the tongue can say what the heart does not. Of the hypocrites who greeted the Prophet ﷺ with a correct confession, it says:

إِذَا جَآءَكَ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ قَالُوا۟ نَشْهَدُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُهُۥ وَٱللَّهُ يَشْهَدُ إِنَّ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ لَكَٰذِبُونَ

"When the hypocrites come to you, they say: we bear witness that you are surely the Messenger of Allah. Allah knows that you are His Messenger, and Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are surely liars."

- Qur'an 63:1

Their sentence was factually correct, and they were called liars, because their hearts refused to back what their tongues said. The Qur'an names the condition:

وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ وَبِٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ

"Among the people are those who say: we believe in Allah and the Last Day, while they are not believers."

- Qur'an 2:8

The two testimonies are true words. The danger is emptying them of what they require. To testify to the Messenger is to be faithful to his message, to the bonds he appointed, and to the trusts he left.

Restraint Without Love

The Imam's ﵇ explanation separates restraint from allegiance. The man avoided shameful acts. He held his tongue when the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ were mentioned. He left off what others dared to do. His discipline came from long worship and religiosity, and from keeping busy with devotion instead of attacking people. None of it came from love. His conduct carried him near the Pool; the direction of his heart kept him from drinking. The Qur'an joins every act of worship to sincerity:

وَمَآ أُمِرُوٓا۟ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ ٱلدِّينَ حُنَفَآءَ

"They were commanded only to worship Allah, devoting religion sincerely to Him."

- Qur'an 98:5

The verse of the two hearts (Qur'an 33:4) applies here at its sharpest. Restraint is good, and it cannot stand in for inward truth. Worship does not erase hostility toward those Allah commands the believer to love.

The two of them are the first two caliphs after the Prophet ﷺ. The Imam's ﵇ verdict names three things: a heart he calls hypocritical, a religion of hostility to the House, and an allegiance set above the love offered to the man. The three stand or fall together, and the criterion is what the heart loves, and whether the tongue's confession matches it. A reader whose allegiance differs from Mismaʿ's meets the same criterion at the Pool, and the examination runs inward first, to the heart, before anyone is turned from the water.

The two men close the report. Mismaʿ, kept from the journey by danger, weeps with a sincere heart and receives the promise of al-Kawthar. The other man, outwardly religious, reaches the Pool itself, and his divided, hostile heart keeps him from drinking. Here the text we have breaks off, mid-sentence.

The Education of the Heart

The education of the heart is the whole of it. The tear matters because it shows love. Love matters because it creates allegiance. Allegiance matters because it shapes action and fixes spiritual companionship.

Its message moves through three verses of the Qur'an. First, the heart softens before recognized truth: the eyes overflow with tears because of what they have recognized (Qur'an 5:83). Second, the heart cannot hold two ultimate loyalties at once: Allah has not placed two hearts within any man (Qur'an 33:4). Third, the purified heart is carried from its tear to the water of abundance: We have given you al-Kawthar (Qur'an 108:1).

A single drop, the Imam ﵇ promises, could put out the heat of the Fire, because it comes from a heart where mercy, truth, and loyalty have won over hardness. Its fruit is a life set against what produced Karbala. Its end is the water of the Pool, with the very ones for whom it was shed.
