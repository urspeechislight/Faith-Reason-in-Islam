A Narration from Kamil al-Ziyarat

The Tear and the Pool: The Hadith of Mismaʿ on Weeping for al-Ḥusayn

Ibn Qulawayh (d.368 AH), Kamil al-Ziyarat, no. 6: Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇ and a man of Basra who could not safely visit the grave of al-Ḥusayn ﵇, with a Qur'anic commentary attached to the promise

Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar al-Ḥimyarī narrated the report from his father, from ʿAlī ibn Muḥammad ibn Sālim, from Muḥammad ibn Khālid, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ḥammād al-Baṣrī, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Aṣamm, from Mismaʿ ibn ʿAbd al-Malik Kirdīn al-Baṣrī.▼ commentary Mismaʿ said:

Abū ʿAbd Allāh, Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇, said to me:

"Mismaʿ, you are a man of Iraq. Do you not visit the grave of al-Ḥusayn ﵇?"▼ commentary

Mismaʿ replied:

"No. I am a man well known among the people of Basra. Among us are those who follow the inclinations of this caliph, and we have many enemies among the tribes, the Nāṣibīs and others. I do not feel safe from their reporting my situation to the descendants of Sulaymān, and then they would make an example of me."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ said:

"Do you not remember what was done to him?"▼ commentary

Mismaʿ replied:

"Yes."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ asked:

"Do you grieve over it?"

Mismaʿ replied:

"Indeed, by Allah, I weep over it until my family sees its effect upon me. I refrain from eating until its traces show upon my face."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ said:

"May Allah have mercy upon your tears. Indeed, you are counted among those who grieve intensely for us: those who rejoice at our joy, grieve at our sorrow, fear when we fear, and feel secure when we are secure.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"At the time of your death, you will surely see my forefathers present with you and instructing the Angel of Death concerning you. The good news with which they will greet you will be even greater. The Angel of Death will be gentler and more compassionate toward you than a tender mother is toward her child."▼ commentary

Mismaʿ said: then the Imam ﵇ began to weep, and I wept with him. He then said:

"Praise belongs to Allah, who has favoured us over His creation through mercy and has especially distinguished us, the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈, with mercy."

"Mismaʿ, the earth and the heaven have continued to weep out of compassion for us since the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib ﵇, was killed. Yet those among the angels who have wept for us are even greater in number. The tears of the angels have not ceased since we were killed.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"No one weeps out of compassion for us and for what we have suffered without Allah showing him mercy, even before the tear leaves his eye. When his tears flow down his cheek, if a single drop of them were to fall into Hell, it would extinguish its heat until no heat remained within it.▼ commentary

"Indeed, the person whose heart aches for us will rejoice when he sees us at the time of his death. That joy will remain in his heart until he comes to us at the Pool.▼ commentary

"Indeed, al-Kawthar will rejoice when one who loves us arrives at it. It will offer him such varieties of nourishment that he will wish for no departure from it."

"Whoever drinks one draught from it will never become thirsty afterward, nor will he ever ask for another drink. It possesses the coolness of camphor, the fragrance of musk, and the taste of ginger. It is sweeter than honey, softer than butter, clearer than a tear, and more fragrant than ambergris.▼ commentary

"It issues forth from Tasnīm and passes through the rivers of Paradise. It flows over pebbles of pearls and rubies. The drinking vessels upon it are more numerous than the stars of the sky, and its fragrance can be perceived from a distance of one thousand years' travel."

"Its drinking vessels are made of gold, silver, and every kind of precious jewel. Every delightful fragrance wafts into the face of the one drinking from it, until he says: if only I could remain here. I desire no substitute for this, and I have no wish to be moved away from it."

"Indeed, son of Kirdīn, you are among those who will drink their fill from it. No eye weeps for us without being blessed by looking upon al-Kawthar, and everyone who loves us will be given drink from it."

"The one who drinks from it will be given enjoyment, flavour, and desire for it in proportion to his love for us, beyond what will be given to someone beneath him in love for us."

"At al-Kawthar will stand the Commander of the Faithful ﵇, and in his hand will be a staff made from buckthorn, with which he will drive away our enemies.▼ commentary

"One of them will say: I testify to the two testimonies of faith! He will reply: go to the imam so-and-so whom you followed, and ask him to intercede for you. The man will say: the imam whom you mention has disavowed me. He will then say: go back behind you. Ask the one whom you befriended, followed, and preferred above all creation to intercede for you, if, as you believed, he was the best of creation. For the best of creation is one who intercedes, and it is only fitting that his intercession should be accepted. The man will say: I am perishing from thirst! He will reply: may Allah increase your thirst. May Allah increase your burning thirst."

Mismaʿ said:

"May I be made your ransom. How was he able to approach the Pool when others like him were unable to do so?"

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ replied:

"He refrained from certain shameful acts, and whenever we, the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈, were mentioned, he restrained himself from insulting us. He also abandoned certain acts that others dared to commit. However, he did not do this out of love for us or affection toward us. It was because of his intense exertion in worship, his religiosity, and because he had occupied himself with these things and left off speaking about people. As for his heart, it was hypocritical. His religion was hostility toward us, his way was that of following the Nāṣibīs, and his allegiance was to those who had passed before, preferring the two of them above everyone else..."▼ commentary

Commentary

The Qur'anic grounding (13 cards)

The Source of the Narration

The report is no. 6 of Kamil al-Ziyarat by Ibn Qulawayh (Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad ibn Qulawayh al-Qummī, d.368 AH), the classical Shiʿi collection on visiting the shrines. Its chain runs through six transmitters from Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar al-Ḥimyarī down to Mismaʿ ibn ʿAbd al-Malik Kirdīn al-Baṣrī, a companion of Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇ from Basra.

The political setting is preserved inside Mismaʿ's own words. The "descendants of Sulaymān" are the house of Sulaymān ibn ʿAlī ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-ʿAbbās, the branch of the Abbasid family that held Basra, and the Nāṣibīs he names were hostile neighbours within the tribes. For a known Shiʿi of Basra to be reported to the governor and made an example of was a live danger. The narration records mourning under surveillance, and the Imam's ﵇ answer meets Mismaʿ exactly there.

The report breaks off mid-sentence at its close, and the page preserves the break. The Qur'anic verses in the cards below are quoted verbatim from the Uthmani text; translations are the author's.

A Visit He Could Not Safely Make

The Imam's ﵇ first question concerns the body: the journey to the grave. Mismaʿ's answer discloses a heart attached to the Imam's house, held back by fear for his life. So the Imam ﵇ turns from the outward act, which danger forbids, to what remains open to a persecuted believer: remembrance and grief. The Qur'an grounds that distinction in the principle of capacity:

لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا

"Allah does not burden any soul beyond its capacity."

- Qur'an 2:286

When bodily visitation is prevented by genuine danger, remembrance, grief, and inward loyalty remain within a soul's capacity. Yet the question also refuses to let remembrance decay into empty history. The Qur'an commands love for the Prophet's ﷺ near family, and love seeks nearness:

قُل لَّآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا إِلَّا ٱلْمَوَدَّةَ فِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ

"Say: I ask of you no reward for it except love for the near relatives."

- Qur'an 42:23

Ziyārah is one bodily expression of that nearness. Grief and remembrance are expressions of the same love, available even to the believer whom the authorities watch. The Imam ﵇ accepts Mismaʿ's tears as the visitation he was denied.

Tears That Come From Recognition

Mismaʿ's sorrow is physical and visible: he loses his appetite, and his face shows it. The Qur'an repeatedly presents sincere weeping as the overflow of an inward recognition:

وَإِذَا سَمِعُوا۟ مَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَى ٱلرَّسُولِ تَرَىٰٓ أَعْيُنَهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ ٱلدَّمْعِ مِمَّا عَرَفُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْحَقِّ

"When they hear what was sent down to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflow with tears because of what they have recognized of the truth."

- Qur'an 5:83

The value of these tears lies in what produced them: recognition of truth. Mismaʿ weeps over a concrete event, and what the event disclosed: truth and falsehood, faithfulness and betrayal, divine authority confronted by worldly domination. His grief is an act of moral recognition.

The Qur'an itself sets prophetic precedent for mourning of this intensity. Yaʿqūb ﵇ grieved for Yūsuf ﵇ until his sight failed:

وَتَوَلَّىٰ عَنْهُمْ وَقَالَ يَٰٓأَسَفَىٰ عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ وَٱبْيَضَّتْ عَيْنَاهُ مِنَ ٱلْحُزْنِ فَهُوَ كَظِيمٌۭ

"He turned away from them and said: how great is my sorrow for Yūsuf. His eyes turned white from grief, and he was choking down his sorrow."

- Qur'an 12:84

The Qur'an never condemns this mourning as rebellion against the divine decree. Yaʿqūb's ﵇ grief proceeds from love and remains joined to patience, in his own words at the first loss of Yūsuf ﵇:

فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ

"So patience is beautiful."

- Qur'an 12:18

Grief and patience travel together in the Qur'an. Sacred grief is fidelity to what Allah has made sacred, and the mourning of the prophets ﵈ is its pattern.

The Softened Heart and the Heart of Stone

The Imam ﵇ invokes mercy upon the tear itself, because the tear represents the softened heart from which it came. The Qur'an asks exactly for that softening:

أَلَمْ يَأْنِ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ أَن تَخْشَعَ قُلُوبُهُمْ لِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ وَمَا نَزَلَ مِنَ ٱلْحَقِّ

"Has the time not come for those who believe, that their hearts should become humble at the remembrance of Allah and what has descended of the truth?"

- Qur'an 57:16

The true opposite of the weeping heart is the hardened heart, which the Qur'an describes after the Children of Israel received their signs:

ثُمَّ قَسَتْ قُلُوبُكُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ فَهِىَ كَٱلْحِجَارَةِ أَوْ أَشَدُّ قَسْوَةًۭ

"Then your hearts hardened after that, so they became like stones, or even harder."

- Qur'an 2:74

Weeping for al-Ḥusayn ﵇ carries its full meaning when it breaks hardness, awakens moral recognition, and makes the believer more responsive to Allah. A person can shed tears while persisting in injustice, and such weeping falls short of the state the Imam ﵇ blesses here. The praised tear is the fruit of recognition, of love, and of a heart wounded by the violation of divine truth.

One Heart, One Allegiance

The Imam's ﵇ description of those who rejoice at the joy of the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ and grieve at their sorrow presents wilāyah as a living alignment of the heart. The believer's emotional world acquires moral direction: he learns to rejoice when truth triumphs and to grieve when Allah's chosen servants are oppressed. The Qur'an fixes the structure of such a heart with a physical impossibility:

مَّا جَعَلَ ٱللَّهُ لِرَجُلٍۢ مِّن قَلْبَيْنِ فِى جَوْفِهِۦ

"Allah has not placed two hearts within any man's body."

- Qur'an 33:4

A single heart cannot sincerely love the oppressed representatives of truth while inwardly approving their enemies. The Qur'an states the exclusion directly:

لَّا تَجِدُ قَوْمًۭا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ يُوَآدُّونَ مَنْ حَآدَّ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ

"You will not find a people who believe in Allah and the Last Day loving those who oppose Allah and His Messenger."

- Qur'an 58:22

Love carries moral consequences: it establishes loyalty, shapes aversion, and decides which side of a conflict the heart inhabits. Two boundaries keep the meaning honest. This allegiance licenses no imitation of injustice against anyone, and it excludes approval of truth's deliberate destruction. To rejoice and to grieve with the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ means the heart has entered their moral world, and its loyalties have become coherent.

Security and the Company at Death

Mismaʿ fears mutilation and death at the hands of the authorities. The Imam ﵇ answers that fear by directing his sight to the final moment. Rulers control outward circumstances; the reception of the believer at death belongs to another jurisdiction. The Qur'an ties security to faith left unclothed by wrongdoing:

ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَلَمْ يَلْبِسُوٓا۟ إِيمَٰنَهُم بِظُلْمٍ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمُ ٱلْأَمْنُ وَهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ

"Those who believe and do not clothe their faith with wrongdoing: for them is security, and they are rightly guided."

- Qur'an 6:82

The promise the Imam ﵇ gives Mismaʿ follows the pattern of the verse revealed about the steadfast servants of Allah:

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَقَٰمُوا۟ تَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ أَلَّا تَخَافُوا۟ وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا۟ وَأَبْشِرُوا۟ بِٱلْجَنَّةِ ٱلَّتِى كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ

"Indeed, those who say: our Lord is Allah, and then remain steadfast: the angels descend upon them: do not fear and do not grieve, but receive the good news of the Garden which you were promised."

- Qur'an 41:30

Fear is exchanged for reassurance, loneliness for presence, and dying for good news. Those for whom Mismaʿ grieved in life become his comfort at death, and the Angel of Death deals with him more gently than a tender mother with her child. Death is met differently by each soul; for the loyal believer it becomes a passage into company. The believer's fear and security have left the orbit of the isolated ego: he fears the loss of faith above worldly loss, and finds security in nearness to Allah, whatever those in power intend.

Heaven and Earth Bear Witness

The Imam's ﵇ claim that heaven and earth weep for the House rests on a Qur'anic contrast. About the people of Pharaoh, drowned and abandoned, the Qur'an states:

فَمَا بَكَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلسَّمَآءُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُنظَرِينَ

"So neither heaven nor earth wept over them, nor were they granted respite."

- Qur'an 44:29

The denial implies its contrast: there are servants of Allah whose loss matters to the created order. Heaven granted the power of Pharaoh's people no weight, so heaven and earth withheld their sorrow. The tyrant imagines the death of the righteous as a political event that can be erased from the record. The narration answers with creation itself bearing witness: the mourning of earth and heaven declares that worldly victory and divine worth are two different measures.

Karbala therefore resists measurement by the visible battlefield alone. The body was killed, and the crime resonated through the moral structure of creation.

The Angels Who Weep Without Disobedience

The narration counts the weeping angels as still more numerous than the weeping heavens. The Qur'an describes the angels as servants whose obedience is complete:

لَّا يَعْصُونَ ٱللَّهَ مَآ أَمَرَهُمْ وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ

"They do not disobey Allah in what He commands them, and they do what they are commanded."

- Qur'an 66:6

Their tears therefore cannot be read as objection to the decree of Allah, and they are free of uncontrolled passion and ignorance of the divine wisdom. Their grief is perfect recognition of the enormity of the crime. Submission to Allah's decree coexists with intense feeling toward injustice, because angelic mourning agrees with His judgment about what is sacred and about what violated it.

Mercy Before the Tear, and a Drop Against the Fire

Mercy before the tear is visible

Allah shows the mourner mercy before the tear leaves his eye, because the heart that produced the tear is already fully known:

يَعْلَمُ خَآئِنَةَ ٱلْأَعْيُنِ وَمَا تُخْفِى ٱلصُّدُورُ

"He knows the treachery of the eyes and what the breasts conceal."

- Qur'an 40:19

The sincerity of the act is settled before any human being can see it, praise it, or reward it. This removes the tear from performance: its value is fixed in secret, beyond the reach of reputation.

Mercy that already encompasses creation

The Qur'an presents divine mercy as prior to the mourner and vaster than his act:

وَرَحْمَتِى وَسِعَتْ كُلَّ شَىْءٍۢ

"And My mercy encompasses all things."

- Qur'an 7:156

كَتَبَ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ ٱلرَّحْمَةَ

"He has prescribed mercy for Himself."

- Qur'an 6:12

قُلْ يَٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا

"Say: My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of Allah's mercy. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins."

- Qur'an 39:53

The tear obligates nothing in Allah, as though He were indebted to the mourner. It reveals a heart that has opened itself to a mercy that already encompasses creation.

The drop and the fire

The image of a single drop extinguishing the heat of Hell should be read first as a declaration of the overwhelming power of divine mercy. Hell is the consequence of separation from Allah, of hardened rebellion and unrepented injustice. The tear praised here arises from the opposite condition: tenderness, recognition, loyalty, and compassion for the oppressed servants of Allah. It carries the sign of mercy into the domain of wrath. A tear is materially small, and what it discloses can be immense.

What the tear must produce

The promise grants no permission to persist in oppression and rely upon a tear. The Qur'an joins hope to moral return:

إِلَّا مَن تَابَ وَءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ عَمَلًۭا صَٰلِحًۭا فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ يُبَدِّلُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمْ حَسَنَٰتٍۢ

"Except those who repent, believe, and act righteously: for them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good deeds."

- Qur'an 25:70

A tear that truly arises from loyalty to al-Ḥusayn ﵇ moves its bearer away from the conduct of those who opposed him. If it leaves cruelty, dishonesty, and injustice within the mourner untouched, its claim to embody that loyalty remains incomplete.

The Heart That Aches, and the Pool

The grief the Imam ﵇ blesses is temporary, and its fruit is lasting joy. Mourning turns into anticipation: the bond formed in grief is disclosed completely at death. The Qur'an promises the friends of Allah freedom from both fear and grief:

أَلَآ إِنَّ أَوْلِيَآءَ ٱللَّهِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

"Indeed, the friends of Allah: no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve."

- Qur'an 10:62

The one who accepted grief for the sake of truth in this world is promised freedom from ultimate grief in the next. The heart's pain purifies, because it refuses to make peace with oppression. Then the narration moves from tears to water, and the movement has its own verse:

إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْكَوْثَرَ

"Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar."

- Qur'an 108:1

The correspondence is exact. The mourner offers a drop that arises from love; Allah receives it with mercy; the mourner is received at the river of abundance; and the temporary thirst of grief is exchanged for a drink after which there is no thirst. Al-Kawthar is the final manifestation of nearness to the Prophet ﷺ and his family, and the drink is poured in proportion to the love that sought it.

The Drinks of Paradise

The Imam's ﵇ description of al-Kawthar, cooler than camphor, fragrant with musk, flavoured with ginger, sweeter than honey, clearer than a tear, echoes the Qur'an's own portrayal of the drinks of Paradise:

إِنَّ ٱلْأَبْرَارَ يَشْرَبُونَ مِن كَأْسٍۢ كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا كَافُورًا

"Indeed, the righteous will drink from a cup whose mixture is camphor."

- Qur'an 76:5

وَيُسْقَوْنَ فِيهَا كَأْسًۭا كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا زَنجَبِيلًا

"And they will be given there a cup whose mixture is ginger."

- Qur'an 76:17

وَسَقَىٰهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ شَرَابًۭا طَهُورًا

"And their Lord will give them a purifying drink."

- Qur'an 76:21

The repeated language of purity carries the point. The Pool suits purified love: as its water holds no impurity, the love that reaches it must be refined of duplicity. This is the verse of the two hearts (Qur'an 33:4) turned to consolation: mixed allegiance divides the soul, and al-Kawthar is the destination of single-hearted attachment.

The Testimony That Did Not Reach the Heart

A man approaches the Pool and protests that he pronounces the two testimonies of faith, and he is refused its water. The Qur'an already warns that verbal testimony proves nothing about the heart. Of the hypocrites who greeted the Prophet ﷺ with a correct confession, it says:

إِذَا جَآءَكَ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ قَالُوا۟ نَشْهَدُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُهُۥ وَٱللَّهُ يَشْهَدُ إِنَّ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ لَكَٰذِبُونَ

"When the hypocrites come to you, they say: we bear witness that you are surely the Messenger of Allah. Allah knows that you are His Messenger, and Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are surely liars."

- Qur'an 63:1

Their spoken sentence was factually correct, and they were called liars, because their hearts refused to testify to what their tongues pronounced. The Qur'an names the condition directly:

وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ وَبِٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ

"Among the people are those who say: we believe in Allah and the Last Day, while they are not believers."

- Qur'an 2:8

The narration leaves the two testimonies standing, and it warns against emptying them of their moral consequences. Testimony to the Messenger entails fidelity to his message, to his divinely appointed bonds, and to the sacred trusts connected to him.

Restraint Without Love

The Imam's ﵇ explanation of the man at the Pool distinguishes restraint from allegiance. The man avoided shameful acts, held his tongue when the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ were mentioned, and abandoned what others dared to commit. His discipline arose from his exertion in worship and his religiosity, and from preoccupation with devotion that left him little time for attacking anyone. None of it arose from love or recognition. His conduct brought him near the Pool; the direction of his heart kept him from drinking. The Qur'an joins every act of worship to sincerity of devotion:

وَمَآ أُمِرُوٓا۟ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ ٱلدِّينَ حُنَفَآءَ

"They were commanded only to worship Allah, devoting religion sincerely to Him."

- Qur'an 98:5

This is the sharpest application of the verse of the two hearts (Qur'an 33:4). External restraint is good, and it cannot substitute for inward truth, and acts of worship do not erase deliberate hostility toward those whom Allah commands the believer to love. The narration closes on the two men as mirrors of each other: Mismaʿ, unable to perform the outward visitation, with a sincere heart, receives the promise of al-Kawthar; the other man, with outward religiosity, reaches the vicinity of the Pool, and his divided and hostile heart turns him away. At this point the source breaks off, mid-sentence, in the middle of the Imam's ﵇ description of the man's allegiance.

The Education of the Heart

The narration is, at its centre, about the education of the heart. The tear matters because it discloses love. Love matters because it creates allegiance. Allegiance matters because it shapes action and fixes spiritual companionship.

Its message moves through three Qur'anic stations. First, the heart softens before recognized truth: the eyes overflow with tears because of what they have recognized (Qur'an 5:83). Second, the heart cannot hold two ultimate loyalties at once: Allah has not placed two hearts within any man (Qur'an 33:4). Third, the purified heart is carried from its tear to the water of abundance: We have given you al-Kawthar (Qur'an 108:1).

The single tear extinguishes fire because it represents a heart in which mercy, truth, and loyalty have overcome hardness. Its proper fruit is a life set against the qualities that produced Karbala, and its end is the water of the Pool, in the company of those for whom it was shed.
