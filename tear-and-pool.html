A Narration from Kamil al-Ziyarat

May Allah Have Mercy on Your Tears: The Promise to Those Who Grieve for al-Ḥusayn

Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇ comforts a man of Basra who could not safely visit the grave of al-Ḥusayn ﵇ and promises him mercy for his tears and a drink from al-Kawthar. From Ibn Qulawayh (d.368 AH), Kamil al-Ziyarat, report no. 6.

Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar al-Ḥimyarī narrated the report from his father, from ʿAlī ibn Muḥammad ibn Sālim, from Muḥammad ibn Khālid, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ḥammād al-Baṣrī, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Aṣamm, from Mismaʿ ibn ʿAbd al-Malik Kirdīn al-Baṣrī.▼ commentary Mismaʿ said:

Abū ʿAbd Allāh, Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇, said to me:

"Mismaʿ, you are a man of Iraq. Do you not visit the grave of al-Ḥusayn ﵇?"▼ commentary

Mismaʿ replied:

"No. I am a man well known among the people of Basra. Among us are those who follow the inclinations of this caliph, and we have many enemies among the tribes, the Nāṣibīs and others. I do not feel safe from their reporting my situation to the descendants of Sulaymān, and then they would make an example of me."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ said:

"Do you not remember what was done to him?"▼ commentary

Mismaʿ replied:

"Yes."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ asked:

"Do you grieve over it?"

Mismaʿ replied:

"Indeed, by Allah, I weep over it until my family sees its effect upon me. I refrain from eating until its traces show upon my face."

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ said:

"May Allah have mercy upon your tears. Indeed, you are counted among those who grieve intensely for us: those who rejoice at our joy, grieve at our sorrow, fear when we fear, and feel secure when we are secure.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"At the time of your death, you will surely see my forefathers present with you and instructing the Angel of Death concerning you. The good news with which they will greet you will be even greater. The Angel of Death will be gentler and more compassionate toward you than a tender mother is toward her child."▼ commentary

Mismaʿ said: then the Imam ﵇ began to weep, and I wept with him. He then said:

"Praise belongs to Allah, who has favoured us over His creation through mercy and has especially distinguished us, the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈, with mercy."

"Mismaʿ, the earth and the heaven have continued to weep out of compassion for us since the Commander of the Faithful, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib ﵇, was killed. Yet those among the angels who have wept for us are even greater in number. The tears of the angels have not ceased since we were killed.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"No one weeps out of compassion for us and for what we have suffered without Allah showing him mercy, even before the tear leaves his eye. When his tears flow down his cheek, if a single drop of them were to fall into Hell, it would extinguish its heat until no heat remained within it.▼ commentary

"Indeed, the person whose heart aches for us will rejoice when he sees us at the time of his death. That joy will remain in his heart until he comes to us at the Pool.▼ commentary

"Indeed, al-Kawthar will rejoice when one who loves us arrives at it. It will offer him such varieties of nourishment that he will wish for no departure from it."

"Whoever drinks one draught from it will never become thirsty afterward, nor will he ever ask for another drink. It possesses the coolness of camphor, the fragrance of musk, and the taste of ginger. It is sweeter than honey, softer than butter, clearer than a tear, and more fragrant than ambergris.▼ commentary

"It issues forth from Tasnīm and passes through the rivers of Paradise. It flows over pebbles of pearls and rubies. The drinking vessels upon it are more numerous than the stars of the sky, and its fragrance can be perceived from a distance of one thousand years' travel."

"Its drinking vessels are made of gold, silver, and every kind of precious jewel. Every delightful fragrance wafts into the face of the one drinking from it, until he says: if only I could remain here. I desire no substitute for this, and I have no wish to be moved away from it."

"Indeed, son of Kirdīn, you are among those who will drink their fill from it. No eye weeps for us without being blessed by looking upon al-Kawthar, and everyone who loves us will be given drink from it."

"The one who drinks from it will be given enjoyment, flavour, and desire for it in proportion to his love for us, beyond what will be given to someone beneath him in love for us."

"At al-Kawthar will stand the Commander of the Faithful ﵇, and in his hand will be a staff made from buckthorn, with which he will drive away our enemies.▼ commentary

"One of them will say: I testify to the two testimonies of faith! He will reply: go to the imam so-and-so whom you followed, and ask him to intercede for you. The man will say: the imam whom you mention has disavowed me. He will then say: go back behind you. Ask the one whom you befriended, followed, and preferred above all creation to intercede for you, if, as you believed, he was the best of creation. For the best of creation is one who intercedes, and it is only fitting that his intercession should be accepted. The man will say: I am perishing from thirst! He will reply: may Allah increase your thirst. May Allah increase your burning thirst."

Mismaʿ said:

"May I be made your ransom. How was he able to approach the Pool when others like him were unable to do so?"

Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇ replied:

"He refrained from certain shameful acts, and whenever we, the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈, were mentioned, he restrained himself from insulting us. He also abandoned certain acts that others dared to commit. However, he did not do this out of love for us or affection toward us. It was because of his intense exertion in worship, his religiosity, and because he had occupied himself with these things and left off speaking about people. As for his heart, it was hypocritical. His religion was hostility toward us, his way was that of following the Nāṣibīs, and his allegiance was to those who had passed before, preferring the two of them above everyone else..."▼ commentary

Commentary

How Each Promise Is Grounded in the Qur'an

The Source of the Narration

The report is number 6 of Kamil al-Ziyarat, a classical Shiʿi book about visiting the shrines of the Imams. Ibn Qulawayh compiled it; he died in 368 AH, about 979 CE. The chain of narrators runs from Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar al-Ḥimyarī, through five more men, down to Mismaʿ ibn ʿAbd al-Malik Kirdīn al-Baṣrī. Mismaʿ was a companion of Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇ and came from Basra.

Ibn Qulawayh wrote about two hundred years after Imam al-Ṣādiq ﵇, and this report reaches him through a single chain of narrators. Later scholars pass it on in full: al-Majlisi in Bihar al-Anwar, vol.44, p.292, and al-Rayshahri, quoting Kamil al-Ziyarat, in Mawsu'at Imam al-Husayn, vol.6, p.175. The text we have today ends in the middle of a sentence.

Mismaʿ's own words show the danger he lived in. The descendants of Sulaymān were the branch of the Abbasid family that ruled Basra. The Nāṣibīs were tribesmen who hated ʿAlī ﵇ and his family. A known Shiʿi of Basra could be reported to the governor and punished as an example to others. In that world, mourning al-Ḥusayn ﵇ had to be done in secret, or paid for.

A Visit Too Dangerous to Make

The Imam's ﵇ first question is about the body, the journey to the grave. Mismaʿ's answer shows a heart that loves the Imam's house but fears for his life. So the Imam ﵇ turns to what danger cannot reach: remembering al-Ḥusayn ﵇ and grieving for him. The Qur'an gives the principle of capacity:

لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا

"Allah does not burden any soul beyond its capacity."

- Qur'an 2:286

A companion asked the Imam ﵇ whether Allah ever asks of a soul what it cannot do. He answered with this verse and its twin: Allah burdens a soul only with what He has given it. On Allah lies the making clear; on the servant lies only what is within him.

The journey stood beyond Mismaʿ's safety. Grief lay inside his heart. The law of capacity leaves him the part of love that danger cannot take away. And the Qur'an asks love for the Prophet's ﷺ near family, and love wants to be near:

قُل لَّآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا إِلَّا ٱلْمَوَدَّةَ فِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ

"Say: I ask of you no reward for it except love for the near relatives."

- Qur'an 42:23

The school joins this wage to a question from another verse. The Qur'an asks the buried infant girl on the Day of Rising: for what sin was she killed? Imam al-Bāqir ﵇ heard the same words ask about love itself: when the love is asked, for what sin was it killed? Those killed for loving the House carry that question, and one report names the answer plainly: it means al-Ḥusayn ﵇. The wage was love, and it was answered with a sword.

That is the question Mismaʿ weeps over. He cannot pay the wage with his feet at the grave, so he pays it the only way left to him, with a heart that still asks the question.

Ziyārah means visiting the grave, and it is one way to draw near with the body. Grief and remembrance are another way, and they stay open even to a believer the authorities are watching. The Imam ﵇ blesses the grief Mismaʿ can safely offer, and he does not ask for the journey danger forbids.

Tears of Recognition

Mismaʿ's sorrow is visible. He loses his appetite, and his face shows it. In the Qur'an, sincere weeping is the body overflowing what the heart has recognized:

وَإِذَا سَمِعُوا۟ مَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَى ٱلرَّسُولِ تَرَىٰٓ أَعْيُنَهُمْ تَفِيضُ مِنَ ٱلدَّمْعِ مِمَّا عَرَفُوا۟ مِنَ ٱلْحَقِّ

"When they hear what was sent down to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflow with tears because of what they have recognized of the truth."

- Qur'an 5:83

Mismaʿ recognizes what was done. Al-Ḥusayn ﵇ was the son of ʿAlī ﵇ and Fāṭimah ﵍ and the grandson of the Prophet ﷺ. He was killed at Karbala in 61 AH (680 CE), together with his family and companions. The army belonged to the Umayyad governor of Kufa and fought in the name of the caliph Yazid; al-Ḥusayn ﵇ had refused them his allegiance. The killing put truth and falsehood face to face, and faithfulness and betrayal, divine authority and worldly power. Mismaʿ's grief sees all of this, and answers it.

The Qur'an itself shows a prophet mourning like this. Yaʿqūb ﵇ wept for Yūsuf ﵇ until his sight failed:

وَتَوَلَّىٰ عَنْهُمْ وَقَالَ يَٰٓأَسَفَىٰ عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ وَٱبْيَضَّتْ عَيْنَاهُ مِنَ ٱلْحُزْنِ فَهُوَ كَظِيمٌۭ

"He turned away from them and said: how great is my sorrow for Yūsuf. His eyes turned white from grief, and he was choking down his sorrow."

- Qur'an 12:84

The interpreters of the school draw out the words. His eyes went white, they say, from the weeping itself: grief that went on until the light left them. And kāẓim means a man swallowing his grief, holding it down inside him. The companions used to ask the Imam ﵇ how deep Yaʿqūb's ﵇ grief actually was.

The Qur'an never calls that mourning a rebellion against Allah's decree. Yaʿqūb's ﵇ grief comes from love, and it stays with patience. His own words at the first loss of Yūsuf ﵇ were:

فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ

"Beautiful patience."

- Qur'an 12:18

The reports of the school put the words back into their moment. When Yaʿqūb ﵇ heard the lie about the wolf, he said: we belong to Allah, and to Him we return. Then he wept. Then he remembered that Allah had prepared him for this trial, and he yielded to it. Only after all that did he speak: beautiful patience.

So the beauty is not in a dry face. Beautiful patience has already wept. It grieves fully, yields fully, and charges no one but the trial itself. That is Mismaʿ's state exactly: tears at what was done, and no complaint against the decree that allowed it. The prophets ﵈ show the pattern, and Karbala stands inside it.

The Softened Heart and the Heart of Stone

The Imam ﵇ asks mercy for the tear itself, because the tear comes from a softened heart. And a softened heart is exactly what the Qur'an asks for:

أَلَمْ يَأْنِ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ أَن تَخْشَعَ قُلُوبُهُمْ لِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ وَمَا نَزَلَ مِنَ ٱلْحَقِّ

"Has the time not come for those who believe, that their hearts should become humble at the remembrance of Allah and what has descended of the truth?"

- Qur'an 57:16

The school reads this question as aimed at the Muslims themselves, and reads the softness asked for as reverent fear. The question carries a warning: has the time come yet? Hearts do not harden in a day. They harden through years of forgetting.

The true opposite of a weeping heart is a hard heart. The Qur'an describes it after the Children of Israel received their signs:

ثُمَّ قَسَتْ قُلُوبُكُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ فَهِىَ كَٱلْحِجَارَةِ أَوْ أَشَدُّ قَسْوَةًۭ ۚ وَإِنَّ مِنَ ٱلْحِجَارَةِ لَمَا يَتَفَجَّرُ مِنْهُ ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ ۚ وَإِنَّ مِنْهَا لَمَا يَشَّقَّقُ فَيَخْرُجُ مِنْهُ ٱلْمَآءُ ۚ وَإِنَّ مِنْهَا لَمَا يَهْبِطُ مِنْ خَشْيَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَمَا ٱللَّهُ بِغَٰفِلٍ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ

"Then your hearts hardened after that, so they became like stones, or even harder. Yet from some stones rivers burst open, and some split and water flows from them, and some fall down from the fear of Allah. And Allah sees everything you do.", so they became like stones, or even harder."

- Qur'an 2:74

Then the verse turns on its own image. Even rock softens: rivers burst from some stones, water pours from others, and some fall down from the fear of Allah. A heart that Karbala cannot soften has fallen below stone. Weeping for al-Ḥusayn ﵇ means the most when it breaks that hardness. A person can cry and still be cruel. Those tears fall short of what the Imam ﵇ blesses here. The praised tear comes from a heart that recognized the truth, loved it, and was wounded by its violation.

One Heart, One Allegiance

The Imam ﵇ counts Mismaʿ among those who rejoice at the joy of the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ and grieve at their sorrow. This is wilāyah: allegiance of love and loyalty to the Prophet's ﷺ house. The believer's feelings take sides. He rejoices when truth wins, and he grieves when Allah's servants are wronged. The Qur'an describes such a heart with an impossible image:

مَّا جَعَلَ ٱللَّهُ لِرَجُلٍۢ مِّن قَلْبَيْنِ فِى جَوْفِهِۦ

"Allah has not placed two hearts within any man's body."

- Qur'an 33:4

In its original setting, the verse answers two pre-Islamic customs. One is zihār, where a husband swore off his wife as though she were his mother. The other is adoption, which gave an adopted son a new name. The school takes the image further: one body, one allegiance, now applied to the loyalties of the heart.

ʿAlī ﵇ read this verse plainly: the love of the House and the love of their enemies will never share one chest. One heart cannot love the oppressed and approve their enemies at the same time. The Qur'an says it directly:

لَّا تَجِدُ قَوْمًۭا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ يُوَآدُّونَ مَنْ حَآدَّ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ وَلَوْ كَانُوٓا۟ ءَابَآءَهُمْ أَوْ أَبْنَآءَهُمْ أَوْ إِخْوَٰنَهُمْ أَوْ عَشِيرَتَهُمْ ۚ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ كَتَبَ فِى قُلُوبِهِمُ ٱلْإِيمَٰنَ وَأَيَّدَهُم بِرُوحٍۢ

"You will not find a people who believe in Allah and the Last Day loving those who oppose Allah and His Messenger. Those are the ones upon whose hearts Allah has written faith, and whom He has strengthened with a spirit from Him." in Allah and the Last Day loving those who oppose Allah and His Messenger."

- Qur'an 58:22

Then the verse gives more than a prohibition. Upon the hearts it approves, Allah writes faith itself, and He strengthens them with a spirit from Him. Taking sides is also receiving.

Love builds loyalty. It shapes what the believer turns away from, and it settles which side his heart lives on. This allegiance forbids imitating injustice against anyone, and it refuses to bless the deliberate destruction of truth. To rejoice and grieve with the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ is to enter their world with a heart that holds together.

The Company at Death

Mismaʿ fears mutilation and death. The Imam ﵇ answers him by turning his eyes to the last moment of life. Rulers control what happens in this world. How a believer is received at death belongs to Allah alone. The Qur'an ties security to faith kept clean of wrongdoing:

ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَلَمْ يَلْبِسُوٓا۟ إِيمَٰنَهُم بِظُلْمٍ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمُ ٱلْأَمْنُ وَهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ

"Those who believe and do not clothe their faith with wrongdoing: for them is security, and they are rightly guided."

- Qur'an 6:82

The companions asked the Imam ﵇ what this wrongdoing is. One answer he gave: doubt. Faith mixed with doubt carries no security. Another answer named the mixing itself: the allegiance Muhammad ﷺ brought, joined to another allegiance beside it. Either way, the verse hangs its promise on an unmixed heart.

That is where Mismaʿ stands. His heart holds one love, and what he is promised is an unmixed hour at death. The promise follows a verse about Allah's steadfast servants:

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَقَٰمُوا۟ تَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ أَلَّا تَخَافُوا۟ وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا۟ وَأَبْشِرُوا۟ بِٱلْجَنَّةِ ٱلَّتِى كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ

"Indeed, those who say: our Lord is Allah, and then remain steadfast: the angels descend upon them: do not fear and do not grieve, but receive the good news of the Garden which you were promised."

- Qur'an 41:30

One of the Imams ﵇ was asked what their steadfastness was. He answered: they stood on the Imams, one after another. The verse's stability is allegiance held through every change. Mismaʿ holds it in secret in Basra, and the verse answers him in the open at his death.

Fear gives way to reassurance, loneliness to company, and death to good news. Those Mismaʿ grieved for in life will comfort him at his death. The Angel of Death will treat him more gently than a tender mother treats her child.

Souls meet death differently, and for the loyal believer death is a passage into company. His fear and his security have moved from himself to Allah. He fears losing his faith more than losing his life, and he finds safety in nearness to Allah, whatever the powerful intend.

Heaven and Earth Bear Witness

Heaven and earth weep for the House. The Qur'an sets the contrast with the people of Pharaoh, who drowned and were abandoned:

فَمَا بَكَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ ٱلسَّمَآءُ وَٱلْأَرْضُ وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُنظَرِينَ

"So neither heaven nor earth wept over them, nor were they granted respite."

- Qur'an 44:29

Scholars read the denial in two ways. Some hear a plain idiom: nobody mourned Pharaoh's people, and the verse says only that. The Shiʿi school hears a measured denial: some dead are mourned by heaven and earth, and some are not. Pharaoh's people earned silence, while the House of the Prophet ﷺ stands among the mourned.

Heaven and earth withheld their sorrow from Pharaoh's people. A tyrant thinks the death of a righteous man is a political event he can erase. Creation answers him. The mourning of earth and heaven declares that worldly victory and divine worth are two different measures.

ʿAlī ﵇ once recited this very verse in the courtyard of Kufa. As he finished, young al-Ḥusayn ﵇ came out through one of the mosque doors, and ʿAlī ﵇ said: this one will be killed, and heaven and earth will weep over him. Decades before Karbala, the verse and the boy were already bound together in the school's memory.

Another report gives the verse its positive side: every believer has doors in heaven through which his deeds rise and his provision descends, and when he dies, those doors weep for him. Heaven knows how to mourn. Pharaoh's people simply gave it nothing to mourn.

So Karbala cannot be measured on the visible battlefield alone. The body was killed, but the crime went through the whole moral structure of creation.

Why Angels May Weep

The weeping angels outnumber even the weeping heavens. The Qur'an calls the angels servants whose obedience is complete:

لَّا يَعْصُونَ ٱللَّهَ مَآ أَمَرَهُمْ وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ

"They do not disobey Allah in what He commands them, and they do what they are commanded."

- Qur'an 66:6

Scripture never says that angels weep; the Imam's ﵇ words say it. What the verse gives is narrower, and it is enough. The angels never disobey Allah in what He commands them, so if they weep, the weeping belongs to their service.

One of the Imams ﵇ was asked whether the angels can fall as the fallen angels of legend fell. He answered: Allah forbid; the angels are kept from error and preserved from every ugliness by the grace of Allah. Their weeping, then, is kept weeping. No passion rules it, and no ignorance stains it. Their grief fully recognizes how great the crime was, and their mourning agrees with Allah's judgment about what is sacred and what violated it.

Why One Tear Can Cool the Fire

Mercy before the tear is visible

Allah shows the mourner mercy before the tear even leaves his eye. He already knows the heart it came from:

يَعْلَمُ خَآئِنَةَ ٱلْأَعْيُنِ وَمَا تُخْفِى ٱلصُّدُورُ

"He knows the treachery of the eyes and what the breasts conceal."

- Qur'an 40:19

Allah settles the act before anyone can see it, praise it, or reward it. The tear is free of any need to be seen. Its value is fixed in secret, beyond the reach of reputation.

Mercy that already encompasses creation

The Qur'an shows Allah's mercy as coming before the mourner and reaching far beyond his act:

وَرَحْمَتِى وَسِعَتْ كُلَّ شَىْءٍۢ

"And My mercy encompasses all things."

- Qur'an 7:156

كَتَبَ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ ٱلرَّحْمَةَ

"He has prescribed mercy for Himself."

- Qur'an 6:12

قُلْ يَٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا

"Say: My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of Allah's mercy. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins."

- Qur'an 39:53

The school preserves a startling reading of this mercy: the mercy that encompasses everything is the guidance Allah placed in the Imams ﵈, reaching every soul through them. Read either way, the width belongs to Allah. The mourner does not stretch mercy by weeping. Mercy was already wide enough to reach him first, and the tear only answers it.

Allah grants this mercy freely; the tear buys nothing from Him. The tear shows a heart that opened itself to a mercy that already covers all creation.

The drop and the fire

One drop would put out the heat of Hell. The image declares the power of divine mercy. Hell is the end of separation from Allah, of rebellion hardened and injustice left unrepented.

The praised tear comes from the opposite condition: tenderness, recognition, loyalty, and compassion for Allah's oppressed servants. It carries the sign of mercy into the place of wrath. A tear is small, but what it carries can be immense.

What the tear must produce

One of the Imams ﵇ consoled a grieving follower with this very verse. Allah has mentioned you in His book, he told him; has this made you glad? The verse works the same way here. Before the mourner asks for anything, mercy has already addressed him by name.

The promise joins hope to turning back:

إِلَّا مَن تَابَ وَءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ عَمَلًۭا صَٰلِحًۭا فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ يُبَدِّلُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمْ حَسَنَٰتٍۢ

"Except those who repent, believe, and act righteously: for them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good deeds."

- Qur'an 25:70

The school reads the righteous deed of this verse as reaching love itself: a return that stops short of love has stopped short. The man at the Pool proves it from the far side. His worship arrived at the water, and his heart could not drink. A tear that truly comes from loyalty to al-Ḥusayn ﵇ moves the weeper away from the conduct of the men who killed him. If cruelty, dishonesty, and injustice in the mourner survive untouched, the tear's claim to that loyalty is still incomplete.

From Grief to the Pool of al-Kawthar

The grief the Imam ﵇ blesses is for a season, but its fruit lasts. The bond that grief forms opens fully at death. The Qur'an promises the friends of Allah freedom from fear and from grief:

أَلَآ إِنَّ أَوْلِيَآءَ ٱللَّهِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

"Indeed, the friends of Allah: no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve."

- Qur'an 10:62

Asked who the friends of Allah are, ʿAlī ﵇ described them: people who purified their worship for Allah alone, who looked at the inward of this world while others looked only at its outward, and who held to the hereafter while the world seduced everyone else. Another report adds their mark for the Day of Rising: light on their faces, and no fear when the people fear.

The weeper at Karbala belongs in that line. Whoever accepts grief for truth in this world is promised freedom from grief in the next. The heart's pain purifies, because it refuses to make peace with oppression. From the tear the promise turns to water, and the water has its own verse:

إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْكَوْثَرَ

"Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar."

- Qur'an 108:1

The mourner gives a drop born of love. Allah receives it with mercy. The mourner is received at the river of abundance, and the thirst of grief is exchanged for a drink after which there is no thirst. Al-Kawthar makes nearness to the Prophet ﷺ and his family visible. The drink is poured in the measure of the love that sought it.

The grading of the reward has its own verse:

وَلِكُلٍّۢ دَرَجَٰتٌۭ مِّمَّا عَمِلُوا۟ ۚ وَمَا رَبُّكَ بِغَٰفِلٍ عَمَّا يَعْمَلُونَ

"For each are degrees from what they did, and your Lord is fully aware of what they do."

- Qur'an 6:132

The Qur'an grades these degrees by what people did. The Imam ﵇ grades his drink by love. In both, the reward is measured, because heaven measures everything. Nothing there is arbitrary and nothing is wasted; every portion follows what a life loved.

The Drinks of Paradise

Cooler than camphor, fragrant with musk, tasting of ginger, sweeter than honey, clearer than a tear. The Qur'an's drinks of Paradise answer each detail:

إِنَّ ٱلْأَبْرَارَ يَشْرَبُونَ مِن كَأْسٍۢ كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا كَافُورًا

"Indeed, the righteous will drink from a cup whose mixture is camphor."

- Qur'an 76:5

وَيُسْقَوْنَ فِيهَا كَأْسًۭا كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا زَنجَبِيلًا

"And they will be given there a cup whose mixture is ginger."

- Qur'an 76:17

Even the vessels, gold and silver and every precious jewel, appear in the Qur'an's picture of Paradise. The verse ends with a curious clause: they measure it out with measure. The interpreters explain: every vessel is made to the exact size of its drinker, nothing missing and nothing extra. Even the cup at al-Kawthar fits the hand that holds it. The report said the same in its own words: the drink is poured in the measure of love.

وَيُطَافُ عَلَيْهِم بِـَٔانِيَةٍۢ مِّن فِضَّةٍۢ وَأَكْوَابٍۢ كَانَتْ قَوَارِيرَا۠ قَوَارِيرَا۟ مِن فِضَّةٍۢ قَدَّرُوهَا تَقْدِيرًۭا

"And circulated among them are vessels of silver and cups that were crystal, crystal of silver, which they measure out in measure."

- Qur'an 76:15-16

وَسَقَىٰهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ شَرَابًۭا طَهُورًا

"And their Lord will give them a purifying drink."

- Qur'an 76:21

And the Pool has a source with a Qur'anic name. Tasnīm, the interpreters of the school say, is the most exalted drink of Paradise, a spring in the highest place, and it belongs to the brought-near. The brought-near, in the school's reading, are the family of Muhammad ﷺ themselves:

وَمِزَاجُهُۥ مِن تَسْنِيمٍ

"And its mixture is of Tasnīm."

- Qur'an 83:27

The Qur'an seals its nectar with musk. Of that sealed drink the Prophet ﷺ said: I will be the first to knock at the gate of Paradise, and ʿAlī ﵇ will be with me; and I will be the first given to drink of the nectar whose seal is musk, and ʿAlī ﵇ will be with me. The report places this same ʿAlī ﵇ at al-Kawthar, staff in hand, giving drink to the lovers and turning the enemies away. The cup returns to the one who was first given it. And the musk appears in the Qur'an, in its picture of that same drink:

خِتَٰمُهُۥ مِسْكٌۭ ۚ وَفِى ذَٰلِكَ فَلْيَتَنَافَسِ ٱلْمُتَنَٰفِسُونَ

"Its seal is musk; for that, let the competitors compete."

- Qur'an 83:26

So the water Mismaʿ is promised rises from the spring of the very family he weeps for. The tear he gives them falls, and the water they give him rises; the exchange runs one way, from the brought-near down to the one who loved them. The Pool draws from a spring the Qur'an itself names. The Qur'an calls the drink purifying, and the Imam's ﵇ words go further: clearer than a tear. Together they fit the Pool for single-hearted love. The verse of the two hearts (Qur'an 33:4) becomes consolation here. Mixed allegiance divides the soul, and al-Kawthar is the home of the undivided heart.

The Testimony That Stayed on the Tongue

At al-Kawthar stands the Commander of the Faithful ﵇, a staff of buckthorn in his hand, driving the enemies of the House away. One of them pleads his faith. He is sent to the leaders he followed, and they disown him. He begs for intercession, and it fails. The last word to him is a prayer that his thirst increase.

The Sunni collections tell a story in much the same shape. In the Basin (ḥawḍ) narrations of Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, people are driven back from the Prophet's ﷺ Basin on the Day of Rising. What they did after him contradicted the confession they brought to the water. A Prophet at the water, turning away those whose lives emptied their words: this is shared ground between the two traditions.

A man walks up to the Pool and says the two testimonies of faith, and the water is refused him. The Qur'an already warns that the tongue can say what the heart does not. About the hypocrites who greeted the Prophet ﷺ with a correct confession, it says:

إِذَا جَآءَكَ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ قَالُوا۟ نَشْهَدُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُهُۥ وَٱللَّهُ يَشْهَدُ إِنَّ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ لَكَٰذِبُونَ

"When the hypocrites come to you, they say: we bear witness that you are surely the Messenger of Allah. Allah knows that you are His Messenger, and Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are surely liars."

- Qur'an 63:1

Their sentence was factually correct, yet they were called liars, because their hearts refused to back what their tongues said. The Qur'an names the condition:

وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ وَبِٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ

"Among the people are those who say: we believe in Allah and the Last Day, while they are not believers."

- Qur'an 2:8

The two testimonies are true words. The danger is emptying them of what they require. To testify to the Messenger is to be faithful to his message, to the bonds he appointed, and to the trusts he left.

Restraint Without Love

The Imam's ﵇ explanation separates restraint from allegiance. The man avoided shameful acts. He held his tongue when the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈ were mentioned. He left off what others dared to do.

The word for what he lacked is ikhlāṣ: worship offered purely for Allah's own face. The man had worship. What he did not have was this. Worship kept him busy, and religiosity kept him from open hatred, but his heart pointed elsewhere the whole time. The tear has ikhlāṣ by nature; it is given to no audience at all. None of it came from love. His conduct carried him near the Pool, but the direction of his heart kept him from drinking. The Qur'an joins every act of worship to sincerity:

وَمَآ أُمِرُوٓا۟ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ ٱلدِّينَ حُنَفَآءَ

"They were commanded only to worship Allah, devoting religion sincerely to Him."

- Qur'an 98:5

The verse of the two hearts (Qur'an 33:4) applies here at its sharpest. Restraint is good, and it cannot stand in for inward truth. Worship does not erase hostility toward those Allah commands the believer to love.

The two of them are the first two caliphs after the Prophet ﷺ. The Imam's ﵇ verdict names three things: a heart he calls hypocritical, a religion of hostility to the House, and an allegiance set above the love offered to the man.

The three stand or fall together. The criterion is what the heart loves, and whether the tongue's confession matches it. A reader whose allegiance differs from Mismaʿ's meets the same criterion at the Pool. The examination runs inward first, to the heart, before anyone is turned from the water.

The two men close the report. Mismaʿ, kept from the journey by danger, weeps with a sincere heart and receives the promise of al-Kawthar. The other man, outwardly religious, reaches the Pool itself, but his divided, hostile heart keeps him from drinking. Here the text we have breaks off, mid-sentence.

The Education of the Heart

The education of the heart is the whole of it. The tear matters because it shows love. Love matters because it creates allegiance. Allegiance matters because it shapes action and decides the company of the hereafter.

The message moves through three verses of the Qur'an. First, the heart softens when it recognizes truth: the eyes overflow with tears because of what they have recognized (Qur'an 5:83). Second, one heart cannot hold two loyalties: Allah has not placed two hearts within any man (Qur'an 33:4). Third, the purified heart is carried from its tear to the water of abundance: We have given you al-Kawthar (Qur'an 108:1).

A single drop, the Imam ﵇ promises, could put out the heat of the Fire, because it comes from a heart where mercy, truth, and loyalty have overcome hardness. Its fruit is a life set against what produced Karbala. Its end is the water of the Pool, with the very ones for whom it was shed.
