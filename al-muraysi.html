Battle Narration

The Expedition of al-Muraysi

Al-Harith ibn Abi Dirar gathered the clans of the Mustaliq for war on Medina, the Prophet ﷺ verified it with a scout, struck first at the well of al-Muraysi, and the captives the victory produced went free within days through a marriage. Part 17 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Scout and the Well

Al-Harith ibn Abi Dirar, chief of the Mustaliq, called his clan and every Arab he could reach to war on Medina, and bought horses and weapons for it. The Prophet ﷺ sent Burayda al-Aslami to verify the report, and Burayda walked into the camp as a friend of the gathering and came back with the truth. The army marched and fell on the Mustaliq at their well, al-Muraysi, and the day ended in rout and captivity. Then the aftermath did what the battle could not.▼ commentary▼ commentary

The Shia abridgement carries the cause and the scout.

يقول عدد من المؤرخين : إن غزوة المريسيع كانت لليلتين خلتا من شعبان سنة ست للهجرة . ويقولون : إن المريسيع ماء لبني خزاعة بينه وبين الفرع يومان (وعند ابن سعد نحو يوم) وبين الفرع والمدينة ثمانية برد . وتسمى هذه الغزوة أيضاً بغزوة بني المصطلق ، وهم بطن من خزاعة . سبب غزوة المريسيع : وسبب هذه الغزوة أن بني المصطلق كانوا ينزلون على بئر يقال لها المريسيع ، من ناحية قديد إلى الساحل . وكان سيدهم الحارث بن أبي ضرار دعا قومه ومن قدر عليه من العرب إلى حرب رسول الله (﵌) ، فأجابوه . وتجمعوا ، وابتاعوا خيلاً ، وسلاحاً ، وتهيأوا للحرب ، والمسير معه . فبلغ رسول الله (﵌) الخبر ، فأرسل بريدة بن الحصيب الأسلمي ليتحقق ذلك . فأتاهم ، ولقي الحارث ، وكلّمه ، مظهراً أنه منهم ، وقد سمع بجمعهم ، ويريد الانضمام بقومه ، ومن أطاعه إليهم ، وعرف منهم صدق ما بلغهم عنهم . فرجع إلى رسول الله فأخبره بأنهم يريدون

"A number of historians say the expedition of al-Muraysi fell two nights into Sha'ban of the sixth year. They say al-Muraysi was a watering place of Khuzaa, two days from al-Farc, and al-Farc eight barid, eight days' stages, from Medina. The march is also called the expedition of Banu al-Mustaliq, a clan of Khuzaa. Its cause was this. The Mustaliq were settled at a well called al-Muraysi, on the side of Qudayd toward the coast, and their chief al-Harith ibn Abi Dirar called his people, and every Arab he could reach, to war against the Messenger of Allah, and they answered him. They gathered, bought horses and weapons, made ready for war, and meant to march with him. The news reached the Messenger of Allah, and he sent Burayda ibn al-Husayb al-Aslami to verify it. He came to them, met al-Harith, and spoke with him as one of them, saying he had heard of the gathering and wanted to bring his people and whoever obeyed him to join it, and he learned from them that the report was true. He went back to the Messenger of Allah and told him they meant war."

- al-Amili, al-Mustafa min Sira al-Mustafa, vol.2, p.275

Al-Kharkushi's line gives the result in one breath.

ثم غزا رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم بني المصطلق - من خزاعة - فأغار عليهم ، فغلبهم وسباهم ، واشترى جويرية بنت الحارث من ذلك السبي فأعتقها وتزوجها

"Then the Messenger of Allah fought Banu al-Mustaliq, of Khuzaa. He raided them, defeated them, and took captives, and he bought Juwayriyya daughter of al-Harith out of that captivity, freed her, and married her."

- al-Kharkushi, Sharaf al-Mustafa, vol.3, p.43

Al-Dhahabi carries Ibn Ishaq's report from Aisha on what the captivity became.▼ commentary

وقال يونس ، عن ابن إِسْحَاق : حدثني مُحَمَّد بْنُ جَعْفَرِ بْنِ الزُّبَيْرِ ، عَنْ عُرْوَةَ ، عَنْ عَائِشَةَ قَالَتْ : لَمَّا قَسَّمَ رَسُولُ اللَّه صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ سَبَايَا بَنِي الْمُصْطَلِقِ وَقَعَتْ جُوَيْرِيَةُ فِي السَّهْمِ لِثَابِتِ بْنِ قَيْسِ بْنِ شَمَّاسٍ ، أَوْ لابْنِ عَمٍّ لَهُ فَكَاتَبْتُهُ عَلَى نَفْسِهَا ، وَكَانَتِ امْرَأَةً حُلْوَةً مُلاحَةً ، لَا يَرَاهَا أَحَدٌ إِلا أَخَذَتْ بِنَفْسِهِ فَأَتَتْ رَسُولَ اللَّه صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ تَسْتَعِينُهُ فِي كِتَابَتِهَا ، فَوَاللَّهِ مَا هُوَ إِلا أَنْ رَأَيْتُهَا فَكَرِهْتُهَا ، وَقُلْتُ : سَيَرَى مِنْهَا مِثْلَ مَا رَأَيْتُ . فَلَمَّا دَخَلْتُ عَلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ قَالَتْ : أَنَا جُوَيْرِيَةُ بِنْتُ الْحَارِثِ سَيِّدِ قَوْمِهِ ، وَقَدْ أَصَابَنِي مِنَ الْبَلاءِ مَا لَمْ يَخْفَ عَلَيْكَ ، وَقَدْ كَاتَبْتُ فَأَعِنِّي . فَقَالَ : أَوْ خَيْرٌ مِنْ ذَلِكَ ، أُؤَدِّي عَنْكِ كِتَابَتَكِ وَأَتَزَوَّجُكِ . فَقَالَتْ : نَعَمْ . فَفَعَلَ رَسُولُ اللَّه صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ فَبَلَغَ النَّاسَ أَنَّهُ قَدْ تَزَوَّجَهَا ، فَقَالُوا : أَصْهَارُ رَسُولِ اللَّه صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ . فَأَرْسَلُوا مَا كَانَ فِي أَيْدِيهِمْ مِنْ بني المصطلق ، فلقد أعتق بها مائة أَهْلَ بَيْتٍ مِنْ بَنِي الْمُصْطَلِقِ ، فَمَا أَعْلَمُ امرأة كانت أعظم بركة على قومها

"Yunus said, from Ibn Ishaq, that Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr told him, from Urwa, from Aisha, and she said. When the Messenger of Allah divided the captives of Banu al-Mustaliq, Juwayriyya fell to the lot of Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas, or to a cousin of his, and she made a contract to buy her own freedom. She was a sweet and beautiful woman, and none saw her but she took hold of him. She came to the Messenger of Allah asking his help in her contract, and by Allah, no sooner did I see her than I felt jealousy of her, and I said, he will see in her what I have seen. When she entered on the Messenger of Allah she said, 'I am Juwayriyya daughter of al-Harith, chief of his people, and what has befallen me is hidden from no one, and I have made my contract, so help me.' He said, 'Or something better than that. I pay your contract for you, and marry you.' She said, 'Yes.' And the Messenger of Allah did it, and the word spread among the people that he had married her, and they said, 'Kinsmen of the Messenger of Allah.' And they sent away whatever of the Mustaliq was in their hands, so that by her a hundred households of Banu al-Mustaliq were freed, and I know of no woman who was a greater blessing upon her people than she was."

- Ibn Ishaq via al-Dhahabi, Tarikh al-Islam, vol.1, p.172, from Urwa from Aisha

The quarrel at the water that stirred the camp is carried by Ibn Kathir from Ibn Ishaq's line.

وقال يونس بن بكير عن ابن إسحاق حدثني محمد بن يحي بن حبان وعبد الله بن أبي بكر وعاصم بن عمر بن قتادة في قصة بني المصطلق فبينا رسول الله ﵌ مقيم هناك اقتتل على الماء جهجاه بن سعيد الغفاري وكان أجيرا لعمر بن الخطاب وسنان بن يزيد قال ابن إسحاق فحدثني محمد بن يحيى بن حبان قال ازدحما على الماء فاقتتلا فقال سنان يا معشر الأنصار وقال الجهجاه يا معشر المهاجرين

"Yunus ibn Bakir said, from Ibn Ishaq, that Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Habban, Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr, and Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada told him, in the story of Banu al-Mustaliq, that while the Messenger of Allah was camped there, Jahjah ibn Sa'id al-Ghifari, a hired man of Umar, and Sinan ibn Zayd fought over the water. Ibn Ishaq said, Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Habban told me the two crowded each other at the water and came to blows, and Sinan called, 'Ansar, to me,' and Jahjah called, 'Emigrants, to me.'"

- Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Kathir, Tafsir Ibn Kathir, vol.4, p.395

Commentary

The Sources for al-Muraysi

Shia lines: al-Amili, al-Mustafa min Sira al-Mustafa, vol.2, p.275, on the cause and the scout. al-Amili, al-Sahih min Sira al-Nabi al-A'zam, vol.12, p.233, on the date

Sunni lines: al-Kharkushi, Sharaf al-Mustafa, vol.3, p.43. al-Dhahabi, Tarikh al-Islam, vol.1, p.172, carrying Ibn Ishaq from Urwa from Aisha. Ibn Kathir, Tafsir, vol.4, p.395, carrying Ibn Ishaq's quarrel-at-the-water line

Citation note: the serial note placed the quarrel on al-Dhahabi's page, but that page carries the marriage hadith only, so the quarrel is quoted from Ibn Kathir's tafsir, where Ibn Ishaq's line grounds

Dating: two nights into Sha'ban, year five by a number of historians, year six by others, with the weight of the traditionists on the latter

The frame is agreed on both sides. A war gathering under al-Harith, a scout sent to verify it, a first strike at the well, rout and captives, and the captives freed. The quarrel at the water belongs to the return camp, and the sources carry it as the spark of the hypocrites' affair that followed the victory.

The Scout and the Strike

Cause: al-Harith ibn Abi Dirar's war gathering, horses and weapons bought

Verification: Burayda al-Aslami entered the camp as a would-be ally and confirmed the report

Strike: the army fell on the Mustaliq at their water, al-Muraysi, on the Qudayd road

Result: rout and captivity, the wealth of the camp taken

Notable dead: the works quoted here do not name the fallen of either side

The march followed the pattern the series has now shown a dozen times, verify first, strike the gathering at its own ground. The victory was total, and the captives many, and it is the aftermath that sets this expedition apart in the record of the wars of that age.

The Captives Who Went Free

Captive: Juwayriyya daughter of al-Harith, the chief's own daughter, allotted to Thabit ibn Qays or a cousin of his

Her contract: she bargained to buy her own freedom

The offer: the Prophet ﷺ paid her contract and married her

The result: the Muslims, hearing it, freed every Mustaliq captive in their hands, a hundred households by Aisha's count

Aisha's verdict: no woman was a greater blessing to her people

Read what the record claims. A tribe that marched to war lost the battle, and its captives won their freedom within days, because the chief's daughter entered the Prophet's ﷺ house and the army would not hold kinfolk of the Messenger of Allah in bondage. The tactical outcome was a rout. The strategic outcome was the absorption of a hostile clan into the Muslim body, and the sources name the mechanism themselves.

The quarrel at the water closed the account on the other side. A shove between two men at the well became a cry of factions, Ansar against Emigrants, and the record keeps it because of what it nearly became, the hypocrites' pretext that the same march produced.

Was the Expedition of al-Muraysi Justified

The cause was a verified war gathering with purchased cavalry aimed at Medina. The Prophet's ﷺ first act was intelligence, a scout into the camp, and his second was the strike the laws of that world gave a threatened city. The gathering was real, the horses were bought, and the march on Medina was the stated aim of the man who collected it.

The aftermath is the measure the sources themselves use. A victory that ends with the enemy's captives freed by the hundred households is a settlement. Their chief's daughter stands in the position of honor, and the clan is joined to the Muslims by marriage. The record of the captives walking free stands against any charge of plunder.

Could it have been prevented? The door stood with al-Harith, in a gathering he could have left ungathered and cavalry he could have left unbought. No embassy is recorded. The scout who visited the camp came as a pretend friend because an open envoy, on the record here, would have been held.
