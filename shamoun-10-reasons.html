Ten Proof-Texts the Qur'an Answers

Jesus the Messiah Is a Servant of Allah, Not the Son of God

A Trinitarian apologist gathers ten biblical proof-texts for the deity of Jesus and answers each one by saying it does not disprove that deity. The proof-texts argue that Jesus is a creature who prays, obeys, depends on another, and dies. The Qur'an answers them directly. It presents Jesus as the Messiah, a servant and messenger of Allah.

The Argument Under Examination

Premise 1. The Qur'an Names Jesus the Messiah, a Servant, Never God

The Qur'an names Jesus the Messiah, a servant (abd) and messenger (rasul) of Allah. It never calls him God and never calls him the son of God. The titles it gives him are the titles of a created emissary.

Premise 2. The Qur'an Forbids the Two Claims the Trinity Rests On

The Qur'an forbids both claims the Trinity depends on. It says Allah has no son (ibn Allah), and it says Allah is not three (thalath). These are not open questions in the Book. They are forbidden statements.

Premise 3. Every Proof-Text Confirms the Qur'anic Jesus

Every proof-text a Trinitarian apologist raises about Jesus, his prayers, his dependence, his ignorance of the Hour, and his human death, is answered by the Qur'an's own picture of a man who worshipped, obeyed, and was sent by Allah.

Conclusion. The Ten Proof-Texts Presuppose a Position the Qur'an Does Not Hold

The ten proof-texts fail because they argue against a unitarian strawman. They show that Jesus prays, obeys, depends, learns, dies, and has a God. The Qur'an agrees with every one of those facts, because it presents Jesus as a servant and messenger of Allah. The Trinity needs Jesus to be God. The Qur'an says he is not.

1. God Is One and Not Three

Source. Qur'an 4:171, 5:73, 112:1-4

Claim. The oneness of God, cited from Mark 12:29, is said to leave the Trinity untouched.

Verse. Qur'an 4:171; 112:1-4

Locus. the apologist's first proof-text

The apologist's first proof-text is Mark 12:29, where Jesus says the Lord our God is one Lord. The apologist answers that oneness does not refute the Trinity, because the Trinity is three persons in one being. The Qur'an settles the matter before the debate begins. It commands the believers not to say three, and it names Allah as one God who is far above having a son.

وَلا تَقُولُوا ثَلاثَةٌ انْتَهُوا خَيْراً لَكُمْ إِنَّمَا اللَّهُ إِلهٌ واحِدٌ سُبْحانَهُ أَنْ يَكُونَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ

wa la taqulu thalathatan intahu khayran lakum inna Allaha ilahun wahidun subhanahu an yakuna lahu walad

"And do not say three. Stop, it is better for you. Allah is but one God. Glory be to Him that He should have a son."

Qur'an 4:171

لَقَدْ كَفَرَ الَّذِينَ قالُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ ثالِثُ ثَلاثَةٍ

laqad kafara alladhina qalu inna Allaha thalithu thalathatin

"They have certainly disbelieved who say that Allah is the third of three."

Qur'an 5:73

The denial is direct. The Qur'an does not argue that oneness is compatible with threeness. It forbids the saying outright and calls the saying disbelief. The sura of purity states the same in four lines.

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُواً أَحَدٌ

qul huwa Allahu ahadun Allahu al-samadu lam yalid wa lam yulad wa lam yakun lahu kufuwan ahad

"Say, He is Allah, the One. Allah is the Eternal. He does not beget and is not begotten. And there is none comparable to Him."

Qur'an 112:1-4

The oneness of God is the premise of the whole Qur'an. The apologist treats it as a weak point to explain away. The Qur'an treats it as the wall the Trinity breaches.

2. Jesus Has a God

Source. Qur'an 19:30, 5:117

Claim. John 20:17 shows Jesus has a God, which the apologist explains by the incarnation.

Verse. Qur'an 19:30; 5:117

Locus. the apologist's second proof-text

The apologist's second proof-text is John 20:17, where the risen Jesus says he is ascending to his God and their God. The apologist answers that the Son, as a man, can have the Father as his God. The Qur'an answers with Jesus' own words as an infant. He declares himself the servant of Allah and commands worship of Allah alone.

إِنِّي عَبْدُ اللَّهِ آتانِيَ الْكِتابَ وَجَعَلَنِي نَبِيّاً

inni 'abdu Allahi ataniya al-kitaba wa ja'alani nabiyyan

"I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Book and made me a prophet."

Qur'an 19:30

ما كُنْتُ أَقُولُ لَهُمْ إِلَّا ما أَمَرْتَنِي بِهِ أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ رَبِّي وَرَبَّكُمْ

ma kuntu aqulu lahum illa ma amartani bihi ani 'budu Allaha rabbi wa rabbakum

"I never told them except what You commanded me, that worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord."

Qur'an 5:117

Jesus in the cradle names himself a servant, not a god. On the Day of Judgment the Qur'an has him repeat the same submission. He directs his followers to worship Allah as his Lord and theirs. A god who tells people to worship another is not God.

3. Jesus Does Nothing of Himself

Source. Qur'an 5:75, 3:59

Claim. John 5:30 and John 8:28 show Jesus depends on the Father, which the apologist reads as shared divinity.

Verse. Qur'an 5:75; 3:59

Locus. the apologist's third proof-text

The apologist's third proof-text joins John 5:30 and John 8:28, where Jesus says he can do nothing of himself. The apologist answers that the Father and Son are inseparable, so what the Father does the Son does. The Qur'an answers that Jesus is a messenger like the messengers before him, and that he ate food like any man.

مَا الْمَسِيحُ ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِنْ قَبْلِهِ الرُّسُلُ وَأُمُّهُ صِدِّيقَةٌ كانا يَأْكُلانِ الطَّعامَ

ma al-masihu ibnu maryama illa rasulun qad khalat min qablihi al-rusulu wa ummuhu siddiqatun kana yakulani al-ta'ama

"The Messiah, son of Mary, was only a messenger. Messengers passed away before him. His mother was a woman of truth. They both used to eat food."

Qur'an 5:75

The verse makes the point without softening it. Amir al-Muminin ﵇, explaining that they ate food, says the verse means they both relieved themselves. Jesus had a body with every human need. The Qur'an then compares his creation to Adam's.

إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَى عِنْدَ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ آدَمَ خَلَقَهُ مِنْ تُرابٍ

inna mathala 'isa 'inda Allahi kamathali adam khalaqahu min turabin

"The example of Jesus before Allah is like the example of Adam. He created him from dust."

Qur'an 3:59

Dependence is not a mask for deity. It is the mark of a creature. The Qur'an compares Jesus to Adam, the first man, not to Allah.

4. The Father Is Greater Than Jesus

Source. Qur'an 43:59, 5:75

Claim. John 14:28 has Jesus say the Father is greater than he is, which the apologist limits to rank.

Verse. Qur'an 43:59

Locus. the apologist's fourth proof-text

The apologist's fourth proof-text is John 14:28, where Jesus says the Father is greater than he is. The apologist answers that the Father is greater in position, not in essence. The Qur'an answers with a single word about Jesus. He is a servant whom Allah blessed.

إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا عَبْدٌ أَنْعَمْنا عَلَيْهِ وَجَعَلْناهُ مَثَلًا لِبَنِي إِسْرائِيلَ

in huwa illa 'abdun an'amna 'alayhi wa ja'alnahu mathalan li-bani isra'ila

"He was not but a servant whom We blessed and made an example for the Children of Israel."

Qur'an 43:59

The word is abd, servant. The Qur'an uses it for Jesus in the same breath as the messengers who came before him. A servant is greater than his Lord in rank only if the words have lost their meaning. The Qur'an keeps the meaning. Jesus is sent, blessed, and made a sign. He is not the sender.

5. Jesus Commends His Spirit

Source. Qur'an 3:55, 19:33

Claim. Luke 23:46 has Jesus commend his spirit to the Father, which the apologist answers by a human soul.

Verse. Qur'an 3:55; 19:33

Locus. the apologist's fifth proof-text

The apologist's fifth proof-text is Luke 23:46, where Jesus commends his spirit to the Father at death. The apologist answers that the man Jesus had a human spirit, while the divine nature had none. The Qur'an answers that Jesus is a man who dies and is raised, exactly as the verse's context requires.

يا عِيسَى إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ وَمُطَهِّرُكَ مِنَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا

ya 'isa inna mutawaffika wa rafi'uka ilayya wa mutahhiruka mina alladhina kafaru

"O Jesus, I will take you and raise you to Me and purify you from those who disbelieved."

Qur'an 3:55

السلامُ عَلَيَّ يَوْمَ وُلِدْتُ وَيَوْمَ أَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ أُبْعَثُ حَيّاً

al-salamu 'alayya yawma wulidtu wa yawma amutu wa yawma ub'athu hayyan

"Peace be upon me the day I was born, the day I die, and the day I am raised alive."

Qur'an 19:33

Jesus speaks of his own death as a settled fact. The Qur'an says Allah takes him and raises him. Death belongs to the creature, not to the Creator. The spirit the verse commends is the spirit of a man returning to his Lord.

6. Jesus Does Not Know the Hour

Source. Qur'an 5:116, 31:34

Claim. Matthew 24:36 has Jesus say only the Father knows the Hour, which the apologist answers by two minds.

Verse. Qur'an 5:116; 31:34

Locus. the apologist's sixth proof-text

The apologist's sixth proof-text is Matthew 24:36, where Jesus says no one knows the day except the Father. The apologist answers that the man Jesus did not know, while the divine Jesus did. The Qur'an answers by having Allah question Jesus on the Day of Judgment about a claim Jesus never made.

أَأَنْتَ قُلْتَ لِلنَّاسِ اتَّخِذُونِي وَأُمِّي إِلهَيْنِ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ

a-anta qulta li-al-nasi attakhidhuni wa ummi ilahayni min duna Allah

"Did you say to the people, take me and my mother as two gods besides Allah?"

Qur'an 5:116

The question assumes Jesus is answerable to Allah, not equal to Him. The verse then affirms that knowledge of the Hour belongs to Allah alone. The Qur'an states this without exception.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ عِنْدَهُ عِلْمُ السَّاعَةِ

inna Allaha 'indahu 'ilmu al-sa'ati

"Indeed, with Allah is the knowledge of the Hour."

Qur'an 31:34

The ignorance of the Hour is not a costume the divine nature puts on. It is the limit of a creature. The Qur'an places that knowledge with Allah and with Him alone.

7. No One Has Seen God

Source. Qur'an 6:103, 42:11

Claim. John 5:37 has Jesus say no one has seen God, which the apologist limits to certain Jews.

Verse. Qur'an 6:103; 42:11

Locus. the apologist's seventh proof-text

The apologist's seventh proof-text is John 5:37, where Jesus says his opponents never saw God's form. The apologist answers that the words were addressed to unbelieving Jews, not to all people. The Qur'an answers with a universal claim about sight and likeness.

لا تُدْرِكُهُ الْأَبْصارُ

la tudrikuhi al-absaru

"No vision grasps Him."

Qur'an 6:103

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ

laysa kamithlihi shay'un

"There is nothing like Him."

Qur'an 42:11

The apologist's move is to say that some people did see God, so the verse must be local. The Qur'an says no vision grasps Him and nothing is like Him. If seeing Jesus were seeing God, these verses would be false. The Qur'an does not allow that inference.

8. Jesus Prays to God

Source. Qur'an 19:30, 5:117

Claim. Luke 5:16 has Jesus pray to God, which the apologist answers by internal Trinitarian communion.

Verse. Qur'an 19:30; 5:117

Locus. the apologist's eighth proof-text

The apologist's eighth proof-text is Luke 5:16, where Jesus withdraws to pray. The apologist answers that the Trinity prays within itself, so Jesus praying to the Father is no problem. The Qur'an answers with the same verses used earlier. Jesus is the servant who worships Allah.

إِنِّي عَبْدُ اللَّهِ آتانِيَ الْكِتابَ وَجَعَلَنِي نَبِيّاً

inni 'abdu Allahi ataniya al-kitaba wa ja'alani nabiyyan

"I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Book and made me a prophet."

Qur'an 19:30

ما كُنْتُ أَقُولُ لَهُمْ إِلَّا ما أَمَرْتَنِي بِهِ أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ رَبِّي وَرَبَّكُمْ

ma kuntu aqulu lahum illa ma amartani bihi ani 'budu Allaha rabbi wa rabbakum

"I never told them except what You commanded me, that worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord."

Qur'an 5:117

Prayer is the act of a creature toward its Lord. Jesus prays to Allah, and the Qur'an has him command prayer to Allah. The apologist turns this into a mystery of three persons. The Qur'an turns it into submission.

9. Jesus Prostrates to His Father

Source. Qur'an 33:56, 19:30

Claim. Matthew 26:39 has Jesus fall prostrate to his Father, which the apologist answers as intimate communion.

Verse. Qur'an 33:56; 19:30

Locus. the apologist's ninth proof-text

The apologist's ninth proof-text is Matthew 26:39, where Jesus falls on his face and prays to his Father. The apologist answers that the prayer shows closeness, not weakness. The Qur'an answers by naming what kind of prayer belongs to Allah. When the Qur'an says Allah prays for the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, the word does not mean worship.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ وَمَلائِكَتَهُ يُصَلُّونَ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ

inna Allaha wa mala'ikatahu yusalluna 'ala al-nabiyyi

"Indeed, Allah and His angels pray upon the Prophet."

Qur'an 33:56

The schools of tafsir state plainly what the word means when said of Allah.

المشهور أن الصلاة من الله الرحمة

"The well-known view is that prayer from Allah is mercy."

Bihar al-Anwar, vol.82, p.279

So when the Qur'an says Allah prays, it means He shows mercy. It does not mean He worships. The apologist's counter that Allah prays therefore proves nothing about Jesus. Jesus prostrating to his Father is worship of Allah, exactly as the Qur'an presents every prophet.

10. The Only True God and Added Books

Source. Qur'an 112:1-4, 9:30

Claim. John 17:3 names the Father the only true God. Deuteronomy 4:2 forbids added books, used against the New Testament.

Verse. Qur'an 112:1-4; 9:30

Locus. the apologist's tenth proof-text and bonus

The apologist's tenth proof-text is John 17:3, where Jesus calls the Father the only true God. The bonus charges that Deuteronomy 4:2 forbids adding books, so the New Testament is unlawful. The Qur'an answers both with the same confession. Allah alone is the true God, and the son-of-God claim is named as a human error.

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُواً أَحَدٌ

qul huwa Allahu ahadun Allahu al-samadu lam yalid wa lam yulad wa lam yakun lahu kufuwan ahad

"Say, He is Allah, the One. Allah is the Eternal. He does not beget and is not begotten. And there is none comparable to Him."

Qur'an 112:1-4

وَقالَتِ النَّصارى الْمَسِيحُ ابْنُ اللَّهِ

wa qalati al-nasara al-masihu ibnu Allah

"The Christians said the Messiah is the son of God."

Qur'an 9:30

The only true God is the God who does not beget and is not begotten. The Qur'an names the Christians' son-of-God claim as a saying from their own mouths. The charge about added books is a question inside the Christian canon. It does not touch the Qur'anic thesis, which is that Allah is one and Jesus is His servant.

The Words the Dispute Turns On

Source. Qur'an 9:30, 5:73, 3:45; Bihar al-Anwar, vol.82, p.279

Claim. The contested words are ibn Allah, thaluth, masih, and salat.

Verse. Qur'an 9:30; 5:73; 3:45

Locus. the root meanings the debate turns on

Four words carry the whole dispute. The Qur'an fixes their meaning before the proof-texts are read.

ibn Allah (son of God). The root means offspring. The Qur'an rejects it for Allah and names who said it. The Christians said the Messiah is the son of God, and the Jews said Uzayr is the son of God. The true relation is stated in the same chapter. They are honored servants, not sons.

thaluth (Trinity). The Qur'an answers the word directly. It forbids saying three and calls the saying disbelief. The school reads the doctrine as a later development that entered the religion after the spread of pagan triads.

masih (Messiah). The angel names Jesus the Messiah, son of Mary. The root means the anointed one. The title is honor, not deity. The Qur'an presents him as a word from Allah and a spirit from Him, created and sent.

إِذْ قالَتِ الْمَلائِكَةُ يا مَرْيَمُ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُبَشِّرُكِ بِكَلِمَةٍ مِنْهُ اسْمُهُ الْمَسِيحُ عِيسَى ابْنُ مُرْيَمَ

idh qalati al-mala'ikatu ya maryamu inna Allaha yubashshiruki bi-kalimatin minhu ismuhu al-masihu 'isa ibnu muryama

"When the angels said, O Maryam, Allah gives you good news of a word from Him. His name is the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary."

Qur'an 3:45

salat (prayer). When said of Allah, the word means mercy and blessing, not the worship that Jesus performs. The lexical fact removes the apologist's closing counter about Allah praying.

Glossary

tawhid. The oneness of Allah, that He has no partner, son, or equal.

rasul. A messenger sent by Allah with scripture. Jesus is one of them.

abd. A servant. Jesus names himself the servant of Allah in the cradle.

ibn Allah. Son of God. The Qur'an rejects this as a name for Allah's relation to Jesus.

thaluth. The Trinity. The Qur'an commands believers not to say three.

masih. The Messiah, the anointed one. A title of honor for Jesus, not a claim of deity.

Final Verdict

The ten proof-texts fail because they argue against a position the Qur'an does not hold.

They show that Jesus prays, obeys, depends, learns, dies, and has a God. The Qur'an agrees with every one of those facts, because it presents Jesus as a servant and messenger of Allah. The Trinity needs Jesus to be God. The Qur'an says he is not.

The two rejections are explicit. The Qur'an forbids saying Allah is three, and it names the son-of-God claim as a human error. The lexical record backs both rejections. Ibn Allah is offspring language the Qur'an denies to Allah. Thaluth is named and forbidden. Masih is an honorific, not a divinity. Salat from Allah is mercy, not worship.

The apologist's method is to assume the conclusion and explain the evidence away. The Qur'an assumes the oneness of Allah and lets the evidence stand. A Jesus who worships Allah, who is raised by Allah, and who disclaims any divine claim on the Day of Judgment is the Jesus of the Qur'an. The Trinity has no hold on him.
