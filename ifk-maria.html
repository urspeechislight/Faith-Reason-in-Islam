Debate · The Verses of the Ifk

The Exoneration of Surat al-Nur Came Down for Maria

The ten verses of Surat al-Nur name a bonded kin band and a chaste, unaware, believing woman. The dictionaries, the codex reading of Hafsa, and the chains from the Imams identify that band and that woman as Maria the Copt and her accusers. The argument weighs that reading against the Sunni reading it answers. From Tafsir al-Qummi (d.329), al-Hidaya al-Kubra, al-Khisal, and Bihar al-Anwar.

The Argument Under Examination

Background

The Sunni tradition reads the ten verses of Surat al-Nur as the exoneration of Aisha over the slander spread on the return march from the Banu al-Mustaliq campaign. A second reading stands beside it in the sources of the school of the Ahl al-Bayt ﵈. Its chains from Imam al-Baqir ﵇ and Imam al-Rida ﵇identify the exonerated woman as Maria the Copt, the mother of Ibrahim. The slander is the charge that her son belonged to her Coptic servant. One reading is the occasion of the ten verses. The other is a later attachment. The argument builds the school's case from the words of the passage itself, tests the Sunni reading at its strongest, and weighs the two on the evidence both sides accept.

The argument addresses readers who accept the Quran as the word of God and weigh transmitted reports by their chains. It declares its school openly. It claims only what its sources claim. The verses came down for Maria on chains the school grades sound. The passage's own words fit Maria. They fail the woman the Sunni reading names.

Premise 1: The word the verse chooses requires kin

The verse says a band, an usba, came with the lie. The dictionaries define the usba as the man's own kin circle, his sons and his paternal relatives bound to one another. The counted numbers run from three to forty. Hafsa's own codex read the verse as a band of four. The four men and women the Sunni reading names share no kinship and no bond. The word fits a household plot. It fails a camp rumor.

Premise 2: The woman the verses describe is another woman

The exoneration verse names a chaste, unaware, believing woman. By the Sunni narration's own data the woman it names heard the rumor for a month. She was doubted by her husband and warned by two verses for the inclination of her heart. Each mark of the verse fits Maria. Each one fails her rival.

Premise 3: The school's chains name the woman

Tafsir al-Qummi carries the occasion from the Imams directly. The slander of Maria over the birth of Ibrahim, the servant found a eunuch on the wall of her chamber. The two wives pressed their fathers to bring the charge, and the verses came down for her exoneration. Al-Khasibi, al-Saduq, and al-Majlisi carry the same affair on their own lines.

Premise 4: The reading it answers carries concessions

The Sunni narration's own scholars dated its pulpit scene after the pulpit was built. They dated its questioning maid to years after she was bought. They dated its hero to a contradiction between the ascetic of the story and the husband of the record. A narration that needs these patches is weighed on its evidence. Assumption plays no part.

Conclusion: The verses came down for Maria

From the four premises together, the band of the verse is a bonded kin group. The woman of the verse is the unaware believer Maria. The school's chains name her affair as the occasion. The rival reading is a patched record. The verses of Surat al-Nur came down for Maria the Copt, and the women whom the rebuke of the two hearts in 66:4 already answers stand behind the charge against her.

1. The Word the Verse Requires

What the Dictionaries Say

Contested words.usba (Qur'an 24:11), the band that came with the lie. The Sunni reading needs the word to mean any loose group. The dictionaries fix it as a bonded kin circle. The counts, three to forty, and Hafsa's codex, a band of four, complete the field.

Witnesses.Taj al-Arus (al-Zabidi, d.1205) quoting al-Azhari; al-Qamus al-Muhit (al-Fayruzabadi, d.817); al-Nahhas (d.338); al-Qurtubi (d.671).

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ جَآءُو بِٱلْإِفْكِ عُصْبَةٌۭ مِّنكُمْ ۚ لَا تَحْسَبُوهُ شَرًّۭا لَّكُم ۖ بَلْ هُوَ خَيْرٌۭ لَّكُمْ ۚ لِكُلِّ ٱمْرِئٍۢ مِّنْهُم مَّا ٱكْتَسَبَ مِنَ ٱلْإِثْمِ ۚ وَٱلَّذِى تَوَلَّىٰ كِبْرَهُۥ مِنْهُمْ لَهُۥ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌۭ

inna alladhīna jāʾū bil-ifki ʿuṣbatun minkum..

"Those who came with the lie are a band of you. Do not think it evil for you. It is good for you.."

- Qur'an 24:11, Uthmani text

The verse itself fixes the frame before any narration is opened. A band of you, one group with one deed, came with the lie. The word the Quran chose for that group is the whole question, and the dictionaries settle it first because the verse stands on their ground.

The master dictionary of the later tradition, the Taj al-Arus, opens the entry with kinship and holds kinship throughout.

والعَصَبَةُ مُحَرَّكَةً : هم الَّذِينَ يَرِثُونَ الرَّجُلَ عن كَلاَلَةٍ من غَيْرِ وَالِدٍ وَلاَ وَلَدٍ . وعَصَبَةُ الرَّجُل : بَنُوه وقَرَابَتُه لأَبِيه . وفي التهذِيبِ : ولم أَسْمَعْ للعَصَبَةِ بِوَاحِد ، والقِيَاسُ أَن يكُون عَاصِباً ، مثلَ طَالِبٍ وطَلَبَة ، وظَالِم وظَلَمَة فأَمَّا فِي الفَرَائِضِ فكُلُّ مَنْ لَمْ يَكُن لَه فَرِيضَةٌ مُسَمَّاةٌ فهو عَصَبَةٌ إِنْ بَقِيَ شَيْءٌ بَعْدَ الفَرَائِض أَخَذَ ، هذَا رَأْيُ أَهْلِ الفَرَائِض والفُقَهَاء عند أَئمة اللغة : العَصَبَة : قَوْمُ الرَّجُل الَّذِين يَتَعَصَّبُون لَه ، كأَنّه على حَذْفِ الزَّائد ، وقِيل : العَصَبَة : الأَقَارِبُ مِن جِهَة الأَب ؛ لأَنهم يَعْصِبُونَه ويَعْتَصِب بِهِم أَي يُحِيطون به ويَشْتَدُّ بهم .

وقال الأَزْهَرِيّ : عَصَبَةُ الرَّجُلِ : أَولياؤُه الذُّكُور من وَرَثَتِهِ ، سُمُّوا عَصَبةً لأَنَّهُم عَصَبُوا بِنَسَبِه أَي استَكَفُّوا بِهِ ؛ فالأَبُ طَرَفٌ ، والابْنُ طَرَفٌ ، والعَمُّ جَانِبٌ ، والأَخُ جَانبٌ ، والجَمْعُ العَصَبَاتُ

Al-usba (with fatha on the a and the b) means those who inherit a man by kalala, from neither father nor son. A man's usba is his sons and his paternal relatives.

In al-Tahdhib, I have not heard a singular for usba, and the analogy is that it be asib, like talib and talaba, or zalim and zalama. As for inheritance law, everyone without a named share is usba.

If anything remains after the shares, he takes it. This is the view of the inheritance jurists and the legal scholars.

According to the language authorities, al-usba is a man's people who bind themselves to him, as though the word is on the pattern of a deleted addition. It is said that al-usba is the near relatives from the father's side, because they surround him and he draws strength from them.

Al-Azhari said, a man's usba is his male guardians among his heirs, called usba because they surround him by lineage. The father is one end, the son is another end, the paternal uncle is one side, the brother is another side, and the plural is usabat.

- al-Zabidi (d.1205), Taj al-Arus, vol 2, p 238, quoting al-Azhari's Tahdhib

So the definition is fixed on the dictionary's own terms. The usba is the man's circle of kin, his sons and his father's side, the heirs who stand around him, the uncles as sides and the sons as ends of the line. Al-Fayruzabadi's Qamus, the compact classical dictionary that the Taj expands, carries the same ruling in the same order, the inheritance kin first, then the bonded group, then the counted number.

والعصبة ، محركة : الذين يرثون الرجل عن كلالة من غير والد ولا ولد ، فأما في الفرائض : فكل من لم يكن له فريضة مسماة فهو : عصبة ، إن بقي شئ بعد الفرض أخذ ، وقوم الرجل الذين يتعصبون له . والعصبة ، بالضم ، من الرجال والخيل والطير : ما بين العشرة إلى الأربعين ، كالعصابة ، بالكسر ، وهنة تلتف على القتادة لا تنزع عنها إلا بجهد .

Al-usba (with fatha) means those who inherit a man by kalala, from neither father nor son. As for the inheritance shares, everyone without a named share is usba.

If anything remains after the shares, he takes it. And it means a man's people who bind themselves to him.

Al-usba (with damma), of men, horses, and birds, from ten to forty, like al-usaba (with kasra).

- al-Fayruzabadi (d.817), al-Qamus al-Muhit, vol 1, p 147

The counts complete the lexical field. The early authorities differ on the number. All of them agree on the bond. Ibn Uyayna says forty, Mujahid ten to fifteen, Ibn Abbas three and three to ten. The language authorities of the tafsir define the word as a group whose members bind themselves to one another.

قال ابن عيينة : * ( العصبة ) * : أربعون رجلا . وقال مجاهد : * ( العصبة ) * : من العشرة إلى الخمسة عشر . قال أبو جعفر : العصبة في اللغة : الجماعة الذين يتعصب بعضهم لبعض .

Ibn Uyaynah said, An asabah is forty men. Mujahid said, An asabah is from ten to fifteen men. Abu Ja'far said.

In Arabic, an asabah is a group whose members support one another.

- al-Nahhas (d.338), Maani al-Quran, vol 5, p 198

The Quran's own usage seals the sense. In the chapter of Yusuf the word names eleven sons of one father.

إِذْ قَالُوا۟ لَيُوسُفُ وَأَخُوهُ أَحَبُّ إِلَىٰٓ أَبِينَا مِنَّا وَنَحْنُ عُصْبَةٌ إِنَّ أَبَانَا لَفِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍ

idh qālū layūsufu wa-akhūhu aḥabbu ilā abīnā minnā wa-naḥnu ʿuṣbah..

"When they said, Joseph and his brother are dearer to our father than we, though we are a strict band. Our father is in clear error."

- Qur'an 12:8, Uthmani text

No verse of the classical poets carrying the word in the kin sense survives in the record the dictionaries preserve. The case stands on the dictionaries, the Quran's own usage, and the Prophet's usage of the word. Eleven brothers of one blood call themselves an usba. Scripture used the word for a kin circle before the verse of the Ifk was revealed.

The Prophet's own usage seals it. He used the word for his daughter.

قال النبي ﷺ: لكل نبي عصبة ينتمون إليه وإن فاطمة عصبتي التي تنتمي

The Prophet said, for every prophet there is a kin-band to whom they trace. Fatima is my kin-band to whom I trace.

- Bihar al-Anwar, vol 43, p 232, from Ibn Abi Shayba, from Fatima the Younger, from Fatima al-Zahra

Asabah serves as the predicate of inna. It may also appear in the accusative as an adverbial phrase. Then the predicate is, Each of them bears the sin he has earned. Ibn Abbas said, An asabah is three men.

Another report from him says it ranges from three to ten men. Ibn Uyaynah said, It is forty men. Mujahid said, It ranges from ten to fifteen men.

In Arabic language and speech, its root meaning is a group whose members support one another. The codex of Hafsa reads, An asabah of four.

- al-Qurtubi (d.671), al-Jami li-Ahkam al-Quran, vol 12, p 198-199

The definition of the tafsir agrees with the dictionaries, the origin of the word is the group whose members bind themselves to each other. Then the codex reading names the number four. Four accusers acting as one body.

The four the school's chains name are the two wives and their two fathers.The count of four is the strongest fact in the field, and the school's reading accounts for it without effort, one household line on each side, Aisha and Abu Bakr, Hafsa and Umar. The Sunni reading's four, a Khazraji chief, an Awsite poet, a poor emigrant of Taym, and a woman of Asad, share nothing. One of them, Ibn Ubayy, was the open enemy of both families of the others' emigrants. The dictionaries' bonded kin circle breaks on four strangers.

What the word proves.The verse, read by its own vocabulary, announces a coordinated accusation from one bonded circle. The Quran also says the band is of you, from inside the community, and that one of them took on the greatest part of it. The lexical evidence leaves the circle unnamed so far. It fixes the kind of circle the verse names. The kind is kin. The next section shows that the verses exonerate a woman other than the woman of the Sunni story, and the section after names the circle on the school's chains.

2. The Woman the Verses Describe

Chaste, Unaware, Believing

The exoneration verse paints the woman it defends with three marks. She is muhsana, chaste and guarded. She is ghafila, unaware, the slander reaching her without her sensing its spread. She is muʾmina, a believer whole in faith. The verse then adds that the men who slandered her are cursed in this world and the next, and for them is a painful punishment, a ruling with no repentance clause attached to it. Read the verse against the Sunni narration's own data, and the woman it describes fails every mark.

إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَرْمُونَ ٱلْمُحْصَنَٰتِ ٱلْغَٰفِلَٰتِ ٱلْمُؤْمِنَٰتِ لُعِنُوا۟ فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا وَٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌۭ

inna alladhīna yarmūna al-muḥṣanāti al-ghāfilāti al-muʾmināti luʿinū fi-d-dunyā wa-l-ākhirah..

"Those who slander chaste, unaware, believing women are cursed in this world and the next, and for them is a great punishment."

- Qur'an 24:23, Uthmani text

The unawareness fails first. In the Sunni narration Aisha hears the rumor from Umm Mistah herself, on her own account, grieves over it for nights, and answers her mother about it in her mother's house. The affair runs a month with her at its center. A woman who weeps for nights over a word is a woman aware of the slander. Ghafila names a woman who never sensed it coming. The mark fits a woman secluded in her chamber, whose slander was arranged in her absence and exposed by a single inspection. Maria never knew of the charge against her until the Prophet ﷺ sent Ali ﵇to her chamber.

The faith mark fails next. The Quran itself bears witness against the inclination of the two wives' hearts, in a verse the Sunni canon also carries.

إِن تَتُوبَآ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ فَقَدْ صَغَتْ قُلُوبُكُمَا ۖ وَإِن تَظَٰهَرَا عَلَيْهِ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ مَوْلَىٰهُ وَجِبْرِيلُ وَصَٰلِحُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ ۖ وَٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ بَعْدَ ذَٰلِكَ ظَهِيرٌ

in tatūbā ila-llāhi fa-qad ṣaghat qulūbukumā..

"If you two repent to God, then your hearts have inclined. And if you two back each other against him, then God is his protector, and Jibril and the righteous of the believers, and the angels after them are backers."

- Qur'an 66:4, Uthmani text

The verse addresses two women jointly. It offers them repentance for an inclination of the heart, and it warns them against backing each other against the Prophet ﷺ. The Sunni tradition itself names the two as Aisha and Hafsa and ties the verse to the affair of the honey and the chamber. The school's chains tie the same two to the slander of Maria. Either way the Quran's own witness stands. The two hearts inclined, and the two acted together.

The doubt of the Prophet ﷺfails the third mark from the other side. In the Sunni narration he spends a month not knowing. He says to his wife on the narration's own tongue that if she committed a wrong she should repent to God. He stands at the pulpit asking the people for judgment. The narration gives him a month of suspicion over the woman it calls the exoneration's object.

ذلك على ما عندي قالت وأتاني فقال اتقي الله يا عائشة وإن كنت قارفت من هذا شيئا فتوبي إلى الله فإن الله يقبل التوبة عن عباده ويعفو عن السيئات قال وطلبت اسم يعقوب فلم أقدر عليه فقلت غير أني أقول كما قال أبو يوسف فصبر جميل والله المستعان على ما تصفون إنما أشكو بثي وحزني إلى الله وأعلم من الله ما لا تعلمون قالت فبينا رسول الله مع أصحابه ووجهه كأنما ذيب عليه الزرنيخ حتى نزل عليه الوحي وكان إذا أوحي إليه لم يطرف فعرف أصحابه أنه يوحى إليه وجعلوا ينظرون إلى وجهه وهو يتهلل ويسفر فلما قضي الوحي قال أبشر يا أبا بكر قد أنزل الله عذر ابنتك وبراءتها فانطلق إليها فبشرها قالت وقرأ عليه ما نزل في قالت وأقبل أبو بكر مسرعا يكاد أن ينكب قالت فقلت بحمد الله لا بحمد صاحبك الذي جئت من عنده فجاء رسول الله فجلس عند ر

She said, while I was unaware of it, he came to me and said, fear God, O Aisha, and if you have committed anything of this, repent to God.

God accepts repentance from His servants and pardons evil deeds. She said, I searched for the name of Yaqub and could not find it.

- al-Tabarani, al-Muʿjam al-Kabir, vol 23, p 118, the al-Aswad recension

Fear God, and if you have committed anything of this, repent to God. That is the husband's own word in the narration's own text, and it is the narration's own confession that the Prophet ﷺheld the accusation open against her for a month. The exoneration verse of the school's reading describes a woman cleared before she ever knew she was accused, and it curses her accusers without leaving them a door of repentance. That verse fits Maria. The narration's woman was under month-long suspicion, counselled to repent, and only then declared innocent.

3. The Chains from the Imams

The Occasion on the School's Own Lines

The school's tafsir records the fork openly, the general reading of the many and the special reading of the school, and then gives the occasion on a chain to the Imam.

The Counsel the Sunni Record Itself Keeps

The Sunni tradition's own record of the counsel scene confirms the reading from its side. When the slander of his wife reached the Prophet ﷺ, he asked counsel, and the counsel preserved on the soundest line of the Sunni books of maghazi is Ali's ﵇.

The Messenger listened to him and acted on his advice. This proves that Ali was right and that Aisha was mistaken to hate and oppose him for it.

The women are many, ask the maidservant. That is the counsel on a chain al-Mufid grades sound, clear in its path, noble in its narrators. The narration's own close carries the fruit of it in her own voice. She never loved Ali ﵇after that day, and asked why he and his companion sat alone with her maid questioning her. The counsel scene in the Sunni record is the counsel of a man treating an accusation as a charge to be tested, and the record of the school explains what the test was for. The testing of maids belongs to the Maria affair, where one servant's body ended the question in a single inspection. The counsel story and the chamber story are one story told twice, and the school's telling holds together without patches.

4. The Sunni Case at Its Strongest

The Weight of the Many Books

The Sunni case rests on the sheer spread of its chains. The narration of the Ifk runs from al-Zuhri, who compiled it from four informants, into the Sahih books, the sunan, the musnads, the tafsirs, and the sira, on more than thirty early lines. Its strongest defender made the argument from sheer spread, and his words deserve their strongest form.

وقذفت بصفوان بن المعطل السلمي في سنة ست ، منصرف رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله من غزاة بنى المصطلق - وكانت معه - فقال فيها أهل الإفك ما قالوا ، ونزل القرآن ببراءتها . وقوم من الشيعة زعموا أن الآيات التي في سورة النور لم تنزل فيها ، وإنما أنزلت في مارية القبطية ، وما قذفت به مع الأسود القبطي ، وجحدهم لإنزال ذلك في عائشة جحد لما يعلم ضرورة من الأخبار المتواترة . ثم كان من أمرها وأمر حفصة وما جرى لهما مع رسول الله ﵌في الامر الذي أسره على إحداهما ما قد نطق الكتاب العزيز به .

واعتزل رسول الله ﵌ نساءه كلهن ، واعتزلهما معهن ثم صالحهن ، وطلق حفصة ثم راجعها ، وجرت بين عائشة وفاطمة إبلاغات ، وحديث يوغر الصدور ، فتولد بين عائشة وبين علي ﵇ نوع ضغينة ، وانضم إلى ذلك إشارته على رسول الله ﵌ في قصة الإفك بضرب الجارية وتقريرها وقوله (إن النساء كثير) . ثم جرى حديث صلاة أبى بكر بالناس ، فتزعم الشيعة أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله لم يأمر بذلك ، وإنه إنما صلى بالناس عن أمر عائشة ابنته ، وأن رسول الله ﵌ خرج متحاملا وهو مثقل ، فنحاه عن المحراب . وزعم معظم المحدثين أن ذلك كان عن أمر رسول الله ﵌ وقوله ، ثم اختلفوا ، فمنهم من قال : نحاه وصلى هو بالناس ، ومنهم من قال : بل ائتم بأبي بكر كسائر الناس ، ومنهم من قال : كان الناس يصلون بصلاة أبى بكر ، وأبو بكر يصلى بصلاة رسول الله ﵌.

ثم كان منها في أمر عثمان ،

In the sixth year, after the Messenger of Allah returned from the campaign against Banu al-Mustaliq, Aisha was accused with Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal al-Sulami. She had accompanied the Messenger of Allah on that campaign.

The people of the slander said what they said about her, and the Qur'an came down declaring her innocence. Some Shia claim that the verses in Surat al-Nur did not come down about Aisha.

They say they came down about Mariyah the Copt and her alleged affair with al-Aswad the Copt. Their denial that these verses came down about Aisha rejects what the mass-transmitted reports establish beyond doubt.

The Qur'an also speaks of what happened between Aisha. Hafsah, and the Messenger of Allah concerning the matter that he confided to one of them.

The Messenger of Allah withdrew from all his wives, including Aisha and Hafsah. He later reconciled with them. He divorced Hafsah and then took her back.

Bitter messages and words that stirred resentment passed between Aisha and Fatimah. This produced a measure of hostility between Aisha and Ali.

Ali had also advised the Messenger of Allah during the slander affair to strike the servant girl and question her closely. He said, "There are many women." Then came the report of Abu Bakr leading the people in prayer.

The Shia claim that the Messenger of Allah did not order this. They say Abu Bakr led the people because of his daughter Aisha's order.

They say the Messenger of Allah came out leaning on others because of his severe illness and moved Abu Bakr away from the prayer niche. Most hadith scholars say that the Messenger of Allah ordered Abu Bakr to lead.

They then disagreed about what followed. Some say he moved Abu Bakr away and led the people himself. Some say he prayed behind Abu Bakr as the other people did.

Some say the people followed Abu Bakr's prayer, while Abu Bakr followed the Messenger of Allah's prayer. Then her part in the affair of Uthman followed.

- Ibn Abi al-Hadid (d.656), Sharh Nahj al-Balagha, vol 14, p 23

That is the real argument. Denying that the verses came down for Aisha, he says, is denying what is known by necessity from the mass-transmitted reports. The spread of a report through many books is the Sunni criterion of tawatur, and the challenge is honest and must be met head on.

It is met with the criterion's own rules. Multiplicity of books is one thing. Multiplicity of chains back to the event is another. The narration's thirty lines converge on one compiler in one city, al-Zuhri in Medina, who says on his own tongue that four men each told him a portion. Every later book takes the text from that one compilation. A report is mass-transmitted when its early lines are independent, and here they are branches of a single tree. The school's chains, by contrast, run from the Imams ﵈through separate lines, Kufan and Qummi and Baghdadi, and they bypass al-Zuhri entirely. If spread of books is the test, both readings have it. If independence of early lines is the test, only one of them does.

The heresiographical ruling shows what the Sunni case builds on that base, and it belongs to the strongest form too, because the school's reading has borne this ruling's weight for centuries.

ما نزلت قال أبو بكر بلى والله يا ربنا إنا لنحب أن تغفر لنا وعاد له بما كان يصنع أي ينفقه عليه وفي رواية للبخاري أيضا عنها في حديث الإفك الطويل وأنزل الله تعالى إن الذين جاءوا بالإفك عصبة منكم العشر الآيات كلها فلما أنزل الله هذا في براءتي قال أبو بكر الصديق ﵁وكان ينفق على مسطح بن أثاثة لقرابته منه وفقره والله لا أنفق على مسطح شيئا أبدا بعد الذي قال في عائشة ما قال فأنزل الله ولا يأتل أولو الفضل منكم والسعة وذكرت الآية السابقة ثم قالت قال أبو بكر بلى والله إني لأحب أن يغفر الله لي فرجع إلى مسطح النفقة التي كان ينفق عليه وقال والله لا أنزعها منه أبدا تنبيه علم من حديث الإفك المشار إليه أن من نسب عائشة إلى الزنا كان كافرا وهو ما صرح به أئمتنا وغيرهم لأن في ذلك تكذيب النصوص القرآنية ومكذبها كافر بإجماع المسلمين وبه يعلم القطع بكفر كثيرين من غلاة الروافض لأنهم ينسبونها إلى ذلك قاتلهم الله أنى يؤفكون

From the hadith of the Ifk one learns that whoever attributes zina to Aisha is an unbeliever. as the imams have stated explicitly, because he belies the Quranic texts whose denier is an unbeliever by consensus.

From this follows the certainty that many of the extremist Rafida are unbelievers.

- Ibn Hajar al-Haytami (d.974), al-Sawaʾiq al-Muhriqa, on the lesson of the Ifk narration

So the ruling runs. Whoever attributes zina to Aisha is an unbeliever, and from it follows the certainty that many of the extremist Rafida are unbelievers. The ruling turns the narration into a creed. That is why the reading of the verses matters beyond history, and why the narration's own record deserves the audit that follows.

The Record Under Audit

The narration's own scholars, not its opponents, supply the audit. The pulpit scene is the first concession. The pulpit on which the narration sets the Prophet ﷺwas built in year eight by the record of the tradition's own chronology. That is two years or more after the campaign of year six.

ع في المسجد قائما ، فقال : إن القيام قد شق علىّ ، فصنع له المنبر . وكان عمله وحنين الجذع في السنة الثامنة - بالميم - من الهجرة ، وبه جزم ابن النجار وعورض : بما في حديث الإفك في الصحيحين ، قالت عائشة : فثار الحيان - الأوس والخزرج - حتى كادوا أن يقتتلوا ورسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم - على المنبر فنزل فخفضهم حتى سكتوا (1) . وجزم ابن سعد بأن عمل المنبر كان في السنة السابعة .

وعورض : بذكر العباس وتميم فيه ، وكان قدوم العباس بعد الفتح في آخر سنة ثمان ، وقدوم سنة تسع . وعن بعض أهل السير : أنه - عليه السّلام - كان يخطب على منبر من طين قبل أن يتخذ المنبر الذي من خشب . وعورض : بأن الأحاديث الصحيحة أنه كان يستند إلى الجذع إذا خطب .

He stood in the mosque praying, and said, standing has become difficult for me. So the pulpit was made for him.

Its construction and the yearning of the palm trunk were in year eight of the hijra, and Ibn al-Najjar was certain of this. It was objected to by what is in the hadith of the Ifk in the two Sahihs.

where Aisha said, the two clans rose up, Aws and Khazraj, until they almost fought, while the Messenger of Allah was on the pulpit. He came down and calmed them until they were silent.

According to some biographers, he used to preach on a pulpit of earth before the pulpit was made.

- al-Qastallani (d.923), al-Mawahib al-Laduniyya, vol 1, p 210

The tradition's own answer is that he stood on something else. The concession is the point. The narration's stage furniture is dated after its scene, and the harmonizers move the furniture.

The witness is the second concession. The narration makes Barira, Aisha's freedwoman, the maid questioned in the affair, while the tradition's own record buys Barira years after the campaign, and the narration's own scholars call her naming there an error. The Prophet's ﷺcounsel to ask the maidservant belongs, on the school's chains, to the chamber of Maria, where the maidservant questioned was Jurayh, and the question was answered by his body.

The hero is the third concession. The narration's Safwan is the man who never uncovered a woman, the continent ascetic of the rearguard. The tradition's own record of the same Safwan is a young husband whose wife stood in the mosque of the Prophet ﷺto complain of him.

The narration's ascetic and the record's husband are two men in one name, and the harmonizers' answer, that he married afterwards, patches the seam with a marriage the narration never mentions.

The oath is the fourth concession, and it comes from the narration's own mouth. When the verses came down and her parents told her to rise to the Prophet ﷺ, she answered with the words the tradition preserves.

You have spoken of it and it has settled in your hearts. Those are her words to her own parents, and the parents are the ones who relayed the slander to the Prophet ﷺin the first days of the month. The narration's own text makes the household a carrier of the word, and the oath indicts the same circle the school's chains name.

What the audit leaves.None of these concessions touches the Quran. They touch one narration, compiled by one man, whose own scholars dated its furniture, corrected its witnesses, and harmonized its hero. The school does not read these seams as forgeries. It reads them as the signs of a story moved from its original house, the house of Maria, onto a different address, the address of the narrator's patrons. The verses stayed in place, and the verses are the evidence. The band of kin fits the two households. The unaware believing woman fits Maria. The cursed accusers without repentance fit the two pairs whom another verse already called to repentance for their hearts' inclination.

5. The Two Readings Weighed

Four Tests, Both Readings

Textual fidelity.Which reading fits the verse's own words without strain.

Coherence.Which reading leaves the Quran's portrait of its persons intact.

Economy.Which reading needs fewer patches to stand.

Authority.Which reading's early lines are independent.

Textual fidelity.The verse says a bonded kin band of four, Hafsa's codex agreeing, and the school names a four of two households joined by the closest blood. The Sunni reading names four strangers and reads the band loose. The verse says the woman was unaware and believing. The school's woman was secluded and never sensed the charge until the inspection. The Sunni narration's woman wept over the rumor for a month and was counselled to repent by her husband. On the verse's own words the school's reading fits without a strain and the Sunni reading fits with three.

Coherence. The Sunni reading gives the Prophet ﷺ a month of open suspicion toward the woman the Quran calls unaware and believing. It also gives the exoneration verse, whose ruling carries no repentance clause, to a woman whom another verse already summoned to repentance for her heart. The school's reading keeps the Prophet ﷺthe tester of the charge and never the doubter of innocence. It keeps the unaware woman truly unaware. It keeps the accusers exactly where 66:4 leaves them, two hearts inclined and summoned to repent. The school's portrait and the Quran's agree. The Sunni portrait and the Quran's compete.

Economy.The Sunni reading needs the pulpit redated, the maid corrected, the hero given a second life, the month of doubt explained, and the counsel scene given a purpose the narration never states. The school's reading needs none of these. Its one assumption is that its own chains are true, and every reading of the affair assumes some chains to be true.

Authority. The Sunni narration's thirty lines converge on one compiler in one city. The school's chains run from the Imams ﵈on separate lines that never pass through that compiler. Its earliest source, al-Qummi, is a century closer to the event than the books that mass-printed the rival. By the criterion the Sunni case itself invokes, independence of transmission, the weighing goes to the school.

The balance.Four tests, four to the school's reading. The verdict gathers them.

Final Verdict

The words of the passage decide it before the chains are counted. The usba of the verse is a bonded kin circle, and Hafsa's own codex counted it four. The woman of the verse is chaste, unaware, and whole in faith. Only one woman in the record answers to all three, Maria the Copt, slandered in her chamber over the birth of her son.

The school's chains from the Imams name that woman and her accusers, two wives moving their two fathers, and the Quran's own rebuke of the two hearts in 66:4 stands over them. The Sunni reading rests on one compilation converging through many books. It needs its own scholars to redate its pulpit, correct its maid, and harmonize its hero. It ends by giving its master a month of suspicion that the verses themselves deny.

The verses of Surat al-Nur came down for Maria. The house that arranged the charge against her is named in the codex reading of its own daughter, and the inclination of its hearts is named by God. The reading that moved the verses to another address carried them into a story its own record cannot hold together. The record itself, read by its own concessions, points back to the house where the Quran first placed it.
