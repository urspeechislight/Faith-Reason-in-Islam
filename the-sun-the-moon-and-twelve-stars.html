The Sun, the Moon, and Twelve Stars

Imami reports associate the sun and moon with Muhammad and Ali, count twelve Imams, and describe the Mahdi as a ruler of justice. Later writers use these reports to interpret Revelation 12 and other biblical passages.

Revelation 12:1 describes a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon beneath her feet and a crown of twelve stars. Genesis 17:20 promises Ishmael twelve princes. Isaiah 11:1-9, Psalm 72, and Daniel 7:13-14 describe righteous rule and dominion.

The Qur'an swears by a sun and by a moon that follows it (Qur'an 91:1-2), and the Imams' transmitted tafsir names that sun as the Prophet ﷺ and that moon as Ali ﵇.

Shia and Sunni collections report twelve successors, and a report cited from Ahmad's Musnad compares their number with the twelve chiefs of the Children of Israel. Imami reports name Ali as the first Imam and the Mahdi as the last; eleven of the twelve are Fatimah's descendants.

Sunni reports identify a Mahdi descended from Fatimah ﵍ and separately describe Jesus's ﵇ descent with an imam from the Muslim community. The quoted Imami reports expressly identify the leader behind whom Jesus prays as the Mahdi or Qa'im.

Biblical images and the Prophet's family

Revelation 12 and Genesis 17 do not name Muhammad ﷺ, Ali ﵇, Fatimah ﵍, or the twelve Imams. Identifying the biblical figures with members of the Prophet's family depends on parallels with Islamic reports.

Catholic readings of the woman

Pope Pius XII, defining the Assumption of Mary in 1950, wrote that the scholastic doctors recognized the Assumption as signified in "that woman clothed with the sun whom John the Apostle contemplated on the Island of Patmos" (Munificentissimus Deus 27). The Catholic Encyclopedia states, "The woman in travail is a personification of the synagogue or the church. Her first-born is Christ, her other seed is the community of the faithful" (Van den Biesen, "Apocalypse," 1907). The same article connects the sun, moon, and twelve stars with Joseph's dream in Genesis 37:9-10.

In Joseph's dream, the sun, moon, and eleven stars bow to him (Genesis 37:9-10). Revelation has twelve stars and depicts a woman with a child.

- The Prophet, Kifayat al-Athar, pp. 65-66, Salman's report

Salman's report lists twelve successors and compares their number with Jacob's tribes and Jesus's apostles. Its celestial images distinguish Ali as the moon, Hasan and Husayn as the two Farqads, and nine later Imams as shining stars. Revelation instead gives the woman a crown of twelve stars. Identifying that crown with all twelve Imams therefore assigns Ali a place among the stars as well as the moon. The quoted Islamic reports do not make that identification.

Al-Qummi's report from al-Baqir interprets Joseph's dream through Joseph's own family.

The sun represents Joseph's mother, the moon Jacob, and the eleven stars his brothers.

The Gospel foretells the twelve Imams.

In the eleventh chapter, the following occurs among the prophecies of John the Theologian.

"A great sign appeared in heaven, a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon beneath her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars."

It is as though he is speaking of our lady Fatima al-Zahra. She was clothed with the sun, meaning her father Muhammad, and married to the moon, who is Ali ibn Abi Talib. Upon her head is a crown from her twelve infallible sons, the first of whom are al-Hasan and al-Husayn and the last of whom is the Mahdi.

Among what John the Theologian foretold would occur, there is nothing resembling what he mentioned except Fatima, her father, her husband, and her sons.

- Labib Baydun, Mawsu'at Karbala, vol. 1, p. 131

Baydun describes the crown as Fatimah's twelve infallible sons, beginning with Hasan and Husayn. The Imami sequence quoted here contains eleven of her descendants and her husband, Ali, so his wording does not match that count. Baydun cites chapter 11 for a quotation from Revelation 12:1. Al-Yazdi al-Ha'iri gives a longer interpretation in Ilzam al-Nasib. His paraphrase includes mountains being lowered and rough places made smooth, clauses absent from Revelation 12.

The seventeenth glad tiding concerning the support of the community is from the Revelations of John, in chapter twelve, verse one. Its translation is as follows. "A sign appeared in heaven. It was a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon beneath her feet and a crown of twelve stars upon her head. While she was pregnant, behold, a serpent will be filled, and every mountain and hill will be lowered, the crooked places will be made straight, and the rough places will become smooth. Those stars are upon the earth, and the serpent stands before the woman who is pregnant and about to give birth, in order to take her child after she gives birth. So she gave birth to a sound male, who rules all the groups with an iron rod. He was carried away and taken to God, and reached His abode and throne." End.

The author said, "What is meant by the woman is Fatimah al-Zahra, for her light surpassed the sun. The moon is beneath her foot, and she is higher and more majestic. Indeed, its light is one part of thousands of parts of portions of her light and is acquired from her. The crown containing the twelve constellations is the twelve Imams, peace be upon them all.

What is meant by that serpent is the tree of Banu Umayyah, the wicked tree. It killed the shining spiritual stars. What is meant by the child is the hidden Imam, the Qa'im. Since the enemies wanted to kill him, he concealed himself and was veiled from sight. The iron rod is a metaphor for the sword. He will fight the groups upon the face of the earth with the sword, fill the earth with equity and justice, and the structure of unbelief, error, and oppression will vanish and collapse. Jesus the Messiah is not meant, because he never fought. Rather, the Messiah was a manifestation of mercy.

It is possible that the twelve stars mean Muhammad, Ali, and ten of his sons, and that the moon is a metaphor for the Qa'im. It does not apply to Mary, Jesus, and the disciples, because although they were twelve, one of them apostatized, which conflicts with the known number."

Twelve caliphs in the Sunni canon

Sahih Muslim transmits Jabir ibn Samura's report of twelve caliphs from Quraysh through several chains.

حدثنا قتيبة بن سعيد حدثنا جرير عن حصين عن جابر بن سمرة قال سمعت النبي ﵌ يقول ح وحدثنا رفاعة بن الهيثم الواسطي (واللفظ له) حدثنا خالد (يعنى ابن عبد الله الطحان) عن حصين عن جابر بن سمرة قال دخلت مع أبي على النبي ﵌ فسمعته يقول إن هذا الامر لا ينقضي حتى يمضى فيهم اثنا عشر خليفة قال ثم تكلم بكلام خفى على قال فقلت لأبي ما قال قال كلهم من قريش حدثنا ابن أبي عمر حدثنا سفيان عن عبد الملك بن عمير عن جابر بن سمرة قال سمعت النبي ﵌ يقول لا يزال امر الناس ماضيا ما وليهم اثنا عشر رجلا ثم تكلم النبي ﷺ بكلمة خفيت على فسألت أبى ماذا قال رسول الله ﷺ فقال كلهم من قريش

Qutaybah ibn Sa'id narrated to us. Jarir narrated to us from Husayn, from Jabir ibn Samurah, who said, "I heard the Prophet say."

It was also narrated to us by Rifa'ah ibn al-Haytham al-Wasiti, and this is his wording. Khalid, meaning Ibn Abd Allah al-Tahhan, narrated to us from Husayn, from Jabir ibn Samurah, who said, "I entered with my father upon the Prophet, and I heard him say, 'This matter will not end until twelve caliphs have passed among them.' He then spoke words that were indistinct to me. I said to my father, 'What did he say?' He said, 'All of them are from Quraysh.'"

Ibn Abi Umar narrated to us. Sufyan narrated to us from Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr, from Jabir ibn Samurah, who said, "I heard the Prophet say, 'The affairs of the people will continue as long as twelve men rule them.' Then the Prophet spoke a word that was indistinct to me, so I asked my father, 'What did the Messenger of God say?' He said, 'All of them are from Quraysh.'"

- The Prophet, Sahih Muslim, Kitab al-Imara, Jabir ibn Samura's reports

In Abu Dawud's chapter on the Mahdi, the quoted report gives the ruler's father the Prophet's father's name. This does not match the Imami identification of the Mahdi as the son of al-Hasan. Al-Kanji, as quoted in Sharh Ihqaq al-Haqq, discusses attempts to reconcile the clause, calls that interpretation strained, and appeals to Ahmad's transmissions without it (vol. 33, pp. 943-944).

His statement, "This matter will not end until twelve caliphs have passed among them, all of them from Quraysh." In one version, "The affairs of the people will continue as long as twelve men rule them, all of them from Quraysh." In another version, "Islam will remain mighty until twelve caliphs, all of them from Quraysh."

Al-Qadi said that two questions arise here. The first is that another report states, "The caliphate after me will last thirty years, then there will be kingship." This conflicts with the report of twelve caliphs, for within thirty years there were only the four Rightly Guided Caliphs and the months during which Hasan ibn Ali received allegiance.

He said that the answer is that the caliphate meant in the report of thirty years is the caliphate of prophethood. This is explained in some versions, "The caliphate of prophethood after me will last thirty years, then there will be kingship." This was not stipulated regarding the twelve.

The second question is that more than this number have ruled. He said that this objection is invalid, because he did not say that only twelve caliphs would rule. Rather, he said that they would rule. This number has ruled, and it does not matter that others were found after them.

- Al-Nawawi, Sharh Sahih Muslim, vol. 12, pp. 200-201

Al-Nawawi quotes al-Qadi Iyad's distinction between the thirty years of prophetic caliphate and the twelve rulers, for whom no such condition is stated. Al-Qadi also argues that a prediction of twelve rulers permits later rulers. Kanz al-Ummal transmits a report attributed to Ibn Abbas. It places al-Mansur, al-Saffah, and the Mahdi after twelve commanders. The Mahdi would then hand authority to Jesus.

39657 عن سعيد بن جبير قال : سمعنا ابن عباس ونحن نقول : اثنا عشر أميرا ثم لا أمير ، واثنا عشر أميرا ثم هي الساعة ، فقال : ما أحمقكم ! إن منا أهل البيت بعد ذلك : المنصور والسفاح والمهدي يدفعها إلى عيسى ابن مريم (كر) .

Sa'id ibn Jubayr said, "Ibn Abbas heard us saying, 'Twelve commanders, then no commander. Twelve commanders, then the Hour.' He said, 'How foolish you are. After that, among us, the people of the House, are al-Mansur, al-Saffah, and al-Mahdi. He will hand it over to Jesus son of Mary.'"

- Ibn Abbas's rebuke, Kanz al-Ummal, vol. 14, entry 39657

Final Verdict

Final Verdict

These biblical passages and Imami reports share celestial imagery, a count of twelve, and descriptions of just rule. Applying the biblical figures to Fatimah ﵍, the Imams, and the Mahdi remains an interpretation. The twelve-star crown differs from the Islamic reports' distribution of symbols, Genesis identifies Ishmael's twelve princes as his sons, and Isaiah and Jeremiah specify Jesse's or David's line.
