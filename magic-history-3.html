Moses against the Sorcerers of Pharaoh

Pharaoh and Haman had themselves learned sorcery and ruled by it, and when Moses ﵇ stood before them the kingdom's sorcerers met his staff with the whole of their craft and came back believers. From al-Jaza'iri's al-Nur al-Mubin, al-Burhan, al-Kafi, and Bihar al-Anwar.

Egypt is where the stolen craft settled into a state. Pharaoh kept sorcerers the way kings keep soldiers, and the throne itself rested on the trade. When God sent Moses ﵇ to that court, the contest that followed was fought between two kinds of power. The masters of the craft were the first to see which one was real, because their own teachers had drawn the dividing line for them.

Before Pharaoh, the Fire of Nimrud

Before Pharaoh gathered his sorcerers, another king had already tested a prophet of God with fire. Al-Sadiq ﵇ compared the two kings directly.

A Kingdom Run on Sorcery

The Day of Adornment

The Qur'an fixes the turning point of that day in one verse, the throw that swallowed every fabrication.

كن لما لا ترجو أرجى منك لما ترجوا - إلى أن قال : - وخرجت سحرة فرعون يطلبون العزة لفرعون فرجعوا مؤمنين

The School of Sorcery

The craft had a schooling behind it. Al-Jaza'iri's second account, drawn from Ibn Abbas and the scholars of the prophets' histories, runs from Pharaoh's schooling of the boys to the prostration of the masters. It carries details the first telling does not hold.

When the people and the sorcerers had gathered, Moses came leaning on his staff, with Aaron with him. Moses said to the sorcerers, "Woe to you, do not invent a lie against God, or He will erase you with a painful punishment." Some of them said to others, this is not the speech of a sorcerer. They said, we will come to you today with sorcery the like of which you have never seen. They had brought staffs and ropes carried by sixty camels. Moses said to them, throw. They threw their ropes and staffs, and there they were, serpents like mountains filling the valley, crawling one over another. Moses felt a fear in himself. Moses said, by God, if these were staffs in their hands, they have become serpents, and they do not count my staff among these. God revealed to him, "Do not fear. You are the highest. Throw what is in your right hand. It will swallow what they have made. What they have made is only a sorcerer's trick, and the sorcerer will never prosper wherever he goes." He threw his staff, and there it was, a serpent, huge, black, and dark, on four short thick legs, bigger and taller than a Bactrian camel, with a tail it stands upon so that it rises above the walls of the city at its head. Whatever its tail strikes, it shatters. It crushes rocks with its legs and sets the walls of houses on fire. Its two nostrils blow out poisons, and on its forelock stands hair like spears. It ranged over what the sorcerers had thrown of their ropes and staffs, while they were serpents in the eye of Pharaoh and the eyes of the people, and it swallowed them one by one until nothing at all could be seen in the valley. The people fled and pressed on one another, trampling each other, until twenty-five thousand died that day. Pharaoh fled in terror, and his bowels loosened that day four hundred times, and after that forty times every day and night without ceasing until he perished. When the sorcerers saw what they saw, they said, if this were sorcery, its reality would not have been hidden from us. If this were sorcery, where are our ropes and our staffs? They fell prostrate, and said, "We believe in the Lord of the worlds, the Lord of Moses and Aaron.""

- Ibn Abbas in al-Jaza'iri, al-Nur al-Mubin, p.251-252

What the Craft Really Was

The staff that beat the craft has its own history, and the corpus holds it from Adam ﵇ to the waiting Imam.

كانت عصا موسى لآدم سقطت إلى شعيب ، ثم صارت إلى موسى ، وإنها لعندنا ، وإن عهدي بها آنفا ، وإنها لخضراء كهيئتها حين انتزعت (3) من شجرتها ، وإنها لتنطق إذا استنطقت ، أعدت لقائمنا يصنع بها ما كان موسى (﵇) يصنع بها ، وإنها لتروع وتلقف ما يأفكون ، وتصنع ما تؤمر ، فكان حيث أقبلت تلقف ما يأفكون ، فتحت لها شعبتان (4) ، كانت إحداهما في الأرض والأخرى في السقف ، وبينهما أربعون ذراعا ، فتلقف ما يأفكون ، بلسانها

"Moses' staff belonged to Adam. It passed to Shuayb, then to Moses. It is with us now. I saw it recently. It remains green, as it was when it was taken from its tree. It speaks when someone makes it speak. It has been prepared for our Qa'im (the one who will rise). He will use it as Moses used it. It terrifies people and swallows what they fabricate. It does whatever it is commanded to do. Wherever it turned, it swallowed what they fabricated with its tongue. Its two branches opened. One lay on the ground and the other reached the ceiling. There were forty cubits between them. It swallowed what they fabricated with its tongue."

- Muhammad ibn Ali , al-Mufid's al-Ikhtisas, carried in al-Burhan vol.2, p.569

Al-Majlisi examined what the sorcerers' art actually was, and his verdict names the kind of thing Pharaoh's magicians did.

النوع الخامس ) * * ( من السحر ) * الاعمال العجيبة التي تطرأ (1) من تركيب الآلات المركبة على النسب الهندسية تارة وعلى ضروب الخيلاء (2) أخرى مثل فارسين يقتتلان فيقتل أحدهما الآخر وكفارس على فرس في يده بوق كلما مضت ساعة من النهار ضرب البوق من غير أن يمسه أحد ، ومنها الصور التي تصورها الروم وأهل الهند حتى لا يفرق الناظر بينها وبين الانسان حتى يصورونها ضاحكة وباكية وحتى يفرق فيها بين ضحك السرور وضحك الخجل وضحك الشامت ، فهذه الوجوه من لطيف أمور التخائيل (3) وكان سحر سحرة فرعون من هذا الضرب

"The fifth kind of sorcery is the astonishing works that come from assembling mechanical devices on geometric ratios or through tricks of the imagination, like two horsemen who fight until one kills the other, and like a rider on a horse with a trumpet in his hand that sounds every hour of the day with no one touching it. Among them are the images that the Romans and the people of India make, until the viewer cannot tell them from a human being. They make them laughing and weeping. It can distinguish between a laugh of joy, a laugh of embarrassment, and a laugh of gloating. These are subtle forms of illusion. Pharaoh's magicians used this kind of magic."

- al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol.56, p.297

Al-Jaza'iri closed the question of strange acts done outside prophethood with four faces, and the third of them concerns Pharaoh himself.

الوجه الثالث أن فرعون وهامان كانا حاذقين في السحر وبه غلبا على قومهما فلعل تلك الأفعال الغريبة كانت مستندة إلى السحر ولا ينافيه سجوده وتضرعه لله تعالى ودعاؤه . فإن السحرة لا يخلو سحر من سحرهم عن الآيات والأدعية وإن ضموا إليها أمورا أخر

"The third possibility is that Pharaoh and Haman were highly skilled in magic, and through it they ruled their people. Perhaps those strange acts came from magic. His prostration, humble prayer to Allah, and supplication do not conflict with this. Magicians always include verses and prayers in their magic, along with other things."

- al-Jaza'iri, al-Nur al-Mubin, p.243

Commentary

The Craft Judged by Craftsmen

The Fire That Went Cold

Source: Tafsir al-Qummi vol.2 p.72-73

Claim: Nimrud tested Ibrahim with fire and lost; Pharaoh tested Moses with sorcery and lost; al-Sadiq compared the two kings directly

Speaker: al-Sadiq

Nimrud had no sorcerers. His court said burn him, and Iblis himself built the catapult because no human could approach the blaze. Ibrahim answered with prayer, the fire went cool, and the craft had its first defeat. Pharaoh's court had learned. They did not say burn him. They said bring every skilled sorcerer. Al-Sadiq called the first court rash and the second wise, and the wisdom was in knowing what the contest would be.

Source: al-Nur al-Mubin p.237 and p.252; al-Kafi vol.5 p.84 via al-Burhan vol.2 p.569

Claim: the sorcerers set the test themselves and converted on its result

Speaker: al-Jaza'iri; Ibn Abbas; Ali ﵇

The conversion of the sorcerers is evidence because of who converted. These men told Pharaoh in advance that a defeated craft would prove a true messenger. They then watched their own ropes and staves vanish, and their verdict was spoken in the language of their trade, that had the thing been sorcery it would not have been hidden from them. Ali ﵇ folded the event into a rule of hope, that Pharaoh's sorcerers left home seeking honor for a tyrant and returned as believers. The craft judged itself and lost, and its own masters had drawn the line in advance, when the dead father told his sons that a staff which acts while prophets sleep is the affair of the Lord of the worlds, not of their trade.

The Sky as Witness

Source: al-Nur al-Mubin p.237

Claim: the sorcerers argued their craft works on earth and never in the sky

Speaker: al-Jaza'iri

The sorcerers built their stage of polished steel and glaring sun, and they said openly that their craft had never reached the sky. The argument is remembered because it returned. Quraysh said the same words at the moon, that sorcery persists on earth but never in heaven, and Part 5 of this series carries their answer. The contest in Egypt set the sentence that every later accuser would quote.

The Staff That Waits

Source: al-Ikhtisas via al-Burhan vol.2 p.569; Bihar al-Anwar vol.56 p.297; al-Nur al-Mubin p.243

Claim: the staff is an heirloom of the prophets kept for the Qa'im, and the sorcerers' art was illusion

Speaker: Muhammad ibn Ali ﵇; al-Majlisi; al-Jaza'iri

The staff that swallowed the sorcerers' work had been Adam's ﵇, then Shu'ayb's ﵇, then Moses' ﵇, and the narration says it is kept now, green as when cut, prepared for the Qa'im to do with it what Moses ﵇ did. The history of magic thus runs beside a second heirloom line, the instruments of the prophets, and the two lines meet again at the end of this series. As for the sorcerers, al-Majlisi's verdict is mechanical, that their art was the art of faces and seeming, and al-Jaza'iri's four faces explain how wonders can appear under unblessed hands without prophethood being granted for them.

Where the Story Runs Next

The craft that Pharaoh ran as a state did not die with him. It passed into the hands of the satans, who fed it to soothsayers, and when Solomon ﵇ ruled the jinn and the wind the question of his power was answered with the same old word. A forged book, a buried throne, and a verse of clearing are the next part.
