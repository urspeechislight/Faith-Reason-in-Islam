A Philosophical Dilemma Weighed Against the School's Reports

The Matter Between Two Matters

Either God determines each human volition or the creature does, the objection says, and either way responsibility dies. The lexicons fix the two horns under the names jabr and tafwid, the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt named and damned both, and the reports carry the middle between them in a parable, a dialogue at Merv, a letter of five conditions, and a vertical frame of causation that never competes. From al-Kafi, Bihar al-Anwar, Tafsir al-Burhan, Wasail al-Shia, Sharh Usul al-Kafi, Ilm al-Yaqin, Sirat al-Haqq, Dirasat fi Ilm al-Usul, Lisan al-Arab, and al-Ayn.

The Argument Under Examination

Premise 1.

The objection presses a dilemma on every revealed religion. Either God determines why a person wills A and never B, and then the person is not the source of his choice. Or the person determines it apart from God, and then something in created history escapes the divine decree. The objection grants that people desire, deliberate, and choose in an ordinary sense. Its target is the ultimate explanation of the particular volition.

Premise 2.

Arabic has exact words for the two horns, and the classical lexicons fix them before any theology begins. Jabr names compulsion, the forcing of a person the way a tyrant forces a slave. Tafwid names delegation, the handing over of an affair so that the recipient runs it alone.

Premise 3.

the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt, the Prophet's Household and the school's carriers of his teaching, rejected both horns by name. Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇, in the 2nd century after the Prophet's migration to Medina set year one of the Islamic calendar, said the matter is an affair between two affairs. Imam al-Rida ﵇ defined the two errors, defined the middle, and distinguished God's will for obedience from His will over existence itself.

Premise 4.

The relation between the divine causation and the human act is vertical, never a competition on one plane. God grants existence, power, reason, time, and opportunity moment by moment. Within that grant, the act's own selection belongs to the servant.

Conclusion.

The final difference-maker question has an answer inside the school's doctrine. The choice is genuinely the servant's because it issues from his own faculties and his own intention. The faculties and the intention exist at every instant because God sustains them. Nothing brute, nothing rival, and nothing forced remains.

The objection deserves its strongest statement, so it gets one here. A philosopher presses a single question. At the point where two volitions stand open before a person, what finally explains why A becomes actual and never B? If the answer is God alone, the person seems carried. If the answer is the person alone, God seems to have lost His sovereignty over that fragment of history. If the answer is nothing, the choice seems random. The sections that follow answer with the school's own texts, quoted and weighed, and show where the dilemma breaks.

1. The Words the Lexicons Fix

Claim. the two horns of the philosophical dilemma have classical Arabic names whose senses the dictionaries fix

Basis. Lisan al-Arab and al-Ayn entries on the two roots

Qualifier. stated

Contested words. jabr and tafwid, the two named horns of the dilemma

Lexicons quoted. Lisan al-Arab of Ibn Manzur (d.711 AH) and al-Ayn of al-Khalil ibn Ahmad (d.175 AH), with the poet al-Ajjaj and the grammarian al-Azhari inside the entries

Claim. the two horns of the philosophical dilemma have classical Arabic names whose senses the dictionaries fix before any theology begins

The whole modern dilemma turns on two Arabic nouns, and the lexicons were settled on them a thousand years before the objection was written. Jabr, in the sense the doctrine rejects, is compulsion. Al-Azhari, quoted in the Lisan, fixes it with two plain words.

جبر : الجَبَّارُ : الله عز اسمه القاهر خلقه على ما أَراد من أَمر ونهي .

ابن الأَنباري : الجبار في صفة الله ﷿ الذي لا يُنالُ ، ومنه جَبَّارُ النخل .

الفرّاء : لم أَسمع فَعَّالاً من أَفعل إِلا في حرفين وهو جَبَّار من أَجْبَرْتُ ، ودَرَّاك من أَدركْتُ ، قال الأَزهري : جعل جَبَّاراً في صفة الله تعالى أَو في صفة العباد من الإِجْبار وهو القهر والإِكراه لا من جَبَرَ .

jabr, al-jabbar, Allahu ʿazza ismuhu al-qahru khalqahu ʿala ma arada min amrin wa nahy

"Jabr. The Compeller is Allah, whose name is exalted, who subdues His creation to whatever He wills of command and prohibition.

Ibn al-Anbari said, the Compeller in Allah's description is the One who cannot be reached, and from this comes the tall palm called jabbar.

Al-Farra said, I never heard the intensive faʿʿal form from afʿaltu in more than two words, jabbar from ajbartu and darraq from adraktu. Al-Azhari said, jabbar in the description of Allah, or of the servants, comes from compulsion, which is subduing and coercing, and never from jabara."

- Ibn Manzur (d.711 AH), Lisan al-Arab, vol.4, p.113

Subduing and coercing. That is the dictionary's own gloss. The philosopher builds his first horn on a story like this. A neuroscientist secretly programs a man's desires so that at noon he willingly murders, and the philosopher asks why moving the programmer from a laboratory to heaven would make the murder the man's own. The Imam's verdict below answers the story in advance. The root's other life, the setting of broken bones and the restoring of loss, the lexicon keeps in the same entry, and the poets show it in use.

ويقال : جَبَرْتُ العظم جَبْراً وجَبَرَ العظمُ بنفسه جُبُوراً أَي انجَبَر ؛ وقد جمع العجاج بين المتعدي واللازم فقال :

قد جَبَر الدِّينَ الإِله فَجَبَرْ

wa yuqalu jabartu al-ʿazma jabran, wa jabara al-ʿazmu bi-nafsihi juburan, qad jabara al-dina al-ilahu fa-jabar

"It is said, I set the bone, and the bone sets itself, meaning it healed. Al-ʿAjjaj joined the transitive and the intransitive in one verse when he said, the God restored the religion, and it stood firm."

- Ibn Manzur (d.711 AH), Lisan al-Arab, vol.4, p.115

So the root carries both forcing a thing and restoring a thing. The doctrine's negation targets the first sense alone. No report of the school ever denied that God heals, restores, and mends. What the school denies is the coercion sense, the sense the dictionaries themselves mark with al-qahr wa-l-ikrah, subduing and coercing.

The second horn has its own entry. Tafwid is the handing over of an affair.

فوض : فوضت إليه الأمر أي جعلته إليه . [ وقال الله - جل وعز - : وأفوض أمري إلى الله ، أي أتكل عليه ] .

fawad, fawadtu ilayhi al-amra ayy jaʿaltuhu ilayh, wa afawdu amri ila Allah

"Fawad. I handed the affair over to him, meaning I made it his.

Allah, mighty and majestic, says, and I commit my affair to Allah, meaning I trust in Him."

- al-Khalil ibn Ahmad (d.175 AH), al-Ayn, vol.7, p.64

The dictionaries define the two errors before the theology begins, and the definitions are the philosopher's own two horns with their Arabic names. The first horn says the human will is forced, jabr, compulsion by subduing. The second says the human will runs its own affair after the handover, tafwid, delegation with abandonment. The Imams ﵈ rejected the first horn and the second horn with these very words, and the middle they taught sits between exactly these two fixed senses, with the lexical ground already laid.

2. The Imam's Formula and Parable

Source. al-Kafi, vol.1, p.631, report 413, and vol.1, p.208

Speaker. Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq ﵇

Dating. compiled by al-Kulayni (d.329 AH)

Claim. the doctrine in one line, then the parable that explains it

The formula comes first, then its explanation comes as a parable, and the parable answers the philosopher's question before the philosopher asked it.

عَنْ أَبِي عَبْدِ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ ، قَالَ : « لَا جَبْرَ وَلَا تَفْوِيضَ ، وَلكِنْ أَمْرٌ بَيْنَ أَمْرَيْنِ (2) » .

قَالَ : قُلْتُ : وَمَا أَمْرٌ بَيْنَ أَمْرَيْنِ ؟

قَالَ : « مَثَلُ ذلِكَ : رَجُلٌ رَأَيْتَهُ عَلى مَعْصِيَةٍ ، فَنَهَيْتَهُ ، فَلَمْ يَنْتَهِ ، فَتَرَكْتَهُ ، فَفَعَلَ تِلْكَ الْمَعْصِيَةَ ؛ فَلَيْسَ (3) حَيْثُ لَمْ يَقْبَلْ مِنْكَ فَتَرَكْتَهُ (4) كُنْتَ أَنْتَ الَّذِي أَمَرْتَهُ بِالْمَعْصِيَةِ » . (5)

"Imam al-Sadiq said, there is no compulsion and there is no delegation, but there is an affair between two affairs.

The narrator said, I asked, and what is an affair between two affairs?

He said, its likeness is this. You see a man committing a sin, so you forbid him, and he does not desist, so you leave him, and he commits that sin.

It does not follow, because he did not accept it from you and you left him, that you were the one who commanded him to commit the sin."

- al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, Dar al-Hadith ed., vol.1, p.631

The parable carries the entire doctrine in one scene. The forbidding stands for guidance, the warning, and every means God sends to turn the servant from sin. The leaving stands for what God does not do, for God does not chain the hand or seize the will. The sin that follows belongs to the man, because he refused the warning and chose the act. The commentator al-Mazandarani drew the mapping out plainly.

(ولكن أمر بين أمرين ، قال : قلت : وما أمر بين أمرين ؟

قال : مثل ذلك رجل رأيته على معصيته فنهيته) عنها (فلم ينته فتركته) بحاله وما زجرته عنها جبراً وقهراً (ففعل تلك المعصية) بقدرته واختياره (فليس حيث لم يقبل منك فتركته) مع قدرتك (1) على زجره عنها جبراً (كنت أنت الَّذي أمرته بالمعصية) أي جبّرته عليها ، أطلق الأمر على الجبر مجازاً فكما أنّك لمّا منعته منها بالزَّواجر والنصائح ما فوّضت الأمر إليه ولمّا رأيته أنّه يفعلها فتركته وما منعته منعاً يوجب تركه ما أجبرته عليها ، كذلك صنع الله بالنسبة إلى أفعال العباد فهذا أمر بين أمرين

"He forbade him from it, and he did not desist, so he left him to his state and did not restrain him from it by compulsion and overpowering.

He committed that sin by his own power and his own choice.

It is not the case that you, though you were able to restrain him by force, and left him, commanded him to commit the sin, meaning compelled him to it.

Just as you, when you barred him from it with warnings and counsel, did not abandon the affair to him, and when you saw that he would do it and left him without a restraint that would force him to leave it, did not compel him to it, so Allah deals with the actions of the servants. This is an affair between two affairs."

- al-Mazandarani (d.1081 AH), Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol.5, p.33

The thread is now visible. The lexicons fixed the two rejected senses, and the parable shows God acting in neither of them. God forbids, warns, and aids, which is the opposite of abandoning the affair. God does not compel, which is the opposite of jabr. The objection's first horn therefore attacks a doctrine the school itself condemns, and condemns in the strongest available language, as the next reports show.

3. Merv Defines Both Horns

Source. Bihar al-Anwar, vol.5, p.14, from Uyun Akhbar al-Rida, and Tafsir al-Burhan, vol.3, p.516, from al-Ihtijaj

Speaker. Imam Ali ibn Musa al-Rida ﵇

Dating. related by Yazid ibn Umayr al-Shami at Merv, compiled by al-Saduq (d.381 AH)

Claim. both horns named, defined, and judged, and the middle defined with the dual will

The definitions come in a dialogue at Merv, and no philosopher has stated the dilemma more sharply than the questioner did.

روي لنا عن الصادق جعفر بن محمد ﵇ أنه قال : لا جبر ولا تفويض بل أمر بين أمرين فما معناه ؟ فقال : من زعم أن الله يفعل أفعالنا ثم يعذبنا عليها فقد قال بالجبر ومن زعم أن الله ﷿ فوض أمر الخلق والرزق إلى حججه ﵈ فقد قال بالتفويض فالقائل بالجبر كافر والقائل بالتفويض مشرك .

"It was related to us from al-Sadiq Jaʿfar ibn Muhammad that he said, there is no compulsion and no delegation, but an affair between two affairs, so what is its meaning?

Al-Rida said, whoever claims that Allah performs our acts and then punishes us for them has spoken of compulsion.

Whoever claims that Allah delegated the affair of creation and provision to His proofs has spoken of delegation.

The speaker of compulsion is an unbeliever, and the speaker of delegation is an idolater."

- al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.5, p.14, relaying al-Saduq (d.381 AH), Uyun Akhbar al-Rida

فقال : «وجود السبيل إلى إتيان ما أمروا به ، وترك ما نهوا عنه» .

قلت له : وهل لله مشيئة وإرادة في ذلك ؟ فقال : «أما الطاعات فإرادة الله تعالى ومشيئته فيها الأمر بها ، والرضا لها ، والمعاونة عليها ، وإرادته ومشيئته في المعاصي النهي عنها ، والسخط لها ، والخذلان عليها» .

قلت : فلله ﷿ [فيها] القضاء ؟ قال : «نعم ، ما من فعل يفعله العباد من خير أو شر إلا ولله فيه قضاء» . قلت : فما معنى هذا القضاء ؟ قال : «الحكم عليهم بما يستحقونه من الثواب والعقاب في الدنيا والآخرة» .

"He said, the middle is the existence of the way to come to what they were commanded and to leave what they were forbidden.

The questioner asked, does Allah have a will and a wish concerning that?

He said, as for acts of obedience, Allah's will and wish concerning them are the command to them, His pleasure in them, and His aid upon them.

His will and wish concerning sins are the prohibition of them, His anger at them, and His abandonment of the sinner to them.

The questioner asked, and does Allah decree in them?

He said, yes. There is no act the servants do, of good or evil, in which Allah does not decree.

He asked, and what does this decree mean?

He said, the judgment upon them of what they deserve for their acts, of reward and punishment, in this world and the next."

- al-Bahrani (d.1107 AH), Tafsir al-Burhan, vol.3, p.516, relaying al-Tabrisi, al-Ihtijaj

These lines dismantle the dilemma's framing in two moves. First, the two wills are named separately. God wills obedience by commanding it and rewarding it. God wills concerning sin by forbidding it and judging it. That is a legislative will, a will of command and prohibition, and it is categorically different from the existential will by which anything exists at all. The objection treats all divine willing as one flat determining force, and the report refuses that flattening. Second, the decree over human acts is defined as judgment, not as inner manufacture. The decree that touches every human act, good or evil, is the decree of what the act deserves. Al-Hurr al-Amili carries the same text in his legal corpus, which shows the doctrine was received as law, never as a philosophy lecture.

وعن تميم بن عبد الله بن تميم ، عن أبيه ، عن أحمد بن علي الأنصاري ، عن يزيد بن عمر الشامي (1) ، عن الرضا (﵇) - في حديث - قال : من زعم أن الله يفعل أفعالنا ثم يعذبنا عليها ، فقد قال بالجبر ، ومن زعم أن الله فوض أمر الخلق والرزق إلى حججه ، فقد قال بالتفويض ، والقائل بالجبر كافر ، والقائل بالتفويض مشرك . (34908)

"From Tamim ibn Abdullah ibn Tamim, from his father, from Ahmad ibn Ali al-Ansari, from Yazid ibn Umar al-Shami, from al-Rida, in a report.

He said, whoever claims that Allah performs our acts and then punishes us for them has spoken of compulsion, and whoever claims that Allah delegated the affair of creation and provision to His proofs has spoken of delegation.

The speaker of compulsion is an unbeliever, and the speaker of delegation is an idolater."

- al-Hurr al-Amili (d.1104 AH), Wasail al-Shia, vol.28, p.340, report 34908

The verdicts attached to the two horns are the school's own, and they are maximal. Compulsion, the horn that says God determines the volition and punishes it, is unbelief. Delegation, the horn that says the creature runs the affair alone, is idolatry, because it sets up a second manager inside creation. The philosopher built a dilemma between two positions the school had already damned on both ends. The middle remains to be filled in, and the filling comes from the same Imams ﵈.

4. Sin Without Authorship

Source. Bihar al-Anwar, vol.5, p.24, and al-Kafi, vol.1, p.208

Speaker. Imam al-Jawad ﵇ and Imam al-Sadiq ﵇

Dating. 3rd century AH

Claim. God is not the maker of the sin as sin, and nothing occurs in His domain against His will

Two sayings complete the frame. The first protects God from authorship of evil. The second protects His domain from escape.

وقد روي عن أبي الحسن الثالث ﵇ أنه سئل عن أفعال العباد أهي مخلوقة لله تعالى ؟ فقال ﵇ لو كان خالقا لها لما تبرأ منها وقد قال سبحانه : " إن الله برئ من المشركين " ولم يرد البراءة من خلق ذواتهم ، وإنما تبرأ من شركهم وقبائحهم

"It is related from Imam al-Jawad that he was asked, are the acts of the servants created by Allah?

He said, if He were their creator He would not declare Himself clear of them, and the Exalted said, Allah is clear of the idolaters.

He did not mean clear of creating their bodies. He meant clear of their act of idolatry."

- al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.5, p.24

عدة من أصحابنا ، عن أحمد بن محمد البرقي ، عن علي بن الحكم ، عن هشام بن سالم ، عن أبي عبد الله ﵇ قال : الله أكرم من أن يكلف الناس ما لا يطيقون والله أعز من أن يكون في سلطانه ما لا يريد .

"Imam al-Sadiq said, Allah is too generous to burden people with what they cannot bear, and Allah is too mighty for there to be, in His dominion, anything He does not will."

- al-Kulayni (d.329 AH), al-Kafi, vol.1, p.208

The pair closes the loop the objection tries to open. The first report blocks horn one at its theological root. God sustains every existent, and the idolater's existence is sustained, yet God declared Himself clear of the shirk itself, which means the sin, as sin, traces to the servant. The second report blocks horn two at its root. Nothing in the dominion occurs against the divine will, because the dominion itself, and the power in the servant's hand, and the moment in which he acts, all exist by continuous divine grant. The objection's dilemma assumed the two claims cannot live together. The school's reports state them together in one breath, and the living together is precisely the doctrine.

The sophisticated version of the decree argument deserves its own statement, because the Final Verdict will return to it. The objector says, either the decree explains the choice, and the choice is not the servant's, or the choice explains the decree's content. In the second case God merely records what the servant freely settles, a scribe, not a sovereign. The school's answer was already given in the two wills. The decree that fixes an act's existence is the existential will, and it does not compete with the choice. Will over the act is the bestowal of its being, not the placing of its direction. A decree that bestows being is not a transcription of the creature's decision, and it is not a rival determination of it either. The scribe picture assumes the only alternative to recording is determining, and the reports reject both pictures at once. God is not the choice's scribe, and He is not its compeller. He is its Sustainer, and the sustained choice is genuinely the servant's. The recording-dilemma collapses into the lateral picture it presupposes, the picture the vessels report already dismantled.

5. The Five Gifts

Source. Bihar al-Anwar, vol.5, p.24, from the letter of Imam al-Askari ﵇ to the people of al-Ahwaz

Speaker. Imam al-Askari ﵇

Dating. 3rd century AH

Claim. responsibility has enumerable conditions, and each condition is itself a divine gift

The middle gets its fullest definition in a letter. Asked about the two errors, the Imam listed what must be present before an act can be laid to the servant's account.

فقال : صحة العقل ، وتخلية السرب ، والمهلة في الوقت ، والزاد من قبل الراحلة ، والسبب المهيج للفاعل على فعله ، فهذه خمسة أشياء فإذا نقص العبد منها خلة كان العمل عنه مطرحا بحسبه

"He said, soundness of reason, freedom of the path, room in the time and provision before the mount, and the motive that moves the doer to his deed.

These are five things, and if one of them is missing for the servant, the act falls from his account to that extent."

- al-Majlisi (d.1111 AH), Bihar al-Anwar, vol.5, p.24, relaying al-Ihtijaj

Every condition on the list is a gift, and none of them is the choice. Reason is given. Safety and freedom of circumstance are given. Time and means are given. The motive arises from what God placed in the world. The list shows exactly where the school draws the line that the objection claims cannot be drawn. The givens establish the arena, and the selection inside the arena is the servant's. The same letter carries the divine speech that fixes ownership from both sides.

قلت لأبي الحسن الرِّضا (﵇) . إنَّ بعض أصحابنا يقول بالجبر وبعضهم يقول بالاستطاعة قال : فقال لي : اكتب بسم الله الرَّحمن الرَّحيم ; قال عليُّ بن الحسين : قال الله عزَّ وجلَّ : يا ابن آدم بمشيئتي كنت أنت الذي تشاء وبقوَّتي أدَّيت إليَّ فرائضي وبنعمتي قويت على معصيتي ; جعلتك سميعا ، بصيرا ، ما أصابك من حسنة فمن الله وما أصابك من سيِّئة فمن نفسك ، وذلك أنّي أولى بحسناتك منك وأنت أولى بسيِّئاتك منّي ، وذلك أنّي لا اُسأل عمّا أفعل وهم يسألون ; قد نظمت لك كلَّ شيء تريد

"I said to Imam al-Rida, some of our companions speak of compulsion and some speak of autonomous ability.

He said, write, in the name of Allah, the Merciful, the Compassionate.

Ali ibn al-Husayn said, Allah, mighty and majestic, said, son of Adam, by My will you were you, the one who wills, and by My power you brought Me My duties, and by My favor you had strength to sin against Me.

I made you hearing, seeing. Whatever good befalls you is from Allah, and whatever evil befalls you is from yourself.

That is because I am nearer to your good deeds than you, and you are nearer to your evil deeds than I.

That is because I am not questioned about what I do, and they are questioned. I have arranged for you everything you want."

- al-Mazandarani (d.1081 AH), Sharh Usul al-Kafi, vol.5, p.33, report 12

This is the answer to the desire problem, the objection's section nine, stated in the seventh century of the common era. The willing is genuinely the servant's, you were you, the one who wills. The will's existence and its power are God's, by My will, by My power, by My favor. The good is credited to God because He is nearer to it, its giver and its arranger. The evil is laid to the servant because he is nearer to it, its selector. One speech, both truths, no competition. The objection demands to know who supplies the final determination of A and never B. The school's answer is already in the sentence. God supplies the willing self with everything it wills with, and the self, thus supplied, wills.

6. Owned Through, Never Apart

Source. Ilm al-Yaqin, vol.1, p.383, and vol.2, p.266

Speaker. Imam al-Rida ﵇, Imam Ali ﵇, and Imam al-Askari ﵇

Dating. compiled by al-Kashani (d.1091 AH)

Claim. the relation of the human power to the divine power is ownership through, never ownership apart from

The objection's strongest weapon is a false picture of what a Muslim means when he says the will exists within God's will. The reports replace the picture with a precise one.

فقال : - «إنَّ اللَّه - عزَّ وجلَّ - لم يطع بإكراه ، ولم يعص بغلبة ، ولم يهمل العباد في ملكه ، وهو المالك لما ملَّكهم ، والقادر على ما أقدرهم عليه ، فإن ائتمر العباد بطاعته لم يكن اللَّه عنها صادّا ، ولا منها مانعا ،

وإن ائتمروا بمعصيته فشاء أن يحول بينه (1) وبين ذلك لفعل ، وإن لم يحل وفعلوه ، فليس هو الذي أدخلهم فيه» .

"He said, Allah, mighty and majestic, is not obeyed through coercion, and is not disobeyed through being overpowered, and He does not neglect the servants in His kingdom.

He is the owner of what He gave them ownership of, and the powerful over what He gave them power over.

If the servants resolve on obeying Him, Allah does not turn them from it and does not bar them from it.

If they resolve on sinning against Him, and He wishes to come between them and it, He does so, and if He does not come between them and they do it, He is not the one who brought them into it."

- al-Kashani (d.1091 AH), Ilm al-Yaqin, vol.1, p.383

Every clause of the philosopher's neuroscientist story is answered here in advance. Coerced obedience does not exist. Overpowered disobedience does not exist. Neglect of the servants does not exist. The ownership is real and layered, God owns what He gave them to own, and He is powerful over what He gave them power over. That layered ownership is the metaphysical content of the middle, and the report of the ability, from Imam Ali ﵇, gives it the formula the objection keeps asking for.

The same text answers the weaker argument the objection sets aside, the argument from physical prevention. God can come between the servant and the sin he resolved on, and the report says so plainly. If He does not come between, and the sin occurs, the sin is still the servant's. A blocked outcome would never have touched the willing itself, and an unblocked outcome never forces the willing either way. The objection is right to discard this argument, and the school discarded it first. The doctrine concerns the volition, the aim of the soul, and the reports keep the volition and the outcome distinct.

قال : (5) «تقول : تملكها باللَّه الذي يملكها من دونك ؛ فإن تملَّككها ، كان ذلك من عطائه ، وإن سلبكها ، كان ذلك من بلائه ؛ هو المالك لما

ملَّكك ، والمالك لما عليه أقدرك ؛ أما سمعت الناس يسألون الحول والقوَّة ، حيث يقولون : لا حول ولا قوَّة إلَّا باللَّه » ؟ .

فقال الرجل : « ما تأويلها - يا أمير المؤمنين » ؟

قال : « لا حول بنا عن معاصي اللَّه إلَّا بعصمة اللَّه ، ولا قوَّة لنا على طاعة اللَّه ، إلَّا بعون اللَّه » .

"He said, you say, she is owned through Allah, who grants her from beyond you.

If He grants her to you, that is from His gift, and if He strips her from you, that is from His trial.

He is the owner of what He granted you, and the owner of that over which He gave you power.

Have you not heard the people ask for strength and ability, when they say, there is no movement and no strength except through Allah?

The man asked, what is its meaning, Commander of the Faithful?

He said, we have no turning back from sins against Allah except by Allah's protection, and we have no strength for Allah's obedience except by Allah's aid."

- al-Kashani (d.1091 AH), Ilm al-Yaqin, vol.1, p.383

Ownership through God is the formula. Ownership apart from God is denied. Ownership shared with God is denied. The remaining possibility, an independence beside God, is the one the questioner was forbidden to say, and the report of the hearts carries the positive side of the same coin.

وجئت تسأل عن مقالة المفوّضة ؟ كذبوا ؛ بل قلوبنا أوعية لمشيَّة اللَّه ، فإذا شاء اللَّه شئنا ، واللَّه يقول : وَما تَشاؤُنَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَشاءَ اللَّهُ [ 76 / 30 ]

"You have come to ask about the doctrine of the delegators. They lie.

Rather, our hearts are vessels of Allah's will, so when Allah wills, we will.

Allah says, and you do not will, except that Allah wills, the Lord of the worlds."

- al-Kashani (d.1091 AH), Ilm al-Yaqin, vol.2, p.266

The philosopher reads that verse as the death of freedom, because he reads every willing on one flat plane. The school reads it through the whole apparatus already assembled. The hearts are vessels, capacity held in being by the Sustainer, and when He wills, that is, when He grants being, power, reason, time, and occasion, we will. The verse names the vertical dependence of the will, and the reports already distinguished that dependence from coercion. The Qur'an itself states the conditional form of the middle. Allah does not change a people's condition until they change what is in themselves, and 13:11 carries the difference-maker as a condition on the creaturely side.

إن الله لا يغير ما بقوم حتى يغيروا ما بأنفسهم

inna Allaha la yughayyiru ma bi-qawmin hatta yughayyiru ma bi-anfusihim

"Allah does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves." (Qur'an 13:11)

- al-Qubaysi (d.1246 AH), Tafsir al-Bayan al-Safi, vol.1, p.9

Read with the vessels report, the verse fixes the order of dependence. The change in the people's condition waits on the change inside them. The verse does not say Allah changes what is in them first, and it does not say they act apart from Him. The self-change is the enabled act inside the sustained dominion, and the divine change follows it. That is the middle doctrine stated in Qur'anic grammar, a conditional whose antecedent belongs to the servant and whose consequent belongs to God.

A vessel does not compete with what fills it. What fills it does not snatch the pouring from the vessel.

The verses the objection leaves out

The objection builds its case on the verses of decree and reads them as compulsion. The Qur'an itself pairs every decree verse with verses that assign the act to the servant in words no decree reading survives. Three of them name the choice outright. The page has met 76:30 and 76:2-3 already. These three complete the set the objection never quotes.

وَقُلِ الحَقُّ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ فَمَنْ شَاءَ فَلْيُؤْمِنْ وَمَنْ شَاءَ فَلْيَكْفُرْ

wa-qul al-haqqum min rabbikum, fa-man shaa'a falyu'min wa-man shaa'a falyakfur

And say, the truth is from your Lord. So whoever wills, let him believe. And whoever wills, let him disbelieve.

Qur'an 18:29, cited in al-Insan bayn al-Jabr wal-Tafwid (Man Between Compulsion and Delegation), p.133

The grammar of 18:29 leaves nothing to a decree reading. The belief and the disbelief are both commanded to the human will directly, whoever wills, let him believe. A book that decreed compulsion could not command belief as an open option without contradicting itself in one sentence. The verse places the will where the objection denies it stands, inside the human being.

وَنَفْسٍ وَما سَوَّاها ، فَأَلْهَمَها فُجُورَها وَتَقْواها

wa-nafsin wa-maa sawwaahaa, fa-alhamahaa fujuurahaa wa-taqwaahaa

And by the soul and He who shaped it. Then He inspired it its defiance and its duty.

The soul He swore by is the same soul He equipped. The inspiration reaches the soul as knowledge of the two roads, never as the choice between them.

Qur'an 91:7-8, cited with Qur'an 91:9-10 in al-Insan bayn al-Jabr wal-Tafwid, p.133

This pair of verses carries the whole architecture in three words. God swears by the soul He shaped, then names what He gave it, the inspiration of its defiance and its duty. The gift is the knowledge of both roads. The act is not named as given, because the next verses hand the outcome to the soul itself, he succeeds who purifies it and he fails who buries it. What God assigns to Himself here is exactly the enabling, and what He assigns to the soul is the selection the objection says belongs to Him.

وَهَدَيْناهُ النَّجْدَيْنِ

wa-hadaynaahu al-najdayn

And We guided him to the two highways.

Qur'an 90:10, cited in al-Masabih, p.12

The two highways complete the grammar. Guidance in the enabling sense is the road being shown, both roads, the climbing and the low. The Qur'an never says God selects which road the servant walks. The selection verses above say the opposite, and the school's formula names this exact division, God creates and sustains the power as enabled, the walking is the servant's.

7. The Vertical Frame

Source. Sirat al-Haqq, vol.2, p.203, p.220, p.221

Speaker. Shaykh Muhammad Asif al-Muhsini

Dating. 15th century AH

Claim. the divine will over a human act is the act's continuous bestowal of existence, and it does not compete with the agent's selection

Modern objection and modern Imami theology meet on this ground, and the school's own scholars state the causal structure with contemporary precision.

فالواجب الوجود هو الذي يبقي النفس ويعطيها القوة والمكنة والاقدار على إيجادها الأفعال ، بل الشوق الذي هو من مرجحات إرادتها وقصدها نحو الفعل مخلوق لله تعالى بلا مدخلية لاختيار النفس ، بل هي مجبولة ومفطورة على اشتياق ما يلائمها والتنفُّر عمَّا يزاحمها ، وإنَّما للنفس - بإذن الله وإقداره وتمكينه - هو الاختيار في قصدها نحو الفعل وعدم قصدها نحوه ، فلها أن تفعل ، ولها أن لا تفعل ، ولكن إذا فعلت فعلت بقوة الله التي أعطاها .

"The Necessary Being is the one who preserves the soul and gives it the strength, the capacity, and the enablement to bring its acts into being.

Even the longing, which is one of the weights that tip its will and its aim toward an act, is created by Allah, with no share for the soul's choice in it.

The soul is formed and patterned to long for what suits it and to recoil from what harms it.

What belongs to the soul, by Allah's permission and His enabling and His grant, is the choice in aiming at the act or not aiming at it.

It may act, and it may not act, but when it acts, it acts by the power of Allah, which He gave it."

- al-Muhsini (contemporary), Sirat al-Haqq, vol.2, p.203

Here the school answers the objection's regress and manipulation sections directly. The desires and the longing are indeed from God, the objection is right about that, and the school says so first. The selection, the aiming or not aiming, is the soul's, by permission and within the grant. The philosopher asks who determines the determination.

One distinction must be stated openly, because the objection lives on its absence. God does not create the act as selected. God creates and sustains the act as existent, and He creates and sustains the power as enabled. The selection, which direction the enabled power takes, is what the school assigns to the servant. The report of the dominion said God wills the act's existence, and the school holds He sustains the servant in prayer and in fornication alike, as a report of the school states. Sustenance is indifferent to direction, and that indifference is exactly the room the selection fills. The regress terminates by design. The selection is determined by nothing outside the soul's own enabled choosing, and the enabled choosing is held in existence by God. The objection presses one level up. Why does God bestow being on this volition, A and never B, when He could withhold? The answer the reports carry is the same two-wills division opened at Merv. The bestowal answers the existential will, which attaches to what the servant settles. The withholding answers the legislative will, which commands and permits and warns. The decree does not manufacture the settling it sustains, and the withholding God threatens as punishment is itself an act He threatens the servant can avoid. What remains, that God eternally knows which settling He will sustain, is knowledge, and knowledge does not force, because the known thing does not follow from the knowing the way smoke follows fire.

The objection will press its trilemma one final time at the level of character. To ask what determined the soul's act of selection is to ask for a cause behind the soul. The school's answer is that there is none. Either the selection is fixed by the God-given desires, or it is random, or something else determines it. The school takes the fourth road the trilemma does not list, because the trilemma assumes every determination is either external causation or chance. The corpus's reports posit the soul as a primitive chooser, a caused cause whose power is created while its selecting is its own. This is not randomness, because the selection follows the soul's own reasons and character. It is not external causation, because the character motivates and does not compel. The criterion for that difference is already in the corpus, and the note can state it plainly. The five-conditions letter counts the motive among the conditions of accountability. A world without motives would be a world without responsible acts at all. The presence of a motive is what makes an act the servant's, never what unfreezes its direction. A necessitating cause, the kind the lexicon ties to compulsion, produces its effect as fire produces burning, and the same effect follows wherever the cause stands. A motive does not work that way. The same desire, urged by the same character, is obeyed at one hour and refused at the next, and the refusal is a real occurrence the reports of struggle assume on every page. If the God-given character necessitated the sin infallibly, the sin would return to its necessitator, and the Merv report clears God of exactly that return. The school takes the Imam's clearing over the inference, and the difference between a necessitating cause and a motive is the ground the clearing stands on. The opponent may call this primitive agent-causation mysterious, but it is the named metaphysical content of the middle, asserted by the Imams against both horns, not a hole accidentally left. A regress needs each level to borrow its determinacy from the level above. The soul's selection borrows its existence and its power, which God gives. It borrows its determinacy from no one. Selecting is what this kind of granted cause does. The causation is stacked, and al-Muhsini names the stacking with a technical pair.

استناد فعل أو شيء إلى العلتين قد يكون عرضياً وقد يكون طولياً وعلى الأوّل يتمّ تخيّل الأشعري وعلى الثاني يتمّ ما هو الحق من الأمر بين الأمرين ، وقد وقع الماديون وطوايف من المسلمين منهم الأشاعرة في الاشتباه بين القسمين فانحرفوا عن الحق .

"The tracing of an act, or a thing, to two causes may be lateral, and it may be vertical.

On the lateral reading the Asharis' picture holds. On the vertical reading the truth of the affair between two affairs holds.

The materialists, and groups of Muslims among them the Asharis, fell into confusing the two kinds, and so swerved from the truth."

- al-Muhsini (contemporary), Sirat al-Haqq, vol.2, p.220

Lateral causation is two agents side by side, competing over one effect, and whoever accepts that picture must indeed choose between God and the creature. Vertical causation is a stack, in which the lower cause has its very power from the higher, and the two never compete because they do not stand on one plane. The dilemma is a theorem inside the lateral picture, and the school rejects the picture before it answers the theorem. On the will itself, the same work is exact.

والحقُّ أن يقال : إنَّ إرادة الله تعلَّقت بكل شيء حتى بالأفعال الاختيارية من دون أن يستلزم الجبر ! إذ الواجب يفيض على الممكن في بقاء وجوده وصدور أفعاله آناً فآناً ، سواء في حال صلواته أم في حال زناه ، كما بيّناه سابقاً ، وإرادته ليست إلا إفاضته

"The truth to be said is that Allah's will attaches to everything, even to free acts, without entailing compulsion.

The Necessary pours out upon the possible being, in the continuance of its existence and the issuing of its acts, instant after instant, in its state of prayer and in its state of fornication alike.

His will is nothing other than His bestowal."

- al-Muhsini (contemporary), Sirat al-Haqq, vol.2, p.221

The will over the act is the bestowal of the act's existence. God wills the praying and the fornicating in the sense that He grants both acts their being, moment by moment, while commanding the one and forbidding the other. Knowledge never enters this chain as a cause. The objection itself concedes, in its own section twelve, that knowledge does not force its object. The school agrees, and adds that the report from Merv already separated God's command will from His existential will. What the decree fixes is that the act, chosen as it is, will exist and will be judged. It fixes the arena and the reckoning. The selection inside the arena belongs to the servant, exactly as the letter of the five conditions laid out.

8. The Final Difference-Maker

Source. Dirasat fi Ilm al-Usul, vol.1, p.153

Speaker. the school of al-Khoei

Dating. 15th century AH

Claim. the free act differs from the tremor in direct experience, and the difference is the ground the objection demands

The objection's last stand is the manipulation argument. Move the programmer from the lab to heaven, it says, and nothing changes. The school's reply is that something changes, and that every human being already knows it changed, because he experiences the difference between his own choice and his own tremor.

أما الأشاعرة فيرد عليهم : أولا : انا بالوجدان نفرِّق بين حركة يد المرتعش وغيرها من الحركات الاختيارية ، وبين حركة النبض وتحريك الإصبع اختيارا ، والوجدان أعظم البراهين

"Against the Asharis it is replied, first, that in direct experience we distinguish between the movement of the trembling hand and other free movements, and between the pulse and the lifting of the finger by choice, and direct experience is the greatest of proofs."

- the school of al-Khoei, Dirasat fi Ilm al-Usul, vol.1, p.153

The same page places the four positions of the Muslims side by side.

واختياره تأثير فيها أصلا ، وانَّ العبد معرض ومحل للفعل نظير الجسم الَّذي يعرضه السواد ، وانما الفرق بينهما في انَّ خلق الفعل في الإنسان يكون مقارنا لقدرته بخلاف خلق السواد في الجسم .

"On that view his choice has no effect at all, and the servant is a surface and a location for the act, like the body on which blackness rests.

The only difference between them is that the creation of the act in the human accompanies his power, unlike the creation of blackness in the body."

- the school of al-Khoei, Dirasat fi Ilm al-Usul, vol.1, p.153

The Ashari picture in that paragraph is the neuroscientist's picture, a human surface on which acts are placed from outside. The Imami position, listed fourth on the same page as the affair between the two affairs, is the vertical one already established from the reports. So the answer to the single question, what ultimately explains why A becomes actual and never B, is now complete in three layers. The selection is made by the servant's own enabled choice, and nothing outside his choosing makes it. The choosing self, its reason, its power, its desires, its time, and its occasion exist from God instant by instant, so nothing escapes the dominion. The vertical order between the two means the layers never compete, and the choice is no more brute than a gift is random because its giver exists. The luck objection dissolves with the competition picture that generated it, for luck is what remains only where nothing settles the difference, and here the servant settles it.

The Qur'anic layer closes the case, and the school's own exegetes carry it.

إِنّا هَدَيْناهُ السَّبِيلَ إِمّا شاكِراً وإِمّا كَفُوراً

inna hadaynaka al-sabila imma shakiran wa-imma kafura

"We guided him the way, either grateful or ungrateful."

- Qur'an 76:3, relayed in al-Masabih

Guidance, then the two open branches. The verse names the givens and names the fork, and the fork is real because the whole apparatus of the reports makes it real. And the claim verse of the delegators, read through the vessels report, closes the circle it opened.

قوله (لا إكراه في الدين) اي لا يكره أحد على دينه الا بعد ان قد تبين له الرشد من الغي

qawluhu la ikraha fi al-din, ayy la yukraha ahadun ʿala dinihi

"His word, there is no coercion in religion, means no one is coerced as to his religion, except after the right way has become clear to him against the error."

- Qur'an 2:256, with Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.1, p.110

The Qur'an states the negation of compulsion in religion as the standing rule. Tafsir al-Qummi's own exception clause reads, except after the right way has become clear, which makes the negation epistemic. God's method is clarification before any coercion could ever enter, and the clarification is what the reports throughout the argument describe. The lexicons, the parable, the two definitions, the five conditions, and the vertical causation all explain how the negation coexists with the dominion. The objection answered a flattened picture of Islam, and that picture is incoherent. The architecture of the reports carries both the willing and the holding without competition, and the objection left it standing.

Glossary
  • jabr. Compulsion, the subduing and coercing of an agent so that his act is forced upon him. The first rejected horn.
  • tafwid. Delegation, the handing of an affair over to another so that he runs it independently. The second rejected horn.
  • amr bayn al-amrayn. The affair between the two affairs. The school's doctrine that the human act is enabled by God in its entirety and genuinely selected by the servant.
  • istitaʿa. The ability or capacity for an act. The school teaches it is owned through God, who grants it.
  • irada takwiniyya. The existential will, by which God grants a thing its being, whether He commands it or forbids it.
  • irada tashriʿiyya. The legislative will, God's commanding what He loves and forbidding what He hates.
  • qada. The decree. In the report of Merv, defined over human acts as the judgment they deserve of reward or punishment.

Final Verdict

The objection asked one honest question, and the school has one exact answer. At the point where two volitions stand open, the human being's own enabled choosing makes the difference between A and B. That choosing exists, with its entire furniture of reason and power and desire and time, inside a dominion whose Owner gives it being instant by instant. The Owner never competes with it, because giver and given never stand on one plane. Compulsion was named and damned by the Imams ﵈ thirteen centuries ago, and the neuroscientist argument is that named and damned doctrine wearing a laboratory coat. Delegation was named and damned beside it. The scribe objection, stated and answered in the body, is the decree argument in its recording form, and it falls with the two wills that split the decree's existence from the servant's selection. The middle, the affair between the two affairs, is neither a mystery word nor a redescription. It is the parable's structure, the letter's five conditions, the two wills of Merv, the ability owned through God, and the vertical bestowal. Each element is stated in corpus texts, each verified here against the editions that carry them. The human being wills, and his willing is his, and his willing is held. The judge may hold him responsible for A and never B because, in the only sense the classical texts ever gave these words, both the willing and the holding are true.
