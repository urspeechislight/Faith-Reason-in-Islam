The Two Angels at Babylon

The full story of Harut and Marut as the corpus holds it. The two recensions of their fall and the judge tradition from Ali ﷺ stand beside the school's counter-reading of the two teachers and the Imams' verdict that angels never fall. From al-Qummi, al-Ayyashi, al-Burhan, Bihar al-Anwar, Majma al-Bayan, and Wasa'il al-Shia.

Babylon is where this history asks its hardest question. The Qur'an names two angels, Harut and Marut, in the same verse that clears Solomon ﵇. It says something was sent down upon them at Babylon, and that people learned from them the craft that separates a man from his wife. What the school carries about those two angels is unusual in its honesty. It carries the whole story in more than one recension, an independent telling of the same report through its own chain. It also carries its own verdicts against the story's plain sense, sometimes inside the very books that record it. This part holds both, and hides neither.

The Angels Wondered at Mankind

Al-Qummi's narration from al-Baqir ﵇ begins in heaven, with the scribes of the earth's sins and their protest. The report runs unbroken from that protest to the hanging of the two in the air over Babylon.

"My father told me from al-Hasan ibn Mahbub, from Ali ibn Riyab, from Muhammad ibn Qays, that Abu Jafar said, Ata asked him about Harut and Marut while we were in Mecca. Abu Jafar replied, "Angels came down from heaven to earth every day and night. They watched over the people of the earth, among Adam's children and the jinn. They recorded their deeds and took them up to heaven. The people of heaven cried out because of the sins of the people on earth. They spoke among themselves about the lies they heard and saw people inventing against Allah, their boldness toward Him, and what people said and described about Him. They declared Allah free from all this. A group of angels said, 'Our Lord, do You not become angry at what Your creatures do on Your earth? They describe You with lies, speak falsehood, and commit sins. You forbade them from these acts, yet You bear with them while they remain within Your power, authority, and well-being.'" Abu Jafar said, Allah wanted to show the angels His power and the force of His command over all creation. He wanted them to know the favor He had given them, how He had spared them from what His creatures did, how He had made them obedient, and how He had protected them from sin. Allah revealed to the angels, "Choose two angels from among yourselves. I will send them down to earth. I will give them the same desire for food, drink, sex, greed, and hope that I gave Adam's children. Then I will test their obedience to Me." They chose Harut and Marut. They had spoken most harshly against Adam's children and most strongly called for Allah's anger upon them. Allah revealed to them, "Go down to earth. I have placed in you the same desire for food, drink, sex, greed, and hope that I placed in Adam's children." Allah then revealed to them, "Take care not to associate anything with Me. Do not kill a life Allah has made sacred. Do not commit adultery. Do not drink wine." He uncovered the seven heavens for them, so that He could show them His power. Then He sent them down to earth in human form and clothing. They came down near Babylon. A tall, shining building appeared before the two of them, and they went toward it. Near it stood a beautiful, well-dressed, perfumed woman. Her face was uncovered, and she walked toward them. When they saw her, spoke with her, and looked at her closely, the desire placed within them took a strong hold on their hearts. They returned to her in temptation and failure, and asked her to give herself to them. She said, "I follow a religion. My religion does not allow me to grant what you want unless you enter the religion I follow." They asked, "What is your religion?" She said, "I have a god. Whoever worships him and bows before him may ask me for anything, and I will grant it." They asked, "Who is your god?" She said, "This idol is my god." One looked at the other and said, "These are two acts Allah forbade us, associating partners with Him and adultery. If we bow to this idol and worship it, we will associate partners with Allah. We would associate partners with Allah to reach adultery. We seek adultery, and we cannot reach it except through associating partners with Allah." They discussed the matter together. The desire placed within them overcame them. They said, "We will grant what you asked." She said, "Then drink this wine. It is an offering to him. Through it, you will reach what you want." They discussed the matter together and said, "These are three acts our Lord forbade us, associating partners with Him, adultery, and drinking wine. We would enter into drinking wine and associating partners with Allah to reach adultery." They discussed the matter together. They said, "How great is the trial you have brought us. We have granted what you asked." She said, "Then drink this wine, worship this idol, and bow before it." They drank the wine and worshipped the idol. Then they asked her to give herself to them. When she prepared herself for them and they prepared themselves for her, a beggar came in asking for help. When the beggar saw them and they saw him, they were frightened. The beggar said, "You are two frightened men who entered with this beautiful, perfumed woman. You are evil men." Then he left them. She said to them, "By my god, you will not reach me now. This man has seen your condition and knows where you are. He will leave and tell others about you. Go after him quickly and kill him before he exposes you and me. Then fulfill your desire while you are calm and safe." They got up, caught the man, and killed him. Then they returned to her, but they did not see her. Their private parts became visible to them, their fine clothing was removed, and they were overcome with regret. Allah revealed to them, "I sent you down to earth among My creatures for one part of a day. You disobeyed Me through four sins, all of which I had forbidden you. You did not watch yourselves before Me or feel shame before Me. You had been among those most eager for My punishment and anger against the people of earth because of their sins. I gave you the nature of My creatures and removed My protection from sin from you. How did you find the place where I abandoned you? Choose the punishment of this world or the punishment of the Hereafter." One said to the other, "Let us enjoy its desires in this world until we reach the punishment of the Hereafter." The other said, "The punishment of this world has a fixed end. The punishment of the Hereafter continues without end. We will not choose the severe, lasting punishment of the Hereafter over the temporary punishment of this world." They chose the punishment of this world, and the two went on teaching people sorcery in the land of Babylon. Then, when they had taught the people sorcery, they were raised from the earth into the air, and they remain punished, turned upside down, and hanging in the air until the Day of Resurrection."

The Woman at the Shining House

Al-Ayyashi carries the same story as a second recension of the same report through Muhammad ibn Qays. It keeps the district name, Mahruz, that al-Qummi's wording drops, and it keeps the moment the two drew back before the desire overcame them.

The Counter-Reading of the Two Teachers

The tafsir attributed to Imam al-Askari ﵈ reads the two angels as teachers of the antidote, sent because sorcery had flooded the earth after Noah ﵇. The reading is carried in full in Part 1 of this series. Its continuation reaches the warning that stood before every lesson.

The Weighing of the School

Al-Majlisi mapped the readings of the verse, and the negation reading carries the strangest fruit of all, that the two kings slandered as sources of sorcery were Jibril and Mika'il themselves.

وثالث الوجوه في الآية أن تحمل " ما " في قوله تعالى " وما أنزل على الملكين " على الجحد والنفي ، فكأنه تعالى قال : واتبعوا ما تتلوا الشياطين على ملك سليمان وما كفر [ سليمان ] وما أنزل الله السحر على الملكين ولكن الشياطين كفروا يعلمون الناس السحر ببابل هاروت وماروت . ويكون قوله تعالى " ببابل هاروت وماروت " من المؤخر الذي معناه التقديم ، فيكون على هذا التأويل هاروت وماروت رجلين من جملة هذان أسماهما ، وإنما ذكرا بعد ذكر الناس تمييزا وتبيينا ، ويكون الملكان المذكوران اللذان نفى تعالى عنهما السحر جبرئيل وميكائيل ، لان سحرة اليهود فيما ذكر كانت تدعي أن الله تعالى أنزل السحر على لسان جبرئيل وميكائيل إلى سليمان ، فأكذبهما الله تعالى بذلك ، ويجوز أن يكون هاروت وماروت يرجعان إلى الشياطين ، كأنه تعالى قال : ولكن الشياطين هاروت وماروت كفروا ، ويسوغ ذلك كما ساغ في قوله " وكنا لحكمهم شاهدين " يعني تعالى حكم داود وسليمان ، ويكون قوله تعالى على هذا التأويل " وما يعلمان من أحد حتى يقولا إنما نحن فتنة " راجعا إلى هاروت وماروت اللذين هما من الشياطين أو من الانس المتعلمين للسحر من الشياطين والعاملين به ، ومعنى قولهما " إنما نحن فتنة فلا تكفر " يكون على طريق الاستهزاء أو التماجن والتخالع كما يقول الماجن من الناس إذا فعل قبيحا أو قال باطلا : هذا فعل من لا يفلح ، وقول من لا ينجو ، والله لا حصلت إلا على الخسران . وليس ذلك منه على سبيل النصيحة للناس وتحذيرهم من مثل فعل فعله ، بل على جهة المجون والتهالك . ويجوز أيضا على هذا التأويل الذي تضمن الجحد والنفي أن يكون هاروت وماروت اسمين للملكين ، ونفى عنهما إنزال السحر بقوله تعالى " وما أنزل على الملكين " ويكون قوله تعالى " وما يعلمان من أحد " يرجع إلى قبيلتين من الجن أو إلى شياطين الجن والإنس فتحسن التثنية لهذا . وقد روي هذا التأويل في حمل " ما " على النفي عن ابن عباس وغيره من المفسرين ، وحكي عنه أيضا أنه كان يقرأ " على الملكين " بكسر اللام ، ويقول : متى كان العلجان ملكين إنما كانا ملكين

"A third interpretation of the verse reads the word "ma" in God's words, "and what was sent down to the two angels," as a denial. The meaning becomes that they followed what the devils recited during Solomon's rule. Solomon did not disbelieve. God did not send down magic to the two angels. The devils disbelieved by teaching people magic in Babylon, Harut and Marut. In this reading, God's words, "in Babylon, Harut and Marut," place later words before earlier ones in meaning. Harut and Marut were then two men among the people, and these were their names. They were mentioned after the people to identify them clearly. The two angels from whom God denied sending magic were Gabriel and Michael. Jewish magicians claimed that God sent magic through Gabriel and Michael to Solomon. God declared their claim false. It is also possible that Harut and Marut refer back to the satans, as if God said, but the satans, Harut and Marut, disbelieved. This works as it worked in His words, "and we were witnesses of their judgment," meaning the judgment of David and Solomon. On this reading, God's words, "and they teach no one until they say, we are only a trial," return to Harut and Marut, who were of the satans or of the humans who learned sorcery from the satans and worked with it. Their words, "we are only a trial, so do not disbelieve," would then be mockery and buffoonery, like what a fool among people says when he does something ugly or says something false. This is the deed of one who will not prosper and the word of one who will not be saved. By God, I have gained nothing but loss. That would not be counsel to the people and a warning against the like of his own deed, but mockery and ruin. It is also possible, on this reading that carries the denial, that Harut and Marut are the names of the two angels. He denied the sending down of sorcery to them with His words, "and what was sent down upon the two angels," and His words, "and they teach no one" return to two tribes of the jinn or to satans of the jinn and of humans, so the dual form fits them. This reading, taking "ma" as a denial, has been related from Ibn Abbas and others of the exegetes. It is also reported of him that he read "upon the two kings" with a broken lam, and said, when were the two non-Arabs angels? They were only two kings."

- al-Majlisi, Bihar al-Anwar, vol.56, p.272-273

His juristic principle on learning sorcery closes the weighing.

"This verse shows that actions differ according to their intentions. Learning magic to remove doubt, guard against it, and avoid it is faith. Believing in it and using it is disbelief."

Commentary

Why the Heaven Protested

Source: Tafsir al-Qummi vol.1 p.81-82

Claim: the angels who recorded mankind's sins protested at sin, and God answered with a designed test

Speaker: al-Baqir ﵇

The angels who wondered how men could sin under God's patience received the appetite of men and the commandments that guard it. The reversal carries the narration's whole lesson. The design named every planted trait, food and drink and desire, greed and hope, and the four prohibitions stood against them. Whatever the reader decides about the story's ending, its opening is a theology lesson, that understanding obedience costs something, and the angels who judged mankind were invoiced for the lesson.

The Choice of the World's Punishment

Source: Tafsir al-Qummi vol.1 p.83-84

Claim: the two chose the world's punishment because it ends, and they hang between earth and sky until the Day of Resurrection

Speaker: al-Baqir ﵇

The protest that opened the test is answered inside its ending. The dialogue of the two choices is the narration's high point. One angel argued for enjoying the world and paying later, and the other answered with a piece of arithmetic, that a punishment with an end cannot be weighed against one without end. Al-Qummi's recension and al-Ayyashi's agree on the ending, that they still hang upside down in the air. The story tradition thus leaves them suspended inside time, punished and teaching, and the image is exactly what the school's verdicts will address.

The Star and the Woman

Source: Tafsir al-Ayyashi vol.1 p.61-62; Bihar al-Anwar vol.56 p.325-326

Claim: Ali ﷺ told the two as judges whose ascension formula the woman extracted, and she became the red star

Speaker: Ali ﵇; al-Saduq; the recension chains

Ali's ﵇ recension changes the offices and keeps the fall. Here the two angels judged the people by day in two quarters and rose to heaven by night, and the beautiful litigant trapped them into giving up the formula of their return. The woman rises, the heavens open to watch, and she is metamorphosed into the star the Arabs could point at. The corpus then does something rare. It carries her names in three recensions, and it carries al-Rida's ﷺ flat denial that she is any star at all, since nothing transformed survives three days. The story and the denial stand on facing pages of the same volume.

The Antidote Reading

Source: Wasa'il al-Shia vol.17 p.147; Bihar al-Anwar vol.56 p.325; Majma al-Bayan vol.1 p.333

Claim: the two angels taught the breaking of sorcery, with a warning before every lesson, and learning it to guard is faith

Speaker: al-Sadiq ﵇; al-Rida ﵇; al-Tabarsi

The counter-reading dissolves the fall without dissolving the mission. After Noah ﵇ the sorcerers multiplied, so God sent two angels to the prophet of the era carrying the sickness and its cure together, like a man taught poison and its antidote in one lesson. No student heard a word without the warning that the teachers were a trial, and the warning named the exact line of disbelief, to claim that by this craft one gives life and death. Al-Tabarsi's principle completes the reading. Knowledge of a sin is not the sin, and the deed is judged by its intention.

The Imam's Answer

Source: al-Burhan vol.1 p.355-356

Claim: the questioners recited the whole popular story and the Imam answered from the Qur'an's verses on the angels

Speaker: al-Hasan al-Askari ﵈

The value of this exchange is that the story was not whispered to the Imam. It was recited to him in full, the two chosen angels, the third companion, the woman, the wine, the killing, the punishment at Babylon, the sorcerers who study under them, and the star. The Imam answered with three verses, that the angels do not disobey, do not tire, and speak nothing before God.

Then he made the argument from office, that had the two ruled on earth they would have been as prophets and Imams, and can prophets and Imams kill and fornicate. Al-Tabarsi's verdict stands with him, that whoever holds the impeccability of the angels cannot accept the reading. The story is not hidden by the school. It is answered by it.

The Readers Who Never Fell

Source: Bihar al-Anwar vol.56 p.327 and p.272-273

Claim: other angels were given desires and passed, and the school's readings include that the two kings of the verse are Jibril and Mika'il slandered

Speaker: al-Sadiq ﵇; al-Majlisi

Two exhibits close the circle. In one, angels who criticized mankind for lawful pleasures were given those pleasures themselves, cried out for restoration, and will greet the people of Paradise on the Last Day. The test that the story says broke two angels is shown passing through others.

In the other, al-Majlisi records the reading that the entire fall narrative rests on a slander, that Jewish sorcerers claimed their magic came down through Jibril and Mika'il, and the verse's denial clears the two kings as it cleared Solomon ﵇. Al-Majlisi sets that reading among the weighed options. He does not rule by it. Taken up, it closes this history's ring on the note it opened, one accusation aimed across the ages at prophets, angels, and the House of the Prophet ﷺ alike.

What Babylon Left in the World

The verse of Babylon guarded the names of two angels and set the price of the craft in one line, that those who buy it have no share in the Hereafter. It would one day clear Solomon ﵇ of the libel pinned on his throne. The word born beside that verse now travels forward with the craft itself. It meets Moses ﵇ in the court of Pharaoh, and that meeting is the next part.
