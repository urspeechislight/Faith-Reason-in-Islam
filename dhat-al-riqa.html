The Ground of Patches

Into Najd again, against Anmar and Tha'laba of Ghatafan. The army marched to Nakhl and the ground called Dhat al-Riqa, met the enemy near Usfan, prayed the fear prayer under their eyes, and drew apart without a battle.▼ commentary

The march carries two of the sira's most retold scenes. One is the enemy who drew a sword on the resting Prophet ﷺ and lost it to one word. The other is the prayer Allah taught an army that must watch its enemy while it worships.▼ commentary▼ commentary

Al-Maqrizi's entry fixes the date, the target, and the name.

ثم خرج ﵌ إلى غزوة ذات الرقاع من نخل ، ليلة السبت ، لعشر خلون من المحرم ، على رأس سبعة وأربعين شهرا ، يريد أنمارا وثعلبة ، فبلغ صرار ، وعاد يوم الأحد لخمس بقين منه ، [فكانت] غيبته خمسة عشرة ليلة ، ولم يلق كيدا ، [واستخلف على المدينة عثمان بن عفان - ﵁] (1) . [قال ابن إسحاق : ثم أقام رسول الله ﵌ بالمدينة بعد غزوة بني النضير شهر ربيع الآخر ، وبعض جمادى ، ثم غرا نجدا يريد بني محارب وبني ثعلبة من غطفان ، حتى نزل نخلا [موضع بنجد] ، وهي غزوة ذات الرقاع] (2) .

[قال ابن هشام : وإنما قبل لها غزوة ذات الرقاع ، لأنهم رفعوا فيها راياتهم ، ويقال : ذات الرقاع شجرة بذلك الموضع يقال لها ذات الرقاع] (2) . [وذكر غيره : أنها أرض فيها يقع سود ، ويقع بيض ، كأنها مرقعة برقاع مختلفة ، فسميت ذات الرقاع لذلك ، وكانوا قد نزلوا في تلك الغزاة] (2) .

"Then he went out to the expedition of Dhat al-Riqa from Nakhl, on Saturday night, ten nights into Muharram, forty seven months on, with Anmar and Tha'laba in view. He reached Sarar, a staging ground on the Najd road, and came back on Sunday with five nights of the month left, and his absence was fifteen nights, and he met no hostility. He put Uthman ibn Affan in charge of Medina. Ibn Ishaq said. The Messenger of Allah stayed in Medina after the expedition against the Nadir through Rabi' al-Akhir and part of Jumada, then marched on Najd, with Muharib and Tha'laba of Ghatafan in view, until he camped at Nakhl, a place in Najd, and this is the expedition of Dhat al-Riqa. Ibn Hisham said it was called Dhat al-Riqa because they raised their banners there, and it is said Dhat al-Riqa is a tree at that place so named, and others say it is ground of black and white patches, as if patched with different rags, and so it was named, and they had camped there on this march."

- al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.8, p.363, carrying Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham with the etymologies

Al-Kharkushi compresses the encounter.

ثم غزا رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم ذات الرقاع ، فلقي بها العدو قريبا من عسفان ، فصلى فيها صلاة الخوف ، ثم انصرف الفريقان

"Then the Messenger of Allah raided Dhat al-Riqa and met the enemy near Usfan. He prayed the fear prayer there, and the two sides drew apart."

- al-Kharkushi, Sharaf al-Mustafa, vol.3, p.41

The prayer Allah taught for exactly this hour is in the Qur'an itself.

وَإِذَا كُنتَ فِيهِمْ فَأَقَمْتَ لَهُمُ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ فَلْتَقُمْ طَآئِفَةٌۭ مِّنْهُم مَّعَكَ وَلْيَأْخُذُوٓا۟ أَسْلِحَتَهُمْ فَإِذَا سَجَدُوا۟ فَلْيَكُونُوا۟ مِن وَرَآئِكُمْ وَلْتَأْتِ طَآئِفَةٌ أُخْرَىٰ لَمْ يُصَلُّوا۟ فَلْيُصَلُّوا۟ مَعَكَ وَلْيَأْخُذُوا۟ حِذْرَهُمْ وَأَسْلِحَتَهُمْ ۗ وَدَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَوْ تَغْفُلُونَ عَنْ أَسْلِحَتِكُمْ وَأَمْتِعَتِكُمْ فَيَمِيلُونَ عَلَيْكُم مَّيْلَةًۭ وَٰحِدَةًۭ ۚ وَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِن كَانَ بِكُمْ أَذًۭى مِّن مَّطَرٍ أَوْ كُنتُم مَّرْضَىٰٓ أَن تَضَعُوٓا۟ أَسْلِحَتَكُمْ ۖ وَخُذُوا۟ حِذْرَكُمْ ۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ أَعَدَّ لِلْكَٰفِرِينَ عَذَابًۭا مُّهِينًۭا

"When you are among them and lead them in prayer, let a group of them stand with you, carrying their weapons. When they have prostrated, let them take position behind you, and let another group who have not yet prayed come and pray with you, carrying their weapons and taking precautions."

- Qur'an 4:102, Uthmani text, the verse of the fear prayer

The full scene of Ghawrath ibn al-Harith, in the Musnad of Abu Yala.

فرأوا من المسلمين غرة فجاء رجل منهم يقال له غورث بن الحارث حتى قام على رأس رسول الله ﵌ بالسيف فقال من يمنعك مني قال الله قال فسقط السيف من يده فأخذ رسول الله ﵌ السيف فقال له من يمنعك مني قال كن خير آخذ قال تشهد أن لا إله إلا الله قال لا ولكني أعاهدك أن لا أقاتلك ولا أكون مع قوم يقاتلونك قال فخلى سبيله فجاء إلى أصحابه فقال جئتكم من عند خير الناس فلما كان عند الظهر أو العصر أمر النبي ﵌ بصلاة الخوف شك أبو عوانة قال فكان الناس طائفتين طائفة بإزاء عدوهم وطائفة يصلون مع رسول الله ﵌ فصلى بالطائفة الذين معه ركعتين ثم انصرفوا فكانوا في مكان أولئك وجاء أولئك فصلوا مع النبي ﵌ ركعتين فكانت لرسول الله أربع ركعات وللقوم ركعتين

"They saw a moment of unawareness among the Muslims, and a man of theirs called Ghawrath ibn al-Harith came until he stood over the Messenger of Allah with his sword and said, 'Who will protect you from me?' He said, 'Allah.' And the sword fell from Ghawrath's hand. The Messenger of Allah took the sword and said to him, 'And who will protect you from me?' Ghawrath said, 'Be the best of takers.' He said, 'Bear witness that there is no god but Allah.' Ghawrath said, 'No, but I make a pact with you, that I will not fight you and will not be with any people who fight you.' And he let him go. Ghawrath came to his companions and said, 'I come to you from the best of people.' When it was noon or mid afternoon, the Prophet ordered the fear prayer, and Abu Awanah was unsure which. The people stood in two groups, one facing the enemy and one praying with the Messenger of Allah. He prayed two rak'as with the group with him, and they went out and stood in the place of the others, and the others came and prayed two rak'as with the Prophet, so the Messenger of Allah had four rak'as and the people two."

- Abu Yala al-Mawsili, Musnad Abi Yala, vol.3, p.313

Al-Tabari carries the harder variant of the same scene, with the verse.

كان رسول الله ﵌ إذا نزل منزلا اختار له أصحابه النبي ﵌ شجرة ظليلة ، فيقيل تحتها ، فأتاه أعرابي ، فاخترط سيفه ثم قال : من يمنعك مني ؟ قال : " الله " . فرعدت يد الأعرابي ، وسقط السيف منه . قال : وضرب برأسه الشجرة حتى انتثر دماغه ، فأنزل الله : وَاللَّهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاسِ

"When the Messenger of Allah camped anywhere, his companions would choose the Prophet a shady tree and he would rest at midday under it. A Bedouin came to him, drew his sword, and said, 'Who will protect you from me?' He said, 'Allah.' The Bedouin's hand trembled and the sword fell from it. He dashed his head against the tree until his brain scattered, and Allah sent down, 'Allah will protect you from the people.'"

- al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan (al-Amiriyya edition), vol.6, p.201, on the verse of protection

"After that al-Waqidi mentioned the expedition of Dhat al-Riqa and said, the Messenger of Allah went out to it on Saturday night, ten nights into Muharram, forty seven months on, was away fifteen days, and came back on Sunday with five nights of Muharram left. But Ibn Ishaq placed Dhat al-Riqa after the Nadir, saying the Messenger of Allah stayed after the Nadir expedition through Rabi' al-Akhir and part of Jumada al-Ula, then marched on Najd, and this is the expedition of Dhat al-Riqa. Commentary

The Sources for Dhat al-Riqa

Source spine: al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.8, p.363, carrying Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Hisham, and the etymologies

Hadith lines: Abu Yala, Musnad, vol.3, p.313, on Ghawrath and the fear prayer. al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan (al-Amiriyya edition), vol.6, p.201, on the variant scene and the verse of protection

Shia line: al-Yusufi al-Gharawi, Mawsu'at al-Tarikh al-Islami, vol.2, p.462, following al-Tabarsi's I'lam al-Wara and Ibn Shahrashub on the dating

Scripture: Qur'an 4:102, the verse of the fear prayer, Uthmani text

Note: the sword scene exists in two versions, a release by pact and a self-destruction, and the works quoted name the same opening for both

The march is dated two ways, al-Waqidi's ten nights into Muharram at forty seven months, and Ibn Ishaq's placement two months after the Nadir, which the Shia chroniclers al-Tabarsi and Ibn Shahrashub followed. The series follows al-Waqidi's list, as it does everywhere, and holds the disagreement in view. The deputy over Medina was Uthman ibn Affan.

The Sword Under the Tree

The attempt: Ghawrath ibn al-Harith stood over the resting Prophet ﷺ with a drawn sword

The answer: one word, Allah, and the sword fell

The release: Ghawrath declined Islam and took a pact, no fighting against the Prophet ﷺ, no joining his enemies, and went home calling him the best of people

The variant: in al-Tabari's line the attacker dashed his own head against the tree, and the verse of protection came down

Two versions of one story, and they teach the same lesson through opposite endings. In the Musnad's version the man lives, sworn off war against the Muslims, a walking witness among his own tribe. In al-Tabari's version he ends himself, and the verse, Allah will protect you from the people, becomes the seal. The record does not force a choice, and both stand here, named by their carriers.

The Fear Prayer and the Field

Prayer: two groups alternating, one facing the enemy, and the rule it produced entered the law

Contact: the two sides drew apart without a battle

Casualties: none reported in these carriers

Duration: fifteen nights away, returning with five nights of Muharram left

The fear prayer is the march's legal monument. Qur'an 4:102 teaches the form directly, a group standing armed with the Prophet while another prays, then trading places, and the march's own record shows the form in the doing. It stayed in the practice of the community after the danger that produced it passed.

No killing is recorded in the works quoted here. The tactical outcome was a standoff, and the strategic outcome was the Najd tribes seeing again that the army of Medina came to their ground on news of gatherings.

Was the Expedition of Dhat al-Riqa Justified

The march belongs to the Najd pattern, the series of marches to the plateau that al-Kudr, Dhat Amr, and Bahran set. The works quoted here name only the targets, Anmar and Tha'laba, and record no gathering and no hostility met. This time the enemy stood and faced the army without attacking.

The two scenes of the march are the best answer to the charge of aggression. An enemy chieftain alone with the unarmed Prophet ﷺ went home with his life and a pact. The army itself worshiped under the enemy's eyes, in the ordered halves of the fear prayer, and both sides drew apart. The restraint is the record's own, carried in both scenes.

The expedition could have been prevented. The gathering was the tribes' answer to the year's politics, and the march was Medina's standing reply. Within the march, both sides chose not to fight. The two worshiping halves of the fear prayer stand in the record as the mark of an army that expected battle and did not start one.
