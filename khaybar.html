The War of Forts

In the seventh year after the Hijra, Khaybar was the fortress of the men who had bought the Trench. The exiled clan of al-Nadir held it alongside the other Jewish tribes of the oasis, and the truce with Mecca had opened the northern road.

Kinana ibn Abi al-Haqiq had gone to Ghatafan with fourteen men, calling the tribe to the Jews' aid for half of Khaybar's produce for a year. The campaign that followed was a war of forts, and its decisive hour came at the last stronghold, in a duel.▼ commentary▼ commentary

"The Jews used to stand to arms every night before dawn and draw up their squadrons in order of battle. Kinana ibn Abi al-Haqiq went out to Ghatafan with fourteen men, calling them to aid them, and theirs would be half the produce of Khaybar for a year. When the Messenger of Allah camped on their ground, they stirred that night no more, and no rooster crowed for them until the sun rose. They woke with their hearts pounding, opened their forts, and went out to their work with the hoes and the mattocks and the baskets, and when they saw the Muslims they cried, 'Muhammad and the army,' and ran back into their forts, while the Messenger of Allah said, 'Allah is greater. Khaybar is ruined. When we alight in the courtyard of a people, evil is the morning of the warned.' The fighting of the men of al-Nata. He fought the men of al-Nata that day until night, and at night he moved the people to al-Rajiʿ. He would go out with the Muslims under their banners, and their cry was, 'O you whom victory is given, strike them down.' He ordered the cutting of their palms, and the Muslims fell to it until they had cut four hundred clusters, and then he called out forbidding it. It is related that when the Messenger of Allah came down at Khaybar the migraine took him, and he did not go out to the people."

- al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.1, p.353

Commentary

Poetry: the Haydara rajaz, the opening verses on both communities' chains, with al-Nawawi's note from the two Sahihs, Bukhari and Muslim, from Salama ibn al-Akwa

Al-Maqrizi's spine carries the campaign from the bribe to the palms, and the Shia line adds the duel at the stronghold. The Jews stand alone in the morning the quote describes, and Ghatafan's answer stays outside the record.

The Duel at the Stronghold

The challenger: Marhab the Khaybari, the man Ali ﵇ faced at the stronghold

The answer: Ali ﵇ answered with verses of his own, and one blow

The blow: his sword Dhu l-Fiqar split stone, helmet, and head to the teeth, and the camp heard it

The banner: promised for the next day to a man whom Allah and His Messenger love, on Ibn Hibban's chain from Salama

The result: the stronghold taken

When Ali ﵇ faced Marhab, the verses he spoke named him by his mother's name for him, Haydara, the lion. The two lines carry the promise of a full measure, the sa', the grain measure, in one and the sword in the other. Al-Mar'ashi's page collects the Sunni attestations, and the two quoted here are al-Nawawi's note from the two Sahihs, Bukhari and Muslim, and Ibn Abi l-Hadid's in Sharh Nahj al-Balagha.

The blow the whole camp heard closed the campaign's decisive gate. The migraine kept the Prophet ﷺ from going out to the people, and Ibn Hibban's chain from the same campaign carries the promise of the banner for the next day, a man whom Allah and His Messenger love, with the victory granted through him.

What the Campaign Left

Tactical: the men of al-Nata fought to the night, the last stronghold taken by the duel

Strategic: the exiles' Khaybar base broken at its last stronghold, completing what the Trench had begun

Restraint on record: the palm cutting ordered and then forbidden, four hundred clusters and no more

The palm order is the campaign's own test of conduct. The trees were the enemy's wealth and the land's future at once. The reports keep both orders, the cutting and the stopping, and the stopping preserved the land's future.

The strategic outcome completed what the Trench had begun. The war cry of the arrival, Khaybar is ruined, came before the day the last stronghold fell to the duel.

Was the Campaign of Khaybar Justified

The cause is easy to state. The men who had bought the ten-thousand-man confederacy against Medina were living armed in the forts of Khaybar. Before the army ever camped at their walls, they tried the bribe again, half the produce of a year to whoever would come to their aid. A state that leaves that base standing invites the next coalition before the truce with Mecca runs out.

The conduct matched the cause. The palms were cut to four hundred clusters, and then the cutting was forbidden by order. The duel that decided the last stronghold was a single combat between two men.

Prevention belonged to the exiled instigators. They could have left the fortress, withheld the bribe, or stopped financing the coalition. The truce with Mecca made the northern war possible. The exiles had spent years making that war necessary.
