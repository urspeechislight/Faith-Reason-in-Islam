The Gathering That Melted

In the middle of Muharram of the third year after the Hijra, word reached Medina of a war gathering. Sulaym and Ghatafan, the two great tribes of Qays on the Najd side, were collecting at the water of Qarqarat al-Kudr, eight barid, eight days' stages, from the city. One later carrier reads Medina as the target.

The Prophet ﷺ marched with two hundred men, Ali ﵇ carrying the white banner. The gathering scattered before he arrived. He stayed three nights at the water, took the herds the tribesmen left behind, and came home without a blow.▼ commentary

Al-Baladhuri's entry gives the cause and the find.▼ commentary

ثم غزاة قرقرة الكدر .

وبعضهم يقول : « قرارة » . والأول أثبت .

وكان في المحرم سنة ثلاث . وكان سببها أنه بلغ رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم أن بها جمعا من غطفان وبنى سليم . فسار إليهم ، فتفرّقوا . ولم يلق كيدا ، ووجد لهم نعما مع رعاتها . ويقال إنه وجد نعما وشاء

"Then the expedition of Qarqarat al-Kudr. Some say Qararah. The first spelling is more established. It was in Muharram of year three. Its cause was that the Messenger of Allah received word that a group of Ghatafan and Banu Sulaym were there. He marched toward them, and they scattered. He did not meet any resistance. He found their camels with their herders. It is said he found camels and sheep."

- al-Baladhuri, Ansab al-Ashraf, vol.1, p.364

Al-Mas'udi fixes the date, the road, and the distance.

ثم غزوة رسول الله ﵌ للنصف من المحرم في مائتين إلى الماء المعروف بقرقرة الكدر ، ناحية معدن بنى سليم ، مما يلي جادة العراق إلى مكة وبين المعدن والمدينة ثمانية برد يريد سليم بن منصور بن عكرمة بن خصفة ابن قيس بن عيلان بن مضر بن نزار ، وغطفان بن سعد بن قيس بن عيلان بن مضر بن نزار ، فانجفلوا وغنم من أموالهم ، ورجع ولم يلق كيدا ، وكان استخلف على المدينة ابن أم مكتوم

"Then the Messenger of Allah marched in the middle of Muharram with two hundred men to the water known as Qarqarat al-Kudr, on the side of the mine of Banu Sulaym, toward the Iraq road to Mecca. The distance between the mine and Medina is eight barid. He had Sulaym and Ghatafan in view. They scattered, and he took spoils from their wealth. He went back without meeting any resistance, and he had left Ibn Umm Maktum in charge of Medina."

- al-Mas'udi, al-Tanbih wa-l-Ishraf, p.223

The Shia historian al-Askari carries the same march.

استخلف ابن أم مكتوم في غزوة قرقرة الكدر ، وسار ﵌ وسلم للنصف من المحرم يريد سليم وغطفان - قبيلتين من قيس عيلان - فانجفلوا ، وغنم من أموالهم ، ورجع ولم يلق كيدا (4) .

"Ibn Umm Maktum was left in charge during the Expedition of Qarqarat al-Kudr. The Messenger of Allah marched in the middle of Muharram toward Sulaym and Ghatafan, two tribes of Qays Aylan. They scattered. He took spoils from their wealth. He returned without meeting any resistance."

- al-Askari, Ma'alim al-Madarisayn, vol.1, p.207

Al-Amili's critical sira adds the banner, the stay, and the spoils count.▼ commentary

يقول المؤرخون : إن النبي «﵌» غزا بنفسه بني سليم بعد بدر بسبع ليال ، واستخلف على المدينة ابن أم مكتوم ، أو سباع بن عرفطة ؛ فلما بلغ ماء من مياههم يقال له : الكدر ، أقام «﵌» هناك ثلاث ليال ، ثم رجع إلى المدينة ، ولم يلقَ كيداً ، وكان يحمل لواءه أمير المؤمنين «﵇» ، وكان اللواء أبيض اللون

ويبدو أن هذه هي نفس الغزوة التي يقال لها : «غزوة قرقرة الكدر» . وسببها أنه قد بلغه «﵌» : أن جمعاً من بني سليم وغطفان بقرقرة الكدر (والظاهر أنهم كانوا ينوون غزو المدينة) فسار إليهم في مائتين من أصحابه ، فغنم منهم خمسمائة بعير ، فخمَّسها ، وقسم الباقي على أصحابه .

"Historians say the Prophet himself marched against Banu Sulaym seven nights after Badr. He left Ibn Umm Maktum, or Sibac ibn Urfata, in charge of Medina. When he reached one of their waters called al-Kudr, he stayed there three nights, then went back to Medina without meeting any resistance. Ali ibn Abi Talib carried his banner, and the banner was white. This appears to be the same expedition called the expedition of Qarqarat al-Kudr. Its cause was word that a group of Banu Sulaym and Ghatafan had gathered at Qarqarat al-Kudr, apparently intending to attack Medina. So he marched toward them with two hundred of his companions, and he took five hundred camels as spoils, gave one fifth as the khums, and divided the rest among his companions."

- al-Amili, al-Sahih min Sira al-Nabi al-A'zam, vol.6, p.319

Commentary

The Sources for al-Kudr

Source spine: al-Baladhuri, Ansab al-Ashraf, vol.1, p.364

Other carriers: al-Mas'udi, al-Tanbih wa-l-Ishraf, p.223

Shia lines: al-Askari, Ma'alim al-Madarisayn, vol.1, p.207. al-Amili, al-Sahih min Sira al-Nabi al-A'zam, vol.6, p.319

Dating note: al-Amili carries a line that puts the march seven nights after Badr, and flags his own reading that it is the same expedition as Qarqarat al-Kudr. The two datings sit months apart, late Ramadan against mid-Muharram, and the sources give no bridge. The series follows the three carriers that date it to Muharram, and holds al-Amili's identification as his reading

The carriers agree on the frame. A war gathering of Sulaym and Ghatafan, a march of two hundred, a scattering enemy, herds captured, no fighting. The deputy over Medina was Ibn Umm Maktum, with one line giving Sibac ibn Urfata.

What the March Found

Cause: a gathering of Sulaym and Ghatafan at Qarqarat al-Kudr, apparently aimed at Medina

Force: two hundred men. Banner, Ali ﵇, white

Stay: three nights at the water of al-Kudr

Spoils: five hundred camels, with their herders, and sheep by one line

Contact: none, the gathering scattered

The march worked as deterrence should. The gathering saw the state's army coming and dissolved into the plateau. The herders appear in al-Baladhuri's line and drop from the record, and their fate is not stated. The spoils paid the army, one fifth to the treasury as khums, the tax on spoils, and the rest divided among the men. No one was killed, and no battle happened.

Was the Expedition of al-Kudr Justified

The march was a preemptive answer to a war gathering. The sources state the gathering plainly, and one of them, al-Amili, reads Medina as its target, held openly as a reading. Sulaym and Ghatafan had collected at a water eight barid from Medina, on the Iraq road side of the plateau. A war gathering eight days' stages from the city left Medina little room to wait.

The conduct matched the threat. The army marched to the water, the enemy scattered, and no blood was shed. The herds left behind were taken under the spoils rule, khums first, then shares, and the stay of three nights showed the plateau that the army could hold the ground.

The march could have been prevented. The prevention was the tribes' own to make, by not gathering for war a week's march from Medina. The march scattered the gathering it answered, and no talks appear in the record.
