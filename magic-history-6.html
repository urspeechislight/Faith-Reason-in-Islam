The Imams against the Craft

The craft met the House of the Prophet ﷺ in every generation, at a salt-flat opened into gardens and fire, at a dry palm made green, at riders sealed until the Qa'im, and in the audience hall of a caliph whose staged sorcerers were devoured by their own illusions at one word of al-Sadiq ﷺ. From Madinat al-Ma'ajiz.

The Prophet's ﷺ era ended with the word sorcerer still in the air, and the House inherited both halves of the legacy, the craft that kept working and the accusation that kept answering every sign. This part follows the confrontations the corpus preserves. In each of them the Imams ﵈ meet sorcery with the instrument that is not sorcery, and in the greatest of them a caliph's staged contest is turned inside out by the Imam standing in the middle of it.

A Sign Like the Witnesses Bore

Companions of Ali ﷺ came asking for what the witnesses of Moses and Jesus had seen, and the salt-flat answered them.

وصار إليه نفر من أصحابه فقالوا (5) له : إن وصي موسى - ﵇ - كان يريهم الدلائل والعلامات والبراهين والمعجزات ، وكان وصي عيسى - ﵇ - يريهم كذلك ، فلو أريتنا شيئا تطمئن به (6) قلوبنا . فقال : إنكم لا تحتملون علم العالم ، ولا تقوون على براهينه وآياته ، فألحوا عليه ، فخرج نحو أبيات الهجريين حتى أشرف بهم على السبخة ، فدعا خفيا ، ثم قال : اكشفي غطاءك ، فإذا بجنات وأنهار في جانب ، وإذا بسعير ونيران من جانب . فقال جماعة : سحر سحر ، وثبت آخرون على التصديق

"A group of his followers came to him and said, "Moses’ successor showed people proofs, signs, evidence, and miracles. Jesus’ successor did the same. Show us something that will settle our hearts." He replied, "You cannot bear the knowledge of the learned one or endure his proofs and signs." They kept insisting. He went toward the homes of the Hajr people until he brought them to a salt flat. He made a quiet call, then said, "Uncover yourself." Gardens and rivers appeared on one side, while blazing fire and flames appeared on the other. Some people said, "Sorcery, sorcery." Others remained firm in their belief."

- Ali via al-Thumali from Rumayla, Madinat al-Ma'ajiz, vol.3, p.204

The companions split at the sight of the gardens and the fire. Some cried the old word, and some stood firm, at a sign that came from the same hand that had been called a sorcerer's hand in Mecca. ▼ commentary

The Word That Turns a Head

The craft of Pharaoh's court was answered once by a staff and once by a word. Ali ﵇ used the word on a quarrelsome Khariji.

فعلا صوت الرجل على المرأة فقال له علي ﵇ : إخسأ - وكان خارجيا - فإذا رأسه رأس كلب ، فقال رجل : يا أمير المؤمنين صحت بهذا الخارجي فصار رأسه رأس كلب فما يمنعك عن معاوية ؟ فقال : ويحك لو أشاء أن آتي بمعاوية إلى ها هنا على سريره لدعوت الله حتى فعل . ولكنا لله خزان . لا على ذهب ، ولا فضة ، ولا إنكار على أسرار تدبير الله . أما تقرأ * (بل عباد مكرمون * لا يسبقونه بالقول وهم بأمره يعملون

"A man raised his voice at a woman. Ali told him, "Be gone!" The man was a Kharijite, and his head became the head of a dog. A man asked, "Commander of the Faithful, you shouted at this Kharijite and his head became a dog's head. What prevents you from doing the same to Muawiyah?" Ali replied, "Woe to you. If I wished, I could bring Muawiyah here on his bed by calling upon Allah, and He would do it. Yet we are Allah's treasurers. We do not control gold or silver. We do not object to the hidden workings of Allah's plan. Have you not read: 'They are honored servants. They do not speak before Him, and they act by His command.'""

- Ali , al-Khara'ij wa-l-Jara'ih, vol.1, p.176

The Table under the Dry Tree

Al-Khara'ij also carries the table-spread of the House, a sign given like Jesus' table, and the crowd's answer to it.

إني أريكم اليوم آية تكون فيكم كمثل المائدة في بني إسرائيل إذ يقول الله * (إني منزلها عليكم فمن يكفر بعد منكم فإني أعذبه عذابا لا أعذبه أحدا من العالمين) * (1) . ثم قال : انظروا إلى الشجرة - وكانت يابسة - وإذا هي قد جرى الماء في عودها ثم اخضرت وأورقت وعقدت (2) وتدلى حملها على رؤوسنا ، ثم التفت إلينا فقال للقوم الذين هم محبوه : مدوا أيديكم وتناولوا وكلوا . فقلنا : " بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم " وتناولنا وأكلنا رمانا لم نأكل قط شيئا أعذب منه وأطيب . ثم قال للنفر الذين هم مبغضوه : مدوا أيديكم وتناولوا ، فمدوا أيديهم فارتفعت وكلما مد رجل منهم يده إلى رمانة ارتفعت ، فلم يتناولوا شيئا ، فقالوا : يا أمير المؤمنين ما بال إخواننا مدوا أيديهم وتناولوا ، وأكلوا ، ومددنا أيدينا فلم ننل ؟ فقال ﵇ : وكذلك الجنة لا ينالها إلا أولياؤنا ومحبونا ، ولا يبعد منها إلا أعداؤنا ومبغوضنا . فلما خرجوا قالوا : هذا من سحر علي بن أبي طالب قليل ! قال سلمان : ماذا تقولون " أفسحر هذا أم أنتم لا تبصرون

"Today I will show you a sign among you like the table sent down to the Children of Israel, when Allah said, "I will send it down to you. Whoever disbelieves afterward, I will punish him with a punishment I have never given anyone in the worlds." Then he said, "Look at the tree." It was dry. Water began to run through its trunk. It turned green, grew leaves, bore fruit, and its fruit hung over our heads. He turned to those who loved him and said, "Reach out your hands. Take some and eat." We said, "In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate," then took and ate pomegranates. We had never eaten anything sweeter or more delicious. Then he told those who hated him, "Reach out your hands and take some." They reached out, and the fruit rose upward. Whenever one of them reached for a pomegranate, it rose higher. Salman answered the crowd's word with the Qur'an's own question about signs called sorcery.

The Definition Given at a Dry Palm

Al-Husayn ﷺ gave the craft's opposite its cleanest definition, at a palm tree, to a camel-driver.

فرفع يده فدعا بكلام لم أفهمه فاخضرت النخلة وصارت إلى حالها (واو رقت) (7) وحملت رطبا ، فقال الجمال الذي اكترى منه : [هذا] (8) سحر ، والله ! فقال الحسين : ويلك ليس بسحر ولكنها (9) دعوة ابن نبي مستجابة

"He raised his hand and prayed with words I could not grasp. The palm tree turned green, became as it had been, its leaves grew soft, and it bore fresh dates. The camel driver from whom he had hired the camel said, "This is sorcery, by Allah." Husayn replied, "Woe to you. This is a prayer from a prophet’s son, and Allah answers it.""

- al-Husayn , Madinat al-Ma'ajiz, vol.3, p.460

Al-Hasan ﵇ gave the same definition at another dry palm, and al-Rawandi's own collection carries it.

فقال الزبيري - وقد رفع رأسه - : لو كان في هذه النخلة رطب لأكلنا منه . فقال له الحسن ﵇ : وإنك لتشتهي الرطب ؟ قال : نعم . فرفع الحسن ﵇ [رأسه و] يده إلى السماء فدعا بكلام ، فاخضرت النخلة ، وأورقت ، وحملت رطبا . فقال الجمال - الذي اكتروا منه - : سحر والله . فقال الحسن ﵇ : ويلك إن هذا ليس بسحر ، ولكنها دعوة ابن نبي مجابة

"Al-Zubayri raised his head and said, "If this palm tree had fresh dates, we would eat some." Hasan asked, "Do you desire fresh dates?" He said, "Yes." Hasan raised his head and hand toward the sky and prayed with certain words. The palm tree turned green, grew leaves, and bore fresh dates. The camel owner from whom they had rented said, "By Allah, this is magic." Hasan replied, "Woe to you. This is not magic. It is an answered prayer from the son of a prophet.""

- al-Hasan , al-Khara'ij wa-l-Jara'ih, vol.2, p.87

The philosophers of a later age pressed the same boundary from the other side, and al-Rawandi records their doubt.

لأنه يمكن أن يظن في المعجز أنه سحر ، وأنه حيلة نحو انشقاق القمر . فأما إذا علم مطابقة ما أتى به لمصالحهم الدنياوية فهو طريق لا يدخله الشبهة ، ومن قال بهذا قال في العلم بصدقه للمعجز فهو طريق العوام والمتكلمين . وأما العلم بمطابقة شرعه للمصالح الدنياوية فهو طريقة المحققين . وقد حكي عنهم أنهم قالوا : إن صدق المدعي لصنعة من الصنائع إنما يظهر إذا أتى بتلك الصنعة التي ادعى العلم بها . ومثله على الناقل بمن ادعى حفظ القرآن [ثم قرأ ، وادعى آخر حفظ القرآن فإذا قيل له : ما دليلك على أنك تحفظ القرآن ؟ قال : دليلي أني اقلب العصا حية وأشق القمر نصفين . ثم فعلهما ، ومن ادعى حفظ القرآن] (1) . فإذا قيل له : ما دليلك على حفظك له ؟ قرأه كله ، فان علمنا بحفظ هذا القاري يكون أقوى من علمنا بحفظ الثاني للقرآن ، لأنه يشتبه الحال في معجزاته ، فيظن أنه من باب السحر أو أنه طلسم

"A person may think a miracle is magic or a trick, as with the splitting of the moon. Yet when he knows that what the prophet brought agrees with their worldly interests, doubt cannot enter this path. Those who hold this view say that knowing a miracle worker speaks the truth is the way of ordinary people and theologians. Knowing that his law agrees with worldly interests is the way of careful investigators. They reported that a person who claims skill in a craft proves his truth by performing the craft he claims to know. The same applies to someone who claims to have memorized the Quran. One person claims to have memorized the Quran, then recites it. Another person also claims to have memorized it. When people ask him, "What proof do you have that you memorized the Quran?" he says, "My proof is that I can turn a staff into a living snake and split the moon into two halves." Then he does both. When people ask the person who claims to have memorized the Quran, "What proof do you have that you memorized it?" he recites the whole Quran. Our knowledge that this reciter has memorized it is stronger than our knowledge that the other person has memorized the Quran. People may confuse his miracles with magic or think they are talismans."

- the philosophers, in al-Khara'ij wa-l-Jara'ih, vol.3, p.88

The Riders Sealed until the Qa'im

A rider split open on the road, and the two found in the split carry a date.

"He then struck what lay before him, and it split open to reveal two people upon a rock. From them rose a dust unlike any mirage. Each wore a chain around his neck, with a devil bound to him. They cried, "Muhammad, Muhammad," while the two devils answered, "You lie." He said, "Close over them until the appointed time, which cannot come early or late. That time is the rising of the awaited Qa'im." The man said, "Sorcery," then turned away, intending to report the opposite. His tongue became mute, and he could not speak."

- Thaqib al-Manaqib, carried in Madinat al-Ma'ajiz, vol.3, p.260

The Caliph and the Staged Sorcerers

The greatest confrontation came in the hall of al-Mansur, and the corpus preserves it whole. The caliph commanded the Imam's presence, and the stage was set.

""Send for Abu Abd Allah and bring him here at once." When he arrived, he entered and saw them, Mansur, and the preparations made against him. He grew angry and said, "Woe to you. Do you know me? Commentary

The Same Word, New Masters

Source: Madinat al-Ma'ajiz vol.3 p.204, p.216; vol.4 p.299

Claim: the signs of the Imams ﵈ met the same cry the prophets met, from their own communities and their opponents

Speaker: Ali ﵇; the narration chains; Abdullah ibn Umar

Nothing about the word improved when it changed hands. Ali's ﷺ salt-flat opened into gardens and rivers on one side and fire on the other, a picture of the two destinations, and part of the crowd answered it exactly as Quraysh had answered the moon. Ibn Umar's word is the more useful because he had just seen a jinn confess the wilaya, and his verdict on the House was still the inherited-sorcery slur of his father's camp, while the narrator of the scene draws the opposite conclusion and records it. The table-spread under the dry tree drew the same word from its crowd, this is of the sorcery of Ali, and Salman answered them with the Qur'an's own question about signs called sorcery. The corpus keeps both sides of the era in one book, the sign and the slur.

The Imam's Own Definition

Source: Madinat al-Ma'ajiz vol.3 p.460

Claim: the Imam named the difference himself, sorcery against the answered prayer of a prophet's son

Speaker: al-Husayn ﵇

The dry palm made green is a small sign told briefly, and it carries the series' whole boundary. The camel-driver cries sorcery, and al-Husayn ﷺ corrects him in one sentence, that this is not sorcery but the answered prayer of the son of a prophet. The definition is the school's in miniature. The craft works illusion and harm for pay, and the miracle is a word asked of God and granted. Part 2's sorcerers knew the difference from inside, here it is named from the Imam's side, and the philosophers' doubt, recorded in the same miracle-book, shows the boundary still argued centuries later.

Sealed until the Rising

Source: Thaqib al-Manaqib in Madinat al-Ma'ajiz vol.3 p.260

Claim: two bound riders with attached devils were sealed into the rock until the rising of the awaited Qa'im

Speaker: the narration chain

This exhibit holds the part's bridge to the end of the series. The bound figures cry O Muhammad while the devils bound with them deny, and the closing command names the term of their sealing, the appointed time that neither comes early nor late, the rising of the Qa'im. The watcher who answers sorcery loses his speech on the spot, one accusation punished in the flesh. The riders stay under the rock until the appointed time the closing command names, the rising of the Qa'im, and the last part of this series stands at that rising.

The Counter-Contest

The Serpent Before the Prayer

Source: Madinat al-Ma'ajiz vol.4 p.410-411

Claim: Iblis himself tested Ali ibn al-Husayn \ufd47 with a ten-headed serpent and failed to break his prayer

Speaker: al-Sadiq \ufd47 via Musa ibn Ja'far \ufd47

Iblis told God he had watched every worshipper from Adam to this era and found none like Ali ibn al-Husayn \ufd47. God forbade the test. Iblis went anyway, became a serpent with ten heads and fire in its eyes, bit the Imam's feet during prayer, and the Imam did not break his prayer. A star struck Iblis from heaven, and the one who had tested every prophet since Adam stood before a worshipper he could not move, asking for intercession. The craft's originator met the same defeat the craft itself had met in every generation.

Source: Madinat al-Ma'ajiz vol.5 p.252

Claim: al-Sadiq ﷺ defeated staged sorcery by command, named himself the heir of Moses' victory, and refused to restore the sorcerers

Speaker: al-Sadiq ﷺ before al-Mansur

Al-Mansur built the contest Pharaoh had built, a throne, an audience, and professionals. The Imam stood in Moses' place and said so, that he is God's proof who annulled the sorcery of their forefathers in the days of Moses, and then he did to the staged beasts what the craft could not do to him, he commanded them by God's permission and they devoured their own makers. The caliph's faint and his plea are recorded, and so is the Imam's refusal to undo the work, with the reason given in the series' own grammar, that Moses' staff did not return Pharaoh's sorcerers. The contest of Egypt is cited by its winner, centuries later, in an Abbasid palace. The craft's history keeps returning to its first defeat.

What the Confrontations Left

The confrontations left no school of counter-sorcery. The Imams ﵈ broke illusions by command, corrected the word sorcery with definitions, and left the community the rulings and the recited shelters already carried in this series.
