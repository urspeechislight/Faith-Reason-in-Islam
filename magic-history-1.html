How Sorcery Began

The Shia narrations trace sorcery to the satans who eavesdropped on heaven and fed mixed lies to the soothsayers, until God sent two angels with the antidote and shut the heavens at the Prophet's ﷺ mission. From al-Qummi, al-Ayyashi, al-Mashhadi, al-Bahrani, al-Majlisi, and al-Hurr al-Amili.

The satans used to climb toward heaven and steal whatever words they could hear. They mixed lies into the stolen words and fed them to the soothsayers of the earth, who wrote them down and taught them to the people. After Noah ﵇ the craft had spread so far that sorcerers and deceivers were everywhere, and God answered it from heaven itself. That answer stands at Babylon, and it is the next part of this history.

A Roof Kept from the Satans

The Qur'an calls the sky a kept roof, and al-Qummi records from his father what the keeping guards against.

وقوله : (وجعلنا السماء سقفا محفوظا) يعني من الشياطين اي لا يسترقون السمع

"His words, "We made the sky a protected roof," mean that it is protected from devils, so they cannot listen secretly."

- Ali ibn Ibrahim al-Qummi, Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.2, p.70

So heaven's own speech needed a guard. The satans wanted that speech because a true word stolen and bent carries further than a plain lie. ▼ commentary

The Stolen Words and the Soothsayers

Al-Mashhadi records how the theft worked and where the stolen words went.

كانوا يسترقون السّمع ، ويضمّون إلى ما سمعوا أكاذيب ، ويلقونها إلى الكهنة ، وهم يدوّنونها ، ويعلَّمون النّاس . وفشى ذلك في عهد سليمان ، حتّى قيل : إنّ الجنّ يعلم الغيب . وإن ملك سليمان تمّ بهذا العلم

"They listened secretly, added lies to what they heard, and passed it to the soothsayers. The soothsayers wrote it down and taught it to the people. This spread during Solomon's time, until people said that the jinn knew the unseen. They said Solomon's kingdom rested on this knowledge."

- al-Mashhadi, Tafsir Kanz al-Daqa'iq, vol.2, p.99

The soothsayers became the craft's teachers, and the trade reached ordinary people. Al-Ayyashi records a question al-Baqir ﵇ was asked about the Qur'an's words on men who sought refuge with the jinn, and the practice behind those words.

سألت أبا جعفر ﵇ عن قول الله وانه كان رجال من الانس يعوذون برجال من الجن فزادوهم رهقا قال : كان الرجل ينطلق إلى الكاهن الذي يوحى إليه الشيطان فيقول : قل للشيطان فلان قد عاذبك

"I asked Abu Jafar about Allah’s words: "Men from humankind sought refuge with men from the jinn, and they increased them in burden." He answered: "A man would go to a fortune-teller inspired by a devil and say: Tell the devil that so-and-so has sought refuge with you.""

- al-Baqir , Tafsir al-Ayyashi, carried in Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.4

The refuge-seeker went to the soothsayer because the soothsayer had a line to the listening satans. And the satans themselves kept walking among people. Abd al-Salam asked al-Sadiq ﵇ how a man can guard himself when the danger is unseen, and received the answer that the listener comes in a body.

عن بكر بن محمد الأزدي عن عمه عبد السلام عن أبي عبد الله ﵇ قال : قال يا عبد السلام : احذر الناس ونفسك ، فقلت : بأبي أنت وأمي اما الناس فقد أقدر على أن أحذرهم ، فاما نفسي فكيف ؟ قال : إن الخبيث المسترق السمع يجيئك فيسترق ثم يخرج في صورة آدمي ، فقال عبد السلام : فقلت : بأبي وأمي هذا ما لا حيلة له قال : هو ذاك

"Bakr ibn Muhammad al-Azdi reported from his uncle Abd al-Salam that Abu Abd Allah said: "Abd al-Salam, guard yourself and beware of people." Abd al-Salam replied, "May my father and mother be your ransom. I can beware of people, but how can I guard myself?" Abu Abd Allah answered, "The wicked one who listens secretly comes to you, listens, then appears in human form." Abd al-Salam said, "May my father and mother be your ransom. There is no way to prevent this." Abu Abd Allah replied, "That is how it is.""

- al-Sadiq , Tafsir al-Ayyashi, carried in Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.4

After the Idols, Noah and the Flood of Sorcery## Iblis Makes the First Idols

Before the Flood, Iblis built a different trap. He did not steal words from heaven; he made images that would stand in for the dead.

في قول الله ﷿ : وقالوا لا تذرن آلهتكم ولا تذرن ود أولا سواعا ولا يغوث ويعوق ونسرا ، قال : كانوا يعبدون الله ﷿ فماتوا فضج قومهم وشق ذلك عليهم ، فجاءهم إبليس لعنه الله فقال لهم : أتخذ لكم أصناما على صورهم فتنظرون إليهم وتأنسون بهم وتعبدون الله ، فأعد لهم أصناما على مثالهم فكانوا يعبدون الله ﷿ ، وينظرون إلى تلك الأصنام ، فلما جاءهم الشتاء والأمطار أدخلوا الأصنام البيوت فلم يزالوا يعبدون الله ﷿ حتى هلك ذلك القرن ونشأ أولادهم ، فقالوا : إن آباءنا كانوا يعبدون هؤلاء ، فعبدوهم من دون الله

"Allah says, "Do not leave your gods. Do not leave Wadd, Suwa, Yaghuth, Yauq, or Nasr." The narrator said, They worshipped Allah. Then they died, and their people cried in grief. Their deaths caused them great pain. Iblis came to them and said, "Shall I make idols in their likeness? You can look at them, find comfort in them, and worship Allah." He made idols resembling them. The people worshipped Allah and looked at those idols. When winter and rain came, they brought the idols inside their homes. They continued worshipping Allah until that generation died. Their children grew up and said, "Our fathers used to worship these." So they worshipped them apart from Allah."

- al-Sadiq \ufd47, Ilal al-Shara'i, carried in Bihar al-Anwar, vol.3, p.252

The eavesdropping fed a trade, and the trade grew into a flood. Al-Burhan carries the tafsir of Imam al-Askari ﵈ on the verse of the two angels, and it opens with that flood and with God's answer to it.

وكان بعد نوح (﵇) قد كثر السحرة والمموهون ، فبعث الله تعالى ملكين إلى نبي ذلك الزمان بذكر ما يسحر به السحرة ، وذكر ما يبطل به سحرهم ، ويرد به كيدهم ، فتلقاه النبي عن الملكين ، وأداه إلى عباد الله بأمر الله ، وأمرهم أن يقفوا به على السحر وأن يبطلوه ، ونهاهم أن يسحروا به الناس ، وهذا كما يدل على السم ما هو ، وعلى ما يدفع به غائلة (3) السم ، ثم يقال لمتعلم ذلك : هذا السم فمن رأيته سم فادفع غائلته بكذا ، وإياك أن تقتل بالسم أحدا

"After Noah, magicians and deceivers became numerous. Allah sent two angels to the prophet of that time. They brought knowledge of the spells used by magicians, and knowledge of what could break those spells and turn back their plots. The prophet received this from the two angels and delivered it to Allah's servants by Allah's command. He commanded them to learn about magic and break it. He forbade them from using it to harm people. This is like showing someone what poison is and what removes its harm. Then the learner is told: "This is poison. When you see someone poisoned, remove its harm with this treatment. Do not kill anyone with poison.""

- al-Sadiq , Tafsir al-Imam al-Askari, carried in al-Burhan, vol.1, p.353

The antidote was sent before the verdicts came. What the two angels taught at Babylon, and what the school says about them, is the next part of this series.

The Rulings That Sealed the Trade

The school closed the trade itself with rulings. Wasa'il al-Shia carries the prohibitions in two chapters.

في حديث المناهي ان رسول الله ﵌ نهى عن اتيان العراف ، وقال : من أتاه وصدقه فقد برئ مما أنزل الله ﷿ على محمد ﵌ . أقول : فسر بعض أهل اللغة العراف بالكاهن وبعضهم بالمنجم . 2 - وفي (الخصال) عن أبيه ، عن سعد بن عبد الله ، عن يعقوب بن يزيد ، عن محمد ابن أبي عمير ، عن علي بن أبي حمزة ، عن أبي بصير ، عن أبي عبد الله ﵇ قال : من تكهن أو تكهن له فقد برئ من دين محمد ﵌

"In the Hadith of Prohibitions, the Messenger of Allah forbade people from visiting a fortune-teller. He said, "Whoever visits one and believes him has rejected what Allah sent down to Muhammad." Some linguists defined a fortune-teller as a soothsayer. Others defined one as an astrologer. In al-Khisal, Abu Abd Allah said: "Whoever practices soothsaying, or has it practiced for him, has renounced the religion of Muhammad.""

- the Prophet and al-Sadiq , Wasa'il al-Shia (al-Islamiyya), vol.12, p.108

One power from the old world survived the closing of heaven's speech, and al-Baqir ﵇ names it with precision.

قال أبو جعفر الباقر ﵇ : إن السحرة لم يسلطوا على شئ إلا العين

"Abu Jafar al-Baqir said: "Magicians have power over nothing except the evil eye.""

- al-Baqir , Tibb al-A'imma, carried in Bihar al-Anwar, vol.60, p.26

Commentary

The Stolen Words

Source: Tafsir Kanz al-Daqa'iq, vol.2, p.99; Tafsir al-Qummi, vol.2, p.70; Tafsir al-Ayyashi in Nur al-Thaqalayn, vol.3, p.4

Claim: sorcery began as words stolen from heaven, mixed with lies, and taught by the soothsayers

Speaker: al-Mashhadi; al-Qummi; al-Baqir ﵇

The school does not begin the history of magic with a rebel angel or a forgotten empire. It begins with theft and counterfeiting, a true word stolen and diluted with lies until the mixture could be sold. This matters for everything that follows, because it makes the sorcerer's capital always second-hand. The soothsayer never speaks first. He repeats what a satan claims to have overheard, and the Qur'an guarded the sky itself against the claim.

The Eye, the One Power Left

The First Idols

Source: Bihar al-Anwar vol.3 p.252-254

Claim: Iblis created the first idols as images of righteous dead men, and each generation worshipped the image itself

Speaker: al-Sadiq \ufd47; al-Baqir \ufd47

The first sorcery needed no spell. Iblis offered the grieving a picture of their dead, and the picture became the god. Five idols across five generations, each one further from the worship the first generation still practiced. The craft was already working before the eavesdropping began, and the two traps together, the stolen word and the standing image, are the twin roots of everything this history follows.

Source: Tibb al-A'imma via al-Baqir ﵇, in Bihar al-Anwar, vol.60, p.26

Claim: sorcerers were granted power over nothing except the envied eye

Speaker: al-Baqir ﵇

The old power that survived is the eye, and it survives because it never needed the eavesdropping. It works through human envy itself, from person to person. The listener who comes in a body, from the same part, stands outside any fence of sight, and the closing of the heavens, which this history dates at the Prophet's ﷺ mission, was the answer to him. This ruling shapes the protections the Imams ﵈ practiced, most of which shelter the sufferer from the eye rather than duel with a sorcerer. Those protections fill a later part of this series, and the story now runs on to the first great contest between the stolen craft and a prophet's staff.

Where the Craft Settled

The craft settled at Babylon first, where God's answer to it had stood from the ages after Noah ﵇, and from Babylon it passed to Egypt, to the court of a king who claimed divinity and kept sorcerers the way kings keep soldiers. Pharaoh had learned the trade himself, and when God sent Moses ﵇ to him, the two crafts met. That meeting is Part 2 of this series.
