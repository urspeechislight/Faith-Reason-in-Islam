The Expedition of Buwat

One month after Waddan, the Prophet ﷺ marched two hundred Emigrants to intercept a rich Quraysh caravan under Umayya ibn Khalaf. The march again ended without a blow. From Ibn Ishaq, carried by Ibn Sayyid al-Nas and al-Suhayli, with the deputies list of al-Tabari. Part 2 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Second March

The Prophet ﷺ had barely returned from Waddan when word came of a far richer prize. A caravan of Quraysh was on its way back from Syria, carrying Umayya ibn Khalaf al-Jumahi, one hundred men of Quraysh, and two thousand five hundred camels. He marched out in Rabi' al-Awwal of the second year, thirteen months after the Hijra, at the head of two hundred men, all of them Emigrants, the Meccan Muslims who had followed him to Medina.▼ commentary

Ibn Ishaq's account of the march survives in several later works.

قال ابن إسحاق : ثم غزا رسول الله ﵌ في شهر ربيع الأول يريد قريشا حتى بلغ بواط من ناحية رضوى ثم رجع إلى المدينة ولم يلق كيدا واستعمل على المدينة السائب بن مظعون فيما ذكر ابن هشام ، وحمل اللواء وكان أبيض سعد بن معاذ فيما ذكر ابن سعد ، وقال وخرج في مائتين من أصحابه يعرض لعير قريش فيها أمية بن خلف الجمحي ومائة رجل من قريش وألفان وخمسمائة بعير .

"Ibn Ishaq said. Then the Messenger of Allah went out on campaign in Rabi' al-Awwal with Quraysh in view, until he reached Buwat from the direction of Radwa. Then he returned to Medina and met no fighting. He had put al-Sa'ib ibn Maz'un in charge of Medina, as Ibn Hisham mentions. Sa'd ibn Mu'adh carried the banner, and it was white, as Ibn Sa'd mentions. He went out with two hundred of his companions to intercept a caravan of Quraysh carrying Umayya ibn Khalaf al-Jumahi, one hundred men of Quraysh, and two thousand five hundred camels."

- Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.1, p.299, carrying Ibn Ishaq with Ibn Hisham's and Ibn Sa'd's notes

Al-Suhayli's commentary on the Sira gives the same line with the deputy named in full, and the stay in Medina after the return.

قال ابن إسحاق ، ثم غزا رسول اللّه صلى اللّه عليه وسلم في شهر ربيع لأوّل يريد قريشا .

قال ابن هشام : واستعمل على المدينة السائب بن عثمان بن مظعون .

قال ابن إسحاق : حتى بلغ بواط ، من ناحية رضوى ، ثم رجع إلى المدينة ولم يلق كيدا ، فلبث بها بقيّة شهر ربيع الآخر ، وبعض جمادى الأولى .

"Ibn Ishaq said. Then the Messenger of Allah went out on campaign in Rabi' al-Awwal with Quraysh in view. Ibn Hisham said. He put al-Sa'ib ibn Uthman ibn Maz'un in charge of Medina. Ibn Ishaq said. He marched until he reached Buwat, from the direction of Radwa, and then returned to Medina and met no fighting. He stayed in Medina for the rest of Rabi' al-Akhir and part of Jumada al-Ula."

- al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unaf, vol.5, p.58

One modern digest adds the distance and the command of the march.

ثم كانت غزوة بواط في شهر ربيع الأول على رأس ثلاثة عشر شهرا من مهاجره ، وحمل لواءه سعد بن أبي وقاص ، وكان أبيض ، واستخلف على المدينة سعد بن معاذ ، ثم خرج في مائتين من أصحابه يعترض عيرا لقريش فيها أمية بن خلف الجمحي ، ومائة رجل من قريش ، والفان وخمسمائة بعير ، فبلغ ( بواطا ) (3) . وبين المدينة وبواط نحو أربعة برد فلم يلق صلى اللّه عليه وسلم كيدا فرجع إلى المدينة (4) .

"Then came the expedition of Buwat in Rabi' al-Awwal, thirteen months after his migration. Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas carried his banner, and it was white. He put Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in charge of Medina. Then he went out with two hundred of his companions to intercept a caravan of Quraysh carrying Umayya ibn Khalaf al-Jumahi, one hundred men of Quraysh, and two thousand five hundred camels, and he reached Buwat. Between Medina and Buwat is about four barid. The Messenger of Allah met no fighting there, so he returned to Medina."

- Jamili, Ghazawat al-Nabi, p.23

The banner crossed into the territory of Juhayna, an Arab tribe of the coast road, and reached Buwat, a mountain near Radwa, about four barid from Medina. A barid is a day's stage on the post road.

No caravan crossed the path. No arrow flew. The march turned back empty.▼ commentary

ولم يمض على رجوعه غير قليل حتى بلغه أن عيرا لقريش ايبة من الشام ، فيها أمية بن خلف ومائة من قريش ، وألفان وخمسمائة بعير ، فسار إليها في مائتين من المهاجرين ، وذلك في ربيع الأول ، وكان يحمل لواءه سعد بن أبي وقاص ، فسار حتى بلغ بواط (2) فوجد العير قد فاتته فرجع ولم يلق كيدا (3) ، وذلك كله لما كان يأخذه المشركون من الحذر على أنفسهم والاجتهاد في تعمية أخبارهم عن أهل المدينة .

"Only a short time passed after his return when word reached him that a caravan of Quraysh was coming back from Syria. In it were Umayya ibn Khalaf and one hundred men of Quraysh, with two thousand five hundred camels. He marched toward it with two hundred of the Emigrants, and that was in Rabi' al-Awwal. Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas carried the banner. He marched until he reached Buwat. He found the caravan had gone ahead of him, so he returned and met no fighting. All of that was because the idolaters guarded themselves and worked hard to keep their movements hidden from the people of Medina."

- al-Khudari, Nur al-Yaqin, p.93

Al-Tahtawi fixes the month and records the two names for the deputy.

وأما غزوة بواط (2) ( بضم المواحدة وفتحها وتخفيف الواو اخرها طاء مهملة ) فالصحيح أيضا أنها كانت في شهر ربيع الأوّل ، وقيل في ربيع الآخر من السنة الثانية . وبواط جبل بالينبع . وسبب هذه الغزوة أن النبي صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم بلغه أن عير قريش نحو ألفين وخمسمائة بعير ، ومائة رجل من قريش ، معهم أمية بن خلف ، ذاهبة إلى مكة ، فخرج صلّى اللّه عليه وسلّم لاعتراضها في مائتين من أصحابه ، وحمل

اللواء - وهو العلم الّذي يحمل في الحرب ، يعرف به موضع أمير الجيش ، وكان أبيض - سعد بن أبي وقاص واستعمل علي المدينة السائب بن عثمان بن مظعون ، وقيل سعد بن معاذ ، فانتهى إلى بواط فلم يلق كيدا أي حربا ، فرجع إلى المدينة بدون حرب

"As for the expedition of Buwat, the sound view is also that it fell in Rabi' al-Awwal, and some said in Rabi' al-Akhir of the second year. Buwat is a mountain at Yanbu'. The cause of this expedition was that the Prophet learned of a caravan of Quraysh, about two thousand five hundred camels and one hundred men of Quraysh, with Umayya ibn Khalaf among them, on its way to Mecca. So he went out to intercept it with two hundred of his companions. Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas carried the banner. The banner is the flag carried in war, by which the position of the army commander is known, and it was white. He put al-Sa'ib ibn Uthman ibn Maz'un in charge of Medina, and some said Sa'd ibn Mu'adh. He reached Buwat and met no fighting, that is, no war, and went back to Medina without a battle."

- al-Tahtawi, Nihayat al-Ijaz, pp.286-287, on the month, the banner, and the two deputies

The Shia chronicler Ibn Jarir al-Tabari lists the deputies of these early marches, and his list gives Buwat's deputy as Sa'd ibn Mu'adh.▼ commentary

واستخلف في غزوة ودان سعد بن عبادة (1) واستخلف في غزوة بواط سعد بن معاذ (2) وفي طلب كرزين بن جابر الفهري ، زيد بن حارثة (3) وفي غزوة العشيرة أبا سلمة ابن عبد الأسد المخزومي (4) وفي غزوة قينقاع أبا لبابة (5) .

"He put Sa'd ibn Ubada in charge of Medina for the expedition of Waddan. He put Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in charge for the expedition of Buwat. For the pursuit of Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri, Zayd ibn Haritha. For the expedition of al-Ushayra, Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad al-Makhzumi. For the expedition against Qaynuqa, Abu Lubaba."

- Ibn Jarir al-Tabari, al-Mustarshad, p.130, on the deputies of the early marches

Commentary

The Sources for Buwat

Source: Ibn Ishaq, carried by Ibn Sayyid al-Nas, 'Uyun al-Athar, vol.1, p.299

Same line, other carriers: al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unaf, vol.5, p.58. Jamili, Ghazawat al-Nabi, p.23. al-Khudari, Nur al-Yaqin, p.93. al-Tahtawi, Nihayat al-Ijaz, pp.286-287

Shia line: Ibn Jarir al-Tabari, al-Mustarshad, p.130

Dating: Ibn Sayyid al-Nas died 734 AH. al-Suhayli died 581 AH

As with Waddan, the story has one root, Ibn Ishaq, with Ibn Hisham's notes folded in. The later carriers add the size of the force, the distance, and the color of the banner. The Shia side contributes the list of deputies.

All lines agree on the core facts. The march happened, it aimed at Umayya's caravan, and it ended without a battle. The month carries one recorded variant, Rabi' al-Awwal against Rabi' al-Akhir, and the variant stays where al-Tahtawi left it.

Why Nothing Happened at Buwat

Caravan: two thousand five hundred camels, one hundred men, Umayya ibn Khalaf

Force: two hundred Muslims, Emigrants only

Ground: Buwat, a mountain of Juhayna near Radwa, about four barid from Medina

Result: the caravan had passed, no engagement, return to Medina

Al-Khudari names the reason the early marches kept finding empty roads. The Meccans guarded themselves and worked to keep their movements hidden from Medina. A two thousand five hundred camel caravan moved slowly, and its owners knew the Muslims would come.

Their countermeasures, scouts and secrecy, worked twice in a row. The Muslims gained ground knowledge, and the Meccans gained the cost of vigilance on every journey. Neither side lost a man.

The Two Sa'ds

Banner, majority line: Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, per Jamili, Nur al-Yaqin, and Nihayat al-Ijaz

Banner, minority line: Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, in one line that Ibn Sayyid al-Nas attributes to Ibn Sa'd

Deputy over Medina, Ibn Hisham: al-Sa'ib ibn Uthman ibn Maz'un

Deputy over Medina, Jamili and al-Tabari's al-Mustarshad: Sa'd ibn Mu'adh

Two men named Sa'd divide the honors of this march in the sources, and the division cuts across the Sunni and Shia lines. The banner belongs to Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas in most carriers, with one line giving it to Sa'd ibn Mu'adh. The deputy post belongs to al-Sa'ib ibn Maz'un in Ibn Hisham, and to Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in Jamili and in the deputies list of al-Mustarshad.

A likely reconciliation sits in view. A line that moved the deputy's name into the banner slot would produce exactly this pattern. The sources do not state that reconciliation, so it stays a reading, held lightly.

Was the March to Buwat Justified

The march repeats the pattern of Waddan one month later, and the same verdict follows. The form was offensive, a march against enemy trade. The aim was defensive, pressure on Quraysh, which had seized the homes and property of the Emigrants, the same wrong the Qur'an names in the verses of permission to fight, quoted in part 1. Umayya ibn Khalaf rode in the caravan, and his record in Mecca was hostile to the last degree.

وكان أمية يعذب بلالاً بمكة لأجل إسلامه عذابًا شديدًا

"Umayya used to torture Bilal in Mecca for his Islam, torturing him severely."

- al-Qastallani, Irshad al-Sari, vol.4, p.156, on Umayya ibn Khalaf

The caravan carried a man of the hard core of Meccan hostility, and the Muslims marched against the wealth of the enemy that had driven them out.

No fighting occurred, so there was no conduct in battle to judge. No companions fled, because there was no battle to flee. No one was killed.

The tactical outcome was a second bloodless interception attempt. The strategic outcome was real. Quraysh now moved its wealth under guard and in fear, and the new state had shown it could put two hundred men in the field within a month of its first march.

No confrontation happened, and the caravan's own scouts prevented contact. The Muslims raised no claim against any tribe on the way. Not marching was always possible, and it would have left the seizure unanswered. The only offer that would have ended the pressure was Mecca returning what it had seized, and no such offer existed.
