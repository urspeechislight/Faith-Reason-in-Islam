The Return Pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya

The Entry Under the Treaty

The treaty had a clause and the Prophet ﷺ kept it to the letter.▼ commentary In Dhu al-Qa'da of the seventh year, after the campaign of Khaybar, the Muslims came back to Mecca as pilgrims, the visit that the truce had promised. The garb of pilgrimage was on, and the stay ran three days, on the report of Ibn Abbas. The reports of the visit carry no drawn sword. The reports keep the scene of the entry, a companion reciting verses ahead of the Prophet ﷺ, Umar scandalized, and the Prophet's ﷺ answer.▼ commentary▼ commentary

The hadith of the entry, in the Shia pilgrimage collection, on the chain of Ja'far ibn Sulayman from Thabit from Anas.▼ commentary▼ commentary

أنّ النبيّ ﵌ دخل مكّة في عمرة القضاء (2) وعبد اللَّه بن رواحة يمشي بين يديه وهو يقول : خلّوا بني الكفار عن سبيله * اليوم نضربكم على تنزيله ضرباً يزيل الهام عن مقيله * ويذهل الخليل عن خليله فقال له عمر : يا بن رواحة بين يدي رسول اللَّه ﵌ وفي حرم اللَّه عزَّ وجلَّ تقول الشِّعر ؟ قال النبيّ ﵌ خلِّ عنه فلهو أسرع فيهم من نضح النبل .

"'The Prophet entered Mecca in the pilgrimage of al-Qada, and Abd Allah ibn Rawaha walked before him reciting, Clear the way, sons of the disbelievers, from his path. Today we strike you by his revelation, a striking that lifts the skull from its resting place and makes the dear friend forget his friend. Umar said, Ibn Rawaha, before the Messenger of Allah and in the sanctuary of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, you recite poetry? The Prophet said, Leave him alone, for it sinks into them faster than the raining of arrows.'"

- Hajj al-Anbiya wa-l-A'imma, p.71, carrying Abd al-Razzaq from Ja'far ibn Sulayman from Thabit from Anas

عمرة القضية ويروى أيضاً : عمرة القضاء ، ويقال لها : عمرة القصاص ، وهذا الاسم أولى بها لقوله تعالى : « الشَّهْرُ الحَرَامُ بالشَّهْرِ الحَرَامِ والحُرُمَاتُ قِصَاصٌ » البقرة وهذه الآية فيها نزلت ، فهذا الاسم أولى بها ، وسميت عمرة القضاء ،

"'The pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya. It is also carried as the pilgrimage of al-Qada, and it is called the pilgrimage of Retaliation, and this name suits it best, for the word of Allah, the sacred month for the sacred month, and the sacred things are retaliation, al-Baqara, came down about it, and so this name suits it best. And it was named the pilgrimage of al-Qada.'"

- al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unuf, vol.4, p.76

"'Abd al-Razzaq also related this hadith from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Anas, like it. And he related, in other than this hadith, that the Prophet entered Mecca in the pilgrimage of al-Qada with Ka'b ibn Malik walking before him, and this is sounder in the eyes of some of the hadith scholars, because Abd Allah ibn Rawaha was killed at Mu'ta, and the pilgrimage of al-Qada came after that. I say, as for the claim that the pilgrimage of al-Qada came after Mu'ta, no pilgrimage stood in Mecca after Mu'ta, on the sounder reading [the source text is corrupt at this clause]. The pilgrimage of Hudaybiyya was the one in which the idolaters barred the Messenger of Allah from the House in Dhu al-Qa'da of the sixth year, and in it the idolaters made peace with him on terms that he return to Medina and make the pilgrimage the next year, and the conquest of Mecca was in Ramadan, after the pilgrimage of al-Qada [the source's 'in' is a slip for 'after'].'"

- al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.9, p.309

Commentary

The Sources for al-Qadiyya

Shia line: Hajj al-Anbiya wa-l-A'imma, p.71, carrying the Abd al-Razzaq chain through Ja'far ibn Sulayman from Thabit from Anas, and Ibn Ishaq's line to Ibn Abbas on the three days

Other reports: al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.9, p.309, on the same report and its variants. Al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unuf, vol.4, p.76, on the naming

The clause: the truce’s own text is carried on the site’s treaty debate page

Naming: al-Qadiyya, al-Qada, and the Pilgrimage of Retaliation, with al-Suhayli preferring the last from Qur'an 2:194

The visit is thin in events and heavy in meaning, and the reports mostly keep its edges, the entry and the stay. The dating dispute al-Maqrizi records turns on the Ibn Rawaha report itself, since Ibn Rawaha died at Mu'ta. Mu'ta came in year eight, months after this visit, on the standard chronology, so Ibn Rawaha was alive at the entry, and al-Maqrizi's note keeps the report standing.

The Three Days Inside Mecca

Entry: the Prophet ﷺ and the pilgrims, in Dhu al-Qa'da of year seven, under the treaty clause

Stay: three days, by Ibn Abbas's line

The scene: Abd Allah ibn Rawaha, or Ka'b ibn Malik in the variant, reciting ahead of the Prophet ﷺ, Umar's objection, and the Prophet's ﷺ word that the verses sank into them faster than arrows

Exit: the reports record no incident in the three days

The picture the reports give is a trial of nerve inside a rite. Ibn Rawaha's verses gave the faith an open voice at the entry, and the Prophet's ﷺ word over Umar's objection is the ruling that matters, and it upheld the poetry. In the whole visit the reports record no killing, no flight, no booty, and no fight.

What the Visit Proved

Tactical: the pilgrimage made in its promised year, and three days inside Mecca

Strategic: the truce held by the Muslim side, exercised at its term, with no drawn sword on any report

Ledger: the conquest of Mecca came in Ramadan of the next year

The visit was the treaty made visible. The right the treaty wrote was exercised at its term, and the clause that had deferred the pilgrimage was honored in the visit itself. The stay ran three days, on Ibn Abbas's line.

Was the Visit Justified

The reports themselves settle this question. The march was a right the treaty had written, exercised at its term, in the garb of pilgrimage, with the stay of three days on Ibn Abbas's line. The community that had been barred from the sanctuary by force came to it by contract.

The one scene of friction, a companion's warlike verses at the entry, was closed by the Prophet's ﷺ own ruling inside the same scene. Across all three days the reports record no killing, no flight, no booty, and no fight.

Mecca could have stopped the visit only by breaking the treaty clause, and no report records a breach.
