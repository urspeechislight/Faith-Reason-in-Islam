The Return Pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya

In Dhu al-Qa'da of the seventh year the Prophet ﷺ entered Mecca as a pilgrim under the treaty clause, three days and out, the town watching from its rooftops while Ibn Rawaha recited ahead of him. Part 23 of 28 in The Battles of the Prophet.

The Entry Under the Treaty

The treaty had a clause and the Prophet ﷺ kept it to the letter. In Dhu al-Qa'da of the seventh year the Muslims came back to Mecca as pilgrims, the visit the truce had promised. The garb of pilgrimage was on, and the town stood emptied of its people for the three days of the visit. No sword was drawn. The reports keep the scene at the gate, a companion reciting verses ahead of the Prophet ﷺ, Umar scandalized, and the Prophet's ﷺ answer.▼ commentary▼ commentary

The hadith of the entry, in the Shia pilgrimage collection, on the chain of Ja'far ibn Sulayman from Thabit from Anas.▼ commentary

أنّ النبيّ ﵌ دخل مكّة في عمرة القضاء (2) وعبد اللَّه بن رواحة يمشي بين يديه وهو يقول : خلّوا بني الكفار عن سبيله * اليوم نضربكم على تنزيله ضرباً يزيل الهام عن مقيله * ويذهل الخليل عن خليله فقال له عمر : يا بن رواحة بين يدي رسول اللَّه ﵌ وفي حرم اللَّه عزَّ وجلَّ تقول الشِّعر ؟ قال النبيّ ﵌ خلِّ عنه فلهو أسرع فيهم من نضح النبل .

"'The Prophet entered Mecca in the pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya, and Abd Allah ibn Rawaha walked before him reciting, Clear the way, sons of the disbelievers, from his path. Today we strike you by his revelation, a striking that lifts the helm from its seat and stuns the friend from his friend. Umar said, Ibn Rawaha, before the Messenger of Allah and in the sanctuary of Allah, you recite poetry? The Prophet said, Leave him, Umar, for it sinks into them faster than the raining of arrows.'"

- Hajj al-Anbiya wa-l-A'imma, p.71, carrying Abd al-Razzaq from Ja'far ibn Sulayman from Thabit from Anas

عمرة القضية ويروى أيضاً : عمرة القضاء ، ويقال لها : عمرة القصاص ، وهذا الاسم أولى بها لقوله تعالى : « الشَّهْرُ الحَرَامُ بالشَّهْرِ الحَرَامِ والحُرُمَاتُ قِصَاصٌ » البقرة وهذه الآية فيها نزلت ، فهذا الاسم أولى بها

"'The pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya. It is also carried as the pilgrimage of al-Qada, and it is called the pilgrimage of Retaliation, and this name suits it best, for the word of Allah, the sacred month for the sacred month, and the sacred things are retaliation, came down about it, and so this name suits it best. And it was named the pilgrimage of al-Qada.'"

- al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unuf, vol.4, p.76

"'Abd al-Razzaq also related this hadith from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Anas, like it. And he related, in other than this hadith, that the Prophet entered Mecca in the pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya with Ka'b ibn Malik walking before him, and this is sounder in the eyes of some of the hadith scholars, because Abd Allah ibn Rawaha was killed at Mu'ta, and the pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya came after that. I say, whoever said the pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya came after Mu'ta, because after Mu'ta no pilgrimage to Mecca stands as sound. The pilgrimage of Hudaybiyya was the one in which the idolaters barred the Messenger of Allah from the House in Dhu al-Qa'da of the sixth year, and in it the idolaters made peace with him that he return to Medina and make the pilgrimage the next year, and the conquest of Mecca was in Ramadan, after the pilgrimage of al-Qadiyya.'"

- al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.9, p.309

Commentary

The Sources for al-Qadiyya

Shia line: Hajj al-Anbiya wa-l-A'imma, p.71, carrying the Abd al-Razzaq chain through Ja'far ibn Sulayman from Thabit from Anas, and Ibn Ishaq's line to Ibn Abbas on the three days

Other reports: al-Maqrizi, Imta' al-Asma', vol.9, p.309, on the same report and its variants. Al-Suhayli, Rawd al-Unuf, vol.4, p.76, on the naming

Naming: al-Qadiyya, al-Qada, and the Pilgrimage of Retaliation, with al-Suhayli preferring the last from Qur'an 2:194

The visit is thin in events and heavy in meaning, and the reports mostly keep its edges, the entry, the stay, the exit. The dating dispute al-Maqrizi records turns on the Ibn Rawaha report itself, since Ibn Rawaha died at Mu'ta, and the discussion is preserved here as the reports preserve it.

The Three Days Inside Mecca

Entry: the Prophet ﷺ and the pilgrims, in Dhu al-Qa'da of year seven, under the treaty clause

Stay: three days, by Ibn Abbas's line

The scene: Abd Allah ibn Rawaha, or Ka'b ibn Malik in the variant, reciting ahead of the Prophet ﷺ, Umar's objection, and the Prophet's ﷺ word that the verses sank into the watchers faster than arrows

Exit: the withdrawal at the term, with no incident recorded

The picture the reports give is a contest of nerves fought in ritual. The people of Mecca stood on their heights to watch the Muslims make the circuits, and Ibn Rawaha's verses answered the town's silence with the faith's open voice. The Prophet's ﷺ word over Umar's objection is the ruling that matters, the poetry served. No one was killed, no one fled, and no battle happened.

What the Visit Proved

Tactical: a completed pilgrimage at the treaty's term, three days and out

Strategic: the truce held by the Muslim side in full view of every tribe, and Mecca's people saw the faith enter their streets with no sword drawn

Ledger: within the year, Mecca opened without a battle

The visit was the treaty made visible. Every tribe that had signed al-Hudaybiyya's ten years watched the clause that hurt the Muslims most, the deferred pilgrimage, honored exactly.

Was the Visit Justified

The question barely arises, and the reports show why. The march was a right the treaty had written, exercised at its term, in the garb of pilgrimage, with the stay kept to the agreed days. The state that had been barred from the sanctuary by force came to it by contract.

The one scene of friction, a companion's warlike verses at the gate, was closed by the Prophet's ﷺ own ruling inside the same scene. In the whole visit, no man was killed, none fled, no booty was taken, and no fight happened.

Prevention would have needed Mecca to break the treaty clause it had signed, and the reports record that it kept the clause.
